Radiation impedance of a flanged pipe

A piston drives a pipe that opens through an infinite flange into free air; the open end is measured as an impedance and then replaces the outside air.

What it computes

OneSimulation reads the half solids of revolution from STEP files and solves the Helmholtz equation in the meridian plane with quadratic elements whose weights carry the factor 2 pi r. The outside air ends in a spherical-wave admittance, the harmonic pressure provider sweeps 40 Hz to 2 kHz, and a model of the pipe alone carries the measured end impedance as an admittance boundary. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

The low-frequency end correction comes out at 0.8209 a on the finest mesh (0.8212 a extrapolated) against Norris and Sheng's 0.8216 a (-0.08 %), the radiation resistance matches Rayleigh's (ka)^2/2 to 4 digits, the pipe alone with the measured impedance reproduces the input impedance of the full model to 4e-5, and the resonances (320, 970, 1620 Hz) follow the quarter-wave rule with that end correction.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Radiation impedance of a flanged pipe".

Builds the air of a pipe (radius 20 mm, length 250 mm) that opens through an infinite flange into a
hemisphere of radius 300 mm, and the air of the pipe alone, as half solids of revolution about the z
axis with the CAD objects of OneSimulation. The first is written to
flanged_pipe_radiation_impedance.step with the faces piston, wall, flange and outer; the second to
flanged_pipe_radiation_impedance_pipe.step with the faces piston, wall and opening.
"""

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d2 import Path, Profile, Segment
from onesim.cad.d3 import Revolution

radius, length, outer = 0.02, 0.25, 0.30
outlines = {
    "flanged_pipe_radiation_impedance": Path(
        (0.0, 0.0),
        (
            Segment((radius, 0.0), name="piston"),
            Segment((radius, length), name="wall"),
            Segment((outer, length), name="flange"),
            Segment((0.0, length + outer), center=(0.0, length), name="outer"),
            Segment((0.0, 0.0), name="axis"),
        ),
    ),
    "flanged_pipe_radiation_impedance_pipe": Path(
        (0.0, 0.0),
        (
            Segment((radius, 0.0), name="piston"),
            Segment((radius, length), name="wall"),
            Segment((0.0, length), name="opening"),
            Segment((0.0, 0.0), name="axis"),
        ),
    ),
}
for name, outline in outlines.items():
    Revolution(profile=Profile(outline), angle=np.pi).rotated(
        (0, 0, 1), -np.pi / 2
    ).save(f"{name}.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A rigid piston at the closed end of a pipe (radius \(a=20\) mm, length \(L=250\) mm) oscillates with velocity \(U e^{i\omega t}\). The pipe opens through an infinite rigid flange into air. For small amplitudes the pressure obeys the Helmholtz equation, and since nothing depends on the angle around the pipe axis it is solved in the meridian plane \((r,z)\):

\[\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Big(\frac{r}{\rho_0}\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}\Big) +\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_0}\frac{\partial p}{\partial z}\Big) +\frac{\omega^2}{\rho_0c^2}\,p=0 .\]
boundarycondition
piston, \(z=0\)normal velocity \(U\) into the air, \(\partial_n p=i\omega\rho_0U\)
pipe wall and flangerigid, \(\partial_n p=0\)
hemisphere of radius 0.3 moutgoing spherical wave, \(v_n=p\,(1+1/(ikr))/(\rho_0c)\)
axis \(r=0\)regular (natural in the axisymmetric form)

The open end acts on the pipe like a load impedance \(Z_L=p/v\). At low frequency it is a small resistance plus the inertia of an extra slug of air of length \(\delta\), the end correction: \(Z_L/\rho_0c\approx\tfrac12(ka)^2+ik\delta\).

Reference. Lord Rayleigh (The Theory of Sound, Vol. II, §302) gives the impedance of a flat piston of radius \(a\) in an infinite baffle,

\[\frac{Z_P}{\rho_0c}=1-\frac{2J_1(2ka)}{2ka}+i\,\frac{2\mathbf H_1(2ka)}{2ka},\qquad \delta_P=\frac{8a}{3\pi}=0.849\,a ,\]

with the Struve function \(\mathbf H_1\) (also L. E. Kinsler et al., Fundamentals of Acoustics, 4th ed., §7.5). The open end of a pipe is not a flat piston: its velocity peaks at the edge, and A. N. Norris and I. C. Sheng (J. Sound Vib. 135, 85-93, 1989) found \(\delta=0.8216\,a\) for the flanged pipe at low frequency. The pipe itself is a plane-wave transmission line,

\[Z_\mathrm{in}=\rho_0c\,\frac{Z_L+i\rho_0c\tan kL}{\rho_0c+iZ_L\tan kL},\]

which turns the computed input impedance at the piston into the load impedance of the open end.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange, RevolvedH1SpaceFactory
from onesim.physics.acoustics import (
    AcousticHarmonicPressure,
    ComplexPressurePencil,
    ConstantPressureForms,
    PressureAdmittanceBoundary,
    PressureTraceSamples,
    SampledPressureAdmittanceForms,
    SampledPressureVolumeForms,
    pressure_space_volume_samples,
)
from onesim.results.comparisons import convergence
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme
from scipy.sparse.linalg import spsolve
from scipy.special import j1, struve

theme.set()

c0, rho0, velocity = 343.0, 1.204, 1e-3
impedance = rho0 * c0
frequencies = np.linspace(40.0, 2000.0, 197)
omegas = 2 * np.pi * frequencies

Geometry and mesh

The two STEP files hold half solids of revolution: the pipe with the hemisphere of outside air, and the pipe alone. section with the plane \(y=0\) returns their meridian half-planes \(r\ge0\). The triangles are smallest at the rim of the open end, where the velocity is singular, and grow towards the hemisphere. Three meshes are made, from 2.5 mm at the rim and 25 mm (a seventh of the shortest wavelength) far away down to a quarter of those sizes.

parts = {
    "pipe and outside air": load("flanged_pipe_radiation_impedance.step"),
    "pipe alone": load("flanged_pipe_radiation_impedance_pipe.step"),
}
planes = {name: part.section((0, 1, 0)) for name, part in parts.items()}
radius, length = planes["pipe alone"].size[:2]
rim = np.array([radius, length])
levels = (1.0, 0.5, 0.25)
meshes = {
    (name, s): TriangularMesher(
        target_size=0.025 * s,
        size_field=lambda p, s=s: np.minimum(
            0.025 * s, 0.0025 * s + 0.2 * np.linalg.norm(p[:, :2] - rim, axis=1)
        ),
    ).generate(plane)
    for name, plane in planes.items()
    for s in levels
}
finest = levels[-1]

full = parts["pipe and outside air"]
view = plots.Camera(elevation=18, azimuth=-150)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(15, 6.5))
plots.geometry(
    full,
    selection={
        "piston": full.boundaries.piston,
        "pipe wall": full.boundaries.wall,
        "flange": full.boundaries.flange,
        "outer hemisphere": full.boundaries.outer,
    },
    labels=False,
    camera=view,
    title="Half of the air volume (STEP)",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes["pipe and outside air", 0.5],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"Meridian plane, middle mesh: {meshes['pipe and outside air', 0.5].cell_count} triangles "
    f"(pipe alone: {meshes['pipe alone', 0.5].cell_count})",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

RevolvedH1SpaceFactory turns the quadratic Lagrange space of the meridian plane into an axisymmetric one: every volume and boundary weight carries the factor \(2\pi r\). The volume forms come from SampledPressureVolumeForms, the boundary traces of a face from the space's trace_data. The outer hemisphere gets the spherical-wave admittance, and the piston load is \(i\omega U\int\phi_i\,\mathrm dS\); AcousticHarmonicPressure solves the sweep.

The second model is the pipe alone. Its opening carries a uniform admittance \(1/Z_L\); the impedance is either the one measured in the first model or Rayleigh's piston.

def solve_pipe(name, level, end_face, open_end):
    """Input impedance at the piston over the sweep; open_end(omega, points) is the admittance of end_face."""
    plane, mesh = planes[name], meshes[name, level]
    space = FiniteElement(
        element=Lagrange(order=2), provider=RevolvedH1SpaceFactory()
    ).bind_space(mesh)
    volume = ConstantPressureForms(
        *SampledPressureVolumeForms(
            size=space.dof_count,
            samples=lambda: pressure_space_volume_samples(space),
            inverse_density=lambda omega, sample: 1 / rho0,
            compressibility=lambda omega, sample: 1 / impedance / c0,
        )(0.0)
    )
    traces = {
        face: [
            PressureTraceSamples(space.cell_dofs[owner], points, weights, basis)
            for owner, points, weights, _, basis, _ in map(
                space.trace_data, mesh.facets(getattr(plane.boundaries, face))
            )
        ]
        for face in ("piston", end_face)
    }
    piston = SampledPressureAdmittanceForms(
        size=space.dof_count,
        samples=lambda: iter(traces["piston"]),
        admittance=lambda omega, sample: 1.0,
    )(0.0) @ np.ones(space.dof_count)
    radiation = SampledPressureAdmittanceForms(
        size=space.dof_count,
        samples=lambda: iter(traces[end_face]),
        admittance=lambda omega, sample: open_end(omega, sample.points),
    )
    result = AcousticHarmonicPressure(
        operator=ComplexPressurePencil(
            volume, boundary=PressureAdmittanceBoundary(radiation)
        ),
        load=lambda omega: 1j * omega * velocity * piston,
        angular_frequencies=omegas,
        linear_solver=spsolve,
    ).run()
    return dict(
        space=space,
        mesh=mesh,
        pressure=result.pressure,
        dofs=space.dof_count,
        z_in=result.pressure @ piston / (np.pi * radius**2) / velocity,
    )


spherical_wave = (
    lambda omega, points: (
        1 + c0 / (1j * omega.real * np.linalg.norm(points - [0.0, length], axis=1))
    )
    / impedance
)
study = {
    s: solve_pipe("pipe and outside air", s, "outer", spherical_wave) for s in levels
}
outside = study[finest]
k = omegas / c0
line_load = (
    lambda z_in: impedance
    * (z_in - 1j * impedance * np.tan(k * length))
    / (impedance - 1j * z_in * np.tan(k * length))
)
load_impedance = line_load(outside["z_in"])
rayleigh = impedance * (
    1
    - j1(2 * k * radius) / (k * radius)
    + 1j * struve(1, 2 * k * radius) / (k * radius)
)
lumped = {
    "measured impedance": solve_pipe(
        "pipe alone",
        finest,
        "opening",
        lambda omega, points: 1 / np.interp(omega.real, omegas, load_impedance),
    ),
    "Rayleigh piston": solve_pipe(
        "pipe alone",
        finest,
        "opening",
        lambda omega, points: 1 / np.interp(omega.real, omegas, rayleigh),
    ),
}

Results

The load impedance of the open end, divided by \(\rho_0c\), against Rayleigh's piston; the end correction \(\delta=\mathrm{Im}\,Z_L/(\rho_0ck)\) against both references, with the mesh study; and the input impedance of the two pipe-alone models against the full model. The measured impedance reproduces the full model; Rayleigh's flat piston has a 3 % longer end correction, which shifts the sharp resonance peaks of \(|Z_\mathrm{in}|\) by a few hertz and so changes the curve by tens of percent right at the peaks.

delta = load_impedance.imag / (impedance * k) / radius
print(

print(
    "  f [Hz]    ka      Re Z_L/rho c   Rayleigh    Im Z_L/rho c   Rayleigh    delta/a   Rayleigh delta/a"

for f in (100, 200, 400, 800, 1200, 1600, 2000):
    i = np.argmin(np.abs(frequencies - f))

        f"   {load_impedance[i].imag / impedance:11.5f}  {rayleigh[i].imag / impedance:9.5f}   {delta[i]:7.4f}"
        f"   {rayleigh[i].imag / (impedance * k[i] * radius):7.4f}"
    )
low = frequencies <= 100
corrections = [
    np.mean(line_load(study[s]["z_in"]).imag[low] / (impedance * k[low] * radius))
    for s in levels
]
trend = convergence(corrections, list(levels))
print(
    "end correction below 100 Hz on the three meshes: "

for name, run in lumped.items():
    print(
        f"pipe alone with the {name}: largest deviation of |Z_in| from the full model "
        f"{np.max(np.abs(run['z_in'] - outside['z_in']) / np.abs(outside['z_in'])):.2e} (relative, over the sweep)"
    )
peaks = [
    frequencies[i]
    for i in range(1, len(frequencies) - 1)
    if abs(outside["z_in"][i])
    > max(abs(outside["z_in"][i - 1]), abs(outside["z_in"][i + 1]))
]
quarter_wave = [
    (2 * n - 1) * c0 / (4 * (length + 0.8216 * radius))
    for n in range(1, len(peaks) + 1)
]
print(
    "resonances of |Z_in| (sweep step 10 Hz) [Hz]:",
    ", ".join(f"{p:.0f}" for p in peaks),
    "| quarter-wave with delta = 0.8216 a:",
    ", ".join(f"{q:.0f}" for q in quarter_wave),
)

Going further

Remove the flange in flanged_pipe_radiation_impedance_geometry.py (a thin pipe wall standing in the air) and write the STEP file again: the end correction drops towards Levine and Schwinger's 0.6133 a. Or move the hemisphere closer to the opening to see where the spherical-wave admittance, exact only for the monopole part of the far field, starts to cost accuracy.
