Monitoring a lithium-ion cell over a hybrid-vehicle drive cycle

A 5 Ah graphite-SiOx | NMC811 cell driven through three charge-sustaining hybrid-vehicle microcycles with pulses of up to 2.6C, then left at rest.

What it computes

OneSimulation solves the Doyle-Fuller-Newman porous-electrode model through the cell thickness on 32 particle shells and 20/10/20 layer cells; every current switch is a breakpoint of the time integration and the pulse voltage window is a cut-off event. State of charge, voltage, salt depletion and the negative-electrode potential that governs lithium plating are monitored.

What it checks

The state of charge of both electrodes equals coulomb counting to 2e-12, both electrodes return to their initial stoichiometry after the zero-net-charge cycles to all printed digits, and 30 min into the rest the voltage is 2.4 mV from the initial open-circuit voltage; the monitoring shows the voltage between 3.49 and 4.26 V, salt down to 21 mol/m³ and the negative electrode at -125 mV vs Li during engine charging, a plating risk.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

In a hybrid vehicle the battery supplies short bursts of power for acceleration and takes back energy during regenerative braking, while the engine keeps the state of charge in a narrow window. The cell sees pulses of several C in both directions. Besides voltage and state of charge, a battery management system cares about quantities that are only visible inside a physical model: the salt concentration in the electrolyte, and the potential of the negative electrode against lithium, \(\phi_s-\phi_e\), at the separator. During fast charging pulses this potential drops; when it falls below 0 V, metallic lithium can plate on the graphite.

The cell is the Doyle-Fuller-Newman model of the discharge-rest-charge example. The current profile is a synthetic charge-sustaining microcycle of 400 s: idle, acceleration bursts of up to 2.5C, cruising and regenerative braking pulses of up to 2C, then 65 s of engine charging whose current (about 2.6C) is set so that the net charge of the microcycle is zero. The microcycle is repeated three times and followed by 30 min of rest.

Reference. Cell parameters of Chen et al., J. Electrochem. Soc. 167 (2020) 080534 (LG M50, 5 Ah); model of Doyle, Fuller and Newman, J. Electrochem. Soc. 140 (1993) 1526. Checks: (i) the state of charge from coulomb counting, \(s(t)=s_0-\int I\,dt/Q\), equals the mean stoichiometry of both electrodes; (ii) after a cycle with zero net charge both electrodes return to their initial mean stoichiometry; (iii) during the final rest the voltage relaxes to the open-circuit voltage of the initial state.

import numpy as np
from onesim.physics.chemistry import (
    BatteryElectrodeLayer,
    BatteryElectrolyteLayer,
    PorousElectrodeBattery,
)
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Cell at 60 % state of charge

faraday, area, soc0 = 96485.33212, 0.1027, 0.6
negative_ocp = lambda x, T: (
    1.9793 * np.exp(-39.3631 * x)
    + 0.2482
    - 0.0909 * np.tanh(29.8538 * (x - 0.1234))
    - 0.04478 * np.tanh(14.9159 * (x - 0.2769))
    - 0.0205 * np.tanh(30.4444 * (x - 0.6103))
)
positive_ocp = lambda y, T: (
    4.4875
    - 0.8090 * y
    - 0.0428 * np.tanh(18.5138 * (y - 0.5542))
    - 17.7326 * np.tanh(15.7890 * (y - 0.3117))
    + 17.5842 * np.tanh(15.9308 * (y - 0.3120))
)
negative = BatteryElectrodeLayer(
    thickness=85.2e-6,
    porosity=0.25,
    electrolyte_diffusivity=1.7694e-10 * 0.25**1.5,
    electrolyte_conductivity=0.9487 * 0.25**1.5,
    particle_radius=5.86e-6,
    particle_diffusivity=3.3e-14,
    active_fraction=0.75,
    maximum_concentration=33133,
    initial_stoichiometry=0.0279 + soc0 * (0.9014 - 0.0279),
    reference_exchange_current=6.48e-7 * np.sqrt(1000) * 33133 / 2,
    electronic_conductivity=215,
    open_circuit_potential=negative_ocp,
)
positive = BatteryElectrodeLayer(
    thickness=75.6e-6,
    porosity=0.335,
    electrolyte_diffusivity=1.7694e-10 * 0.335**1.5,
    electrolyte_conductivity=0.9487 * 0.335**1.5,
    particle_radius=5.22e-6,
    particle_diffusivity=4e-15,
    active_fraction=0.665,
    maximum_concentration=63104,
    initial_stoichiometry=0.9077 + soc0 * (0.2661 - 0.9077),
    reference_exchange_current=3.42e-6 * np.sqrt(1000) * 63104 / 2,
    electronic_conductivity=0.18,
    open_circuit_potential=positive_ocp,
)
separator = BatteryElectrolyteLayer(
    thickness=12e-6,
    porosity=0.47,
    electrolyte_diffusivity=1.7694e-10 * 0.47**1.5,
    electrolyte_conductivity=0.9487 * 0.47**1.5,
)
cell = PorousElectrodeBattery(
    negative=negative,
    separator=separator,
    positive=positive,
    electrolyte_concentration=1000,
    transference_number=0.2594,
)
capacity = (
    faraday
    * negative.active_fraction
    * negative.thickness
    * negative.maximum_concentration
    * (0.9014 - 0.0279)
    * area
)

Drive cycle

Segments of the microcycle as (start time in s, C-rate), positive on discharge.

The switch times are passed as breakpoints, so the integrator restarts exactly at every change of the current; the pulse voltage window 2.5-4.3 V of the cell is set as cut-off and would stop the run if the cycle left it. Particles with 32 shells, layers with 20, 10 and 20 control volumes.

segments = np.array(
    [
        (0, 0.0),
        (20, 2.5),
        (35, 1.0),
        (75, -2.0),
        (85, 0.0),
        (100, 2.0),
        (112, 0.8),
        (172, -1.5),
        (186, 0.0),
        (200, 1.5),
        (230, 0.5),
        (290, -1.0),
        (305, -0.5),
        (370, 0.0),
    ]
)
period, cycles = 400.0, 3
segments[-2, 1] -= (np.diff(np.r_[segments[:, 0], period]) @ segments[:, 1]) / (
    370 - 305
)
profile = (
    lambda t: 5.0
    / area
    * segments[np.searchsorted(segments[:, 0], t % period, side="right") - 1, 1]
    * (t < cycles * period)
)
end = cycles * period + 1800.0
times = np.r_[
    np.arange(0.0, cycles * period, 1.0), np.linspace(cycles * period, end, 181)
]
switches = np.r_[
    (np.arange(cycles)[:, None] * period + segments[:, 0]).ravel(), cycles * period, end
]
run = cell.solve(
    mode="DFN",
    current_density=profile,
    final_time=end,
    radial_cells=32,
    through_cells=(20, 10, 20),
    output_times=times,
    breakpoints=switches,
    cutoff_voltage=(2.5, 4.3),
)

Monitoring and checks

charge = np.r_[0.0, np.cumsum(np.diff(times) * run.current_density[:-1])] * area
coulomb = soc0 - charge / capacity
mean = [
    np.mean(c @ np.diff(np.linspace(0, 1, 33) ** 3), axis=1)
    / layer.maximum_concentration
    for c, layer in zip(run.particle_concentration, (negative, positive))
]
soc_negative = (
    soc0
    - (negative.initial_stoichiometry - mean[0])
    * faraday
    * area
    * 0.75
    * 85.2e-6
    * 33133
    / capacity
)
soc_positive = (
    soc0
    - (mean[1] - positive.initial_stoichiometry)
    * faraday
    * area
    * 0.665
    * 75.6e-6
    * 63104
    / capacity
)
plating = run.solid_potential[:, 19] - run.electrolyte_potential[:, 19]
relaxed = positive_ocp(positive.initial_stoichiometry, 0) - negative_ocp(
    negative.initial_stoichiometry, 0
)
driving = times < cycles * period
print(f"run ended by: {run.termination_reason}")
print(
    f"net charge of one microcycle: {np.diff(np.r_[segments[:, 0], period]) @ segments[:, 1] * 5.0:.2e} A s; "
    f"rms current {area * np.sqrt(np.mean(run.current_density[driving] ** 2)):.2f} A, peaks {area * run.current_density.max():.1f} A "
    f"and {area * run.current_density.min():.1f} A"
)
print(
    f"state of charge from the particles against coulomb counting: largest difference {np.abs(soc_negative - coulomb).max():.1e} (negative), "
    f"{np.abs(soc_positive - coulomb).max():.1e} (positive)"
)
print(
    f"after {cycles} cycles: mean stoichiometries {mean[0][-1]:.7f} and {mean[1][-1]:.7f}, start {negative.initial_stoichiometry:.7f} and "
    f"{positive.initial_stoichiometry:.7f}"
)
print(
    f"state-of-charge window while driving {coulomb[driving].min():.4f} - {coulomb[driving].max():.4f}; voltage window "
    f"{run.terminal_voltage[driving].min():.3f} - {run.terminal_voltage[driving].max():.3f} V"
)
print(
    f"lowest salt concentration {run.electrolyte_concentration.min():.0f} mol/m³; lowest negative-electrode potential at the separator "
    f"{1e3 * plating.min():.1f} mV vs Li (plating below 0 mV)"
)
for minutes in (1, 5, 30):
    k = np.searchsorted(times, cycles * period + 60 * minutes)
    print(
        f"{minutes:2d} min into the rest: V - U_oc(initial) = {1e3 * (run.terminal_voltage[k] - relaxed):+.2f} mV"
    )
print(
    f"energy delivered {np.trapezoid(np.maximum(run.current_density, 0) * run.terminal_voltage, times) * area / 3600:.3f} Wh, "
    f"taken back {-np.trapezoid(np.minimum(run.current_density, 0) * run.terminal_voltage, times) * area / 3600:.3f} Wh"
)

Going further

The engine-charging phase uses up the plating margin and nearly empties the electrolyte near the positive collector. Spread the same charge over a longer, gentler phase, or lower the temperature through the exchange current and the diffusivities, and watch both margins change.
