Buckley-Leverett waterflood

Water injected at one end of an oil-filled porous column pushes the oil out with a sharp saturation front.

What it computes

OneSimulation solves incompressible immiscible water-oil flow with Corey relative permeabilities as a true 1D finite-volume model on line meshes of 50, 100 and 200 cells with a declared cross-section; front, water cut and recovery follow from the saturation field and the boundary fluxes.

What it checks

The saturation profile converges to the Buckley-Leverett solution (mean deviation 0.033, 0.018, 0.011), breakthrough appears in the first output after the predicted 0.58 pore volumes, the recovery after 1 and 2 pore volumes (0.663 and 0.756 on 200 cells) lies within 0.4 % of Welge's 0.666 and 0.759, and both phases balance their mass to below 2e-7 kg.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

When water displaces oil in a reservoir rock, both phases flow at once, each through the part of the pores it occupies. With the water saturation \(S\), the porosity \(\phi\) and the total Darcy velocity \(u\), mass conservation of the water in one dimension reads

\[\phi\,\frac{\partial S}{\partial t}+u\,\frac{\partial f(S)}{\partial x}=0,\qquad f(S)=\frac{k_{rw}(S)/\mu_w}{k_{rw}(S)/\mu_w+k_{ro}(S)/\mu_o} ,\]

where the fractional flow \(f\) follows from the relative permeabilities, here the Corey laws \(k_{rw}=S^2\), \(k_{ro}=(1-S)^2\). Capillary pressure and gravity are neglected.

Setup. A column of length \(L=100\) m, porosity 0.2, permeability 100 mD, water viscosity 1 mPa s, oil viscosity 5 mPa s, initially full of oil. Water is injected at \(x=0\) with the Darcy velocity \(u=10^{-6}\) m/s (8.6 cm per day); the outlet produces whatever arrives.

| end | condition | |---|---| | \(x=0\) (inlet) | total inflow \(u\), pure water | | \(x=L\) (outlet) | pressure 0, outflow of both phases |

Reference. S.E. Buckley and M.C. Leverett, Trans. AIME 146, 1942, 107-116: each saturation moves with the speed \(u f'(S)/\phi\), and the multivalued part is replaced by a shock at the saturation \(S_f\) where the tangent from the initial state touches \(f\) (H.J. Welge, Trans. AIME 195, 1952, 91-98):

\[f'(S_f)=\frac{f(S_f)}{S_f},\qquad t_\mathrm{bt}=\frac{\phi L}{u\,f'(S_f)},\qquad \bar S=S_2+\big(1-f(S_2)\big)\,Q\quad\text{with}\quad f'(S_2)=\frac1Q ,\]

with \(Q\) the injected pore volumes and \(\bar S\) the mean water saturation, equal to the oil recovered per pore volume.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d1 import Line
from onesim.meshing import StructuredLineMesher
from onesim.physics.geotechnics import (
    ImmiscibleReservoir,
    ReservoirFiniteVolume,
    ReservoirFluxBoundary,
    ReservoirGeometryFactory,
    ReservoirPressureBoundary,
)
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Model

The column is a line of finite volumes with a cross-section of 1 m²; the outlet is held at zero pressure. Three grids of 50, 100 and 200 cells; the results are written every 0.05 injected pore volumes up to 2.

length, porosity, velocity = 100.0, 0.2, 1e-6
rock = ImmiscibleReservoir(
    porosity=porosity,
    permeability=1e-13,
    water_viscosity=1e-3,
    oil_viscosity=5e-3,
    water_density=1000.0,
    oil_density=850.0,
    corey_exponent=2.0,
    cross_section=1.0,
)
pore_volume_time = porosity * length / velocity
injected = np.linspace(0.0, 2.0, 41)
columns = [50, 100, 200]
column = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(length, 0.0, 0.0))
meshes = {n: StructuredLineMesher(cells=n).generate(column) for n in columns}
results = {
    n: rock.solve(
        mesh=mesh,
        water_saturation=0.0,
        times=injected * pore_volume_time,
        max_step=pore_volume_time / n,
        spatial=ReservoirFiniteVolume(geometry_factory=ReservoirGeometryFactory()),
        flux_boundaries=(
            ReservoirFluxBoundary(
                boundary=column.boundaries.start, flux=-velocity, water_saturation=1.0
            ),
        ),
        pressure_boundaries=(
            ReservoirPressureBoundary(boundary=column.boundaries.end, pressure=0.0),
        ),
    )
    for n, mesh in meshes.items()
}

Reference: Buckley-Leverett and Welge

The fractional flow on a fine saturation grid, the front saturation where the chord \(f(S)/S\) from the initial state is steepest (the tangent condition), the saturation profile \(x(S)=u t f'(S)/\phi\) behind the front, and the outlet saturation, water cut and recovery after breakthrough.

fraction = lambda s: s**2 / (s**2 + (1 - s) ** 2 / 5)
s = np.linspace(0.0, 1.0, 200001)
slope = np.gradient(fraction(s), s)
front = s[1:][np.argmax(fraction(s[1:]) / s[1:])]
behind = s >= front
breakthrough = 1 / np.interp(front, s, slope)
profile = lambda q, x: np.interp(
    x / length / q, slope[behind][::-1], s[behind][::-1], right=0.0
)
outlet = lambda q: np.interp(1 / q, slope[behind][::-1], s[behind][::-1], left=1.0)
recovery = lambda q: np.where(
    q < breakthrough, q, outlet(q) + (1 - fraction(outlet(q))) * q
)

Comparison with the reference

The mean saturation in the column is the oil recovered; the breakthrough is the first output with water at the outlet cell; the profile error is the pore-volume weighted mean deviation at 0.4 injected pore volumes. Last, the mass balance of both phases: the change of the mass in the column against what entered and left through the ends.

print(
    f"front saturation S_f = {front:.4f}, breakthrough after {breakthrough:.4f} pore volumes = {breakthrough * pore_volume_time / 86400:.0f} days"
)
print(
    f"{'cells':>6s} {'profile error at Q = 0.4':>25s} {'breakthrough [PV]':>18s} {'recovery at Q = 1':>18s} {'Welge':>7s} {'recovery at Q = 2':>18s} {'Welge':>7s}"
)
errors = []
for n, result in results.items():
    x = (meshes[n].faces[:-1] + meshes[n].faces[1:]) / 2
    mean = result.water_saturation @ result.pore_volume / result.pore_volume.sum()
    errors.append(
        result.pore_volume
        @ np.abs(result.water_saturation[8] - profile(0.4, x))
        / result.pore_volume.sum()
    )
    first = injected[np.argmax(result.water_saturation[:, -1] > 0.05)]
    print(
        f"{n:6d} {errors[-1]:25.4f} {first:18.2f} {mean[20]:18.4f} {recovery(1.0):7.4f} {mean[40]:18.4f} {recovery(2.0):7.4f}"
    )
print(
    f"profile error ratio per halving of the cells: {errors[0] / errors[1]:.2f}, {errors[1] / errors[2]:.2f}"
)
print(
    "largest mass-balance error of the phases over the run [kg]: "
    + ", ".join(
        f"{np.abs(result.phase_mass - result.phase_mass[0] - (result.well_phase_volume.sum(axis=1) - result.boundary_phase_volume) * (1000.0, 850.0)).max():.1e} ({n} cells)"
        for n, result in results.items()
    )
)

Going further

Raise the oil viscosity: the front saturation drops and water breaks through early, the classical argument for polymer flooding. Add capillary_pressure_scale to smear the front, or turn the column upright and add gravity.
