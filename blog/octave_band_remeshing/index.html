What it computes

OneSimulation reads a quarter of a concentric expansion-chamber muffler from a STEP file and meshes it once per octave band with curved quadratic tetrahedra of a sixth of the band's shortest wavelength; the harmonic pressure provider sweeps 13 frequencies per band between plane-wave ports, and one fine mesh over the whole sweep is the comparison. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

The band meshes reproduce the fine-mesh transmission loss to 0.13 dB at most (0.004-0.012 dB up to the 1 kHz band) with 2.8 times less work in unknowns times frequencies; in the low bands the result matches the plane-wave formula to 0.05 dB, while above 500 Hz both meshes depart from it by the end-correction effect of the evanescent modes at the area steps.

The geometry script

The script octave_band_remeshing_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the file. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Remeshing per octave band".

Builds a quarter of the air in a concentric expansion-chamber muffler with the CAD objects of
OneSimulation: a chamber of radius 60 mm and length 250 mm between an inlet and an outlet pipe of
radius 20 mm and length 100 mm, cut in the two symmetry planes x = 0 and y = 0. It writes
octave_band_remeshing.step with the faces inlet, outlet, symmetry and walls.
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import Box, Cylinder

chamber_radius, chamber_length, pipe_radius, pipe_length = 0.06, 0.25, 0.02, 0.10
muffler = (
    Cylinder(radius=chamber_radius, length=chamber_length).named("chamber")
    | Cylinder(
        radius=pipe_radius, length=pipe_length, origin=(0.0, 0.0, -pipe_length)
    ).named("inlet_pipe")
    | Cylinder(
        radius=pipe_radius, length=pipe_length, origin=(0.0, 0.0, chamber_length)
    ).named("outlet_pipe")
)
quarter = muffler & Box(
    length=0.2, width=0.2, height=0.8, origin=(0.0, 0.0, -0.2)
).named("cut")
faces = quarter.boundaries
inlet, outlet, symmetry = (
    faces["inlet_pipe.bottom"],
    faces["outlet_pipe.top"],
    faces["cut.x_min"] | faces["cut.y_min"],
)
named = (inlet | outlet | symmetry).members
walls = quarter.select(faces=lambda face: face.id not in named)
quarter.grouped(inlet=inlet, outlet=outlet, symmetry=symmetry, walls=walls).save(
    "octave_band_remeshing.step"
)

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

The element size a frequency-domain acoustic model needs scales with the wavelength: about six quadratic elements per wavelength keep the error small. A sweep over several octaves therefore needs a mesh 32 times finer at the top than at the bottom; using that mesh everywhere costs far more unknowns than needed. Remeshing per band follows the wavelength instead: band \(b\) with upper edge \(f_b\) gets elements of size \(h_b=c/(6f_b)\), limited by what the geometry itself requires.

The test object is a concentric expansion chamber (radius 60 mm, length 250 mm) between pipes of radius 20 mm. A quarter of the air is computed, cut in the two symmetry planes.

boundarycondition
inletincoming plane wave \(p_\mathrm{in}\), reflected wave absorbed: \(v_n=(p-2p_\mathrm{in})/(\rho_0c)\)
outletanechoic, \(v_n=p/(\rho_0c)\)
walls and symmetry planesrigid, \(\partial p/\partial n=0\)

References. Concentric pipes excite only axisymmetric modes, and the first of them cuts on at \(3.8317\,c/(2\pi R)=3486\) Hz, above the sweep. At low frequency the plane-wave formula holds (L. E. Kinsler et al., Fundamentals of Acoustics, 4th ed., §10.10; M. L. Munjal, Acoustics of Ducts and Mufflers, 2nd ed., §3.5):

\[TL=10\log_{10}\Big[1+\tfrac14\big(m-\tfrac1m\big)^2\sin^2kL\Big],\qquad m=(R/r)^2 .\]

With rising frequency the evanescent higher modes at the two area steps lengthen the chamber acoustically and shift the domes of the transmission loss (A. Selamet and P. M. Radavich, J. Sound Vib. 201, 407, 1997), so the formula is the check of the low bands. The accuracy of the band meshes in every band is measured against one fine mesh, half the size of the top band's.

The octave bands follow IEC 61260: centre frequencies 125 Hz to 4 kHz halved, here 125 Hz to 2 kHz, band edges at \(f_c/\sqrt2\) and \(\sqrt2 f_c\).

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.meshing import TetrahedralMesher
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange
from onesim.physics.acoustics import (
    AcousticHarmonicPressure,
    ComplexPressurePencil,
    ConstantPressureForms,
    PressureAdmittanceBoundary,
    PressureTraceSamples,
    SampledPressureAdmittanceForms,
    SampledPressureVolumeForms,
    pressure_space_volume_samples,
)
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme
from scipy.sparse.linalg import spsolve

theme.set()

c0, rho0, incident = 343.0, 1.204, 1.0
impedance = rho0 * c0
chamber_radius, chamber_length, pipe_radius = 0.06, 0.25, 0.02
centres = 125.0 * 2.0 ** np.arange(5)
bands = {fc: np.geomspace(fc / np.sqrt(2), fc * np.sqrt(2), 13) for fc in centres}

Geometry and meshes

load returns the quarter muffler with its named faces. Each band gets a mesh of curved quadratic tetrahedra with \(h_b=c/(6f_b)\), at most 100 mm; a curved element may turn by 60° on the round walls. The fine reference mesh uses half the size of the top band throughout.

muffler = load("octave_band_remeshing.step")
faces = muffler.boundaries
size = {fc: min(0.100, c0 / (6 * np.sqrt(2) * fc)) for fc in centres}
fine = size[centres[-1]] / 2
meshes = {
    h: TetrahedralMesher(
        target_size=h, geometry_order=2, curvature_angle=np.pi / 3
    ).generate(muffler)
    for h in sorted(set(size.values()) | {fine})
}

view = plots.Camera(elevation=25, azimuth=-60)
fig = plots.figure(1, 3, size=(21, 7))
plots.geometry(
    muffler,
    selection={
        "inlet": faces.inlet,
        "outlet": faces.outlet,
        "walls": faces.walls,
        "symmetry planes": faces.symmetry,
    },
    labels=False,
    camera=view,
    title="Quarter of the muffler (STEP)",
    ax=fig[0],
)
for panel, fc in zip((fig[1], fig[2]), (centres[0], centres[-1])):
    plots.mesh(
        meshes[size[fc]],
        groups=("all",),
        legend=False,
        camera=view,
        title=f"Mesh of the {fc:.0f} Hz band: {meshes[size[fc]].cell_count} tetrahedra",
        ax=panel,
    )
plots.show()

Model

Quadratic Lagrange elements; SampledPressureVolumeForms integrates the volume forms and the port traces come from the space's trace_data. One function sets up the model on a mesh and sweeps the given frequencies with AcousticHarmonicPressure; it is called once per band with the band's mesh, and once per band with the fine mesh.

def solve_band(h, frequencies):
    """Transmission loss at the frequencies on the mesh of size h, and the number of unknowns."""
    mesh = meshes[h]
    space = FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2)).bind_space(mesh)
    volume = ConstantPressureForms(
        *SampledPressureVolumeForms(
            size=space.dof_count,
            samples=lambda: pressure_space_volume_samples(space),
            inverse_density=lambda omega, sample: 1 / rho0,
            compressibility=lambda omega, sample: 1 / impedance / c0,
        )(0.0)
    )
    ports = {
        face: SampledPressureAdmittanceForms(
            size=space.dof_count,
            admittance=lambda omega, sample: 1.0,
            samples=lambda face=face: iter(
                [
                    PressureTraceSamples(space.cell_dofs[owner], points, weights, basis)
                    for owner, points, weights, _, basis, _ in map(
                        space.trace_data, mesh.facets(getattr(faces, face))
                    )
                ]
            ),
        )(0.0)
        for face in ("inlet", "outlet")
    }
    drive = ports["inlet"] @ np.ones(space.dof_count)
    pressure = (
        AcousticHarmonicPressure(
            operator=ComplexPressurePencil(
                volume,
                boundary=PressureAdmittanceBoundary(
                    lambda omega: (ports["inlet"] + ports["outlet"]) / impedance
                ),
            ),
            angular_frequencies=2 * np.pi * frequencies,
            load=lambda omega: 2j * omega * incident / impedance * drive,
            linear_solver=spsolve,
        )
        .run()
        .pressure
    )
    transmitted = np.real(
        np.sum(pressure.conj() * (ports["outlet"] @ pressure.T).T, axis=1)
    )
    return dict(
        tl=10 * np.log10(incident**2 * drive.sum() / transmitted), dofs=space.dof_count
    )


remeshed = {fc: solve_band(size[fc], bands[fc]) for fc in centres}
reference = {fc: solve_band(fine, bands[fc]) for fc in centres}

Results

Per band: element size and unknowns of the band mesh, its deviation from the fine mesh and from the plane-wave formula, and the work in unknowns times frequencies.

m = (chamber_radius / pipe_radius) ** 2
formula = lambda f: 10 * np.log10(
    1 + 0.25 * (m - 1 / m) ** 2 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * f / c0 * chamber_length) ** 2
)
print(f"fine mesh: h = {1e3 * fine:.1f} mm, {reference[centres[0]]['dofs']} unknowns")
print(

for fc in centres:
    print(
        f"{fc:8.0f}   {1e3 * size[fc]:6.1f}   {remeshed[fc]['dofs']:8d}   {np.max(np.abs(remeshed[fc]['tl'] - reference[fc]['tl'])):23.3f}"
        f"   {np.max(np.abs(remeshed[fc]['tl'] - formula(bands[fc]))):23.3f}   {np.max(np.abs(reference[fc]['tl'] - formula(bands[fc]))):34.3f}"

work = sum(remeshed[fc]["dofs"] * len(bands[fc]) for fc in centres), sum(
    reference[fc]["dofs"] * len(bands[fc]) for fc in centres

Going further

Switch to third-octave bands (2.0 ** (np.arange(16) / 3)) for a finer staircase of meshes, or lower the elements per wavelength to see where the band meshes start to lose accuracy at their upper edges. The same pattern applies to any geometry in a STEP file, for example a room or a car cabin.
