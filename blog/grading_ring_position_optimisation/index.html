Position optimisation of grading rings on an insulator

A 1 m rod insulator at 100 kV with grading rings at both ends, whose distance from the rod ends is chosen to minimise the field on the insulator surface.

What it computes

OneSimulation cuts the meridian plane from STEP solids for seven ring positions, meshes air and silicone rod with triangles that match on the rod surface, and solves the axisymmetric electrostatic potential with quadratic elements on the revolved measure. A parabola through the best positions gives the optimum; a mesh study and the charge balance check the computation. The script that builds the STEP files comes with the notebook.

What it checks

The largest surface field falls from 1.89 kV/cm with the rings at the ends to 1.27 kV/cm at the optimum ring distance of 8.2 cm and rises to 2.58 kV/cm at 30 cm; the two finest meshes agree to 0.35 %, and the charges on the high-voltage and grounded electrodes balance to 3e-4.

The geometry script

The script grading_ring_position_optimisation_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Position optimisation of grading rings on an insulator".

Builds half of the air around a 1 m long rod insulator with the CAD objects of OneSimulation: a
cylinder of air (radius 3 m) from which the two metal end fittings (radius 6 cm) and two grading
rings (torus, ring radius 15 cm, tube radius 2 cm) are cut out. The faces of the high-voltage
fitting and its ring form the group hv, those of the grounded fitting and its ring the group ground,
the outer faces of the air the group outer. One file is written for each distance of the rings from
the ends of the rod, 0 to 30 cm: grading_ring_position_optimisation.step (10 cm) and
grading_ring_position_optimisation_00cm.step ... _30cm.step. The rod (radius 4 cm, dielectric) is
written to grading_ring_position_optimisation_rod.step; its end faces carry the names of the fittings
(hv.bottom, ground.top).
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import Box, Cylinder, Torus

rod_radius, length, fitting_radius, fitting_length = 0.04, 1.0, 0.06, 0.15
ring_radius, tube_radius, air_radius = 0.15, 0.02, 3.0
half = Box(
    length=air_radius, width=2 * air_radius, height=4.0, origin=(0.0, -air_radius, -1.5)
)
air = Cylinder(radius=air_radius, length=4.0, origin=(0.0, 0.0, -1.5)).named("air")
for offset in (0.0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30):
    hv = Cylinder(
        radius=fitting_radius, length=fitting_length, origin=(0.0, 0.0, length)
    ) | Torus(
        major_radius=ring_radius,
        minor_radius=tube_radius,
        origin=(0.0, 0.0, length - offset),
    )
    ground = Cylinder(
        radius=fitting_radius, length=fitting_length, origin=(0.0, 0.0, -fitting_length)
    ) | Torus(
        major_radius=ring_radius, minor_radius=tube_radius, origin=(0.0, 0.0, offset)
    )
    region = (air - hv.named("hv") - ground.named("ground")) & half
    region = region.grouped(
        hv=region.select(faces=lambda face: face.id.startswith("hv")),
        ground=region.select(faces=lambda face: face.id.startswith("ground")),
        outer=region.select(faces=lambda face: face.id.startswith("air")),
    )
    region.save(
        "grading_ring_position_optimisation.step"
        if offset == 0.10
        else f"grading_ring_position_optimisation_{round(100 * offset):02d}cm.step"
    )
rod = (
    Cylinder(
        radius=rod_radius,
        length=length + 2 * fitting_length,
        origin=(0.0, 0.0, -fitting_length),
    )
    - Cylinder(
        radius=fitting_radius, length=fitting_length, origin=(0.0, 0.0, length)
    ).named("hv")
    - Cylinder(
        radius=fitting_radius, length=fitting_length, origin=(0.0, 0.0, -fitting_length)
    ).named("ground")
)
(rod & half).save("grading_ring_position_optimisation_rod.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A rod insulator of length \(L=1\) m and radius 4 cm (silicone rubber, \(\varepsilon_r=4.3\)) carries a metal end fitting at each end: the upper one at the peak phase voltage \(V_0=100\) kV, the lower one grounded. Without any field control, the field on the insulator surface peaks next to the fittings. Grading rings (torus, ring radius 15 cm, tube radius 2 cm) at the potential of the neighbouring fitting take over part of the charge and lower that peak. Their distance \(s\) from the end of the rod is the design parameter: the surface field is lowest for one position, and with it the risk of flashover.

The insulator and the rings are rotationally symmetric, so the potential satisfies

\[\frac1r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Bigl(r\,\varepsilon\frac{\partial V}{\partial r}\Bigr) +\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Bigl(\varepsilon\frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\Bigr)=0\]

in the meridian plane \((r,z)\), solved with quadratic elements and the axisymmetric measure \(2\pi r\,dr\,dz\).

Half of the air around the insulator, with the fittings and rings cut out, comes from the CAD file grading_ring_position_optimisation.step (\(s=10\) cm) and from one file for each other ring position, ..._00cm.step to ..._30cm.step; the rod is grading_ring_position_optimisation_rod.step. The script grading_ring_position_optimisation_geometry.py builds them with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.

boundarycondition
upper fitting and ring (hv.*)\(V=V_0\)
lower fitting and ring (ground.*)\(V=0\)
outer faces of the air (air.*)\(D_n=0\)
axis \(r=0\) (part.x_min)symmetry, \(D_n=0\)

Reference. There is no closed form for the optimum; it is the minimum of the computed surface field over the ring distance (Kuffel, Zaengl and Kuffel, High Voltage Engineering: Fundamentals, 2nd ed., Ch. 4 on field control; IEC/TS 60815). The computation is checked by a mesh study of the surface field at the optimum and by the charge balance: without any charge leaving through the outer faces, the charge on the high-voltage electrodes must equal minus the charge on the grounded ones.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import VacuumPermittivity
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher, joined
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange, RevolvedH1SpaceFactory
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.physics.electrostatics import (
    FixedPotential,
    PoissonEquation,
    ZeroNormalElectricDisplacement,
)
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation, regionwise
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

section with the plane \(y=0\) returns the meridian half-plane of each solid. The air with the rod cut out and the rod itself are meshed with triangles that match on the rod surface and joined into one mesh, which carries the edge names of both: the ends of the rod (top, bottom) touch the fittings. The cells are 5 mm on the rod surface and on the conductors and grow with the distance.

offsets = (0.0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.30)
solids = {
    s: load(
        "grading_ring_position_optimisation.step"
        if s == 0.10
        else f"grading_ring_position_optimisation_{round(100 * s):02d}cm.step"
    )
    for s in offsets
}
rod = load("grading_ring_position_optimisation_rod.step").section((0, 1, 0))
planes = {s: solid.section((0, 1, 0)) for s, solid in solids.items()}
rod_radius, length = rod.size[:2]
selections = {
    s: {edge.id: plane.boundaries[edge.id] for edge in plane.edges}
    for s, plane in planes.items()
}
edges = lambda s, prefix: [name for name in selections[s] if name.startswith(prefix)]
mesh_for = lambda s, h: joined(
    [
        TriangularMesher(
            target_size=0.3,
            grading=0.2,
            hard_sizes={"cut.outer_wall": h},
            local_sizes={edge: h for edge in edges(s, "hv.") + edges(s, "ground.")},
        ).generate(planes[s] - rod),
        TriangularMesher(target_size=2 * h, hard_sizes={"outer_wall": h}).generate(rod),
    ],
    geometry=planes[s],
)
meshes = {s: mesh_for(s, 0.005) for s in offsets}

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 6.5))
plots.geometry(
    [
        solids[0.10].named("air"),
        load("grading_ring_position_optimisation_rod.step").named("insulator"),
    ],
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=20, azimuth=-60),
    title="Half of the air around the insulator",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes[0.10],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"Meridian plane, {meshes[0.10].cell_count} triangles",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

RevolvedH1SpaceFactory makes the quadratic Lagrange space of the meridian plane axisymmetric. The permittivity is \(4.3\,\varepsilon_0\) in the rod and \(\varepsilon_0\) in the air. Each ring position is one simulation; the position of 10 cm is also solved on two coarser meshes.

voltage, eps_r = 100e3, 4.3
eps0 = float(VacuumPermittivity().evaluate())
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2), provider=RevolvedH1SpaceFactory()),
    linear_solver=SparseDirect(),
)
runs = [(s, meshes[s]) for s in offsets] + [
    (0.10, mesh_for(0.10, h)) for h in (0.020, 0.010)
]
models = [
    PoissonEquation(
        domain=planes[s].domain(),
        permittivity=regionwise(((rod, eps_r * eps0),), default=eps0, unit="F/m"),
        charge_density=0.0,
    )
    for s, mesh in runs
]
results = [
    Simulation(
        problem=Problem(
            name="Grading rings",
            geometry=planes[s],
            physics=(model,),
            boundary_conditions=(
                *(
                    FixedPotential(
                        boundary=selections[s][edge], physics=model, value=voltage
                    )
                    for edge in edges(s, "hv.")
                ),
                *(
                    FixedPotential(
                        boundary=selections[s][edge], physics=model, value=0.0
                    )
                    for edge in edges(s, "ground.")
                ),
                *(
                    ZeroNormalElectricDisplacement(
                        boundary=selections[s][edge], physics=model
                    )
                    for edge in edges(s, "air.") + edges(s, "part.")
                ),
            ),
        ),
        mesh=mesh,
        numerics=numerics,
    ).solve_stationary()
    for model, (s, mesh) in zip(models, runs)
]

Results

The design value is the largest field on the insulator surface, read just outside the rod and leaving out 1 cm next to each fitting, where the junction of metal, insulator and air makes the field singular. A parabola through the three best positions gives the optimum.

z = np.linspace(0.01, length - 0.01, 393)
surface = np.column_stack((np.full_like(z, rod_radius + 1e-6), z))
profiles = [
    np.linalg.norm(result.field(model.electric_field).evaluate(surface), axis=1)
    for model, result in zip(models, results)
]
peaks = np.array([profile.max() for profile in profiles[: len(offsets)]])
best = np.clip(np.argmin(peaks), 1, len(offsets) - 2)
curve = np.polyfit(offsets[best - 1 : best + 2], peaks[best - 1 : best + 2], 2)
optimum = -curve[1] / (2 * curve[0])
print("ring distance s   largest surface field [kV/cm]")
for s, peak in zip(offsets, peaks):
    print(f"{100 * s:13.0f} cm {peak / 1e5:20.3f}")
print(
    f"optimum ring distance {100 * optimum:.1f} cm with {np.polyval(curve, optimum) / 1e5:.3f} kV/cm; "
    f"rings at the ends (s = 0): {peaks[0] / 1e5:.3f} kV/cm, uniform field V0/L = {voltage / length / 1e5:.3f} kV/cm"
)

study = [profiles[-2].max(), profiles[-1].max(), profiles[2].max()]
print(
    f"mesh study at s = 10 cm (20, 10, 5 mm on the rod): {', '.join(f'{v / 1e5:.4f}' for v in study)} kV/cm; "
    f"change between the two finest meshes {100 * (study[2] / study[1] - 1):+.3f} %"
)
for (s, mesh), model, result in list(zip(runs, models, results))[: len(offsets) : 3]:
    potential = result.field(model.potential)
    high = sum(potential.reaction(selections[s][edge]) for edge in edges(s, "hv."))
    low = sum(potential.reaction(selections[s][edge]) for edge in edges(s, "ground."))
    print(
        f"s = {100 * s:4.0f} cm: charge on the high-voltage side {1e9 * high:.4f} nC, on the grounded side {1e9 * low:.4f} nC, "
        f"balance {(high + low) / high:+.1e}"
    )
