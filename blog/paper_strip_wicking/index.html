Wicking in a paper strip

A dry strip of filter paper hanging with its lower edge in water draws the water up by capillary suction until gravity slows the rise.

What it computes

OneSimulation cuts the strip from a STEP file and solves the mixed-form Richards equation for the pressure head on linear triangles, with a van Genuchten retention and Mualem conductivity, the saturated water surface h = 0 at the dipped edge and gravity along the strip. Backward Euler keeps the stored water exact in every step, edge conductivities averaged over the head range keep the saturated inlet and the sharp front robust, and the uptake comes from the integral of the water content. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

After one minute the uptake is 1.840 mm against S sqrt(t) = 1.845 mm from Parlange's sorptivity (0.3 % below) and 1.805 mm from the Green-Ampt rise; after 100 minutes gravity has slowed it to 0.88 S sqrt(t), and the Green-Ampt sharp-front estimate lies 3 % below the simulation at 10 minutes and 10 % below at 100 minutes, where the real front is diffuse.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A strip of filter paper, 20 mm wide and 40 mm high, hangs with its lower edge in water. The pores of the dry paper suck the water up: fast at first, the wetted height growing with the square root of time, then ever slower as the weight of the lifted water balances the capillary suction. The water flow in the paper is unsaturated porous-media flow, the Richards equation for the pressure head \(h\),

\[\frac{\partial\theta(h)}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot\big(K(h)\,(\nabla h+\mathbf e_y)\big),\]

with the van Genuchten water retention \(\theta(h)\) and the Mualem conductivity \(K(h)\) of the paper: porosity 0.7, \(\alpha=12\) 1/m, \(n=2.5\), saturated conductivity \(10^{-6}\) m/s.

Reference. While gravity is still negligible the uptake per unit width grows as \(I=S\sqrt t\) (Lucas-Washburn behaviour, J. R. Philip, Soil Sci. 84 (1957) 257-264), with the sorptivity from Parlange's integral (J.-Y. Parlange, Soil Sci. 119 (1975) 158-161),

\[S^2=\int_{h_i}^{0}\big(\theta_s+\theta(h)-2\theta_i\big)\,K(h)\,\mathrm dh .\]

Later, gravity slows the rise. Treating the wetted zone as a sharp front driven by a constant suction \(h_f=S^2/(2K_s\Delta\theta)\) gives Washburn's rise with gravity in the Green-Ampt form (W. H. Green, G. A. Ampt, J. Agric. Sci. 4 (1911) 1-24),

\[t(L)=\frac{\Delta\theta\,h_f}{K_s}\Big(-\ln\big(1-L/h_f\big)-L/h_f\Big),\qquad \Delta\theta=\theta_s-\theta_i ,\]

an approximation for the wetted height \(L\) that is accurate when the front is sharp; the uptake is then \(I=\Delta\theta\,L\).

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.numerics.nonlinear import DampedNewton
from onesim.numerics.time import BackwardEuler
from onesim.physics.hydrodynamics import PressureHead, Richards, VanGenuchtenRetention
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.simulation.initial_conditions import InitialValue
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme
from scipy.integrate import quad

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

section cuts the loaded strip at half thickness; \(x\) runs across the strip, \(y\) upwards from the water surface. Linear triangles of 1 mm.

sheet = load("paper_strip_wicking.step")
strip = sheet.section((0, 0, 1), origin=(0, 0, 1e-4))
width, height = strip.size[:2]
mesh = TriangularMesher(target_size=1e-3).generate(strip)

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(12, 6))
plots.geometry(
    sheet,
    selection={"edge in the water": sheet.boundaries.y_min},
    labels=False,
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=20, azimuth=-60),
    title="Paper strip from the STEP file",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    mesh,
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"Mid-plane: {mesh.cell_count} triangles",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

Richards with the van Genuchten retention; gravity acts along \(-y\) of the section by default. Backward Euler keeps the storage change exact in every step (mixed form); 1200 steps of 5 s follow the first 100 minutes. The damped Newton method handles the sharp front in the dry paper.

paper = Richards(
    domain=strip.domain(),
    retention=VanGenuchtenRetention(alpha=12.0, n=2.5),
    conductivity=1e-6,
    saturated_water_content=0.7,
    residual_water_content=0.0,
)
problem = Problem(
    name="Paper strip wicking",
    geometry=strip,
    physics=(paper,),
    initial_conditions=(InitialValue(paper.pressure_head, -1.0),),
    boundary_conditions=(
        PressureHead(physics=paper, boundary=strip.boundaries.y_min, value=0.0),
    ),
)
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=1), quadrature_order=2),
    nonlinear_solver=DampedNewton(
        linear_solver=SparseDirect(),
        relative_tolerance=1e-8,
        absolute_tolerance=1e-10,
        maximum_iterations=60,
    ),
    time_integrator=BackwardEuler(),
)
result = Simulation(problem=problem, mesh=mesh, numerics=numerics).run(
    start_time=0.0, end_time=6000.0, time_step=5.0
)
water = result.field(paper.water_content)

Results

The uptake per unit width is the integral of the water content gained, divided by the strip width; it is compared with \(S\sqrt t\). The wetted height is where the water content crosses half of the porosity \(\theta_s\) above \(\theta_i\) on the centre line. The uptake is compared with \(S\sqrt t\) and with the Green-Ampt rise.

theta_i, theta_0, k_0 = (
    float(paper.volumetric_water_content(-1.0)),
    paper.saturated_water_content,
    paper.conductivity,
)
sorptivity = np.sqrt(
    quad(
        lambda h: (theta_0 + paper.volumetric_water_content(h) - 2 * theta_i)
        * paper.hydraulic_conductivity(h),
        -1.0,
        0.0,
    )[0]
)
uptake = water.integrate()
times, gained = (
    np.asarray(uptake.times),
    (np.asarray(uptake.values) - uptake.values[0]) / width,
)
y = np.linspace(0.0, height, 481)
centre = np.column_stack((np.full_like(y, width / 2), y))
wetted = lambda t: y[
    np.argmax(water.evaluate(centre, time=t) < (theta_0 + theta_i) / 2)
]
suction = sorptivity**2 / (2 * k_0 * (theta_0 - theta_i))
rise = np.linspace(0.0, 0.999 * suction, 4000)
green_ampt = lambda t: np.interp(
    t,
    (theta_0 - theta_i)
    * suction
    / k_0
    * (-np.log(1 - rise / suction) - rise / suction),
    rise,
)
print(
    f"sorptivity (Parlange): {1e3 * sorptivity:.3f} mm/s^0.5, Green-Ampt suction head {1e3 * suction:.1f} mm"
)
print(
    "   t [s]   uptake [mm]   S sqrt(t) [mm]   ratio   Green-Ampt [mm]   ratio   wetted height [mm]"
)
for t in (60.0, 120.0, 300.0, 600.0, 1200.0, 3000.0, 6000.0):
    i = int(np.argmin(np.abs(times - t)))
    ga = (theta_0 - theta_i) * green_ampt(times[i])
    print(
        f"{times[i]:8.0f} {1e3 * gained[i]:12.3f} {1e3 * sorptivity * np.sqrt(times[i]):15.3f} {gained[i] / (sorptivity * np.sqrt(times[i])):8.3f}"
        f" {1e3 * ga:16.3f} {gained[i] / ga:8.3f} {1e3 * wetted(times[i]):17.1f}"
    )

Figure

The water content in the strip after 2, 15 and 100 minutes; the uptake against \(S\sqrt t\) and the Green-Ampt rise; and the water content on the centre line at the same times.

nearest = lambda t: float(times[np.argmin(np.abs(times - t))])

Going further

Lay the strip flat (gravity=0.0 in Richards): the uptake then follows \(S\sqrt t\) for ever. Or change the pore-size parameter \(\alpha\) to see how far finer or coarser papers lift the water.
