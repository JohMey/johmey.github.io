Stresses around a multilateral well junction

A vertical well with an inclined lateral leg is drilled into rock under anisotropic in-situ stresses and mud pressure; the stresses around the junction show where the wall may fail in shear.

What it computes

OneSimulation reads half of the rock block from a STEP file, meshes it with curved quadratic tetrahedra refined on both bore walls and solves 3D linear elasticity with the far-field stresses as tractions and the mud pressure on the walls; a Mohr-Coulomb failure index is evaluated on the bore walls. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

Away from the junction the main-bore wall stresses follow the Kirsch solution (hoop stress within 1.8 %, axial within 0.7 %, radial within 3.7 % of the peak hoop stress) and the failure index 0.866 matches the plane-strain value 0.879; near the junction it rises to 1.24 on the flank of the lateral leg.

The geometry script

The script multilateral_well_stability_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the file. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Stresses around a multilateral well junction".

Builds half of a rock block (2 m x 1 m x 2 m, cut by the symmetry plane y = 0) around a vertical
main bore of 100 mm diameter with an inclined, 0.8 m long lateral leg of 80 mm diameter that branches off
at mid height, with the CAD objects of OneSimulation. The bore walls and the outer faces are named and the
part is written to multilateral_well_stability.step.
"""

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d3 import Box, Cylinder, UnionFromObjects

half = Box(length=2.0, width=1.0, height=2.0, origin=(-1.0, 0.0, -1.0))
main = Cylinder(radius=0.05, length=2.4, origin=(0.0, 0.0, -1.2))
angle = np.radians(45.0)
lateral = Cylinder(
    radius=0.04,
    length=0.8,
    origin=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0),
    axis=(np.sin(angle), 0.0, -np.cos(angle)),
)
rock = half - UnionFromObjects(objects=[main, lateral])
rock = rock.grouped(
    main_bore=rock.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "cylinder" and abs(face.radius - 0.05) < 1e-6
    ),
    lateral_bore=rock.select(
        faces=lambda face: (face.kind == "cylinder" and abs(face.radius - 0.04) < 1e-6)
        or (
            face.kind == "plane"
            and 0.1 < face.center[0] < 0.9
            and -0.9 < face.center[2] < -0.1
        )
    ),
    symmetry=rock.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "plane" and abs(face.center[1]) < 1e-9
    ),
    x_low=rock.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "plane" and abs(face.center[0] + 1.0) < 1e-9
    ),
    x_high=rock.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "plane" and abs(face.center[0] - 1.0) < 1e-9
    ),
    y_high=rock.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "plane" and abs(face.center[1] - 1.0) < 1e-9
    ),
    z_low=rock.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "plane" and abs(face.center[2] + 1.0) < 1e-9
    ),
    z_high=rock.select(
        faces=lambda face: face.kind == "plane" and abs(face.center[2] - 1.0) < 1e-9
    ),
)
rock.save("multilateral_well_stability.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A multilateral well taps several reservoir zones from one main bore through branching legs. Where a leg leaves the main bore, the rock between the two openings carries the stress that the removed rock carried before, from two directions at once. This junction is the weak spot of the well: if the stress concentration there exceeds the shear strength of the rock, the wall breaks out.

Setup. A rock block of 2 m × 2 m × 2 m; by the symmetry of the well about the vertical plane \(y=0\) only the half \(y\ge0\) is modelled. A vertical main bore of 100 mm diameter runs through the block; at mid height a lateral leg of 80 mm diameter branches off downwards at 45° in the plane \(y=0\) and ends 0.8 m from the axis. Rock with \(E=20\) GPa and \(\nu=0.25\); in-situ stresses (compression) \(\sigma_H=30\) MPa along \(x\), \(\sigma_h=20\) MPa along \(y\), \(\sigma_v=40\) MPa vertical; mud pressure \(p_w=12\) MPa in both bores. The block comes from the CAD file multilateral_well_stability.step, written by multilateral_well_stability_geometry.py with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.

| face | condition | |---|---| | x_high, y_high, z_high | traction \(-\sigma_H\), \(-\sigma_h\), \(-\sigma_v\) normal to the face | | x_low, z_low | normal displacement 0 | | symmetry | \(u_y=0\) | | main_bore, lateral_bore | mud pressure \(p_w\) |

Reference. Far from the junction and from the block faces the main bore is a hole in a plate under plane strain. With \(\theta\) measured from the \(x\) axis, the wall stresses (compression positive) are (Kirsch 1898; Fjaer et al., Petroleum Related Rock Mechanics, 2nd ed., Ch. 4)

\[\sigma_\theta=\sigma_H+\sigma_h-2(\sigma_H-\sigma_h)\cos2\theta-p_w,\qquad \sigma_r=p_w,\qquad \sigma_z=\sigma_v-2\nu(\sigma_H-\sigma_h)\cos2\theta .\]

The Mohr-Coulomb failure index \(F=\tfrac12(\sigma_1-\sigma_3)/\big(c\cos\varphi+\tfrac12(\sigma_1+\sigma_3)\sin\varphi\big)\), with cohesion \(c=10\) MPa and friction angle \(\varphi=30°\), reaches 1 where the rock fails in shear.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import IsotropicSolid
from onesim.meshing import LocalRefinement, TetrahedralMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.physics.solid_mechanics import (
    FixedDisplacementComponents,
    LinearElasticity,
    Pressure,
    Traction,
)
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns the half block with its named faces; quadratic tetrahedra of 0.15 m, refined to 15 mm on both bore walls and bound to their curved surfaces.

rock = load("multilateral_well_stability.step")
faces = rock.boundaries
radius = 0.05
mesh = TetrahedralMesher(
    target_size=0.15,
    geometry_order=2,
    refinements=(
        LocalRefinement(region=faces.main_bore | faces.lateral_bore, target_size=0.015),
    ),
).generate(rock)

view = plots.Camera(elevation=20, azimuth=-110)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(15, 6.5))
plots.geometry(
    rock,
    selection={
        "main bore": faces.main_bore,
        "lateral leg": faces.lateral_bore,
        "symmetry plane": faces.symmetry,
    },
    labels=False,
    camera=view,
    title="Half rock block around the well",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    mesh,
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    camera=view,
    title=f"{mesh.cell_count} curved tetrahedra",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

LinearElasticity with the in-situ stresses as tractions on the far faces and the mud pressure as Pressure on the bore walls, solved with a sparse direct solver.

stone = IsotropicSolid(
    density=2500.0,
    specific_heat_capacity=900.0,
    thermal_conductivity=2.5,
    youngs_modulus=20e9,
    poisson_ratio=0.25,
    thermal_expansion_coefficient=8e-6,
)
model = LinearElasticity(domain=rock.volume, material=stone)
major, minor, vertical, mud, poisson = 30e6, 20e6, 40e6, 12e6, 0.25
problem = Problem(
    name="Multilateral junction",
    geometry=rock,
    physics=(model,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        FixedDisplacementComponents(
            boundary=faces.x_low, physics=model, components=("x",)
        ),
        FixedDisplacementComponents(
            boundary=faces.symmetry, physics=model, components=("y",)
        ),
        FixedDisplacementComponents(
            boundary=faces.z_low, physics=model, components=("z",)
        ),
        Traction(boundary=faces.x_high, physics=model, value=(-major, 0.0, 0.0)),
        Traction(boundary=faces.y_high, physics=model, value=(0.0, -minor, 0.0)),
        Traction(boundary=faces.z_high, physics=model, value=(0.0, 0.0, -vertical)),
        Pressure(
            boundary=faces.main_bore | faces.lateral_bore, physics=model, value=mud
        ),
    ),
)
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2)), linear_solver=SparseDirect()
)
result = Simulation(problem=problem, mesh=mesh, numerics=numerics).solve_stationary()
stress = result.field(model.stress)

Comparison with the Kirsch solution

The wall stresses of the main bore half-way between the junction and the top face, 0.5 m above the junction, for \(0\le\theta\le180°\), against the Kirsch solution; then the failure index there and at the junction.

theta = np.linspace(0.0, np.pi, 37)
wall = np.column_stack(
    (
        radius * (1 + 2e-4) * np.cos(theta),
        radius * (1 + 2e-4) * np.sin(theta),
        np.full_like(theta, 0.5),
    )
)
tensors = lambda s: np.stack(
    [
        [s[:, 0], s[:, 3], s[:, 5]],
        [s[:, 3], s[:, 1], s[:, 4]],
        [s[:, 5], s[:, 4], s[:, 2]],
    ]
).transpose(2, 0, 1)
local = tensors(np.asarray(stress.evaluate(wall)))
hoop = -np.einsum(
    "ni,nij,nj->n",
    np.column_stack((-np.sin(theta), np.cos(theta), 0 * theta)),
    local,
    np.column_stack((-np.sin(theta), np.cos(theta), 0 * theta)),
)
radial = -np.einsum(
    "ni,nij,nj->n",
    np.column_stack((np.cos(theta), np.sin(theta), 0 * theta)),
    local,
    np.column_stack((np.cos(theta), np.sin(theta), 0 * theta)),
)
axial = -local[:, 2, 2]
kirsch = {
    "hoop": major + minor - 2 * (major - minor) * np.cos(2 * theta) - mud,
    "radial": mud + 0 * theta,
    "axial": vertical - 2 * poisson * (major - minor) * np.cos(2 * theta),
}
cohesion, friction = 10e6, np.radians(30.0)
failure = lambda s, position: (
    lambda e: 0.5
    * (e[:, 2] - e[:, 0])
    / (cohesion * np.cos(friction) + 0.5 * (e[:, 2] + e[:, 0]) * np.sin(friction))
)(np.linalg.eigvalsh(-tensors(np.asarray(s))))
for name, values in (("hoop", hoop), ("radial", radial), ("axial", axial)):
    print(
        f"{name:6s} stress: largest deviation from Kirsch {np.abs(values - kirsch[name]).max() / 1e6:.2f} MPa "
        f"({100 * np.abs(values - kirsch[name]).max() / kirsch['hoop'].max():.2f} % of the peak hoop stress)"
    )
print(
    f"hoop stress at θ = 0: {hoop[0] / 1e6:.2f} MPa (Kirsch {kirsch['hoop'][0] / 1e6:.2f}); at θ = 90°: {hoop[18] / 1e6:.2f} MPa (Kirsch {kirsch['hoop'][18] / 1e6:.2f})"
)
along_main = failure(np.asarray(stress.evaluate(wall)), None)
far_field = failure(
    np.stack(
        [
            -kirsch["radial"],
            -kirsch["hoop"],
            -kirsch["axial"],
            0 * theta,
            0 * theta,
            0 * theta,
        ],
        axis=1,
    ),
    None,
)
walls = stress.sample(along=faces.main_bore | faces.lateral_bore, subdivisions=2)
near = np.linalg.norm(walls.positions, axis=1) < 5 * radius
index = failure(walls.values[near], walls.positions[near])
worst = walls.positions[near][int(np.argmax(index))]
print(
    f"failure index on the main bore 0.5 m above the junction: {along_main.max():.3f} at θ = {np.degrees(theta[np.argmax(along_main)]):.0f}° "
    f"(Kirsch: {far_field.max():.3f})"
)
print(
    f"within 5 bore radii of the junction centre: largest failure index {index.max():.3f} at ({worst[0]:.3f}, {worst[1]:.3f}, {worst[2]:.3f}) m; "
    f"{100 * np.mean(index > 1):.1f} % of the wall samples there exceed 1"
)

Figure

Left the wall stresses of the main bore against the Kirsch solution; right the failure index on the walls of both bores. Away from the junction the main bore stays at the plane-strain value of about 0.88; the highest values, above 1, sit on the flank of the lateral leg just below the junction, where the leg cuts into the most loaded side of the main bore. The flat closed end of the lateral leg, an idealisation with a sharp edge, is left out of the junction statistics.

plots.line(

    },

Going further

Raise the mud weight until the failure index stays below 1 everywhere, turn the lateral towards the direction of the smaller horizontal stress, or replace the elastic rock by a Mohr-Coulomb plastic material to follow the breakout itself.
