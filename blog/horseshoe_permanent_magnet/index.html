Horseshoe permanent magnet

A soft-iron horseshoe whose two pole pieces are magnetized in opposite directions, solved for its 3D field.

What it computes

OneSimulation reads the air, the soft iron and the pole piece of one quarter from STEP files, embeds them in one tetrahedral mesh and solves the vector potential with second-order Nedelec elements; the remanence enters as a magnetization load on the curl space, the iron permeability region-wise. Symmetry planes carry tangential or normal field conditions. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

With the iron replaced by air the field matches the closed-form field of the two magnetized blocks within 0.5 % across the gap and 3.2 % below the magnet (47.84 mT against 48.00 mT 5 mm below the poles); with the iron the field there is 75.4 mT, spread 1.2 % over three meshes, a gain of 1.57 from the soft iron.

The geometry script

The script horseshoe_permanent_magnet_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Horseshoe permanent magnet".

Builds one quarter of a horseshoe magnet and its surroundings with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.
The horseshoe has a square cross-section of 20 mm, a half-ring of 20 mm inner radius on top and two
legs 60 mm long below it; the lowest 20 mm of each leg are the magnetized pole pieces, the rest is
soft iron. The quarter on the side x > 0, z > 0 is cut by the two symmetry planes.

Files: horseshoe_permanent_magnet.step holds the air box (the modelled region) with the face groups
symmetry_x, symmetry_z and outer; horseshoe_permanent_magnet_yoke.step holds the iron of the
quarter (half-ring and upper leg), horseshoe_permanent_magnet_pole.step the magnetized pole piece.
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import AnnularCylinder, Box

gap, width, leg, pole, air = 0.040, 0.020, 0.060, 0.020, 0.250
half_thickness = width / 2

region = Box(length=air, width=2 * air, height=air, origin=(0.0, -air, 0.0))
faces = region.boundaries
region = region.grouped(
    symmetry_x=faces.x_min,
    symmetry_z=faces.z_min,
    outer=faces.x_max | faces.y_min | faces.y_max | faces.z_max,
)
region.save("horseshoe_permanent_magnet.step")

ring = AnnularCylinder(
    inner_radius=gap / 2, outer_radius=gap / 2 + width, length=half_thickness
) & Box(length=gap / 2 + width, width=gap / 2 + width, height=half_thickness)
yoke = ring | Box(
    length=width,
    width=leg - pole,
    height=half_thickness,
    origin=(gap / 2, pole - leg, 0.0),
)
yoke.save("horseshoe_permanent_magnet_yoke.step")

Box(length=width, width=pole, height=half_thickness, origin=(gap / 2, -leg, 0.0)).save(
    "horseshoe_permanent_magnet_pole.step"
)

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A horseshoe magnet of square cross-section (20 mm) consists of a half-ring and two straight legs. The lowest 20 mm of each leg are pole pieces of a rare-earth magnet (remanence \(B_r=1.2\) T), magnetized along the leg and in opposite directions: one pole piece points down, the other up. The rest of the horseshoe is soft iron (\(\mu_r=1000\)) that carries the flux from one pole piece to the other, so that the field concentrates in the gap between the poles.

There are no free currents. The magnet enters through its magnetization \(\mathbf M\) in \(\mathbf B=\mu(\mathbf H+\mathbf M)\), with \(\mathbf M=B_r/\mu_0\) along the leg in the pole pieces and zero elsewhere, and OneSimulation solves

\[\nabla\times\big(\mu^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf A\big)=\nabla\times\mathbf M,\qquad \mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A\]

with second-order Nédélec edge elements and a Coulomb gauge. The recoil permeability of the magnet is taken as 1.

One quarter is modelled. The air box comes from horseshoe_permanent_magnet.step with the face groups symmetry_x, symmetry_z and outer; the iron of the quarter and its pole piece come from horseshoe_permanent_magnet_yoke.step and horseshoe_permanent_magnet_pole.step. The script horseshoe_permanent_magnet_geometry.py builds all three with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.

| faces | condition | |---|---| | symmetry_z, the mid-plane of the horseshoe | field tangential, \(\mathbf n\times\mathbf A=0\) | | symmetry_x, the plane between the legs | field normal, \(\mathbf n\times\mathbf H=0\) | | outer, 25 cm from the magnet | \(\mathbf n\times\mathbf A=0\) |

Reference. A uniformly magnetized block in air acts like two sheets of magnetic charge \(\pm\mathbf M\cdot\mathbf n\) on its end faces, whose field has a closed form (E. P. Furlani, Permanent Magnet and Electromechanical Devices, Academic Press 2001, Sec. 4.3; Akoun and Yonnet, IEEE Trans. Magn. 20, 1984). With the iron replaced by air the model must reproduce the field of the two pole blocks exactly. With the iron there is no closed form; a mesh study checks the result.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d3 import Box
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import VacuumPermeability
from onesim.meshing import LocalRefinement, TetrahedralMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Nedelec
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.physics.magnetostatics import (
    CoulombGauge,
    MagneticFieldBoundary,
    MagneticInsulation,
    MagneticVectorPotential,
    MagnetizationContribution,
    Magnetostatics,
    SampledMagnetizationLoads,
    curl_magnetization_load,
    curl_space_magnetization_patches,
)
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation, regionwise
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns the air box with its named faces, the iron and the pole piece. The mesher embeds both bodies in the mesh of the air, refines them and the air around the pole pieces, and grows the cells towards the outer faces. Three meshes differ in the cell size in and around the magnet.

air, yoke, pole = (
    load(f"horseshoe_permanent_magnet{name}.step") for name in ("", "_yoke", "_pole")
)
faces = air.boundaries
low, high = (np.array(corner) for corner in pole.bounding_box)
sizes = (0.0060, 0.0045, 0.0034)
meshes = [
    TetrahedralMesher(
        target_size=0.05,
        embedded_regions=(yoke, pole),
        refinements=(
            LocalRefinement(region=yoke, target_size=size),
            LocalRefinement(region=pole, target_size=size),
            LocalRefinement(
                region=Box(
                    length=0.06, width=0.06, height=0.03, origin=(0.0, -0.09, 0.0)
                ),
                target_size=1.5 * size,
            ),
        ),
    ).generate(air)
    for size in sizes
]

view = plots.Camera(elevation=25, azimuth=-60)
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 6))
plots.geometry(
    [yoke.named("soft iron"), pole.named("pole piece")],
    camera=view,
    title="Quarter of the horseshoe",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes[0],
    groups=("symmetry_z",),
    legend=False,
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=90, azimuth=-90),
    title=f"Coarsest mesh: {meshes[0].cell_count} tetrahedra (mid-plane)",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

The magnetization is \(B_r/\mu_0\) along \(-y\) in the pole piece of the quarter (its mirror image points along \(+y\)). The permeability is \(\mu_0\mu_r\) in the iron, which regionwise assigns from a test of the half-ring and the upper leg. The magnetization enters the weak form as \(\int\mathbf M\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf v\,\mathrm dV\) on the curl space of the edge elements.

remanence, mu_iron = 1.2, 1000.0
mu0 = float(VacuumPermeability().evaluate())
in_pole = lambda points: np.all((points >= low) & (points <= high), axis=1)
radius = lambda points: np.hypot(points[:, 0], points[:, 1])
in_iron = lambda points: (points[:, 2] <= high[2]) & np.where(
    points[:, 1] > 0,
    (radius(points) >= low[0]) & (radius(points) <= high[0]),
    (points[:, 1] >= high[1]) & (points[:, 0] >= low[0]) & (points[:, 0] <= high[0]),
)
magnetization = MagnetizationContribution(
    lambda points, time: np.where(
        in_pole(points)[:, None], (0.0, -remanence / mu0, 0.0), 0.0
    ),
    SampledMagnetizationLoads(
        curl_space_magnetization_patches,
        lambda points, cell, offset, context: offset(points, context.time),
        curl_magnetization_load,
    ),
)
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Nedelec(order=2)), linear_solver=SparseDirect()
)

models = {
    mu_r: Magnetostatics(
        domain=air.volume,
        formulation=MagneticVectorPotential(gauge=CoulombGauge()),
        magnetization_provider=magnetization,
        permeability=regionwise(((in_iron, mu0 * mu_r),), default=mu0, unit="H/m"),
    )
    for mu_r in (1.0, mu_iron)
}
problems = {
    mu_r: Problem(
        name="Horseshoe magnet",
        geometry=air,
        physics=(model,),
        boundary_conditions=(
            MagneticInsulation(boundary=faces.symmetry_z | faces.outer, physics=model),
            MagneticFieldBoundary(
                boundary=faces.symmetry_x, physics=model, value=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)
            ),
        ),
    )
    for mu_r, model in models.items()
}
without_iron = (
    Simulation(problem=problems[1.0], mesh=meshes[-1], numerics=numerics)
    .solve_stationary()
    .field(models[1.0].magnetic_flux_density)
)
with_iron = [
    Simulation(problem=problems[mu_iron], mesh=mesh, numerics=numerics)
    .solve_stationary()
    .field(models[mu_iron].magnetic_flux_density)
    for mesh in meshes
]

Validation without the iron

The closed-form field of the two pole blocks of the full horseshoe, along the line across the gap at the height of the pole pieces and along the axis of symmetry below the magnet. Points closer than 1.5 mm to a pole face, where the field jumps, are left out of the deviation. block is the charge-sheet formula of Furlani (2001, Sec. 4.3) for a block magnetized along \(y\), summed over its two end faces and four corners.

block = (
    lambda p, low, high, br: br
    / (4 * np.pi)
    * np.einsum(
        "fab,pkfab->pk",
        np.multiply.outer((1, -1), ((1, -1), (-1, 1))),
        np.stack(
            (
                np.log(
                    (
                        du := np.reshape((low[2], high[2]), (2, 1))
                        - p[:, None, None, None, 2]
                    )
                    + (
                        r := np.sqrt(
                            du**2
                            + (dv := (low[0], high[0]) - p[:, None, None, None, 0]) ** 2
                            + (
                                dw := p[:, None, None, None, 1]
                                - np.reshape((high[1], low[1]), (2, 1, 1))
                            )
                            ** 2
                        )
                    )
                ),
                np.arctan2(du * dv, dw * r),
                np.log(dv + r),
            ),
            axis=1,
        ),
    )
)
right = (np.array((low[0], low[1], -high[2])), high)
left = (np.array((-high[0], low[1], -high[2])), np.array((-low[0], high[1], high[2])))
blocks = lambda points: block(points, *right, -remanence) + block(
    points, *left, remanence
)
middle = (low[1] + high[1]) / 2
lines = {
    "across the gap": np.column_stack(
        (np.linspace(0.001, 0.07, 70), np.full(70, middle), np.full(70, 0.001))
    ),
    "below the magnet": np.column_stack(
        (np.full(70, 0.001), np.linspace(-0.065, -0.2, 70), np.full(70, 0.001))
    ),
}
print(
    "line               largest deviation from the closed form   field at the start of the line"
)
for label, points in lines.items():
    exact, computed = blocks(points), without_iron.evaluate(points)
    away = np.all(np.abs(points[:, :1] - np.array((low[0], high[0]))) > 0.0015, axis=1)
    print(
        f"{label:18s} {100 * np.max(np.linalg.norm(computed - exact, axis=1)[away]) / np.max(np.linalg.norm(exact, axis=1)):18.2f} % of the largest field"
        f"   {1e3 * np.linalg.norm(computed[0]):8.2f} mT (closed form {1e3 * np.linalg.norm(exact[0]):8.2f} mT)"
    )

Results with the soft iron

The field strength 5 mm below the opening between the pole ends on the three meshes, and the gain from the iron.

centre = np.array([[0.001, low[1] - 0.005, 0.001]])
gap_field = [np.linalg.norm(field.evaluate(centre)) for field in with_iron]
print("cell size [mm]   tetrahedra   |B| 5 mm below the pole ends [mT]")
for size, mesh, value in zip(sizes, meshes, gap_field):
    print(f"{1e3 * size:14.1f} {mesh.cell_count:12d} {1e3 * value:22.2f}")
print(
    f"spread over the three meshes {100 * (max(gap_field) - min(gap_field)) / gap_field[-1]:.2f} % of the finest value"
)
print(
    f"without the iron: {1e3 * np.linalg.norm(without_iron.evaluate(centre)):.2f} mT (closed form {1e3 * np.linalg.norm(blocks(centre)):.2f} mT); "
    f"the soft iron raises the field there by a factor {gap_field[-1] / np.linalg.norm(blocks(centre)):.2f}"
)

Figure

The field strength in the mid-plane of the horseshoe on a logarithmic scale (the quarter mirrored), and the field across the gap with and without the iron.

strength = lambda B, position: 1e3 * np.linalg.norm(B, axis=1)
gap_line = lines["across the gap"]
fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(15, 6.5))
plots.surface(
    with_iron[-1],
    boundary=faces.symmetry_z,
    component=lambda B, position: np.log10(np.maximum(strength(B, position), 1e-3)),
    symmetry={"planes": [((1, 0, 0), (0, 0, 0))]},
    limits=(0, 3),
    contours=13,
    subdivisions=2,
    label="log10(|B| / mT)",
    title="Field strength in the mid-plane",
    xlimits=(-0.1, 0.1),
    ylimits=(-0.13, 0.07),
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.line(
    points={
        "with soft iron": (
            1e3 * gap_line[::3, 0],
            strength(with_iron[-1].evaluate(gap_line[::3]), None),
        ),
        "without iron": (
            1e3 * gap_line[::3, 0],
            strength(without_iron.evaluate(gap_line[::3]), None),
        ),
    },
    reference={
        "closed form without iron": (
            1e3 * gap_line[:, 0],

        )
    },
    vlines={"pole face": 1e3 * low[0]},

)
plots.show()
