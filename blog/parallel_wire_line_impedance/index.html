Characteristic impedance of a parallel-wire line

The capacitance, inductance and characteristic impedance of two 0.5 mm wires 5 mm apart in air, a balanced transmission line whose field reaches into the open surroundings.

What it computes

OneSimulation cuts the air read from STEP to its cross-section, meshes it with quadratic triangles graded from the wires and solves the electrostatic field of the TEM wave with antisymmetric wire voltages. Capacitance follows from the wire charge and from the field energy, inductance from L'C' = mu0 eps0; three air cylinders of 25, 50 and 125 mm radius extrapolate the grounded far boundary away. The script that builds the STEP files comes with the notebook.

What it checks

Charge and energy give the same capacitance; the grounded far boundary lowers the impedance by 0.82, 0.19 and 0.017 % at 25, 50 and 125 mm, falling at the observed order 2.01, and the extrapolated unbounded line gives 274.946 against the closed form 274.902 ohm (+0.016 %); the potential through both wires follows the line-charge solution within 0.14 % of V.

The geometry script

The script parallel_wire_line_impedance_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Characteristic impedance of a parallel-wire line".

Builds a 1 mm long piece of the air around a parallel-wire line with the CAD objects of
OneSimulation: two wires of 0.5 mm radius, 5 mm apart (centre to centre), inside an air cylinder
that stands in for the unbounded surroundings. The air cylinder of 125 mm radius is written to
parallel_wire_line_impedance.step, smaller ones of 50 mm and 25 mm radius to
parallel_wire_line_impedance_r50.step and parallel_wire_line_impedance_r25.step. The faces keep the
names left.outer_wall and right.outer_wall (the wire surfaces) and outer_wall (the far boundary).
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import Cylinder

wire, spacing, length = 0.5e-3, 5.0e-3, 1.0e-3
for far, suffix in ((125e-3, ""), (50e-3, "_r50"), (25e-3, "_r25")):
    air = Cylinder(radius=far, length=length)
    for name, x in (("left", -spacing / 2), ("right", spacing / 2)):
        air = air - Cylinder(radius=wire, length=length, origin=(x, 0.0, 0.0)).named(
            name
        )
    air.save(f"parallel_wire_line_impedance{suffix}.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Two parallel round wires of radius \(a\) with centre spacing \(D\) in air form a balanced transmission line (twin lead). Unlike a coaxial cable, the fields are not enclosed: they reach into the unbounded surroundings and decay away from the line. For the TEM wave the transverse electric field solves Laplace's equation of the cross-section,

\[\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_0\nabla\phi)=0,\qquad \phi=\pm V/2\ \text{on the two wires},\]

and the magnetic field is the electric field turned by 90° and divided by \(\eta_0=\sqrt{\mu_0/\varepsilon_0}\). The capacitance per metre follows from the charge on one wire, \(C'=Q'/V\), or from the field energy \(W'=\tfrac12C'V^2\); the inductance from \(L'C'=\mu_0\varepsilon_0\); and the characteristic impedance is \(Z_0=\sqrt{L'/C'}\).

quantityvalue
wire radius \(a\)0.5 mm
centre spacing \(D\)5 mm
surroundingsair, truncated at radii of 25, 50 and 125 mm, grounded

The air comes from the CAD files parallel_wire_line_impedance.step (125 mm), ..._r50.step and ..._r25.step with the faces left.outer_wall, right.outer_wall and outer_wall, written by parallel_wire_line_impedance_geometry.py with the CAD objects of OneSimulation. The model lives on its cross-section. With antisymmetric wire voltages the potential far away vanishes, so grounding the far boundary changes the result only by the order of \((D/R)^2\).

Reference (D. M. Pozar, Microwave Engineering, 4th ed., Wiley 2012, Table 2.1; S. Ramo, J. R. Whinnery, T. Van Duzer, Fields and Waves in Communication Electronics, 3rd ed., Wiley 1994, §1.16). The field outside the wires is that of two line charges at \(x=\pm s\) with \(s=\sqrt{D^2/4-a^2}\); the wire surfaces are equipotential circles of this pair, and

\[C'=\frac{\pi\varepsilon_0}{\operatorname{arccosh}(D/2a)},\qquad Z_0=\frac{\eta_0}{\pi}\operatorname{arccosh}\frac{D}{2a},\qquad \phi(x,0)=\frac{V}{2\operatorname{arccosh}(D/2a)}\ln\left|\frac{x+s}{x-s}\right| .\]
import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import VacuumPermeability, VacuumPermittivity
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.physics.electrostatics import FixedPotential, PoissonEquation
from onesim.results.comparisons import convergence
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

load returns the air pieces; section cuts each at half length. Quadratic triangles grow from the wires to the far boundary. The figure shows the smallest piece, in which the wires are visible.

parts = {
    radius: load(f"parallel_wire_line_impedance{suffix}.step")
    for radius, suffix in ((0.125, ""), (0.050, "_r50"), (0.025, "_r25"))
}
sections = {
    radius: part.section((0, 0, 1), origin=(0, 0, part.size[2] / 2))
    for radius, part in parts.items()
}
surface = {face.id: face for face in parts[0.125].faces}
a, spacing = (
    surface["right.outer_wall"].radius,
    surface["right.outer_wall"].center[0] - surface["left.outer_wall"].center[0],
)
meshes = {
    radius: TriangularMesher(
        target_size=radius / 12,
        grading=0.2,
        local_sizes={"left.outer_wall": 0.08e-3, "right.outer_wall": 0.08e-3},
        size_field=lambda points, radius=radius: np.clip(
            0.15 * np.hypot(points[:, 0], points[:, 1]), 0.2e-3, radius / 12
        ),
    ).generate(section)
    for radius, section in sections.items()
}

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(13, 5.5))
plots.geometry(
    parts[0.025],
    selection={
        "wires": parts[0.025].boundaries["left.outer_wall"]
        | parts[0.025].boundaries["right.outer_wall"]
    },
    labels=False,
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=35, azimuth=-60),
    title="Air around the line, 25 mm radius",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    meshes[0.025],
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"{meshes[0.025].cell_count} triangles",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

One electrostatic solve per air cylinder with \(+V/2\) and \(-V/2\) on the wires and the far boundary grounded; the reaction of the potential on the right wire is its charge per metre.

eps0, mu0, voltage = (
    float(VacuumPermittivity().evaluate()),
    float(VacuumPermeability().evaluate()),
    1.0,
)
models = {
    radius: PoissonEquation(
        domain=section.domain(), permittivity=VacuumPermittivity(), charge_density=0.0
    )
    for radius, section in sections.items()
}
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2)), linear_solver=SparseDirect()
)
results = {
    radius: Simulation(
        problem=Problem(
            name="Parallel-wire line",
            geometry=section,
            physics=(models[radius],),
            boundary_conditions=(
                FixedPotential(
                    boundary=section.boundaries["right.outer_wall"],
                    physics=models[radius],
                    value=voltage / 2,
                ),
                FixedPotential(
                    boundary=section.boundaries["left.outer_wall"],
                    physics=models[radius],
                    value=-voltage / 2,
                ),
                FixedPotential(
                    boundary=section.boundaries.outer_wall,
                    physics=models[radius],
                    value=0.0,
                ),
            ),
        ),
        mesh=meshes[radius],
        numerics=numerics,
    ).solve_stationary()
    for radius, section in sections.items()
}

Line constants and impedance

The grounded far boundary pulls a little extra charge onto the wires; the effect falls off as \((D/R)^2\), so the three air cylinders extrapolate to the unbounded line.

closed_capacitance = np.pi * eps0 / np.arccosh(spacing / (2 * a))
closed_impedance = np.sqrt(mu0 / eps0) / np.pi * np.arccosh(spacing / (2 * a))
energy = lambda E, position: 0.5 * eps0 * np.sum(E**2, axis=1)
charge = {
    radius: result.field(models[radius].potential).reaction(
        sections[radius].boundaries["right.outer_wall"]
    )
    / voltage
    for radius, result in results.items()
}
print(
    "air radius   triangles   C' from Q [pF/m]   C' from W [pF/m]   Z0 [ohm]   deviation of Z0"
)
for radius, result in results.items():
    from_energy = (
        2
        * result.field(models[radius].electric_field).integrate(component=energy).value
        / voltage**2
    )
    impedance = np.sqrt(mu0 * eps0) / charge[radius]
    print(
        f"{1e3 * radius:7.0f} mm {meshes[radius].cell_count:11d} {1e12 * charge[radius]:18.4f} {1e12 * from_energy:18.4f} {impedance:10.4f} "
        f"{100 * (impedance / closed_impedance - 1):+13.4f} %"
    )
study = convergence(list(charge.values()), [spacing / radius for radius in charge])
extrapolated = np.sqrt(mu0 * eps0) / study.extrapolated
print(
    f"unbounded line (Richardson extrapolation in D/R): C' = {1e12 * study.extrapolated:.4f} pF/m, Z0 = {extrapolated:.4f} ohm "
    f"({100 * (extrapolated / closed_impedance - 1):+.4f} %); observed order {study.order:.2f}"
)
print(
    f"closed form: C' = {1e12 * closed_capacitance:.4f} pF/m, L' = {1e9 * mu0 * eps0 / closed_capacitance:.2f} nH/m, Z0 = {closed_impedance:.4f} ohm"
)

s = np.sqrt(spacing**2 / 4 - a**2)
line_charge = (
    lambda x: voltage
    / (2 * np.arccosh(spacing / (2 * a)))
    * np.log(np.abs((x + s) / (x - s)))
)
x = np.concatenate(
    (
        np.linspace(-20e-3, -spacing / 2 - a * 1.02, 30),
        np.linspace(-spacing / 2 + a * 1.02, spacing / 2 - a * 1.02, 15),
        np.linspace(spacing / 2 + a * 1.02, 20e-3, 30),
    )
)
potential = (
    results[0.125]
    .field(models[0.125].potential)
    .evaluate(np.column_stack((x, np.zeros_like(x))))
    .ravel()
)
print(
    f"potential on the x axis against the line-charge solution: largest deviation {100 * np.abs(potential - line_charge(x)).max() / voltage:.3f} % of V"
)
