Induction currents from a circular coil in a copper ring

A circular coil induces currents in a coaxial short-circuited copper ring, alternating from 10 Hz to 10 kHz and after switching on.

What it computes

OneSimulation cuts the meridian section from STEP parts, meshes it so that the triangles follow coil and ring, and solves the eddy-current equation for the azimuthal vector potential: time-harmonic at four frequencies and transient with second-order BDF after a current step. The ring is a massive conductor with zero terminal voltage, so its current and its distribution follow from the field. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

Against the circuit model of two coupled loops (Grover's mutual and self inductance) the ring current deviates by -0.24 %, -0.35 % and -0.02 % at 10 Hz, 100 Hz and 1 kHz with matching phase, and by +5.65 % at 10 kHz, where the 0.66 mm skin depth breaks the uniform-current assumption of the circuit model; after switch-on the ring current decays with 1.194 ms against L2/R2 = 1.192 ms (+0.2 %).

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A circular coil of 100 turns (winding section 10 mm × 10 mm, mean radius 45 mm) sits 20 mm above a coaxial copper ring of square section \(s=6\) mm (mean radius \(R=43\) mm, \(\sigma=5.8\cdot10^7\) S/m) whose ends are short-circuited. A changing coil current induces a voltage in the ring, and the ring current flows so as to oppose the change of flux (Lenz's rule).

OneSimulation solves the eddy-current equation for the azimuthal vector potential on the meridian half-plane with quadratic elements (EddyCurrents with the revolved space):

\[\nabla\times\big(\mu_0^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf A\big)+\sigma\,\partial_t\mathbf A=\mathbf J_s .\]

The coil is a stranded winding with a prescribed current; the ring is a massive conductor with zero terminal voltage, so its current and its distribution over the section follow from the field (Coil(..., voltage=0.0, conductor="massive")). Two analyses are made: time-harmonic at 10 Hz to 10 kHz, and transient after the coil current is switched on to 1 A.

The domain is the meridian section of induction_currents_coil_and_ring.step, a cylinder of air of 0.3 m radius; coil and ring are the sections of _coil.step and _ring.step, which the mesh follows. The script induction_currents_coil_and_ring_geometry.py builds them with the CAD objects of OneSimulation. The far boundary carries \(A_\varphi=0\).

Reference. Coil and ring as two coupled loops (Grover, Inductance Calculations, 1946): the mutual inductance of coaxial circles of radii \(r_1\), \(r_2\) at the distance \(d\) is \(M=\mu_0\sqrt{r_1r_2}\,[(2/m-m)K(m)-(2/m)E(m)]\) with \(m^2=4r_1r_2/((r_1+r_2)^2+d^2)\); the ring has \(L_2=\mu_0R[\ln(8R/g)-2]\) with the geometric mean distance \(g=0.447s\) of its square section, and \(R_2=2\pi R/(\sigma s^2)\). Then

\[I_2=-\frac{\mathrm j\omega MNI_1}{R_2+\mathrm j\omega L_2}\quad\text{(harmonic)},\qquad i_2(t)=-\frac{MNI_1}{L_2}\,e^{-t/\tau},\ \tau=\frac{L_2}{R_2}\quad\text{(switch-on)}.\]

The circuit model assumes a uniform current over the ring section; once the skin depth falls below the section size the field computation departs from it.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import VacuumPermeability, regionwise
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange, RevolvedH1SpaceFactory
from onesim.numerics.linear import ComplexSparseDirect, SparseDirect
from onesim.numerics.time import BDF
from onesim.physics.magnetostatics import (
    Coil,
    EddyCurrents,
    MagneticInsulation,
    coil_current,
)
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme
from scipy.special import ellipe, ellipk

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

section cuts the parts in the plane \(y=0\); the section coordinates are \((r, z)\). The mesh follows coil and ring, is fine in the ring and grows towards the far boundary.

meridian = lambda name: load(f"induction_currents_coil_and_ring{name}.step").section(
    (0, 1, 0)
)
domain, winding, ring = meridian(""), meridian("_coil"), meridian("_ring")
axis = domain.select(
    edges=lambda edge: all(abs(point[0]) < 1e-12 for point in edge.points)
)
mesh = TriangularMesher(
    target_size=0.03,
    grading=0.25,
    embedded_regions=(winding.named("coil"), ring.named("ring")),
    region_sizes=(("coil", 0.002), ("ring", 0.0003)),
).generate(domain)

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 6))
plots.geometry(
    [
        load(f"induction_currents_coil_and_ring{name}.step").named(label)
        for name, label in (("_coil", "coil"), ("_ring", "copper ring"))
    ],
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=25, azimuth=-60),
    title="Coil and ring (x > 0)",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    mesh,
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"Meridian section: {mesh.cell_count} triangles",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Time-harmonic analysis

Coil current 1 A (peak) with 100 turns; one solve per frequency.

sigma, turns, current = 5.8e7, 100, 1.0
mu0 = float(VacuumPermeability().evaluate())
conductivity = regionwise({"ring": sigma}, default=0.0, unit="S/m")
revolved = FiniteElement(
    element=Lagrange(order=2), quadrature_order=4, provider=RevolvedH1SpaceFactory()
)
frequencies = (10.0, 100.0, 1000.0, 10000.0)
rings = {f: Coil(ring, voltage=0.0, conductor="massive") for f in frequencies}
harmonic = {
    f: EddyCurrents(
        domain=domain.domain(),
        permeability=VacuumPermeability(),
        conductivity=conductivity,
        frequency=f,
        coils=(Coil(winding, turns=turns, current=current), rings[f]),
    )
    for f in frequencies
}
ring_current = {
    f: coil_current(
        Simulation(
            problem=Problem(
                name="Coil and ring",
                geometry=domain,
                physics=(model,),
                boundary_conditions=(
                    MagneticInsulation(
                        boundary=domain.boundaries.all - axis, physics=model
                    ),
                ),
            ),
            mesh=mesh,
            numerics=Numerics(spatial=revolved, linear_solver=ComplexSparseDirect()),
        ).solve_stationary(),
        model,
        rings[f],
    )
    for f, model in harmonic.items()
}

Transient analysis

The coil current is switched on to 1 A at \(t=0\); second-order BDF steps of \(\tau/20\) up to \(4\tau\).

r1, r2, s, d = 0.045, 0.043, 0.006, 0.020
m2 = 4 * r1 * r2 / ((r1 + r2) ** 2 + d**2)
M = (
    mu0
    * np.sqrt(r1 * r2)
    * ((2 / np.sqrt(m2) - np.sqrt(m2)) * ellipk(m2) - 2 / np.sqrt(m2) * ellipe(m2))
)
L2 = mu0 * r2 * (np.log(8 * r2 / (0.447 * s)) - 2)
R2 = 2 * np.pi * r2 / (sigma * s**2)
tau = L2 / R2
switched_ring = Coil(ring, voltage=0.0, conductor="massive")
transient = EddyCurrents(
    domain=domain.domain(),
    permeability=VacuumPermeability(),
    conductivity=conductivity,
    coils=(Coil(winding, turns=turns, current=current), switched_ring),
)
history = Simulation(
    problem=Problem(
        name="Coil switch-on",
        geometry=domain,
        physics=(transient,),
        boundary_conditions=(
            MagneticInsulation(
                boundary=domain.boundaries.all - axis, physics=transient
            ),
        ),
    ),
    mesh=mesh,
    numerics=Numerics(
        spatial=revolved, linear_solver=SparseDirect(), time_integrator=BDF(order=2)
    ),
).run(start_time=0.0, end_time=4 * tau, time_step=tau / 20)
times = np.array(history.times)
induced = np.array(
    [coil_current(history, transient, switched_ring, time=t) for t in times]
)

Results

circuit = (
    lambda f: -1j * 2 * np.pi * f * M * turns * current / (R2 + 1j * 2 * np.pi * f * L2)
)
print(
    f"circuit model: M = {1e9 * M:.2f} nH, L2 = {1e9 * L2:.2f} nH, R2 = {1e6 * R2:.2f} µOhm, tau = {1e3 * tau:.3f} ms"
)
print(
    "f [Hz]   skin depth [mm]   |I2| simulation [A]   circuit [A]   deviation   phase simulation / circuit [deg]"
)
for f in frequencies:
    print(
        f"{f:7.0f} {1e3 * np.sqrt(2 / (2 * np.pi * f * mu0 * sigma)):15.2f} {abs(ring_current[f]):20.4f} {abs(circuit(f)):13.4f} "
        f"{100 * (abs(ring_current[f]) / abs(circuit(f)) - 1):+10.2f} % {np.degrees(np.angle(ring_current[f])):12.1f} / {np.degrees(np.angle(circuit(f))):.1f}"
    )
late = times > 0.5 * tau
fitted = -np.polyfit(times[late], np.log(np.abs(induced[late])), 1)[0] ** -1
print(
    f"transient: ring current after the first step {induced[1]:.4f} A (circuit {-M * turns * current / L2:.4f} A); "
    f"decay time {1e3 * fitted:.3f} ms, circuit {1e3 * tau:.3f} ms ({100 * (fitted / tau - 1):+.1f} %)"
)

Going further

Feed the coil by a voltage instead of a current to see the reaction of the ring on the coil current, replace the ring by a solid disc, or make it of steel to add the magnetic pull of the ring towards the coil.
