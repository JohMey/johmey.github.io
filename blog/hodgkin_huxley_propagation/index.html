Hodgkin-Huxley membrane action potential

The space-clamped membrane of the squid giant axon fires an all-or-nothing action potential once a sudden depolarisation passes its threshold.

What it computes

OneSimulation assembles the four membrane equations (potential and the gates m, h, n) as a signal-flow graph from its published HodgkinHuxley1952 channel set and integrates them with the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method for initial depolarisations of 6, 7, 15 and 90 mV and a fine scan between 6 and 7 mV.

What it checks

As in the original 1952 computation a 6 mV displacement dies away back to rest (-64.9 mV after 20 ms) while 7 mV and more give a full spike: the threshold lies between 6.5 and 6.6 mV (Hodgkin and Huxley: between 6 and 7 mV), the spikes overshoot to +37 to +44 mV, stay above -40 mV for about 2.2 ms and end in an after-hyperpolarisation to -76.2 mV.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) described the membrane of the squid giant axon by a capacitor in parallel with a voltage-gated sodium channel (\(m^3h\)), a voltage-gated potassium channel (\(n^4\)) and a passive leak. With the whole membrane at one potential (a space-clamped "membrane action potential", no propagation along the axon) the model is a system of four ordinary differential equations:

\[C_m\,\frac{dV}{dt} = -\bar g_\mathrm{Na}\,m^3h\,(V-E_\mathrm{Na}) - \bar g_\mathrm{K}\,n^4\,(V-E_\mathrm{K}) - g_L\,(V-E_L),\qquad \frac{dy}{dt}=\alpha_y(V)\,(1-y)-\beta_y(V)\,y,\quad y\in\{m,h,n\}.\]
quantityvalue
membrane capacitance \(C_m\)1 µF/cm²
sodium: \(\bar g_\mathrm{Na}\), \(E_\mathrm{Na}\)120 mS/cm², +50 mV
potassium: \(\bar g_\mathrm{K}\), \(E_\mathrm{K}\)36 mS/cm², -77 mV
leak: \(g_L\), \(E_L\)0.3 mS/cm², -54.4 mV
resting potential, temperature-65 mV, 6.3 °C

OneSimulation ships these channels with their rate functions \(\alpha_y\), \(\beta_y\) as HodgkinHuxley1952. The four equations are assembled as a signal-flow graph: an integrator block holds each state, and custom blocks evaluate the gate kinetics and the ionic current from the channel set. The membrane starts at rest with the gates at their resting values, and the potential is displaced suddenly by \(\Delta V\) (a short shock that charges the membrane), as in the original computation. The graph is integrated with the classical fourth-order Runge-Kutta method.

Reference. A. L. Hodgkin and A. F. Huxley, "A quantitative description of membrane current and its application to conduction and excitation in nerve", J. Physiol. 117, 500-544 (1952), Part V and Fig. 12: membrane action potentials computed at 6 °C for initial depolarisations of 6, 7, 15 and 90 mV. A displacement of 6 mV dies away, 7 mV and more give a full action potential: the threshold lies between 6 and 7 mV. The spike overshoots zero, lasts a few milliseconds at this temperature and ends with an after-hyperpolarisation below rest.

import numpy as np
from onesim.numerics.time import DEFAULT_TIME_INTEGRATOR_REGISTRY
from onesim.physics.biophysics import HodgkinHuxley1952
from onesim.physics.systems import ControlSignalFlowProvider, SignalBlock
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Model

The channel set gives the conductances, the reversal potentials, the gate exponents and the rate functions. The ionic current is summed over the channels, so the same graph runs any channel set of the same form. Each run starts from rest displaced by \(\Delta V\) and lasts 20 ms.

membrane = HodgkinHuxley1952()
capacitance, rest = 0.01, -0.065
state = ("V",) + tuple(gate.name for gate in membrane.gates)
ionic = lambda s: sum(
    channel.conductance
    * np.prod([s[state.index(g)] ** power for g, power in channel.gate_powers.items()])
    * (s[0] - channel.reversal_potential)
    for channel in membrane.channels
)
action_potential = lambda shift: ControlSignalFlowProvider(
    blocks=(
        SignalBlock(
            "V",
            "integrator",
            inputs=("dV",),
            parameters={"initial_value": rest + shift},
        ),
        SignalBlock(
            "dV",
            "custom",
            inputs=state,
            custom_evaluator=lambda s, t, p: -ionic(s) / capacitance,
            custom_derivative=lambda *a: (),
        ),
    )
    + tuple(
        block
        for gate in membrane.gates
        for block in (
            SignalBlock(
                gate.name,
                "integrator",
                inputs=("d" + gate.name,),
                parameters={"initial_value": gate.initial_value},
            ),
            SignalBlock(
                "d" + gate.name,
                "custom",
                inputs=("V", gate.name),
                custom_evaluator=lambda s, t, p, rate=gate.rate: float(
                    np.dot(rate(np.asarray(s[0])), (1 - s[1], -s[1]))
                ),
                custom_derivative=lambda *a: (),
            ),
        )
    ),
    final_time=0.02,
    time_step=1e-5,
    time_integrator=DEFAULT_TIME_INTEGRATOR_REGISTRY.resolve_id("classical_rk4"),
).execute()

print(
    "channels:",
    ", ".join(f"{c.name} {c.conductance / 10:g} mS/cm²" for c in membrane.channels),
    "| gates at rest:",
    ", ".join(f"{g.name} = {g.initial_value:.4f}" for g in membrane.gates),
)

Results

The four displacements of the original figure, and a scan between 6 and 7 mV that locates the threshold: a run counts as an action potential when the potential overshoots 0 mV.

shifts = (0.006, 0.007, 0.015, 0.090)
runs = {shift: action_potential(shift) for shift in shifts}
scan = {
    shift: max(action_potential(shift).signals["V"])
    for shift in np.round(np.arange(0.0060, 0.00701, 0.0001), 4)
}
times = 1e3 * np.asarray(runs[shifts[0]].times)
voltage = {shift: 1e3 * np.asarray(run.signals["V"]) for shift, run in runs.items()}
threshold = min(shift for shift, peak in scan.items() if peak > 0.0)

print(
    "ΔV [mV]   peak [mV]   time of peak [ms]   after-hyperpolarisation [mV]   duration above -40 mV [ms]"
)
for shift, v in voltage.items():
    above = times[v > -40.0]
    print(
        f"{1e3 * shift:7.0f} {v.max():11.1f} {times[v.argmax()]:19.2f} {v.min():30.1f} "
        f"{(above[-1] - above[0]) if len(above) else 0.0:27.2f}"
    )
print(
    f"threshold from the scan: between {1e3 * (threshold - 0.0001):.1f} and {1e3 * threshold:.1f} mV "
    f"(Hodgkin and Huxley 1952: between 6 and 7 mV)"
)
print(
    f"potential 20 ms after a 6 mV displacement: {voltage[0.006][-1]:.2f} mV (rest {1e3 * rest:.0f} mV)"
)

Figure

The membrane potential after the four displacements, as in Fig. 12 of the original paper, and the sodium and potassium gates during the action potential after 15 mV.

Going further

Replace the initial displacement by a constant stimulus current (a source block added to the current balance): above about 6 µA/cm² the membrane fires repetitively. Raise the temperature with HodgkinHuxley1952(temperature=...) and the spike becomes shorter and smaller through the built-in \(Q_{10}=3\) of the rates. The propagating action potential along the axon is the monodomain cable equation with the same channel set (Monodomain).
