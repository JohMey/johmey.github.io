Discharge, rest and recharge of a lithium-ion cell

A 5 Ah graphite-SiOx | NMC811 cell with published parameters, discharged at 1C, left at rest and recharged, resolved through its thickness and into its particles.

What it computes

OneSimulation solves the Doyle-Fuller-Newman porous-electrode model on 32 particle shells and 20/10/20 layer cells, with the current switches as breakpoints of the time integration. Salt concentration, reaction distribution and particle profiles show where the cell works, and a mesh study refines particles and layers separately.

What it checks

Lithium is conserved to 1e-12; the mean stoichiometry change after 45 min at 1C equals coulomb counting to six digits in both electrodes, and both return to their start values after the charge; after one hour of rest the voltage is 0.02 mV from the open-circuit voltage of the mean stoichiometries (0.22 mV after the final rest); the end-of-discharge voltage converges at second order in particle shells and layer cells (3.20233 V on the finest mesh).

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

The cell is a sandwich of a porous negative electrode, a separator and a porous positive electrode between two current collectors. The Doyle-Fuller-Newman (DFN) model resolves the thickness coordinate \(x\) and, at every \(x\) in the electrodes, a spherical active particle with radius coordinate \(r\):

\[\frac{\partial c_s}{\partial t}=\frac{D_s}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Big(r^2\frac{\partial c_s}{\partial r}\Big),\qquad \varepsilon\frac{\partial c_e}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big(D_e\frac{\partial c_e}{\partial x}\Big)+\frac{(1-t_+)\,a\,j}{F},\]
\[\frac{\partial i_e}{\partial x}=a\,j,\qquad i_e=-\kappa\frac{\partial\phi_e}{\partial x}+\frac{2(1-t_+)RT\kappa}{F}\frac{\partial\ln c_e}{\partial x},\qquad i_s=-\sigma\frac{\partial\phi_s}{\partial x},\qquad j=2j_0\sinh\frac{F(\phi_s-\phi_e-U(c_s^\mathrm{surf}))}{2RT}.\]

| boundary | condition | |---|---| | current collectors | \(i_s=I\), \(i_e=0\), no salt flux | | separator | \(i_e=I\), no electrode phase | | particle centre / surface | symmetry / \(-D_s\,\partial_r c_s = j/F\) |

The cell starts at 90 % state of charge. The current program is 45 min at 1C (5 A), one hour at rest, 45 min of 1C charge and half an hour of rest. The cell is isothermal at 25 °C.

Reference. Parameters, open-circuit curves and stoichiometry limits of the LG M50 cell are those of Chen et al., J. Electrochem. Soc. 167 (2020) 080534; the model is that of Doyle, Fuller and Newman, J. Electrochem. Soc. 140 (1993) 1526. Independent checks: (i) lithium in particles and electrolyte is conserved; (ii) coulomb counting fixes the mean stoichiometry of each electrode, \(\Delta\bar\theta=-\int I\,dt/(F\varepsilon_sLc_{s,\max}A)\); (iii) at rest the terminal voltage relaxes to the open-circuit voltage of the mean stoichiometries, \(U_+(\bar\theta_+)-U_-(\bar\theta_-)\); (iv) the voltage converges under mesh refinement.

import numpy as np
from onesim.physics.chemistry import (
    BatteryElectrodeLayer,
    BatteryElectrolyteLayer,
    PorousElectrodeBattery,
)
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Cell

Effective electrolyte transport follows Bruggeman's relation with exponent 1.5; the electrode area is 0.1027 m², so 1C is 48.7 A/m².

faraday, area = 96485.33212, 0.1027
one_c = 5.0 / area
negative_ocp = lambda x, T: (
    1.9793 * np.exp(-39.3631 * x)
    + 0.2482
    - 0.0909 * np.tanh(29.8538 * (x - 0.1234))
    - 0.04478 * np.tanh(14.9159 * (x - 0.2769))
    - 0.0205 * np.tanh(30.4444 * (x - 0.6103))
)
positive_ocp = lambda y, T: (
    4.4875
    - 0.8090 * y
    - 0.0428 * np.tanh(18.5138 * (y - 0.5542))
    - 17.7326 * np.tanh(15.7890 * (y - 0.3117))
    + 17.5842 * np.tanh(15.9308 * (y - 0.3120))
)
negative = BatteryElectrodeLayer(
    thickness=85.2e-6,
    porosity=0.25,
    electrolyte_diffusivity=1.7694e-10 * 0.25**1.5,
    electrolyte_conductivity=0.9487 * 0.25**1.5,
    particle_radius=5.86e-6,
    particle_diffusivity=3.3e-14,
    active_fraction=0.75,
    maximum_concentration=33133,
    initial_stoichiometry=0.0279 + 0.9 * (0.9014 - 0.0279),
    reference_exchange_current=6.48e-7 * np.sqrt(1000) * 33133 / 2,
    electronic_conductivity=215,
    open_circuit_potential=negative_ocp,
)
positive = BatteryElectrodeLayer(
    thickness=75.6e-6,
    porosity=0.335,
    electrolyte_diffusivity=1.7694e-10 * 0.335**1.5,
    electrolyte_conductivity=0.9487 * 0.335**1.5,
    particle_radius=5.22e-6,
    particle_diffusivity=4e-15,
    active_fraction=0.665,
    maximum_concentration=63104,
    initial_stoichiometry=0.9077 + 0.9 * (0.2661 - 0.9077),
    reference_exchange_current=3.42e-6 * np.sqrt(1000) * 63104 / 2,
    electronic_conductivity=0.18,
    open_circuit_potential=positive_ocp,
)
separator = BatteryElectrolyteLayer(
    thickness=12e-6,
    porosity=0.47,
    electrolyte_diffusivity=1.7694e-10 * 0.47**1.5,
    electrolyte_conductivity=0.9487 * 0.47**1.5,
)
cell = PorousElectrodeBattery(
    negative=negative,
    separator=separator,
    positive=positive,
    electrolyte_concentration=1000,
    transference_number=0.2594,
)

Simulation

The current density is a function of time (positive on discharge); its switch times are passed as breakpoints, so the integrator restarts exactly at every switch. The particles are divided into 32 spherical shells, the layers into 20, 10 and 20 control volumes. A mesh study repeats the 45 min discharge with 8 to 64 particle shells and with 5 to 40 control volumes in each electrode.

program = lambda t: one_c * (float(t < 2700) - float(6300 <= t < 9000))
times = np.linspace(0.0, 10800.0, 1081)
run = cell.solve(
    mode="DFN",
    current_density=program,
    final_time=10800.0,
    radial_cells=32,
    through_cells=(20, 10, 20),
    output_times=times,
    breakpoints=(0.0, 2700.0, 6300.0, 9000.0, 10800.0),
)
radial = {
    n: cell.solve(
        mode="DFN",
        current_density=one_c,
        final_time=2700.0,
        radial_cells=n,
        through_cells=(20, 10, 20),
        output_times=(0.0, 2700.0),
    ).terminal_voltage[-1]
    for n in (8, 16, 32, 64)
}
layered = {
    n: cell.solve(
        mode="DFN",
        current_density=one_c,
        final_time=2700.0,
        radial_cells=32,
        through_cells=(n, n // 2, n),
        output_times=(0.0, 2700.0),
    ).terminal_voltage[-1]
    for n in (5, 10, 20, 40)
}

Results

mean = [
    np.mean(c @ np.diff(np.linspace(0, 1, 33) ** 3), axis=1)
    / layer.maximum_concentration
    for c, layer in zip(run.particle_concentration, (negative, positive))
]
relaxed = positive_ocp(mean[1], 0) - negative_ocp(mean[0], 0)
counted = (
    one_c
    * 2700
    / (
        faraday
        * np.array(
            [
                layer.active_fraction * layer.thickness * layer.maximum_concentration
                for layer in (negative, positive)
            ]
        )
    )
)
ends = {
    "end of discharge": 269,
    "end of first rest": 629,
    "end of charge": 899,
    "end of program": 1080,
}
print(
    f"lithium inventory: largest relative drift {np.ptp(run.lithium_inventory) / run.lithium_inventory[0]:.1e}"
)
print(
    f"coulomb counting after 45 min at 1C: mean stoichiometry change negative {mean[0][0] - mean[0][270]:.6f} "
    f"(exact {counted[0]:.6f}), positive {mean[1][270] - mean[1][0]:.6f} (exact {counted[1]:.6f})"
)
print(
    f"after the full program: mean stoichiometries {mean[0][-1]:.6f} and {mean[1][-1]:.6f} (start {negative.initial_stoichiometry:.6f} and {positive.initial_stoichiometry:.6f})"
)
for name, k in ends.items():
    print(
        f"{name:18s} t = {times[k] / 60:5.0f} min   V = {run.terminal_voltage[k]:.4f} V   "
        f"open-circuit voltage of the mean stoichiometries {relaxed[k]:.4f} V   difference {1e3 * (run.terminal_voltage[k] - relaxed[k]):+.2f} mV"
    )
errors = {
    "particle shells": {
        1 / n: abs(v - radial[64]) for n, v in radial.items() if n < 64
    },
    "electrode cells": {
        1 / n: abs(v - layered[40]) for n, v in layered.items() if n < 40
    },
}
print(
    "voltage at the end of discharge: "
    + ", ".join(f"{v:.5f} V ({n} shells)" for n, v in radial.items())
    + "; "
    + ", ".join(f"{v:.5f} V ({n} cells)" for n, v in layered.items())
)
for name, e in errors.items():
    h = sorted(e)
    print(
        f"observed order in the {name}: {np.log2(e[h[2]] / e[h[1]]):.2f}, {np.log2(e[h[1]] / e[h[0]]):.2f}"
    )
