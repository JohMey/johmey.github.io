What it computes

OneSimulation runs the electro-thermal LumpedBattery model inside a SimulationStudyService; its least-squares fit (Levenberg-Marquardt, every residual evaluation a real battery simulation) adjusts four parameters against voltage and temperature and returns the covariance in physical units. The data are synthetic: generated from known parameters with noise.

What it checks

From a start 20-100 % off, 30 simulations recover Q = 2.5001 +/- 0.0007 Ah, R = 39.96 +/- 0.05 mOhm, C_th = 44.6 +/- 0.3 J/K and hA = 0.1199 +/- 0.0002 W/K (true 2.5, 40, 45, 0.12), all within 1.4 standard errors; residuals at the noise level.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

A lumped battery model needs no knowledge of the electrode design or materials. It describes the cell by a handful of parameters: the capacity \(Q\), the open-circuit voltage \(U_\mathrm{OCV}(s)\) as a function of the state of charge \(s\), an internal resistance \(R\) that collects ohmic, kinetic and transport losses, and one thermal mass with heat capacity \(C_\mathrm{th}\) that exchanges heat with the surroundings through the conductance \(hA\):

\[\frac{ds}{dt}=-\frac{I}{Q},\qquad U=U_\mathrm{OCV}(s)-R\,I,\qquad C_\mathrm{th}\frac{dT}{dt}=R\,I^2-hA\,(T-T_\infty).\]

Over a load cycle the terminal voltage carries the information on \(Q\) (through the drift of \(U_\mathrm{OCV}\)) and on \(R\) (through the jumps at every load change); the temperature carries the information on \(C_\mathrm{th}\) and \(hA\) (rise rate and level).

OneSimulation solves the model with LumpedBattery and the classical Runge-Kutta method. The SimulationStudyService turns it into a least-squares problem: fit varies the four parameters, runs the battery simulation for every trial point, forms the residuals of voltage and temperature divided by their noise levels and minimises their sum of squares with the Levenberg-Marquardt method. It returns the estimates with their linearised covariance in physical units.

Reference. The data are synthetic: generated by the same model from the "true" values \(Q=2.5\) Ah, \(R=40\) mΩ, \(C_\mathrm{th}=45\) J/K, \(hA=0.12\) W/K with normally distributed noise of 2 mV on the voltage and 0.05 K on the temperature. A correct estimation recovers them within the standard errors that this noise allows and leaves residuals of the size of the noise (D. Bernardi, E. Pawlikowski and J. Newman, A general energy balance for battery systems, J. Electrochem. Soc. 132, 5-12 (1985); G. L. Plett, Battery Management Systems, Vol. I: Battery Modeling, Artech House 2015).

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.geometry import LumpedDomain
from onesim.meshing import LumpedMesh
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.discretization import LumpedParameters
from onesim.numerics.time import ClassicalRungeKutta4
from onesim.physics.chemistry import BatteryChemistry, LumpedBattery
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.simulation.initial_conditions import InitialValue
from onesim.simulation.studies import SimulationStudyService, StudyParameter
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Cell and load cycle

A cylindrical lithium-ion cell at 25 °C ambient, starting at 95 % state of charge. A ten-minute pattern of discharge at 0.5C to 2C, a short charge pulse (regenerative braking) and rests is repeated for one hour; voltage and temperature are sampled every 10 s.

ambient, start, noise_voltage, noise_temperature = 298.15, 0.95, 2e-3, 0.05
open_circuit = lambda s: 3.0 + 1.1 * s - 0.35 * s**2 + 0.05 * np.log(s / (1.0 - s))
pattern = np.array(
    [
        (0, 1.25),
        (120, 5.0),
        (180, 2.5),
        (300, -2.5),
        (330, 0.0),
        (390, 3.75),
        (510, 0.0),
    ]
)
load = lambda t: pattern[np.searchsorted(pattern[:, 0], t % 600.0, side="right") - 1, 1]
times = np.arange(0.0, 3601.0, 10.0)
cell = LumpedDomain()
print(
    f"charge drawn in one hour {np.mean([load(t) for t in np.arange(0.0, 3600.0)]):.3f} Ah, "
    f"rms current {np.sqrt(np.mean([load(t) ** 2 for t in np.arange(0.0, 3600.0)])):.2f} A"
)

Model

cycle(values) builds the battery simulation for one parameter set (capacity in Ah, resistance in mΩ, heat capacity in J/K, conductance in W/K); the study runs it with steps of 2 s and reads voltage and temperature at the sampling times.

cycle = lambda values: (
    model := LumpedBattery(
        domain=cell.system,
        charge_capacity=3600.0 * values["capacity"],
        current=load,
        thermal_capacity=values["heat_capacity"],
        heat_transfer=values["conductance"],
        ambient_temperature=ambient,
        chemistry=BatteryChemistry(
            "lumped cell",
            open_circuit_voltage=lambda s, T: open_circuit(s),
            internal_resistance=lambda s, T: 1e-3 * values["resistance"],
        ),
    ),
    Simulation(
        problem=Problem(
            name="Load cycle",
            geometry=cell,
            physics=(model,),
            initial_conditions=(
                InitialValue(model.state_of_charge, start),
                InitialValue(model.temperature, ambient),
            ),
        ),
        mesh=LumpedMesh(cell),
        numerics=Numerics(
            spatial=LumpedParameters(), time_integrator=ClassicalRungeKutta4()
        ),
    ),
)
names = ("capacity", "resistance", "heat_capacity", "conductance")
study = SimulationStudyService(
    (
        StudyParameter("capacity", 1.5, 4.0),
        StudyParameter("resistance", 10.0, 100.0),
        StudyParameter("heat_capacity", 10.0, 150.0),
        StudyParameter("conductance", 0.02, 0.5),
    ),
    cycle,
    lambda run: {
        f"{quantity}_{i}": value
        for quantity, field in (
            ("voltage", run[0].terminal_voltage),
            ("temperature", run[0].temperature),
        )
        for i, value in enumerate(np.interp(times, *run[1].field(field).time_series()))
    },
    solve=lambda built: (
        built[0],
        built[1].run(start_time=0.0, end_time=3600.0, time_step=2.0),
    ),
)

Synthetic measurements

The true parameters are run once and normally distributed noise is added (fixed seed).

true = {"capacity": 2.5, "resistance": 40.0, "heat_capacity": 45.0, "conductance": 0.12}
clean = study.sweep(combinations=[true]).runs[0].measurements
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
voltage = np.array([clean[f"voltage_{i}"] for i in range(times.size)]) + rng.normal(
    0.0, noise_voltage, times.size
)
temperature = np.array(
    [clean[f"temperature_{i}"] for i in range(times.size)]
) + rng.normal(0.0, noise_temperature, times.size)

Least-squares fit

Residuals of voltage and temperature, each divided by its noise level; Levenberg-Marquardt from a start far from the truth: 2.0 Ah, 70 mΩ, 90 J/K, 0.25 W/K.

initial = {
    "capacity": 2.0,
    "resistance": 70.0,
    "heat_capacity": 90.0,
    "conductance": 0.25,
}
fit = study.fit(
    residuals=lambda m: np.r_[
        [(m[f"voltage_{i}"] - voltage[i]) / noise_voltage for i in range(times.size)],
        [
            (m[f"temperature_{i}"] - temperature[i]) / noise_temperature
            for i in range(times.size)
        ],
    ],
    start=initial,
    method="levenberg_marquardt",
    maximum_runs=150,
)

Comparison with the true values

print(
    f"{fit.run_count} battery simulations (including the data run), converged: {fit.converged}, rank {fit.rank} of {len(names)}, "
    f"χ² = {fit.residuals @ fit.residuals:.1f} for {fit.degrees_of_freedom} degrees of freedom"
)
print("parameter        estimate ± standard error     true      deviation")
for name, unit in zip(names, ("Ah", "mΩ", "J/K", "W/K")):
    print(
        f"{name:14s} {fit.parameters[name]:10.4f} ± {fit.standard_errors[name]:.4f} {unit:3s} {true[name]:9.3f}   "
        f"{(fit.parameters[name] - true[name]) / fit.standard_errors[name]:+6.2f} σ"
    )
print(
    f"correlation capacity-resistance {fit.covariance[0, 1] / np.sqrt(fit.covariance[0, 0] * fit.covariance[1, 1]):+.2f}, "
    f"heat capacity-conductance {fit.covariance[2, 3] / np.sqrt(fit.covariance[2, 2] * fit.covariance[3, 3]):+.2f}"
)
print(
    f"rms residuals: voltage {1e3 * noise_voltage * np.sqrt(np.mean(fit.residuals[:times.size] ** 2)):.2f} mV (noise 2 mV), "
    f"temperature {noise_temperature * np.sqrt(np.mean(fit.residuals[times.size:] ** 2)):.3f} K (noise 0.05 K)"
)

Going further

Fit only the voltage and the thermal parameters become unidentifiable: the fit reports a reduced rank and no covariance. Let the resistance depend on temperature through an Arrhenius factor and add its activation energy as a fifth parameter; the electrical and thermal parts then couple, and the correlations in the covariance show how well the data separate them. Measured cycler data replace the synthetic voltage and temperature arrays directly.
