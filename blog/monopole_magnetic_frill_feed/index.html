What it computes

OneSimulation solves the azimuthal magnetic field on the meridian plane read from STEP; a waveguide port on the coaxial line feeds its TEM mode, so the aperture field in the ground plane is the magnetic frill of the actual antenna current, and a first-order absorbing boundary closes the hemisphere. The far field is mirrored in the ground plane. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

The power from voltage and current in the coaxial line matches the power accepted at the port to 1e-3 at all frequencies; the radiated power from the far field is 0.933 W against 0.925 W accepted at 1 GHz; the pattern follows the sinusoidal-current pattern to 8.8e-3; the resonance lies at 1.052 GHz (h = 0.246 wavelengths) with 41.0 ohm against 34.7 ohm of the thin-wire induced-EMF value.

The geometry script

The script monopole_magnetic_frill_feed_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the file. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Monopole antenna fed by a magnetic frill".

Builds, as a half solid of revolution about the z axis with the CAD objects of OneSimulation, the
air around a monopole 70 mm high and 1.5 mm in radius on an infinite ground plane, out to a
hemisphere of 0.3 m, together with the 10 mm of coaxial line (outer radius 3.45 mm, 50 ohm) that
feeds it through the ground plane (monopole_magnetic_frill_feed.step; faces antenna (wire and inner
conductor), ground (ground plane and outer conductor), feed (the coaxial port), outer (the
hemisphere) and axis).
"""

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.d2 import Path, Profile, Segment
from onesim.cad.d3 import Revolution

height, wire, shield, line, outer = 0.07, 0.0015, 0.00345, 0.01, 0.3
outline = Path(
    (0.0, height),
    (
        Segment((wire, height), name="antenna"),
        Segment((wire, -line), name="antenna"),
        Segment((shield, -line), name="feed"),
        Segment((shield, 0.0), name="ground"),
        Segment((outer, 0.0), name="ground"),
        Segment((0.0, outer), center=(0.0, 0.0), name="outer"),
        Segment((0.0, height), name="axis"),
    ),
)
Revolution(profile=Profile(outline), angle=np.pi).rotated((0, 0, 1), -np.pi / 2).save(
    "monopole_magnetic_frill_feed.step"
)

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Voltages are not defined in an electromagnetic wave model, so an antenna needs a feed that is a field. The classic one is the magnetic frill: the radial electric field \(E_r=V/(r\ln(b/a))\) in the annular aperture of a coaxial line between its inner radius \(a\) and outer radius \(b\), equivalent to a ring of magnetic current \(M_\varphi=-2E_r\) on the ground plane. Here the frill is not imposed by hand: 10 mm of the 50 ohm coaxial line are part of the model, and a SectionWaveguidePort at its end feeds the TEM mode and absorbs what is reflected. The field in the aperture then is the frill field of the actual antenna current.

The monopole (height \(h=70\) mm, radius \(a=1.5\) mm) and its feed are bodies of revolution, and the field has only \(H_\varphi\), \(E_r\) and \(E_z\): FrequencyDomainMaxwell with polarization Hphi on the meridian plane (\(r\), \(z\)). A first-order absorbing ImpedanceBoundary on the hemisphere of 0.3 m (one wavelength at 1 GHz) lets the radiation leave.

boundarycondition
feed (end of the coaxial line)waveguide port, TEM mode, 1 W
antenna, groundperfect electric conductor
outer (hemisphere)impedance boundary \(Y_0=\sqrt{\varepsilon_0/\mu_0}\)
axisregular: \(H_\varphi=0\)

The impedance is read inside the coaxial line, 5 mm below the ground plane, where the field is a pure TEM wave: \(V=\int_a^bE_r\,\mathrm dr\) and \(I=2\pi rH_\varphi\) (Ampère) give \(Z=V/I\), whose reflection \(|Z-Z_c|/|Z+Z_c|\) (\(Z_c=\eta_0\ln(b/a)/2\pi\)) must match the reflection of the port and \(\tfrac12\mathrm{Re}(VI^*)\) the power accepted at the port. Moving the reflection coefficient \(\Gamma\) along the 5 mm of line, \(\Gamma_L=\Gamma e^{-2\mathrm ik\ell}\), gives the input impedance at the ground plane, \(Z_{in}=Z_c(1+\Gamma_L)/(1-\Gamma_L)\). far_field with ground_plane=0 mirrors the hemisphere in the ground plane and returns the pattern of the full space.

Reference (C. A. Balanis, Antenna Theory, 4th ed., §4.6 and §4.7.4; S. A. Schelkunoff and H. T. Friis, Antennas: Theory and Practice, 1952). With the sinusoidal current \(I(z)=I_0\sin k(h-z)\) the monopole radiates

\[E_\theta=\frac{\mathrm i\eta_0I_0}{2\pi}\frac{e^{\mathrm ikr}}{r} \frac{\cos(kh\cos\theta)-\cos kh}{\sin\theta},\qquad R_{in}=\frac{\eta_0}{2\pi\sin^2kh}\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{(\cos(kh\cos\theta)-\cos kh)^2}{\sin\theta}\mathrm d\theta,\]

the input resistance referred to the base current (36.5 ohm for \(h=\lambda/4\)). The thickness of a real wire shortens the resonance by a few percent but leaves the resistance there close to the thin-wire value.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import VACUUM_PERMEABILITY, VACUUM_PERMITTIVITY
from onesim.materials.electromagnetic import IsotropicElectromagneticMaterial
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange, RevolvedH1SpaceFactory
from onesim.numerics.linear import ComplexSparseDirect
from onesim.physics.electromagnetics import (
    FrequencyDomainMaxwell,
    ImpedanceBoundary,
    PerfectElectricConductor,
    SectionWaveguidePort,
    far_field,
    scattering_parameters,
)
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

height, wire, shield = 0.07, 0.0015, 0.00345
light = 1 / np.sqrt(VACUUM_PERMEABILITY * VACUUM_PERMITTIVITY)
impedance = np.sqrt(VACUUM_PERMEABILITY / VACUUM_PERMITTIVITY)
characteristic = impedance * np.log(shield / wire) / (2 * np.pi)

Geometry and mesh

load reads the half solid of revolution; section with the plane \(y=0\) returns its meridian plane with the named sides. Quadratic triangles of 12 mm (a 25th of the wavelength), 0.7 mm along the antenna and 0.4 mm in the coaxial line.

plane = load("monopole_magnetic_frill_feed.step").section((0, 1, 0))
sides = plane.boundaries
mesh = TriangularMesher(
    target_size=0.012, local_sizes={"antenna": 7e-4, "feed": 4e-4}
).generate(plane)

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 7))
plots.geometry(
    plane,
    selection={
        "feed (coaxial port)": sides.feed,
        "antenna": sides.antenna,
        "ground": sides.ground,
        "outer": sides.outer,
    },
    labels=False,
    title="Meridian plane of monopole and feed (STEP)",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    mesh,
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"{mesh.cell_count} quadratic triangles",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model

One model per frequency from 0.8 to 1.3 GHz; the port feeds 1 W in the TEM mode of the coaxial line.

numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=Lagrange(order=2), provider=RevolvedH1SpaceFactory()),
    linear_solver=ComplexSparseDirect(),
)
model = lambda frequency: FrequencyDomainMaxwell(
    domain=plane.domain(),
    frequency=frequency,
    material=IsotropicElectromagneticMaterial(),
    polarization="Hphi",
    phasor_convention="exp(-i*omega*t)",
    amplitude_convention="peak",
)
solve = lambda physics: Simulation(
    problem=Problem(
        name="monopole",
        geometry=plane,
        physics=(physics,),
        boundary_conditions=(
            SectionWaveguidePort(
                physics=physics, boundary=sides.feed, modes=1, incident_power=1.0
            ),
            PerfectElectricConductor(
                physics=physics, boundary=sides.antenna | sides.ground
            ),
            ImpedanceBoundary(
                physics=physics, boundary=sides.outer, admittance=1 / impedance
            ),
        ),
    ),
    mesh=mesh,
    numerics=numerics,
).solve_stationary()

frequencies = np.linspace(0.8e9, 1.3e9, 11)
models = [model(frequency) for frequency in frequencies]
results = [solve(physics) for physics in models]

Results

Voltage and current in the coaxial line, the reflection and power they imply against those of the port, and the input impedance at the ground plane.

across, below = np.linspace(wire, shield, 201), 0.005
aperture = np.column_stack((across, -below + 0 * across))
voltage = np.array(
    [
        np.trapezoid(
            result.field(physics.electric_field).evaluate(aperture)[:, 0], across
        )
        for result, physics in zip(results, models)
    ]
)
middle = np.array([[(wire + shield) / 2, -below]])
current = np.array(
    [
        2
        * np.pi
        * middle[0, 0]
        * result.field(physics.magnetic_flux_density).evaluate(middle)[0, 1]
        / VACUUM_PERMEABILITY
        for result, physics in zip(results, models)
    ]
)
line_impedance = voltage / current
reflection = np.array(
    [
        abs(scattering_parameters(result, physics)[(0, 0)])
        for result, physics in zip(results, models)
    ]
)
from_impedance = np.abs(
    (line_impedance - characteristic) / (line_impedance + characteristic)
)
load_reflection = (
    (line_impedance - characteristic)
    / (line_impedance + characteristic)
    * np.exp(-2j * 2 * np.pi * frequencies / light * below)
)
input_impedance = characteristic * (1 + load_reflection) / (1 - load_reflection)
print(f"coaxial line: Z_c = {characteristic:.2f} ohm")
print(
    "f [GHz]   Z_in at the ground [ohm]   |S11| port   |Z-Zc|/|Z+Zc|   accepted power [W]: 1-|S11|^2   Re(V I*)/2"
)
for f, z, s, r, v, i in zip(
    frequencies, input_impedance, reflection, from_impedance, voltage, current
):
    print(
        f"{f / 1e9:6.2f}   {z.real:7.2f} {z.imag:+8.2f}i      {s:.4f}       {r:.4f}            {1 - s**2:.4f}          {0.5 * (v * np.conj(i)).real:.4f}"
    )

The resonance lies where the reactance changes sign; the induced EMF resistance of the sinusoidal current is evaluated at the same electrical height.

resonance = np.interp(0.0, input_impedance.imag[::-1], frequencies[::-1])
kh = 2 * np.pi * resonance / light * height
theta = np.linspace(1e-4, np.pi / 2, 4001)
emf = (
    impedance
    / (2 * np.pi * np.sin(kh) ** 2)
    * np.trapezoid(
        (np.cos(kh * np.cos(theta)) - np.cos(kh)) ** 2 / np.sin(theta), theta
    )
)
print(
    f"\nresonance at {resonance / 1e9:.3f} GHz (h = {height / (light / resonance):.3f} wavelengths); "
    f"R_in there {np.interp(resonance, frequencies, input_impedance.real):.1f} ohm, induced EMF (thin wire) {emf:.1f} ohm"
)

The radiated power from the far field over the upper half space against the power accepted at the port, and the pattern against the sinusoidal-current pattern, at 1 GHz.

at = int(np.argmin(np.abs(frequencies - 1.0e9)))
angles = np.linspace(0.01, np.pi / 2 - 0.01, 90)
pattern = far_field(
    results[at], models[at], angles, radius=0.2, center=(0.0, 0.0), ground_plane=0.0
)
radiated = np.trapezoid(
    np.sum(np.abs(pattern) ** 2, axis=1) / (2 * impedance) * 2 * np.pi * np.sin(angles),
    angles,
)
kh = 2 * np.pi * frequencies[at] / light * height
ideal = np.abs((np.cos(kh * np.cos(angles)) - np.cos(kh)) / np.sin(angles))
shape = np.linalg.norm(pattern, axis=1)
print(
    f"at {frequencies[at] / 1e9:.1f} GHz: radiated {radiated:.4f} W, accepted 1 - |S11|^2 = {1 - reflection[at]**2:.4f} W; "
    f"pattern against the sinusoidal current (both normalized at the horizon): max deviation {np.max(np.abs(shape / shape[-1] - ideal / ideal[-1])):.1e}"
)

Going further

Make the wire thinner in monopole_magnetic_frill_feed_geometry.py: the resonance moves towards a quarter wavelength and the impedance curve towards the thin-wire values. A top hat (a disc on the antenna) or a dielectric sleeve shortens the antenna for the same resonance.
