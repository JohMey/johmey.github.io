Optimizing the currents of a coil system for a uniform field

The currents of six coaxial coils are tuned with real simulations so that the axial field stays at 1 mT over 40 cm.

What it computes

OneSimulation cuts the meridian section from STEP parts and solves the axisymmetric magnetostatic equation for the azimuthal vector potential with quadratic elements; the simulation study service fits the three coil currents by Levenberg-Marquardt, with the 21 relative field deviations on the axis as residuals and their Jacobian from solved simulations. The script that builds the STEP file comes with the notebook.

What it checks

After 24 solved simulations the fit converges from 129 % to 0.47 % rms deviation; the fitted currents lie within 0.6 % of the least-squares currents from the closed-form axial field of thick coils (0.830, 0.819, 1.151 A against 0.832, 0.814, 1.146 A), and each set is best in its own model: 0.586 % against 0.533 % in the closed form.

The geometry script

The script coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric_geometry.py builds the geometry with the CAD objects of OneSimulation and writes the files. Change a dimension there, run it, and run the notebook again.

"""Geometry of the OneSimulation example "Optimizing the currents of a coil system for a uniform field".

Builds, with the CAD objects of OneSimulation, the upper half (z > 0) of the space around a system
of six coaxial coils placed symmetrically about the plane z = 0: a half cylinder of 1 m radius and
1 m height on the side x > 0, whose meridian section is the axisymmetric domain, written to
coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric.step. The three coils above the midplane (inner radius
0.10 m, outer radius 0.12 m, 20 mm high, centred at z = 0.05, 0.15 and 0.25 m) go to
coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric_coil_1.step to _coil_3.step, each cut to the side x > 0.
"""

from onesim.cad.d3 import AnnularCylinder, Box, Cylinder

extent, inner, outer, height = 1.0, 0.10, 0.12, 0.02
side = Box(length=extent, width=2 * extent, height=extent, origin=(0.0, -extent, 0.0))
(Cylinder(radius=extent, length=extent) & side).save(
    "coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric.step"
)
for index, centre in enumerate((0.05, 0.15, 0.25), start=1):
    coil = (
        AnnularCylinder(
            inner_radius=inner,
            outer_radius=outer,
            length=height,
            origin=(0.0, 0.0, centre - height / 2),
        )
        & side
    )
    coil.save(f"coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric_coil_{index}.step")

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

Magnetic resonance, beam lines and magnetometer calibration need a magnetic field that is uniform over a long stretch of an axis. Here six identical coils (inner radius 0.10 m, outer radius 0.12 m, 20 mm high) sit symmetrically about the plane \(z=0\), at \(z=\pm0.05\), \(\pm0.15\) and \(\pm0.25\) m. Each symmetric pair carries the same ampere-turns \(NI_k\); the three values are the design variables. The goal is \(B_z=B_0=1\) mT on the axis for \(|z|\le0.2\) m, measured by the mean squared relative deviation at 21 points,

\[J(NI_1,NI_2,NI_3)=\frac1{21}\sum_j\Big(\frac{B_z(0,z_j)}{B_0}-1\Big)^2 .\]

The coils are rotationally symmetric, so OneSimulation solves the axisymmetric magnetostatic equation for the azimuthal vector potential \(A_\varphi(r,z)\) with quadratic elements on the meridian half-plane above the midplane. SimulationStudyService.fit minimizes \(J\) with real solves by the Levenberg-Marquardt method: the 21 relative deviations are the residuals, their Jacobian over the three currents comes from difference quotients of solved simulations.

The domain is the meridian section of coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric.step, a half cylinder of 1 m radius and height; the windings are the sections of coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric_coil_1.step to _coil_3.step. The script coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric_geometry.py builds them with the CAD objects of OneSimulation.

edgecondition
midplane \(z=0\)field normal, \(\mathbf n\times\mathbf H=0\) (MagneticSymmetry)
axis \(r=0\)axis of the revolved space
\(r=1\) m and \(z=1\) m\(A_\varphi=0\) (MagneticInsulation)

Reference. On its axis a coil of rectangular winding section (radii \(R_1\), \(R_2\), height \(h\), centre \(z_k\)) with uniform current density has the closed-form field (e.g. Montgomery, Solenoid Magnet Design, 1969)

\[B_z(z)=\frac{\mu_0NI}{2h(R_2-R_1)}\Big[u\ln\frac{R_2+\sqrt{R_2^2+u^2}}{R_1+\sqrt{R_1^2+u^2}}\Big]_{u=z-z_k-h/2}^{u=z-z_k+h/2}.\]

The field is linear in the three ampere-turns, so the best currents in free space follow from a linear least-squares fit; the currents fitted with the simulation must come out close to it. The insulated boundary 1 m away changes the axial field by about 0.1 %.

import numpy as np
from onesim.cad.exchange import load
from onesim.materials import VacuumPermeability
from onesim.meshing import TriangularMesher
from onesim.numerics import Numerics
from onesim.numerics.fem import FiniteElement, Lagrange, RevolvedH1SpaceFactory
from onesim.numerics.linear import SparseDirect
from onesim.physics.magnetostatics import (
    MagneticInsulation,
    MagneticSymmetry,
    Magnetostatics,
    RegionCurrent,
)
from onesim.simulation import Problem, Simulation
from onesim.simulation.studies import SimulationStudyService, StudyParameter
from onesim.visualization import plots, theme

theme.set()

Geometry and mesh

section cuts the parts in the plane \(y=0\); the section coordinates are \((r, z)\). The current density is assigned at the quadrature points inside the winding sections, so the triangles are small at the windings and grow towards the far boundary.

meridian = lambda name: load(
    f"coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric{name}.step"
).section((0, 1, 0))
domain = meridian("")
windings = [meridian(f"_coil_{k}") for k in (1, 2, 3)]
edges = domain.boundaries
midplane = domain.select(
    edges=lambda edge: all(abs(point[1]) < 1e-12 for point in edge.points)
)
axis = domain.select(
    edges=lambda edge: all(abs(point[0]) < 1e-12 for point in edge.points)
)
inner, outer = windings[0].bounding_box[0][0], windings[0].bounding_box[1][0]
height = windings[0].size[1]
centres = np.array(
    [0.5 * (w.bounding_box[0][1] + w.bounding_box[1][1]) for w in windings]
)
near = lambda points: np.min(
    [
        np.hypot(
            np.maximum(
                np.abs(points[:, 0] - 0.5 * (inner + outer)) - 0.5 * (outer - inner), 0
            ),
            np.maximum(np.abs(points[:, 1] - c) - 0.5 * height, 0),
        )
        for c in centres
    ],
    axis=0,
)
mesh = TriangularMesher(
    target_size=0.12,
    refinements=1,
    size_field=lambda points: np.clip(0.005 + near(points) / 2, 0.005, 0.12),
).generate(domain)

fig = plots.figure(1, 2, size=(14, 6))
plots.geometry(
    [
        load("coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric.step").named(
            "space around the coils"
        ),
        *(
            load(f"coil_current_optimization_axisymmetric_coil_{k}.step").named(
                f"coil {k}"
            )
            for k in (1, 2, 3)
        ),
    ],
    camera=plots.Camera(elevation=20, azimuth=-60),
    title="Upper half of the coil system (x > 0)",
    ax=fig[0],
)
plots.mesh(
    mesh,
    groups=("all",),
    legend=False,
    title=f"Meridian section: {mesh.cell_count} triangles",
    ax=fig[1],
)
plots.show()

Model and optimisation

Each winding has 100 turns; the parameters are the coil currents (A). The study service builds and solves one simulation per point and reads the axial field at the 21 points.

turns, target = 100, 1e-3
mu0 = float(VacuumPermeability().evaluate())
z_target = np.linspace(0.0, 0.2, 21)
on_axis = np.column_stack((np.zeros_like(z_target), z_target))
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(
        element=Lagrange(order=2), quadrature_order=4, provider=RevolvedH1SpaceFactory()
    ),
    linear_solver=SparseDirect(),
)
model_for = lambda values: Magnetostatics(
    domain=domain.domain(),
    permeability=VacuumPermeability(),
    current_sources=tuple(
        RegionCurrent.coil(
            winding,
            turns=turns,
            current=values[f"I{k}"],
            cross_section=(outer - inner) * height,
            within=domain.domain(),
        )
        for k, winding in zip((1, 2, 3), windings)
    ),
)
models = []
simulation_for = lambda model: Simulation(
    problem=Problem(
        name="Coil system",
        geometry=domain,
        physics=(model,),
        boundary_conditions=(
            MagneticSymmetry(boundary=midplane, physics=model),
            MagneticInsulation(boundary=edges.all - midplane - axis, physics=model),
        ),
    ),
    mesh=mesh,
    numerics=numerics,
)
service = SimulationStudyService(
    tuple(StudyParameter(f"I{k}", 0.0, 10.0) for k in (1, 2, 3)),
    lambda values: simulation_for(models.append(model_for(values)) or models[-1]),
    lambda result: {
        f"b{j}": value
        for j, value in enumerate(
            result.field(models[-1].magnetic_flux_density).evaluate(on_axis)[:, 2]
            / target
        )
    },
)
best = service.fit(
    residuals=lambda m: np.array([m[f"b{j}"] - 1 for j in range(len(z_target))]),
    start={"I1": 2.0, "I2": 2.0, "I3": 2.0},
    method="levenberg_marquardt",
    maximum_runs=100,
)
deviation = lambda run: float(
    np.mean([(run.value(f"b{j}") - 1) ** 2 for j in range(len(z_target))])
)

Reference: least squares with the closed-form coil field

coil_field = (
    lambda z, zc: mu0
    * turns
    / (2 * height * (outer - inner))
    * sum(
        sign * u * np.log((outer + np.hypot(outer, u)) / (inner + np.hypot(inner, u)))
        for sign, u in ((1, z - zc + height / 2), (-1, z - zc - height / 2))
    )
)
matrix = np.column_stack(
    [coil_field(z_target, c) + coil_field(z_target, -c) for c in centres]
)
reference, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(matrix, np.full_like(z_target, target), rcond=None)

Results

The two optima belong to two models: the fit minimizes the deviation of the simulated field (with the insulated boundary 1 m away and the windings on the mesh), the closed-form least squares that of the free-space formula. Each set of currents is therefore best in its own model; both rms values are shown in both models.

optimum = np.array([best.parameters[f"I{k}"] for k in (1, 2, 3)])
print(
    f"{best.run_count} solved simulations, converged: {best.converged} ({best.message})"
)
print(
    "coil   centre [m]   fitted current [A]   least squares, closed form [A]   difference"
)
for k, (c, value, ref) in enumerate(zip(centres, optimum, reference), start=1):
    print(
        f"{k:4d} {c:12.2f} {value:23.4f} {ref:32.4f} {100 * (value / ref - 1):+11.2f} %"
    )
start_run = service.executed_runs[0]
closed_rms = lambda currents: 100 * np.sqrt(
    np.mean((matrix @ currents / target - 1) ** 2)
)
print(
    f"rms deviation from 1 mT on |z| <= 0.2 m at the start: {100 * np.sqrt(deviation(start_run)):.3f} % (simulation)"
)
print(
    f"fitted currents:       simulation {100 * np.sqrt(np.mean(best.residuals ** 2)):.3f} %, closed form {closed_rms(optimum):.3f} %"
)
print(f"least-squares currents: closed form {closed_rms(reference):.3f} %")

Going further

Add the coil positions as further StudyParameters (one geometry file per position or a move_faces on the winding parts), weight the off-axis field to widen the uniform region, or add a second objective such as the total ampere-turns to trade uniformity against power.
