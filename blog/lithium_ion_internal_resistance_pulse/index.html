Internal resistance of a lithium-ion cell from current pulses

Current pulses on a 5 Ah graphite-SiOx | NMC811 cell at rest reveal its instantaneous and growing internal resistance.

What it computes

OneSimulation solves the Doyle-Fuller-Newman porous-electrode model through the cell thickness with spherical particles at every point. C/20, 1C and 3C pulses of 10 s at 50 % state of charge and 10 s pulses from 10 to 90 % state of charge give the pulse resistance over time, amplitude and state of charge.

What it checks

The instantaneous resistance converges at first order in the particle mesh to the Newman-Tobias porous-electrode value of 30.49 mOhm (+0.39 % with 320 shells, -0.13 % after Richardson extrapolation); over the state of charge the C/20 values stay within 2.1 % of the closed form on 80 shells, and at 1C the resistance grows from 24.1 to 29.1 mOhm within 10 s.

The notebook

The complete notebook, cell by cell. Run it to produce the figures and the comparison printout.

When a current step \(I\) is applied to a cell at rest, the voltage first drops at once and then keeps falling while concentration gradients build up in the particles and the electrolyte. The pulse resistance \(R(t)=\big(U_\mathrm{oc}-V(t)\big)/I\) is the usual way to characterise the internal resistance of a cell. In the Doyle-Fuller-Newman model (equations in the discharge-rest-charge example) the instantaneous part comes from electronic and ionic conduction and from the charge-transfer reaction distributed through the porous electrodes.

For a linearised Butler-Volmer law, \(a\,j = (a/r_\mathrm{ct})\,(\phi_s-\phi_e-U)\) with \(r_\mathrm{ct}=RT/(F\,j_0)\), the porous electrode of thickness \(L\) with conductivities \(\sigma\) (solid) and \(\kappa\) (electrolyte) has the area resistance

\[R_\mathrm{el}=\frac{L}{\kappa+\sigma}\left[1+\frac{2+\big(\frac{\sigma}{\kappa}+\frac{\kappa}{\sigma}\big)\cosh\nu}{\nu\,\sinh\nu}\right],\qquad \nu=L\sqrt{\frac{a}{r_\mathrm{ct}}\Big(\frac{1}{\kappa}+\frac{1}{\sigma}\Big)} ,\]

and the cell adds the separator, \(R_0=R_{\mathrm{el},-}+L_\mathrm{sep}/\kappa_\mathrm{sep}+R_{\mathrm{el},+}\).

Reference. J. Newman and C. W. Tobias, J. Electrochem. Soc. 109 (1962) 1183, for the porous-electrode resistance; cell parameters of Chen et al., J. Electrochem. Soc. 167 (2020) 080534 (LG M50, 5 Ah). The comparison uses a pulse of C/20, small enough for linear kinetics; 1C and 3C pulses show the nonlinear reaction resistance.

socs = np.round(np.linspace(0.05, 0.95, 19), 2)
cells = {

        cell,

rest = {
    soc: positive_ocp(c.positive.initial_stoichiometry, 0)
    - negative_ocp(c.negative.initial_stoichiometry, 0)
    for soc, c in cells.items()
}

Newman-Tobias resistance

The closed form with the exchange current \(j_0=2\,j_{0,\mathrm{ref}}\sqrt{\theta(1-\theta)}\) of each electrode at its initial stoichiometry.

newman_tobias = (
    lambda cell: cell.separator.thickness / cell.separator.electrolyte_conductivity
    + sum(
        layer.thickness
        / (k + s)
        * (1 + (2 + (s / k + k / s) * np.cosh(nu)) / (nu * np.sinh(nu)))
        for layer, k, s in (
            (layer, layer.electrolyte_conductivity, layer.electronic_conductivity)
            for layer in (cell.negative, cell.positive)
        )
        for nu in [
            layer.thickness
            * np.sqrt(
                3
                * layer.active_fraction
                / layer.particle_radius
                * 2
                * layer.reference_exchange_current
                * np.sqrt(
                    layer.initial_stoichiometry * (1 - layer.initial_stoichiometry)
                )
                / thermal
                * (1 / k + 1 / s)
            )
        ]
    )
)

Pulses

The instantaneous response is the voltage at the switch-on of a C/20 step; it is computed on particle meshes from 10 to 320 shells. Then 10 s pulses of C/20, 1C and 3C at 50 % state of charge followed by 30 s of rest, and 10 s pulses of C/20 and 1C from 10 to 90 % state of charge.

small = 0.05 * 5.0 / area
shells = (10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320)
instant = {
    n: (
        rest[0.5]
        - cell.solve(
            mode="DFN",
            current_density=small,
            final_time=1e-6,
            radial_cells=n,
            through_cells=(20, 10, 20),
            output_times=(0.0,),
        ).terminal_voltage[0]
    )
    / small
    for n in shells
}
times = np.r_[0.0, np.geomspace(1e-3, 9.99, 80), np.linspace(10.01, 40.0, 60)]
pulses = {
    rate: cell.solve(
        mode="DFN",
        current_density=lambda t, rate=rate: rate * 5.0 / area * float(t < 10.0),
        final_time=40.0,
        radial_cells=80,
        through_cells=(12, 6, 12),
        output_times=times,
    )
    for rate in (0.05, 1.0, 3.0)
}
sweep = {
    (rate, soc): cells[soc]
    .solve(
        mode="DFN",
        current_density=rate * 5.0 / area,
        final_time=10.0,
        radial_cells=80,
        through_cells=(12, 6, 12),
        output_times=(0.0, 10.0),
    )
    .terminal_voltage
    for rate in (0.05, 1.0)
    for soc in socs[1:-1:2]
}

Results

reference = newman_tobias(cell)
extrapolated = 2 * instant[320] - instant[160]
print(
    f"Newman-Tobias resistance at 50 % SOC: {1e4 * reference:.4f} Ω·cm² = {1e3 * reference / area:.3f} mΩ for the 5 Ah cell"
)
for n, value in instant.items():
    print(
        f"  {n:4d} particle shells: {1e4 * value:.4f} Ω·cm², deviation {100 * (value / reference - 1):+.3f} %"
    )
print(
    f"  Richardson extrapolation of 160 and 320 shells: deviation {100 * (extrapolated / reference - 1):+.3f} %"
)
for rate, run in pulses.items():
    resistance = (rest[0.5] - run.terminal_voltage) / (rate * 5.0 / area)
    print(
        f"{rate:4.2f}C pulse: R(0) = {1e3 * resistance[0] / area:.2f} mΩ, R(1 s) = {1e3 * np.interp(1.0, times, resistance) / area:.2f} mΩ, "
        f"R(10 s) = {1e3 * resistance[times < 10][-1] / area:.2f} mΩ; voltage 30 s after the pulse "
        f"{1e3 * (run.terminal_voltage[-1] - rest[0.5]):+.2f} mV from rest"
    )
print(
    "SOC     R(0) at C/20   Newman-Tobias   deviation   R(10 s) at C/20   R(10 s) at 1C   [mΩ]"
)
for soc in socs[1:-1:2]:
    r0, nt = (rest[soc] - sweep[0.05, soc][0]) / (0.05 * 5.0), newman_tobias(
        cells[soc]
    ) / area
    print(
        f"{soc:4.1f}   {1e3 * r0:12.2f}   {1e3 * nt:13.2f}   {100 * (r0 / nt - 1):+8.2f} %   {1e3 * (rest[soc] - sweep[0.05, soc][1]) / 0.25:15.2f}"
        f"   {1e3 * (rest[soc] - sweep[1.0, soc][1]) / 5.0:13.2f}"
    )
