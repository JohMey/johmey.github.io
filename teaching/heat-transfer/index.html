Energy conservation

For an isotropic stationary solid, the local energy balance is \(\rho c_p\partial_tT=\nabla\cdot(k\nabla T)+\dot q\). The outward conductive heat flux is \(-k\nabla T\cdot\mathbf n\). Prescribing this flux, prescribing temperature and using a convection law are three different boundary conditions. A convecting boundary gives \(-k\nabla T\cdot\mathbf n=h(T-T_\infty)\).

If material properties are constant, the diffusivity \(\alpha=k/(\rho c_p)\) sets the diffusion time \(L^2/\alpha\). Integrate the PDE over the domain to compare the energy stored with volumetric heating and net boundary heat flow. In a stationary source-free system, net outward heat flow is zero.

Resistance and lumped capacity

A plane wall of area \(A\), thickness \(L\) and conductivity \(k\) has conduction resistance \(L/(kA)\). A convection film adds \(1/(hA)\) in series at a wall face. In a lumped body with negligible internal temperature gradients, \(T-T_\infty=(T_0-T_\infty)e^{-t/\tau}\) and \(\tau=\rho c_p V/(hA)\).

The Biot number \(Bi=h(V/A)/k\) compares internal and surface thermal resistances. A small value supports the lumped model. Thermal radiation uses absolute temperature and a fourth-power law, so the exponential convection solution does not apply to pure radiation.

Worked example and lab

A 20 mm plane wall with \(k=2\) W/(m·K), area 1 m² and face temperatures 310 K and 290 K conducts 2 kW. Adding a convection film with \(h=100\) W/(m²·K) doubles the thermal resistance. At the same hot-face to ambient temperature difference, the rate is 1 kW.

  1. Run a steady conduction case and integrate both face fluxes. Compare with the resistance model.
  2. Run a transient wall or lumped example. Compare the temperature history and the heat balance, then halve the time step.
  3. Use the free-convection notebook to see how the Nusselt number measures departures from conduction.

Model the physics in OneSimulation

onesim.physics.heat_transfer

Use HeatConduction for a spatial Fourier balance with material properties and optional volumetric heating in W/m\(^3\). Supply FixedTemperature in K, HeatFlux as positive outward flux in W/m\(^2\), or ConvectiveHeatFlux with an ambient temperature and transfer coefficient. RadiativeHeatFlux applies a nonlinear grey diffuse boundary law to a spatial model. ConvectiveHeatTransfer adds a prescribed velocity field to the volume problem, as in Chapter 3. HeatAdvectionDiffusion is the line model with specified thermal diffusivity and advection speed. RadiationCooling and PhaseChangeHeating use lumped thermal states. Phase change uses ThermalPhaseChangeLawSet and a finite mushy interval. Plot temperature and enthalpy or liquid fraction across the transition to make the latent-heat effect visible.

For a first conduction notebook, prescribe a linear temperature trace on all faces of a box, set zero volumetric source and choose P1 FEM. A linear solution and constant conductive flux give independent verification. See test_public_stationary.py. The source exposes separate thermal service configurations, validation reports and feature entry points for integrations that use the facade instead of Simulation.

Additional worked models

6.10.1 Enthalpy and liquid fraction during heating

Use specific enthalpy as the initial state and derive temperature and liquid fraction from the phase law. The mushy interval spreads the latent heat across a finite temperature range.

from onesim.physics.heat_transfer import PhaseChangeHeating, ThermalPhaseChangeLawSet

domain = LumpedDomain()
model = PhaseChangeHeating(
    domain=domain.system,
    mass=2,
    heating_power=4,
    phase_law=ThermalPhaseChangeLawSet("mushy", 300, 302, 4, 2, 3),
)
t, data = history_case(
    domain,
    model,
    dict(specific_enthalpy=-2.0),
    ("specific_enthalpy", "temperature", "liquid_fraction"),
    end=6,
    dt=0.02,
)
np.testing.assert_allclose(data["specific_enthalpy"], -2 + 2 * t, atol=1e-10)
fig = history_plot("Enthalpy and liquid fraction during heating", model, t, data)
plt.show()

6.10.2 Advection and decay of a temperature wave

Use a periodic Fourier mode to distinguish phase transport from amplitude decay. The carrier here stores cell averages. The analytic curve includes the cell-average sinc factor.

from onesim.physics.heat_transfer import HeatAdvectionDiffusion

line = Line(start=(0, 0, 0), end=(1, 0, 0))
speed, diffusivity = 0.6, 0.02
model = HeatAdvectionDiffusion(
    domain=line.line, velocity=speed, thermal_diffusivity=diffusivity
)
n, end = 64, 0.15
result = line_experiment(
    line,
    model,
    dict(temperature=lambda x: 300 + 10 * np.cos(2 * np.pi * x)),
    FiniteVolume(
        convection=Rusanov(), reconstruction=MUSCL(), diffusion=TwoPointDiffusion()
    ),
    cells=n,
    end=end,
    dt=0.0005,
)
exact = lambda x: 300 + 10 * np.sinc(1 / n) * np.exp(
    -diffusivity * (2 * np.pi) ** 2 * end
) * np.cos(2 * np.pi * (x - speed * end))
field = result.field(model.temperature)
np.testing.assert_allclose(
    field.values, exact(field.space.dof_coordinates[:, 0]), atol=0.06, rtol=0
)
fig = profile_figure(
    "Periodic heat transport",
    result,
    model,
    ("temperature",),
    reference={"temperature": exact},
)
plt.show()
