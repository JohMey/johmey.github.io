Determine a gap from an inductance measurement

A 200-turn toroidal coil has cross-section 80 mm², core length 0.16 m and relative permeability 1600. A low-current test gives \(L=8.0\) mH. Infer the gap and determine the fraction of magnetic energy held there. State one reason this inference can misestimate the physical gap.

Convert the inductance to an effective length

For uniform area and negligible leakage, \(L=\mu_0N^2A/(g+\ell_c/\mu_r)\). The inferred effective length is \(0.503\) mm, of which 0.100 mm is contributed by the core. The gap is about 0.403 mm and the energy fraction is \(0.403/0.503=0.801\). Fringing gives the gap an effective area larger than the core area. Variations in actual incremental permeability would likewise bias a gap determined solely from an inductance reading.

Drop a sliding rod under gravity

Two vertical rails and their conducting top link form a closed circuit through a sliding rod of mass 0.020 kg and length 0.25 m. A uniform 0.50 T field is normal to the loop. The total resistance is 0.10 Ω. Derive the speed from rest, find the terminal speed and compare electrical power with gravitational power there. Neglect inductance and friction.

Solve the coupled force and current laws

The current magnitude is \(B\ell v/R\), giving upward drag \(B^2\ell^2v/R\). Thus \(m\dot v=mg-B^2\ell^2v/R\), with solution \(v=v_t(1-e^{-t/\tau})\), \(v_t=mgR/(B^2\ell^2)=1.26\) m/s and \(\tau=mR/(B^2\ell^2)=0.128\) s. At terminal speed the current is 1.57 A and heating is 0.246 W, matching \(mgv_t\). Changing resistance changes the terminal speed; the mechanically available power per second therefore also changes.

Drive a stationary loop with a changing field

A one-turn circular loop of area 0.012 m² and resistance 3.0 Ω lies normal to \(B(t)=0.080\cos(2\pi 50t)\) T. Find the peak emf, mean resistive power and total charge passing a reference point during a half-cycle from maximum positive to maximum negative flux. What changes if a second identical turn is added in series?

Distinguish derivative and integrated flux

The peak emf is \(A B_0\omega=0.302\) V. With negligible inductance, mean power is \(\mathcal E_0^2/(2R)=15.2\) mW. Integrating \(I=-\dot\Phi/R\) gives signed transported charge \(2AB_0/R=0.640\) mC in the selected positive direction. Two identical series turns double both the emf and the resistance. Current and half-cycle charge remain the same, while total mean power doubles. This assumes the same field threads each turn.
