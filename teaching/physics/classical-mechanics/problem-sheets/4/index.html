An unresolved binary

Spectroscopy and an eclipsing geometry give circular orbital speeds of 40 and 60 km/s. The period is 20.0 days. Find the separation, total mass and individual masses. What goes wrong for an unknown inclination? Use \(G=6.674\times10^{-11}\) SI and \(M_\odot=1.989\times10^{30}\) kg.

Show the orbital reconstruction

In the centre-of-mass frame the bodies are on opposite sides, so their relative speed is 100 km/s. One relative circular path in \(1.728\times10^6\) s gives \(a=v_{\rm rel}P/(2\pi)=2.75\times10^{10}\) m. Centripetal acceleration requires \(GM/a^2=v_{\rm rel}^2/a\) and gives \(M=4.12\times10^{30}\) kg or \(2.07M_\odot\). Centre-of-mass momentum balance gives \(m_{40}/m_{60}=60/40\). The masses are \(1.24M_\odot\) and \(0.83M_\odot\). Spectroscopic velocities alone measure \(v\sin i\) for inclination \(i\) from face-on. The mass inferred this way would be \(M\sin^3 i\).

A shadow in a beam

A collimated beam crosses a dilute slab with \(2.0\times10^{17}\) scatterers per square metre of projected area. Its undeflected fraction is 0.960. Determine the scattering cross section and the equivalent hard-sphere radius for point incident particles. Predict transmission for twice the slab thickness.

Show the attenuation calculation

For independent scatterers the fractional loss from the original beam in a small areal-density interval is \(dI/I=-\sigma\,dN_A\). Hence \(\sigma=-\ln(0.960)/N_A=2.04\times10^{-19}\) m². For a hard sphere the intercepted area is \(\pi R^2\), giving \(R=2.55\times10^{-10}\) m. Doubling thickness multiplies survival probabilities, giving \(0.960^2=0.9216\). A finite detector aperture accepting slightly scattered particles would make the measured loss smaller and bias this estimate downward.

Which impacts populate a detector ring?

For repulsive inverse-square scattering write \(b=C\cot(\theta/2)\) with \(C=0.50\) nm. Find the impact-parameter interval and cross section for scattering into all azimuths between \(60^\circ\) and \(120^\circ\). Compare the result when incident speed doubles at fixed interaction strength.

Show the annular area

The smaller deflection comes from the larger impact parameter. The radii are \(0.50\cot60^\circ=0.289\) nm and \(0.50\cot30^\circ=0.866\) nm. Integration of the beam annulus gives \(\pi(b_{\max}^2-b_{\min}^2)=2.09\) nm². This procedure includes all azimuths, so there is no additional solid-angle factor. Since \(C\) is proportional to inverse kinetic energy, doubling speed divides each radius by four and the cross section by sixteen, to \(0.131\) nm².
