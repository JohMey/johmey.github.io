Fit a weak cubic spring

A mass of 0.20 kg has a natural frequency of 10.00 Hz at negligible amplitude. At 10.0 mm amplitude it oscillates at 10.03 Hz. Assume a restoring force \(-kx-\beta x^3\). Estimate \(k\) and \(\beta\), predict the shift at 5.0 mm and test the perturbation assumption.

Show the stiffness estimates

The linear coefficient is \(k=m(2\pi f_0)^2=790\) N/m. Retaining the fundamental of \(\cos^3\omega t\) gives \(\Delta f/f_0\simeq3\beta A^2/(8k)\). Hence \(\beta=8k(0.0030)/(3\times10^{-4})=6.32\times10^4\) N/m³. The positive sign describes hardening. At half amplitude the shift falls by four, to \(0.0075\) Hz. The maximum cubic-to-linear force ratio at 10 mm is \(\beta A^2/k=0.0080\), compatible with a first-order estimate.

Which equilibria can hold a bead?

A bead slides without friction on a vertical hoop of radius 0.400 m rotating about its vertical diameter. Find the critical angular speed and stable positions at 6.00 rad/s. Determine the small oscillation frequency about either tilted position and compare with operation at 4.00 rad/s.

Show the stability calculation

With angle measured from the bottom the effective potential per unit mass is \(-gR\cos\theta-\Omega^2R^2\sin^2\theta/2\). Its derivative is \(R\sin\theta(g-\Omega^2R\cos\theta)\). The critical speed is \(\sqrt{g/R}=4.95\) rad/s. At 6.00 rad/s the tilted minima have \(\cos\theta=9.81/(36\times0.4)=0.6813\), hence \(\theta=\pm47.1^\circ\). Curvature divided by \(R^2\) gives \(\omega^2=\Omega^2\sin^2\theta\), or \(\omega=4.39\) rad/s. At 4.00 rad/s only the bottom is stable and \(\omega=\sqrt{g/R-\Omega^2}=2.92\) rad/s.

Tilt a symmetric double well

For dimensionless displacement \(x\), a potential is \(V/E_0=x^4/4-ax^2/2-hx\) with \(a=0.12\). Find equilibria and barrier height at \(h=0\). Determine the magnitude of bias where one metastable well disappears and explain why the transition can depend on sweep history.

Show the barrier and the loss of stability

At zero bias stationary points solve \(x(x^2-a)=0\). Curvature \(3x^2-a\) gives minima at \(\pm\sqrt{a}=\pm0.346\) and a maximum at zero. The energy barrier is \(a^2E_0/4=0.00360E_0\). A minimum and adjacent maximum merge when \(3x^2=a\) and \(h=x^3-ax\). Thus \(|h|=2(a/3)^{3/2}=0.0160\). A weakly damped or cold system initially captured in the unfavoured well can remain there as the bias crosses zero. In a slowly driven, damped experiment without appreciable fluctuations it switches near the disappearance of that minimum; noise can trigger earlier switching.
