A moment has an energy as well as a direction

Consider \(N\) independent identical magnetic moments in volume \(V\), each with allowed projection \(\pm\mu\) along a uniform applied induction \(B\). The energies are \(\mp\mu B\); the state aligned with the field has lower energy. For \(\beta=1/(k_BT)\) the one-moment partition function is \(Z_1=2\cosh(\beta\mu B)\). From \(\langle m_z\rangle=k_BT\,\partial_B\ln Z_1\) obtain

\[M=n\mu\tanh(\mu B/k_BT),\qquad n=N/V .\]

The full curve approaches \(n\mu\) at high field or low temperature. In the weak-field regime \(\mu B\ll k_BT\), expansion of the hyperbolic tangent gives \(M\simeq n\mu^2 B/(k_BT)\). Under weak magnetisation and negligible demagnetising corrections use \(B\simeq\mu_0H\); the dimensionless SI susceptibility \(\partial M/\partial H\) is then \(\chi=\mu_0n\mu^2/(k_BT)\). Be explicit about differentiating with respect to \(B\) or \(H\), which carry different units.

Work a moment count from two measurements

Suppose a dilute two-state sample exhibits saturation magnetisation \(9.27\times10^3\) A/m and the moments are known to have \(\mu=\mu_B=9.27\times10^{-24}\) J/T. The implied density is \(1.00\times10^{27}\) m⁻³. At 10 K and 1 T the argument \(\mu B/k_BT\) is 0.0672, safely in the linear range for a rough estimate: \(M\simeq622\) A/m. At the same temperature a field of about 14.9 T gives argument one and \(M/M_\mathrm{sat}=0.762\). Fitting a straight line up to that field would underestimate the zero-field slope.

If the moment magnitude is unknown, saturation determines \(n\mu\) and the small-field slope supplies \(n\mu^2\). Taken together they can separate density from moment within this model. A mixture of moment species, orbital contributions or interactions can defeat this two-equation inference. Plotting curves at several temperatures against \(B/T\) tests the independent-moment scaling without needing to know the overall density beforehand.

Compare with itinerant spin response

For a weakly perturbed degenerate metal, the two spin species shift by \(\mp\mu B\). Almost all states below \(E_F\) remain doubly occupied, so only a thin shell at the Fermi surface changes its net spin. At low temperature and nearly constant total density of states \(D_V(E_F)\), the spin imbalance is approximately \(D_V(E_F)\mu B\), giving \(M\simeq\mu^2D_V(E_F)B\). The leading Pauli response is nearly temperature independent, in contrast to the \(1/T\) scaling of independent localised moments. This argument addresses spin susceptibility; orbital diamagnetism also contributes to a real measured susceptibility.

Diagnose a small impurity contribution

If bulk data show a roughly constant offset plus a low-temperature upturn, test a simple additive model \(\chi=\chi_0+C/T\) over a weak-field interval. Plot \(\chi\) against \(1/T\) rather than taking an inverse before subtracting the offset. An upturn can arise from a dilute impurity population even when the host electronic sea dominates heat capacity. If scaling fails systematically, examine interactions between moments; the next chapter shows how cooperative ordering changes the small-field susceptibility.
