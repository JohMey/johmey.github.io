How a time-dependent perturbation transfers population

For a time-independent Hamiltonian \(H_0\) the energy components of a state accumulate phases without changing populations. Let a weak applied perturbation \(V(t)\) act for a known interval. Prepare the unperturbed eigenstate \(|i\rangle\) at \(t=0\) and write \(|\psi(t)\rangle=\sum_n c_n(t)e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}|n\rangle\). The interaction-picture amplitudes isolate changes beyond the free phases. Substitution into Schrödinger’s equation and projection onto \(|f\rangle\) give

\[i\hbar\dot c_f=\sum_n V_{fn}(t)e^{i\omega_{fn}t}c_n,\qquad \omega_{fn}=(E_f-E_n)/\hbar .\]

At first order retain \(c_i=1\) on the right-hand side. For \(f\ne i\) the resulting amplitude is the Fourier component of the perturbation at the transition frequency, \(c_f(T)=-(i/\hbar)\int_0^T V_{fi}(t)e^{i\omega_{fi}t}\,dt\). An energetically resonant drive still fails to populate a state if the coupling matrix element vanishes by symmetry. The operator describing the drive is as important as its frequency.

A rectangular pulse

Let \(V_{fi}(t)=V_{fi}\cos(\omega t)\) for \(0<t<T\), with an upward transition so \(\omega_{fi}>0\). Splitting the cosine into exponentials gives frequencies \(\omega_{fi}-\omega\) and \(\omega_{fi}+\omega\). Near resonance, for a pulse lasting many carrier cycles, the rapidly oscillating sum-frequency term contributes little. With detuning \(\Delta=\omega_{fi}-\omega\) the remaining integral gives

\[P_{i\to f}\simeq \frac{|V_{fi}|^2T^2}{4\hbar^2}\left[\frac{\sin(\Delta T/2)}{\Delta T/2}\right]^2 .\]

At exact resonance all contributions build with the same phase, so for a weak short pulse probability grows quadratically in time. The first zeros occur at \(|\Delta|=2\pi/T\), or ordinary-frequency detuning \(1/T\). The line shape measures coherent addition over the finite observation time. It can be broad even when the isolated energy levels have negligible natural width.

Calibrate a pulse

Take a 2.00 ms rectangular pulse with \(|V_{fi}|/\hbar=200\) rad/s. The resonant transition probability is \((200\times0.002/2)^2=0.0400\). At 250 Hz detuning the sinc argument is \(\pi/2\), leaving probability \(0.0162\). The first zeros lie 500 Hz on either side. Doubling pulse duration at the same coupling predicts four times the peak probability and half the first-zero spacing within the weak-drive approximation. A measurement can test both the area dependence and frequency scaling.

Once the predicted population is no longer small, depletion of the initial level removes the first-order approximation. For an isolated resonantly driven two-state system the rotating-wave solution is \(\sin^2(|V_{fi}|T/(2\hbar))\), describing coherent population transfer and return. Lifetime decay or field fluctuations introduce still other envelopes. Varying pulse duration at fixed drive strength distinguishes finite-time broadening from linewidths that persist as the pulse is lengthened.

The pulse envelope provides an additional independent control. Replacing the sudden rectangular turn-on and turn-off with smooth edges reduces spectral sidelobes because the Fourier transform of the smoother envelope falls faster away from resonance. Peak transfer at resonance, in the weak-drive regime, depends on the time integral of the drive envelope. Two pulses of different shapes can therefore have the same low-power resonant transition probability yet excite a nearby unwanted level with different probabilities. Specifying duration alone would be insufficient to reproduce such a measurement.
