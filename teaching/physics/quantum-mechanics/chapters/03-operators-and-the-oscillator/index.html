Operators and the quantum harmonic oscillator

Use commutators and ladder operators to derive an energy spectrum and connect its ground state with the position–momentum uncertainty bound.

Quantise near a stable minimum

A molecular bond stretched slightly from equilibrium has a potential \(V(x)\simeq V(0)+kx^2/2\) if the equilibrium derivative vanishes and the curvature \(k\) is positive. For relative motion the relevant mass is the reduced mass of the two nuclei. Discard the additive constant and write \(k=m\omega^2\). Quantum mechanics assigns the Hamiltonian \(\hat H=\hat p^2/(2m)+m\omega^2\hat x^2/2\). Its two terms do not commute because position and momentum obey \([\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar\).

For a normalised state, the expectation of an operator is \(\langle A\rangle=\langle\psi|\hat A|\psi\rangle\). Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to \((\hat x-\langle x\rangle)|\psi\rangle\) and \((\hat p-\langle p\rangle)|\psi\rangle\) shows that the product of their norms is at least the magnitude of their inner product. The imaginary part of that inner product is controlled by the commutator. One obtains \(\Delta x\,\Delta p\ge\hbar/2\), where the spreads are standard deviations of repeated measurements on equally prepared systems.

Factor a positive Hamiltonian

Define dimensionless operators \(a=\sqrt{m\omega/(2\hbar)}\,\hat x+i\hat p/\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}\) and \(a^\dagger\) as its adjoint. Multiplying them in the indicated order and using the commutator yields \([a,a^\dagger]=1\) and

\[\hat H=\hbar\omega(a^\dagger a+\tfrac12).\]

For any state the expectation of \(a^\dagger a\) is \(\|a\psi\|^2\), which cannot be negative. The Hamiltonian therefore has a lower bound \(\hbar\omega/2\). Interchanging operator order while treating \(x\) and \(p\) as ordinary numbers would miss this term. It accounts for the fact that a state cannot simultaneously place the particle at the bottom of the well and give it precisely zero momentum.

Now compute the commutator of \(\hat H\) with \(a\). The identity \([AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B\) gives \([\hat H,a]=-\hbar\omega a\). Operating with \(a\) on an energy eigenstate either gives zero or produces a new eigenstate lower by \(\hbar\omega\). Successive lowering must terminate since the energy cannot become negative. The final state obeys \(a|0\rangle=0\). Raising from it gives \(E_n=(n+1/2)\hbar\omega\) and the normalisation factors \(a|n\rangle=\sqrt n\,|n-1\rangle\) and \(a^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle\).

Find the shape behind the lowest level

Use \(\hat p=-i\hbar\,d/dx\) in the ground-state condition. It reduces to \(d\psi_0/dx=-(m\omega/\hbar)x\psi_0\), whose square-integrable solution is proportional to \(\exp[-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)]\). Normalising the squared Gaussian gives position variance \(\hbar/(2m\omega)\). Its momentum variance is \(m\hbar\omega/2\). The product of standard deviations is exactly \(\hbar/2\), so the ground state saturates the general uncertainty bound.

The mean potential and kinetic energies in this state are both \(\hbar\omega/4\). Increasing the stiffness narrows the position distribution and raises the momentum spread. This provides a physical interpretation of the increasing ground energy which is independent of the ladder notation. In an excited energy eigenstate the mean position still vanishes by parity, yet its variance is larger.

Read a vibrational line and a selection rule

Take effective mass \(1.0\times10^{-26}\) kg and harmonic frequency \(f=5.0\) THz. Then \(\omega=2\pi f\), adjacent levels differ by \(hf=20.7\) meV, and the zero-point energy is 10.3 meV. The ground-state position standard deviation is \(1.30\times10^{-11}\) m. A spectral measurement of adjacent level spacings determines the curvature-to-mass ratio but cannot separate mass and stiffness without another measurement.

A weak oscillatory perturbation proportional to displacement has matrix elements of \(\hat x\propto a+a^\dagger\). It therefore connects adjacent number states, giving the dipole selection rule \(\Delta n=\pm1\) within the harmonic model and a linear coupling. If a real molecular spectrum contains overtones or nonuniform spacings, anharmonicity or a nonlinear dependence of dipole moment on displacement can supply the missing physics.

It is possible to recover the zero-point energy without solving a differential equation. A centred packet of spatial variance \(\sigma_x^2\) has potential energy \(m\omega^2\sigma_x^2/2\). The uncertainty relation bounds its kinetic energy below by \(\hbar^2/(8m\sigma_x^2)\) when mean momentum vanishes. Minimizing their sum over width gives \(\sigma_x^2=\hbar/(2m\omega)\) and an energy bound \(\hbar\omega/2\). A Gaussian saturates the uncertainty inequality and reaches the bound. Making a classical oscillator sit identically at rest would require both energy contributions to vanish simultaneously, which the commutator forbids.
