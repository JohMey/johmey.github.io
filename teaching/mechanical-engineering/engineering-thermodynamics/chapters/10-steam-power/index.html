Follow a kilogram through the loop

A simple steam plant pumps condensed water to boiler pressure, heats it to steam, expands it through a turbine and condenses it at low pressure. Water is nearly incompressible in the pump, while vapour occupies a much greater specific volume during expansion. A reversible steady adiabatic device satisfies \(dh=v\,dp\) because \(Tds=dh-v\,dp\) and \(ds=0\). Pressure increase therefore costs little work per kilogram when the fluid is liquid. This is one reason for condensing the working fluid before pumping it.

Number the saturated liquid leaving the condenser as state 1, the pump outlet as 2, the turbine inlet as 3 and the exhaust as 4. Neglect changes in kinetic and potential energy. Using positive magnitudes for pump work input and turbine work output gives

\[w_p\simeq v_1(p_2-p_1),\quad w_t=h_3-h_4,\quad q_{\rm in}=h_3-h_2,\quad \eta_{\rm th}=(w_t-w_p)/q_{\rm in}.\]

The exhaust pressure is set by the low-pressure side. An ideal turbine exit at that pressure has \(s_{4s}=s_3\); a real adiabatic turbine delivers a smaller enthalpy drop. Its isentropic efficiency is \(\eta_t=(h_3-h_4)/(h_3-h_{4s})\).

Calculate an ideal and an actual expansion

Use illustrative rounded state data. Condenser pressure is 10 kPa, with \(h_1=191.8\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\), \(v_1=0.00101\,\mathrm{m^3/kg}\), \(s_f=0.649,\ s_{fg}=7.50\,\mathrm{kJ/(kg\,K)}\) and \(h_{fg}=2393\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\). Boiler pressure is 4000 kPa and superheated inlet properties are \(h_3=3214\,\mathrm{kJ/kg},s_3=6.77\,\mathrm{kJ/(kg\,K)}\). The ideal pump requires \(0.00101(4000-10)=4.03\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\), so \(h_2=195.8\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\).

At the low pressure, the ideal exhaust quality is \(x_{4s}=(6.77-0.649)/7.50=0.816\), giving \(h_{4s}=191.8+0.816(2393)\simeq2145\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\). With turbine efficiency 0.85 the actual exit enthalpy is \(3214-0.85(3214-2145)=2305\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\). The corresponding quality is \((2305-191.8)/2393=0.883\). At fixed inlet and exhaust pressure this less efficient turbine leaves more enthalpy in the exhaust, and therefore a higher quality inside the two-phase region.

Interpret the condenser and generator duties

The actual turbine delivers about 909 kJ/kg. Subtracting pump work leaves approximately 905 kJ/kg net shaft work. Boiler heat input is \(3214-195.8=3018\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\), giving thermal efficiency about 30.0%. The condenser rejects \(2305-191.8=2113\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\), which closes the cycle energy balance within the rounding of the property data. At 2.0 kg/s, the idealised net mechanical output is about 1.81 MW before generator and auxiliary losses.

A lower condenser pressure generally increases the available expansion work for a fixed turbine inlet. It can also lower exhaust quality in the ideal expansion, increasing liquid droplet loading on the later blades. Evaluating such a change requires new saturation properties and a fresh state calculation. Treating the exhaust as an ideal gas would omit the phase fraction precisely where it affects both the energy balance and turbine operating requirements.

Estimate the consequence of a pump loss

If the pump’s isentropic efficiency falls to 0.70, actual input rises from 4.03 to \(4.03/0.70=5.76\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\). The turbine work remains the same under the specified inlet and outlet assumptions. Boiler duty falls by 1.73 kJ/kg because its feed arrives with more enthalpy. Both effects should enter the revised efficiency, even though pump work is small compared with the turbine output.
