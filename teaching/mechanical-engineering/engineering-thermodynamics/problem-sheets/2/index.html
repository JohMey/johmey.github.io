Two insulated copper blocks

Blocks of masses 2 and 1 kg at 400 and 280 K touch inside an insulated enclosure. Use \(c=0.390\,\mathrm{kJ/(kg\,K)}\). Find the final temperature and total entropy generation. Why may one block lose entropy?

Combine energy and entropy balances

Energy conservation gives \(T_f=(2\times400+280)/3=360\,\mathrm K\). The warmer block has \(\Delta S_h=0.780\ln(360/400)=-0.0822\,\mathrm{kJ/K}\). The other gains \(0.390\ln(360/280)=0.0980\,\mathrm{kJ/K}\). The net 0.0158 kJ/K is generated internally. Entropy crosses the interface with heat, so only the total for the insulated pair has to be nondecreasing.

Interpret a heat-engine test

An engine absorbs 15 kW from a reservoir at 750 K and rejects 10 kW to a second reservoir at 300 K. Find its power, thermal efficiency, entropy generation and reversible maximum power at the same heat input.

Interpret the missing output

The cyclic energy balance gives \(15-10=5\,\mathrm{kW}\) of power and efficiency \(5/15=0.333\). The reservoirs generate a combined entropy change rate \(10/300-15/750=0.01333\,\mathrm{kW/K}\); the working substance returns to its initial state. A reversible engine reaches \(1-300/750=0.600\), or 9 kW. The difference of 4 kW equals \(300\dot S_{\rm gen}\) at this sink temperature.

Measure cooling at the evaporator

Refrigerant enthalpies before and after compression are 405 and 451 kJ/kg. Liquid leaves the condenser at 257 kJ/kg and throttles into the evaporator. For 0.045 kg/s, calculate cooling duty, compressor input and heating COP. If the evaporator and condenser reservoirs are at 263 and 308 K, check the heating COP against its reversible bound.

Close the cycle

The valve gives \(h_4=h_3=257\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}\). Evaporation absorbs \(0.045(405-257)=6.66\,\mathrm{kW}\); compression requires \(0.045(451-405)=2.07\,\mathrm{kW}\). The condenser duty is 8.73 kW, hence the heating COP is 4.22. The reversible heating bound is \(308/(308-263)=6.84\). Both COPs refer to heat delivered to the warm side per unit work, which permits values above unity.
