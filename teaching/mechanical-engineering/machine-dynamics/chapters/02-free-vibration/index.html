Displace a machine from its static position

A pump on a spring mount settles under its weight. Pull it slightly farther down and release it. The weight still acts, but the extra motion is driven by the change in spring force. Define \(x\) as displacement from the loaded equilibrium, take constant local spring stiffness \(k\), and represent dissipation by a force opposing velocity of magnitude \(c|\dot x|\). Newton’s law becomes \(m\ddot x+c\dot x+kx=0\). This model describes one isolated mode over an amplitude range where inertia, stiffness and damping are approximately constant.

Multiply the equation by \(\dot x\). The mass term differentiates kinetic energy, and the spring term differentiates stored elastic energy. Consequently \(d(m\dot x^2/2+kx^2/2)/dt=-c\dot x^2\). Energy cannot grow for positive damping. Displacement can increase temporarily as kinetic energy converts back into spring energy, so the instantaneous displacement alone is not an energy meter.

Obtain the decay and oscillation rates

Trying \(x=e^{st}\) gives \(ms^2+cs+k=0\). Define \(\omega_n=\sqrt{k/m}\) and \(\zeta=c/(2m\omega_n)\). The roots are \(s=-\zeta\omega_n\pm\omega_n\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\). For \(0<\zeta<1\) their imaginary part gives the damped angular frequency \(\omega_d=\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\). The real part gives a decaying exponential. A general underdamped trace is

\[x(t)=e^{-\zeta\omega_nt}\left[A\cos(\omega_dt)+B\sin(\omega_dt)\right].\]

At \(\zeta=1\) the repeated root gives \((A+Bt)e^{-\omega_nt}\). Above critical damping both roots are negative and real. A lack of visible oscillation therefore does not by itself determine the stiffness. For an underdamped trace, use two peaks of the same sign: their separation is \(T_d=2\pi/\omega_d\), and their amplitude ratio depends only on the envelope decay.

Infer parameters from four cycles

A 20.0 kg mounted component rings down from a positive peak of 8.0 mm to a positive peak of 2.0 mm four cycles later, after 1.60 s. The period is \(T_d=0.400\) s and \(\omega_d=15.71\) rad/s. The log decrement per cycle is \(\delta=\ln(8/2)/4=0.3466\). Since \(x_j/x_{j+1}=\exp(\zeta\omega_n T_d)\), the envelope decay rate is \(\alpha=\delta/T_d=0.8664\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\).

Now \(\omega_n=\sqrt{\omega_d^2+\alpha^2}=15.73\) rad/s and \(\zeta=\alpha/\omega_n=0.0551\). The inferred stiffness is \(m\omega_n^2=4.95\) kN/m, while \(c=2m\alpha=34.7\) N·s/m. After eight cycles the prediction is 0.50 mm. Using peaks separated by several cycles reduces sensitivity to noise in any one amplitude or timestamp.

Challenge the damping assumption

Subtract the equilibrium sensor reading before taking amplitude ratios. A fixed offset changes the ratio of small late peaks more strongly than large early peaks. Plot \(\ln |x_j|\) against time for peaks whose signal clearly exceeds the noise floor. A straight trend supports the viscous envelope. Approximately constant loss of amplitude per cycle suggests a different mechanism such as dry friction. Two nearby natural frequencies may produce a beat envelope that grows and shrinks even though total energy decays.

A second experiment can separate stiffness from added inertia. If a small known mass is bolted to the same moving assembly and the support stiffness is unchanged, \(1/\omega_n^2\) should increase in proportion to effective mass. This check assumes that the added mass participates fully in the chosen coordinate. A flexible appendage that moves out of phase changes the system being identified.

Correct a biased displacement signal

Suppose a displacement sensor reads 0.50 mm high relative to the true equilibrium. The first and fifth positive peaks then appear as 8.50 and 2.50 mm. Their raw log ratio over four cycles gives \(0.25\ln(8.5/2.5)=0.306\), underestimating the true 0.347 decrement. The error grows as the decaying signal approaches the offset. One remedy is to measure equilibrium independently when excitation has stopped. Another is to use the difference between consecutive positive and negative extrema and compare such differences one whole period apart. The constant offset cancels while, for a single underdamped mode, successive peak-to-trough ranges retain the same exponential ratio. Late peaks below the sensor resolution still carry little reliable damping information.
