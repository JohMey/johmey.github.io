Torsional stiffness of shafts and closed sections

Derive torque–twist relations and explain the large difference in stiffness between an intact tube and an open strip.

Chapter 7 of 12

Rotate two adjacent sections

A torque meter measures twist between two stations on a shaft. To infer torque, first ask how material between the stations deforms. A straight circular shaft in uniform, small-deformation torsion has cross-sections that rotate as rigid discs. Two discs separated by \(dx\) rotate relative to one another by \(d\theta\). A fibre at radius \(r\) moves sideways by \(r\,d\theta\), giving shear strain \(\gamma=r\,d\theta/dx\). With elastic shear modulus \(G\), the shear stress increases linearly from the centre to the surface.

Each area element transmits a torque \(r\tau\,dA\). Integrating produces the polar second moment of area \(J\), whose dimensions are length to the fourth power. With constant torque and cross-section, integration along the shaft then converts twist per length into total relative angle.

\[T=\int_A r\tau\,dA=G\theta'\int_A r^2dA,\qquad J=\frac{\pi}{2}(r_o^4-r_i^4),\qquad \theta=\frac{TL}{GJ}.\]

Follow a torque through a stepped shaft

A 0.75 m aluminium tube of outer radius 15 mm and inner radius 12 mm transmits 45 N m. For \(G=26\,\mathrm{GPa}\), \(J=4.695\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m^4}\). Its twist is \(45(0.75)/(26\times10^9J)=0.02765\,\mathrm{rad}=1.58^\circ\). Surface shear is \(45(0.015)/J=14.4\,\mathrm{MPa}\). The inside surface still carries 80% of this stress. Drilling a central hole removes the least highly stressed material first.

Twist can also identify an unknown joint compliance. If an instrument measures 2.00° across this tube plus its end couplings, and all parts carry the same torque, the couplings contribute about 0.42°. Their combined torsional compliance is approximately \(0.42\pi/[180(45)]=1.6\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{rad/(N\,m)}\). Such a comparison assumes that the gauges do not include a second shaft segment and that the torque is transmitted without slip.

Carry torque around a single closed cell

For a long, thin-walled closed tube with a single cell, use the shear flow \(q=\tau t\), the force per unit length of wall. Longitudinal equilibrium makes this flow approximately constant around the perimeter if no intermediate longitudinal force is introduced. A length \(ds\) carries tangential force \(q\,ds\). Its moment about an arbitrary origin is \(q\) times twice the triangular area swept by the radius vector. Around the median wall contour this gives \(T=2A_mq\).

For a rectangular cell with median dimensions 80 by 40 mm, a torque of 320 N m gives \(q=320000/[2(80)(40)]=50\,\mathrm{N/mm}\). A 2 mm wall carries 25 MPa while a 4 mm wall carries 12.5 MPa. A slender longitudinal slit interrupts the closed path. The constant circulating flow solution then ceases to apply. A seam that reconnects the two lips must transmit the longitudinal shear flow if the component is to recover its closed-section behaviour.

Distinguish two section properties

For a noncircular closed tube, the polar area moment and torsion constant generally differ. Compatibility around a single thin-walled cell gives \(\theta'=T\oint ds/(Gt)/(4A_m^2)\). Making only a short wall segment very thin substantially increases this integral. A wall segment can therefore control angle of twist even when equilibrium fixes the same shear flow everywhere around the cell.
