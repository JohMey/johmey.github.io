What do several plant tests tell us?

The gain of a drive changes as it warms up, and a flexible mounting introduces poorly measured phase lag at high frequency. Let \(P_0\) be a nominal model. One useful description is multiplicative uncertainty, \(P=P_0(1+W\Delta)\), with stable dimensionless \(\Delta\) of gain at most one and a specified frequency weight \(W\). The magnitude of \(W\) bounds relative error. Estimate it from \(|P/P_0-1|\) across an ensemble of measurements, retaining phase in the complex ratio. A 20% amplitude difference and a 20° phase difference generally produce a larger relative error than either alone.

Where \(P_0\) is close to zero this relative description can be poorly conditioned. An additive uncertainty \(P=P_0+W_a\Delta\) can describe an absolute error floor there. A bound fitted only at sampled frequencies requires a reason to hold between samples, especially near flexible resonances. A robustness calculation is conditional on this modelling work.

Factor the perturbed characteristic equation

For the nominal controller \(C\), set \(L_0=P_0C\) and \(T_0=L_0/(1+L_0)\). Under multiplicative perturbation,

\[1+PC=(1+L_0)(1+W T_0\Delta).\]

Assume internal stability of the nominal interconnection and stable, proper uncertainty factors with no hidden unstable cancellation. If \(\sup_\omega|W(i\omega)T_0(i\omega)|<1\), the perturbation loop has gain less than one and cannot destabilise the feedback. One way to see the mechanism is the convergent inverse series \(1-G+G^2-\cdots\) for \(G=WT_0\Delta\) in the stable-system norm. The pointwise bound protects against all allowed dynamic phase choices. Failure of a sufficient test alone does not prove the particular measured plant unstable.

Work a frequency-domain budget

At one frequency a nominal loop has \(L_0=-0.8+0.4i\). Then \(|1+L_0|=\sqrt{0.2}\), \(|L_0|=\sqrt{0.8}\), and \(|T_0|=2\). Relative uncertainty below 50% at that frequency keeps \(|W T_0|<1\) there. A measured 60% bound crosses this threshold. Geometrically the perturbed loop samples a disk centred at \(L_0\) of radius \(|L_0 W|\). At 50% its rim touches \(-1\). Other frequencies still require examination, and a physically narrower family such as a single real gain may occupy far less than the full disk.

Add a performance requirement

A stable loop can still amplify an output disturbance unacceptably. For the uncertain plant the sensitivity is \(S=S_0/(1+WT_0\Delta)\). The triangle inequality gives \(|S|\le |S_0|/(1-|WT_0|)\) where the denominator is positive. If \(|S_0|=0.10\) and \(|WT_0|\le0.25\), disturbances can be amplified by at most 0.133 in this bound. Very near the robustness limit that bound becomes large. Shaping the nominal loop for a small uncertainty product near troublesome resonances can therefore protect both stability and useful performance. Record the sensor configuration, loading and frequency band over which the underlying plant measurements were made.
