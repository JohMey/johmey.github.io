Predict a sequence before applying the first action

For \(x_{k+1}=Ax_k+Bu_k\) with measured or estimated \(x_k\), successive substitutions give \(x_{k+2}=A^2x_k+ABu_k+Bu_{k+1}\). The initial state sets the free response; the planned input sequence contributes linearly. With quadratic tracking and effort penalties, linear dynamics and linear bounds on states or inputs, planning becomes a convex quadratic programme. At each update apply only the first planned command, acquire a new measurement and solve again. Receding the horizon provides feedback when disturbances make actual motion depart from the earlier prediction.

Solve a two-step constrained problem

Take the dimensionless integrator \(x_{k+1}=x_k+u_k\), \(x_k=2\), and target zero. Minimise \(x_{k+1}^2+x_{k+2}^2+u_k^2+u_{k+1}^2\). With \(u=u_k,v=u_{k+1}\), the objective is \((2+u)^2+(2+u+v)^2+u^2+v^2\). Its stationary equations are \(3u+v=-4,\ u+2v=-2\). The unconstrained plan \(u=-1.2,\ v=-0.4\) reaches states 0.8 and 0.4. If both input magnitudes are limited to one, the first command is infeasible. Fixing \(u=-1\) and reoptimising the second gives \(v=-0.5\), reaching 1 and 0.5. The derivative with respect to \(u\) points toward smaller \(u\) at this boundary, confirming the active limit.

After applying −1, suppose the new state is exactly 1. The next two-step unconstrained plan scales the original one by one half, giving first action −0.6. The earlier second action was −0.5. This change is consistent even without disturbances, since the new optimisation includes a further future step. A terminal cost approximating the remaining cost of control can reduce this artificial dependence on the planning horizon.

Feasible commands and feasible trajectories differ

A command bound always has feasible choices if zero lies within it. Adding a hard state constraint, such as a position stop, may make all predicted trajectories infeasible from the current state. Before asking for a time-optimal move, compute elementary reachability bounds. With \(|u|\le1\), a scalar integrator can move at most \(N\) units in \(N\) samples. A terminal constraint demanding zero from state 3 in two samples is impossible. Allowing slack in a comfort-related temperature band may be defensible; a physical actuator limit must still be enforced. Report which constraints were active and what performance was sacrificed.

Close the engineering loop

For a practical comparison, first establish a verified baseline with the OneSimulation proportional-loop record. Reconstruct its pole and finite-record bias from the model parameters. This record witnesses how an unconstrained linear loop behaves; it does not itself exercise a predictive optimiser. Now design a separate held-input or constrained test whose command reaches a known bound, record measured timestamps and actuator signals, and compare with a by-hand prediction of the first active limit. The useful performance claims differ: accurate steady tracking, stable sampled dynamics, adequate estimator uncertainty and feasibility under limits each require an observation capable of testing them.
