1. Select damping from a peak

A unity-static-gain second-order system has no zeros. Specify 10% overshoot and first peak at 1 s after a step. (a) Derive damping ratio and damped frequency. (b) Find the desired characteristic polynomial. (c) State which prediction fails if a numerator zero is added and the poles are held fixed.

Start with the logarithm of the overshoot

The positive logarithmic decrement to the first peak is \(-\ln(0.10)=2.303=\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\), giving \(\zeta=0.591\). The damped frequency is \(\omega_d=\pi\) rad/s, and the exponential decay rate is \(2.303\) s⁻¹. The poles are \(-2.303\pm3.142i\), so the monic polynomial is \(s^2+4.605s+15.17\). A zero changes the residues and the step shape. The poles still give the natural frequencies and decay rates, but first-peak time and overshoot need recalculation.

2. Three time constants and one gain

For \(L=g/[s(s+1)(s+3)]\) under unity negative feedback, (a) derive the closed-loop characteristic polynomial, (b) find the positive gain range for stability and the crossing frequency, and (c) compare the pole result with the open-loop frequency response at the crossing.

Locate the imaginary-axis pair

The polynomial is \(s^3+4s^2+3s+g\). The first column of the Routh array is \(1,4,(12-g)/4,g\); all signs agree for \(0\lt g\lt12\). At \(g=12\) factor the polynomial as \((s+4)(s^2+3)\). The crossing frequency is \(\sqrt3\) rad/s, with the third pole still at \(-4\). Substituting \(s=i\sqrt3\) in the open-loop denominator gives \(-12\), so \(L=-1\) at the same gain and frequency.

3. Delay on a positioning stage

With \(L=2/[s(1+s/2)]\), (a) find the gain crossover, (b) estimate the delay that would use all phase margin, and (c) evaluate the remaining margin after including 0.10 s delay. Count angles consistently.

Convert delay to a phase at crossover

Writing \(x=\omega_c^2\) gives \(x(1+x/4)=4\), so \(\omega_c=\sqrt{-2+2\sqrt5}=1.572\) rad/s. The phase margin is \(90^\circ-\arctan(1.572/2)=51.8^\circ\), or 0.905 rad. A pure delay leaves the crossover magnitude fixed, so the first crossing occurs at about \(0.905/1.572=0.576\) s. A 0.10 s delay costs 0.157 rad or 9.0°, leaving approximately 42.8°. This calculation assumes the given loop is otherwise accurately modelled.
