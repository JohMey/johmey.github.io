Forces and motion

Start with a cut through a loaded beam. Use force balance again for a column of soil and layers of moving liquid. For a freely spinning body, turn to angular momentum and track what a small change in the initial rotation can do.

  1. Why does a beam bend less when turned on edge?

    Use moment balance to predict how far the tip of a cantilever moves when you rotate its cross-section.

  2. Why does the ground sink when groundwater is pumped?

    Trace how a drop in pore pressure transfers load to the soil skeleton and estimate the resulting settlement.

  3. Why does a narrower channel carry so much less fluid?

    Derive the velocity profile between parallel plates and see how a change in gap alters the flow rate.

  4. Why does a freely spinning handle flip?

    Use angular momentum to distinguish stable and unstable rotation about the principal axes of a rigid body.

Transport and thermal response

Follow a disturbance through time or space. Compare the return to equilibrium in a cooling body and a reacting mixture, then examine how electrical leakage limits the reach of a signal and how a support changes a heated solid’s response.

  1. How long does a metal part take to cool?

    Balance stored thermal energy against heat loss to air and calculate the time to a target temperature.

  2. What sets a reaction’s equilibrium and how quickly does it approach it?

    Use a species balance to distinguish the final composition of a reversible reaction from its rate of approach.

  3. How far does a voltage change spread along a passive fibre?

    Balance axial current against membrane leakage and calculate the distance over which a steady voltage decays.

  4. When does heating a solid produce stress?

    Separate thermal strain from elastic strain and use the support conditions to find displacement and stress.

Electric and magnetic fields

Work from static fields to fields that drive currents. First decide what must be continuous at a material interface. Then follow what changes when a coil has finite length, a magnetic field varies with time or a conducting fluid moves.

  1. Why can a thin air gap take up half the voltage?

    Use Gauss’s law to determine how two insulating layers share the voltage between capacitor plates.

  2. When does the long-solenoid formula work?

    Add the fields of current loops along a coil and estimate the centre-field error caused by its finite length.

  3. How far does an alternating magnetic field penetrate a metal?

    Relate magnetic diffusion to skin depth and predict the attenuation and phase lag inside a conductor.

  4. Why does a magnetic field flatten duct flow?

    Balance viscous and magnetic forces to find the length scale of a Hartmann layer.

Waves and oscillations

Begin with energy moving between a capacitor and an inductor. Identify the restoring force for an electron displacement, then use boundary conditions to find which waves a room can sustain and how a dielectric surface reflects light.

  1. Why does a discharging capacitor reverse polarity?

    Follow the exchange of electric and magnetic energy and predict when the capacitor voltage changes sign.

  2. What sets the rhythm of an electron plasma?

    Derive the restoring force on displaced electrons and predict how oscillation frequency depends on density.

  3. Which notes can a closed room sustain?

    Apply rigid-wall conditions to the wave equation and predict the frequencies and shapes of standing sound waves.

  4. Why does reflected light become polarised?

    Resolve an incident wave into two polarisations and find the angle where one reflected component vanishes.
