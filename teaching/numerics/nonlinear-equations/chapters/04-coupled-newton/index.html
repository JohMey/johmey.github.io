Newton’s method for coupled balances

Linearise the whole model at one state

Two unknown physical quantities may influence both measured balances. Changing one to erase one residual can disrupt the other. The vector Taylor expansion \(F(x+s)=F(x)+J(x)s+O(\|s\|^2)\) collects the simultaneous first-order response. Entry \(J_{ij}\) is the derivative of residual \(i\) with respect to unknown \(j\) while holding the other unknowns fixed. A Newton step solves the linear system \(J(x)s=-F(x)\) and sets \(x_{new}=x+s\). Factor or iteratively solve that system rather than explicitly computing an inverse.

Suppose \(J\) is Lipschitz with constant \(L\) near a root, and the inverses in that neighbourhood satisfy \(\|J^{-1}\|\leq K\). Integrate \(J\) along the line from the current point to the root. The error left by replacing \(J\) with its value at the start is bounded by \(L\|x-r\|^2/2\). Multiplying by the inverse yields the local Newton bound \(KL\|x-r\|^2/2\). This derivation makes explicit which objects must stay well behaved: derivative variation and amplification by the linear solve.

Intersect an energy constraint with a matching condition

Take the dimensionless equations \(F_1=x^2+y^2-1,\ F_2=x-y\), and choose the positive branch. The linearised system at \((0.8,0.6)\) has \(F=(0,0.2)\) and rows \((1.6,1.2),(1,-1)\). The point already lies on the unit circle but violates matching. Newton requires \(s_y=s_x+0.2\); substituting in the first row gives \(2.8s_x=-0.24\). Thus \(s_x=-0.0857143,\ s_y=0.114286\) and the new state is \((5/7,5/7)\).

The linear constraint is now exact. The circle residual is \(2(5/7)^2-1=1/49\), a second-order consequence of moving along its tangent rather than along the circle itself. At the next iteration the two corrections are equal. Solving \((20/7)s=-1/49\) gives \(s=-1/140\), hence \(x=y=99/140=0.707143\). The desired value is \(1/\sqrt2=0.707107\). A different initial state could lead to the negative intersection. Physical admissibility must therefore supplement the algebraic equations.

Select scales and verify a derivative action

Changing units from metres to millimetres multiplies the displayed displacement by 1000 without changing the model. Yet an unscaled Euclidean norm of a state mixing displacement and temperature can then change radically. Choose characteristic scales in a positive diagonal matrix \(D_x\) and residual scales \(D_F\). Form the dimensionless unknown \(z=D_x^{-1}x\) and residual \(G=D_F^{-1}F(D_xz)\). By the chain rule \(J_G=D_F^{-1}J_FD_x\). For an exact undamped Newton solve this produces the same physical correction, but stopping tests, trust regions and finite differences now acquire interpretable scales.

To check a programmed Jacobian, choose a direction \(d\) with nonzero entries in multiple unknowns and compare \(Jd\) with \([F(x+sd)-F(x)]/s\) over several \(s\). For the circle constraint the discrepancy is exactly \(s(d_x^2+d_y^2)\), while for the linear matching condition it is zero in exact arithmetic. A one-direction check can miss a coding error orthogonal to that direction. Try additional directions emphasizing different couplings, keeping clear of nonsmooth switches. This test inspects the local derivative independently of the solver’s final residual.

Separate state sensitivity from linearisation error

Suppose the matching constraint in the intersection example is perturbed to \(x-y=\epsilon\), while the unit-circle measurement remains exact. About the positive root \(x=y=1/\sqrt2\), differentiating the circle implies \(\delta x+\delta y=0\). Differentiating the matching constraint gives \(\delta x-\delta y=\epsilon\). Thus a measurement offset of 0.02 moves the coordinates by approximately \(+0.01\) and \(-0.01\), even if the perturbed nonlinear equations are solved to roundoff.

Geometrically, uncertainty becomes much larger when the two constraint curves meet almost tangentially. Their Jacobian rows then point in nearly the same direction, leaving a poorly resolved transverse displacement. Reducing the Newton tolerance improves the algebraic solution of the supplied measurements, but cannot increase their independence. Inspecting singular directions of the scaled Jacobian near the converged state helps distinguish this physical uncertainty from difficulties caused by a poor initial guess.
