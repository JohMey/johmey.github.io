A locally excellent algorithm can cycle

Consider \(F(x)=x^3-2x+2\). Starting from zero gives \(F(0)=2,F'(0)=-2\), so Newton proposes \(x_1=1\). At one, \(F(1)=1,F'(1)=1\), and the next iterate returns to zero. The sequence will repeat indefinitely in exact arithmetic. A real root does exist between \(-2\) and \(-1\), where the function changes sign. The trouble lies in the update chosen from this starting point. It does not refute the local convergence result near a simple root.

For coupled equations define the merit function \(\phi(x)=\|F(x)\|_2^2/2\). Its directional derivative along an exact Newton step \(s\) is \(\nabla\phi^Ts=F^TJs=-\|F\|^2\), strictly negative when \(F\ne0\). Thus a sufficiently short step in that direction decreases the squared mismatch. This identity assumes \(Js=-F\) is solved accurately. With a strongly truncated inner iteration, check the actual directional derivative before treating the step as a descent direction.

Compute an accepted step

A backtracking line search starts at \(\alpha=1\) and reduces it until \(\phi(x+\alpha s)\leq\phi(x)+c\alpha\nabla\phi^Ts\) with \(0<c<1\). This sufficient-decrease condition compares actual progress with the local prediction. In the cycling example at \(x=1\) the Newton direction is \(s=-1\), the initial merit is 0.5 and its slope along \(s\) is \(-1\). With \(c=10^{-4}\), the full trial returns to zero and gives \(\phi=2\), which fails. A half-step gives \(F(0.5)=1.125\) and fails too. A quarter-step gives \(F(0.75)=0.921875\), so \(\phi\simeq0.425\), satisfying the condition.

A smaller mismatch at this point does not certify a nearby root. This example has a positive local minimum of \(\phi\) near \(x=\sqrt{2/3}\), where \(F'\) vanishes but \(F\) stays nonzero. From a poor starting point, globalisation may lead near such a stationary point. Check the residual itself, use a bracket when available and try physically motivated alternative starts. A long sequence of tiny accepted steps should prompt inspection of local derivatives or constraints rather than being counted as successful convergence.

Compare a regional model with the real residual

A trust-region method minimises the local model \(m(s)=\|F+Js\|^2/2\) subject to \(\|s\|\leq\Delta\). Its radius states how far the current linearisation is trusted in the chosen variable scales. After a trial, compute the ratio \(\rho=[\phi(x)-\phi(x+s)]/[m(0)-m(s)]\) when predicted reduction is positive. A negative ratio means the true merit increased. A ratio near one indicates accurate local prediction. One can shrink the radius after a poor trial or enlarge it when the model predicts progress well near the boundary.

In the quarter-step above the linear model predicts residual \(1-0.25=0.75\) and merit \(0.28125\), a reduction of 0.21875. The actual reduction is about 0.075, giving \(\rho\approx0.34\). The trial moved in the intended direction but the predicted gain was optimistic. Using such comparisons is especially valuable when model coefficients change sharply with state. On a simple monotone thermal stage, damped and full Newton can converge to the same root. The resulting residual and a comparison at identical time step test this expectation. Compare two nonlinear cooling solves.

Include physical bounds in a step decision

A variable representing a concentration cannot be negative. If a positive current value \(c\) receives a negative proposed correction \(s\), then \(c+\alpha s\geq0\) imposes \(\alpha\leq c/(-s)\). For a concentration of 0.04 and a direction of −0.10 in matching units, only factors up to 0.4 keep this component admissible. Starting a merit-function search at 0.36 leaves a small distance from the boundary. Across many components use the most restrictive bound before evaluating an expensive residual.

Admissibility alone cannot guarantee progress: a physically acceptable state may have a larger balance mismatch than the old one. Conversely, clipping a negative candidate to zero changes the search direction and can alter conservation. A genuinely constrained equilibrium may require a complementarity or inequality formulation rather than repeated clipping. Record which condition limited each step when diagnosing persistent stagnation near a boundary.
