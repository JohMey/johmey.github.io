Recover the invisible frequency

A recorder samples \(\sin(7x)\) at \(x_j=2\pi j/10\). Identify its low-frequency alias. Give a point where the two functions disagree, and find the mean-square energy of their difference over a period.

Worked solution

Since 7 is congruent to −3 modulo 10, the sampled signal agrees with \(-\sin(3x)\). At \(x=\pi/10\) their difference is \(2\sin(3\pi/10)\approx1.618\).

On the continuous period the two sine modes are orthogonal. Each has mean square \(1/2\), so their difference has mean square 1. A vanishing discrepancy at every sample therefore coexists with an order-one continuous error.

Which modes deserve space?

Let \(f(x)=1+0.6\sin x+0.8\cos(4x)\). Compute its mean square, the root-mean-square error after retaining frequencies at most one, and the best additional single real basis function among all higher-frequency sine and cosine modes.

Worked solution

Pairwise orthogonality gives \(1+0.36/2+0.64/2=1.5\). Omitting the fourth cosine leaves root-mean-square error \(\sqrt{0.32}\approx0.566\).

The only omitted nonzero coefficient multiplies \(\cos(4x)\). Adding this mode removes the entire remaining mean-square error. Choosing an intermediate frequency for its lower index would make no change for this specific signal.

Project an odd function

Find the best linear mean-square approximation to \(x^3\) on \([-1,1]\) with unit weight. Compute its squared integrated error and compare the optimum slope with endpoint interpolation.

Worked solution

Symmetry forces zero constant. Orthogonality to \(x\) requires \(a=\int x^4/\int x^2=(2/5)/(2/3)=3/5\).

Expanding the square gives \(2/7-2(3/5)(2/5)+(9/25)(2/3)=8/175\). Endpoint interpolation gives slope 1. The least-squares line trades endpoint accuracy for lower integrated loss.
