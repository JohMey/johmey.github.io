Choosing where to sample

Exploit endpoint structure, test aliasing and design a stratified random estimate with a known variance.

Remove a singular factor

Evaluate \(\int_0^1 x^{-2/3}(1+x)\,dx\) using \(x=t^q\). Select \(q\), derive the transformed integrand, and decide whether one Simpson panel pair gives the exact integral.

Worked solution

To neutralise the power require \(q(1-2/3)-1=0\), giving \(q=3\). Then \(x^{-2/3}dx=t^{-2}3t^2dt=3dt\) and the finite endpoint value is 3.

Integrating \(3(1+t^3)\) gives \(3+3/4=3.75\). Simpson is exact on cubics; its three samples are \(3,3.375,6\), and their weighted sum divided by six is 3.75.

Expose a false agreement

For \(f(x)=\cos(12\pi x)\) on \([0,1]\), find the trapezoid results with 3 and 6 panels. Test 12 panels and explain why the initial equal answers cannot justify a zero error estimate.

Worked solution

With three panels the phase advances \(4\pi\) at each sample; with six it advances \(2\pi\). Both return the constant sample average 1.

With twelve panels samples alternate between 1 and −1. The half-weight endpoints together count as one positive value, so all contributions cancel. The exact integral is zero. Correlated aliasing on the first two grids invalidates a smooth-error extrapolation.

Two strata with different variability

A response has variance 1 on the first half of \([0,1]\) and 4 on the other half. Estimate the integral from twelve independent evaluations. Compare the variances for allocations (6,6) and (4,8). Derive the continuous optimum under the same fixed budget.

Worked solution

Each stratum mean has variance \(\sigma_i^2/n_i\) and weight \(1/2\), so total variance is \((1/n_1+4/n_2)/4\). Equal allocation gives \(5/24\); (4,8) gives \(3/16\), slightly smaller.

Differentiating \(1/n_1+4/(12-n_1)\) gives \(-1/n_1^2+4/(12-n_1)^2\). Its zero requires \(12-n_1=2n_1\). Positive second derivative yields the optimum (4,8), allocating in proportion to each stratum's standard deviation.
