An error budget that applies everywhere

A calibration curve may have a specification requiring its largest absolute deviation anywhere in an interval to stay below a threshold. Assign a polynomial \(p\) of degree at most \(m\) and consider \(E(p)=\max_{a\leq x\leq b}|f(x)-p(x)|\) for a continuous target. Interpolating selected measurement locations fixes the polynomial there. It need not allocate the remaining error efficiently. Shifting an interpolant by a constant sometimes halves its largest deviation at once.

Suppose \(m+2\) ordered locations attain the same error magnitude with alternating signs. Any polynomial with strictly smaller maximum error must move up at each positive extreme and down at each negative extreme. Its difference from the candidate then has at least one zero between each consecutive pair. This requires \(m+1\) zeros in a polynomial of degree at most \(m\), a contradiction. Those extreme points thus certify optimality globally once the candidate's error is bounded over the entire interval.

Find the best line by hand

Approximate \(x^2\) on \([-1,1]\) by \(a+bx\). Averaging that line with its reflection gives the constant \(a\). For each \(x\), the averaged error is the mean of the errors at \(x\) and \(-x\), so the uniform norm cannot increase. For constants the target covers the whole range from zero to one. The midpoint \(a=1/2\) balances its two extreme distances. At \(-1,0,1\), the errors are \(+1/2,-1/2,+1/2\). These three points certify the optimum among all lines, including nonsymmetric ones.

The endpoint interpolant is the constant 1. Its error is zero at both ends and reaches magnitude 1 at zero. Lowering the line sacrifices exact agreement with the endpoints in exchange for a factor of two in the worst case. If the endpoint values encode compulsory boundary conditions, such a shift is inadmissible and the optimisation problem must include those constraints.

Exchange trial extrema

For an unknown target, guess \(m+2\) extreme locations and solve the linear system \(f(x_i)-p(x_i)=(-1)^iE\) for the coefficients and \(E\). Search the full interval, including its boundaries, for the largest deviation. When it exceeds the trial level, exchange a support point while retaining sign alternation and repeat. For smooth targets, the zeros of \(f'-p'\) supply candidate interior extrema. A coarse plotting grid alone cannot certify the resulting maximum.

For a constant approximation to \(e^x\) on \([0,1]\), monotonicity identifies the endpoints immediately. Equalising \(a-1=e-a\) gives \(a=(1+e)/2\) and error \((e-1)/2\), approximately 0.85914. Replacing the upper endpoint by a larger value raises both quantities. The constant optimum depends on the range, providing a simple independent check on an implementation of the exchange procedure.

A lower bound from three measurements

Suppose measurements at \(-1,0,1\) give the exact values \(1,0,1\) but the interior shape is otherwise unknown. Any line still has maximum error at least one half on those three measurements: averaging its two endpoint predictions recovers its value at zero, so it cannot be within less than one half of both endpoint targets and the central target. The lower bound survives with sparse data. The matching upper bound requires information between the measurements, supplied in the earlier example by the analytic formula \(x^2\).
