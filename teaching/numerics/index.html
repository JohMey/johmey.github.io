Numerical mathematics

When does a computed answer deserve another digit? Study the approximation, the algorithm and the error separately.

Lecture notesCurriculum references

Lecture notes

  1. Numerical linear algebra

    Separate conditioning from algorithmic error, solve dense and sparse systems, and use residuals and spectral information to assess the computed result.

    Problem sheetsNumerical experiment

  2. Approximation and quadrature

    Reconstruct functions from samples or projections, derive deterministic and Monte Carlo quadrature rules, and test whether their error estimates are credible.

    Problem sheetsNumerical experiment

  3. Nonlinear equations

    Bracket and certify roots, derive Newton and secant updates for coupled equations, and follow branches and active constraints as parameters change.

    Problem sheetsNumerical experiment

  4. Numerical ordinary differential equations

    Derive and compare time integrators, preserve physical invariants, and use sensitivities and shooting to solve for trajectories and boundary data.

    Problem sheetsNumerical experiment

  5. Numerical partial differential equations

    Discretise field equations with conservation and boundary data, resolve interfaces and pressure constraints, and estimate error in a chosen output.

    Problem sheetsNumerical experiment

  6. Inverse problems and least squares

    Identify observable parameter combinations, quantify noise amplification, regularise unstable fits, quantify uncertainty and design measurements that test a reconstruction.

    Problem sheetsNumerical experiment

Curriculum references

The selection draws on KIT’s numerical mathematics lectures and Heidelberg’s introduction to numerical analysis. It follows root finding, approximation, quadrature and linear systems through to time integration and spatial discretisation, with least squares and further course material from TUM, ETH Zürich and MIT.
