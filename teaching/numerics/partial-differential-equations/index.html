Chapters

  1. Flux differences for an elliptic balance

    Assemble a conservative boundary-value problem from face fluxes, trace the effect of boundary conditions and establish a discrete energy identity.

  2. Weak forms and finite elements

    Derive a variational statement by integration by parts, assemble element matrices for a one-dimensional rod and explain what the Galerkin solution minimises.

  3. Finite volumes and moving fronts

    Derive an upwind transport update by conservation, interpret the Courant number as a fraction of cell contents and determine what conservation says about front speed.

  4. Time-dependent diffusion

    Derive the diffusion time-step restriction from two independent arguments and compare explicit and implicit decay of spatial modes.

  5. Wave speeds and numerical dispersion

    Derive the leapfrog dispersion relation, use the Courant condition to distinguish boundedness from accurate travel time and initialise a standing-wave calculation consistently.

  6. Verification, refinement and local error

    Use a manufactured solution to verify an implementation, estimate observed convergence and identify when adaptivity is needed for the physical quantity of interest.

  7. Interfaces, contact resistance and Robin data

    Derive a face flux that transports the same heat through two different materials and through a convective boundary.

  8. Anisotropic diffusion and mesh alignment

    Relate a diffusion tensor to principal directions and test whether a face-flux approximation can represent a rotated material.

  9. Cell Péclet number and monotonicity

    Derive the sign restriction for centred transport, identify a numerical boundary layer and quantify the diffusion introduced by upwinding.

  10. Incompressibility and discrete pressure modes

    Derive the velocity–pressure block system and use a periodic mesh to detect a pressure pattern that exerts no discrete force.

  11. Multigrid and coarse-grid correction

    Combine smoothing with a coarse residual equation and compute which components a two-grid correction can remove exactly.

  12. Adjoints and errors in a chosen output

    Weight equation residuals by the response of a chosen observation and distinguish algebraic error from mesh error.

Applied study

Discretise a boundary-value problem and separate mesh error from solver and sampling errors.

Replace a continuum balance by nodal equations

Suppose a scalar diffuses along a unit interval, with a fixed value at each end. The steady balance is \(-u''=f\) for constant unit diffusivity. At equally spaced interior nodes \(x_i=ih\), approximate the derivative at each face by the difference between its neighbouring values divided by \(h\). Subtracting the outgoing and incoming diffusive fluxes and dividing by cell width gives

\[\frac{2u_i-u_{i-1}-u_{i+1}}{h^2}=f(x_i).\]

The endpoints are known, leaving \(N-1\) interior unknowns for \(N\) intervals. Each interior flux appears once with each sign in neighbouring balances. Summing balances therefore leaves the net boundary flux and the total source. With variable diffusivity \(D\), multiply each face gradient by its face value of \(D\) before taking the difference.

Find the error made by a single stencil

Insert the exact values \(u(x_i-h)\) and \(u(x_i+h)\), expand both in Taylor series and add them. Odd terms cancel. The central second difference satisfies \(\delta_{xx}u=u''+h^2u^{(4)}/12+O(h^4)\) for sufficiently smooth \(u\). This local consistency calculation predicts the scale of the defect when the exact solution is inserted in the discrete equations.

Write the discrete problem \(A_hu_h=f_h\) and define \(\tau_h=A_hu_\star-f_h\) using exact nodal values \(u_\star\). Then \(A_h(u_\star-u_h)=\tau_h\). A stability bound on \(A_h^{-1}\), uniform in mesh size in the chosen norm, turns second-order consistency into second-order solution error. A tight iterative solve reduces the residual of \(A_hu_h=f_h\), but leaves \(\tau_h\) in place.

Use an exact sine mode to predict the discrete amplitude

Prescribe \(u(0)=u(1)=0\) and \(f(x)=\pi^2\sin(\pi x)\). The exact solution is \(u_\star=\sin(\pi x)\). For its grid samples \(s_i=\sin(\pi ih)\), the sine addition formula yields \(A_hs=\lambda_hs\), with \(\lambda_h=4\sin^2(\pi h/2)/h^2\). The right-hand side is \(\pi^2s\), so the discrete solution has the same nodal shape and amplitude \(\pi^2/\lambda_h\).

With four intervals, \(\lambda_h=64\sin^2(\pi/8)=9.3726\) and the numerical midspan value is 1.0530. The exact value is 1. The sign makes sense: the discrete stiffness of this mode is below \(\pi^2\), so the same load produces a larger amplitude. Expansion gives \(\pi^2/\lambda_h=1+\pi^2h^2/12+O(h^4)\), predicting a fourfold error reduction on refinement.

Problem class

Test a polynomial before trusting the mesh

Keep the homogeneous end values and choose \(u_\star=x(1-x)\). Derive \(f\), then predict the nodal error for any uniform mesh in exact arithmetic. Would this make a good test for measuring convergence order?

Show the calculation

Since \(u_\star''=-2\), use \(f=2\). The centred second difference of any quadratic equals its exact second derivative. The nodal vector therefore solves the discrete equations exactly. It is a useful stencil consistency check, but its zero leading error prevents a meaningful order measurement from a ratio of errors.

Manufacture a source for variable diffusion

On the unit square let \(D(x)=1+x\), \(u_\star=\sin(ax)\sin(ay)\), and \(a=m\pi\) for positive integer \(m\), using metre coordinates. What forcing makes \(-\nabla\cdot(D\nabla u)+2u=f\) hold with zero boundary values?

Show the calculation

Apply the product rule, \(-\nabla\cdot(D\nabla u)=-D\Delta u-D'(x)u_x\). Here \(\Delta u_\star=-2a^2u_\star\) and \(D'=1\). Thus \(f=[2(1+x)a^2+2]u_\star-a\cos(ax)\sin(ay)\). Each boundary has a zero sine factor. Omitting \(D'u_x\) would produce a continuous modelling error that persists even when the mesh is refined.
