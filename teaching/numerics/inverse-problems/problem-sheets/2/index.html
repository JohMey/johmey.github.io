Spectral filtering and recoverable detail

Calculate what a reconstruction can borrow from the data and what it assumes when it suppresses a noisy direction.

Problem sheet 2 · Chapters 3 and 4

1. A small residual after a large error

In singular coordinates let \(B=\operatorname{diag}(1,0.02)\), true \(x=(1,1)\), and data perturbation \((0,0.01)\). (a) Solve the perturbed inverse exactly. (b) Reconstruct with the second mode truncated and compare residuals and errors. (c) Give an example of a stable target functional.

Compare in parameter and measurement space

The observed data are \((1,0.03)\). Exact inversion gives \((1,1.5)\) with zero residual and parameter error 0.5. Truncation gives \((1,0)\) with residual norm 0.03 and parameter error 1. In this particular realisation truncation loses a real feature and has greater error. The target \(x_1\) is stable under either estimator; its gain from data noise in this example is one.

2. Choose a discrepancy level

A scalar system has \(B=0.20,\ b=0.30\). (a) Express the magnitude of the residual under zero-order Tikhonov as a function of \(\lambda\). (b) Choose \(\lambda\) to give residual 0.06 and compute the estimate. (c) Quantify the resulting noiseless resolution factor.

Solve for the strength in measurement units

The fitted response is \(b\,0.04/(0.04+\lambda^2)\), leaving magnitude \(0.30\lambda^2/(0.04+\lambda^2)\). For residual 0.06 solve \(\lambda^2=0.01\), so \(\lambda=0.10\) and \(x_\lambda=0.20(0.30)/0.05=1.20\). The retained noiseless amplitude is \(0.04/0.05=0.8\). Interpreting 0.06 as a sensible discrepancy requires an external noise or model-error estimate.

3. Penalising jumps without penalising the mean

For three cell values use \(D x=(x_2-x_1,x_3-x_2)\). (a) Find its null space. (b) Show whether measuring \(x_3-x_1\) alone yields a unique regularised solution. (c) Repeat with the mean as the sole measurement, and state what the resulting solution assumes.

Intersect the null spaces

The penalty vanishes on all constant vectors. A measurement of \(x_3-x_1\) also vanishes on constants, so adding a constant to every candidate preserves both terms in the objective. A mean measurement sees constants, and the common null space becomes trivial. With observed mean \(b\) the unique solution for positive \(\lambda\) is \((b,b,b)\) since both residual and differences then vanish. The flat profile reflects the smoothness assumption; the measurement established only its mean.
