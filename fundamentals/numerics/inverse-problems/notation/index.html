Inverse problems and least squares · Notation and assumptions

Read each defining equation with its assumptions and units, then follow the link to the argument that gives the equation its meaning.

Adjoint sensitivity

\[J^\mathsf T r\]

This operation maps a data-space vector back to parameter space. For a discretised physical model the chosen inner products determine the correct adjoint.

Derivation · Adjoint gradients through a forward solver

Forward operator

\[y=G(m)+\eta\]

The operator maps parameters to predicted observations. The noise and discrepancy model must use the same physical units as the readout.

Derivation · Forward maps, information and identifiability

Posterior predictive

\[p(y_*|y)=\int p(y_*|m)p(m|y)\,dm\]

A distribution over parameters induces uncertainty in a new observation. Measurement noise and uncertainty of the prediction mean are distinct contributions.

Derivation · Predicting a new measurement when the fitted parameters are correlated

Resolution matrix

\[R=G^\dagger_{\rm reg}G\]

For a linear regularised inverse, a row describes how the estimated parameter mixes the actual components. A sharp-looking result may still have broad resolution kernels.

Derivation · A reconstruction audit from observations to a defensible claim

Tikhonov estimate

\[\arg\min_m\{\|Gm-y\|^2+\alpha\|Lm\|^2\}\]

The regularisation operator selects which departures are costly. Compare values of alpha only after fixing how both terms are scaled.

Derivation · Regularisation, resolution and the price of stability

Whitened residual

\[r=\Sigma^{-1/2}(y-G(m))\]

For a known positive covariance this expresses mismatch in noise units and removes the modelled correlations between measurements.
