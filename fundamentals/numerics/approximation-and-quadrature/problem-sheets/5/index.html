Recover the invisible frequency

A recorder samples \(\sin(7x)\) at \(x_j=2\pi j/10\). Identify its low-frequency alias. Give a point where the two functions disagree, and find the mean-square energy of their difference over a period.

Worked solution

Since 7 is congruent to −3 modulo 10, the sampled signal agrees with \(-\sin(3x)\). At \(x=\pi/10\) their difference is \(2\sin(3\pi/10)\approx1.618\).

On the continuous period the two sine modes are orthogonal. Each has mean square \(1/2\), so their difference has mean square 1. A vanishing discrepancy at every sample therefore coexists with an order-one continuous error.

Which modes deserve space?

Let \(f(x)=1+0.6\sin x+0.8\cos(4x)\). Compute its mean square, the root-mean-square error after retaining frequencies at most one, and the best additional single real basis function among all higher-frequency sine and cosine modes.

Worked solution

Pairwise orthogonality gives

\[1+0.36/2+0.64/2=1.5.\]

Omitting the fourth cosine leaves root-mean-square error \(\sqrt{0.32}\approx0.566\).

The only omitted nonzero coefficient multiplies \(\cos(4x)\). Adding this mode removes the entire remaining mean-square error. Choosing an intermediate frequency for its lower index would make no change for this specific signal.

Project an odd function

Find the best linear mean-square approximation to \(x^3\) on \([-1,1]\) with unit weight. Compute its squared integrated error and compare the optimum slope with endpoint interpolation.

Worked solution

Symmetry forces zero constant. Orthogonality to \(x\) requires

\[a=\int x^4/\int x^2=(2/5)/(2/3)=3/5.\]

Expanding the square gives

\[2/7-2(3/5)(2/5)+(9/25)(2/3)=8/175.\]

Endpoint interpolation gives slope 1. The least-squares line trades endpoint accuracy for lower integrated loss.

Find the zeros in an eight-sample spectrum without eight long sums

A sensor returns \((1,2,0,0,1,2,0,0)\) at eight equally spaced times. Before computing any transforms, predict which four Fourier bins vanish. Use a four-point transform to find the other bins and verify the discrete energy identity for the unnormalised DFT.

Worked solution

Pair sample \(n\) with \(n+4\). The second weight differs by \(e^{-i\pi k}=(-1)^k\), so all odd bins cancel. For even \(k=2j\) the two halves reinforce and give twice the four-point DFT of \((1,2,0,0)\). Its entries are \(3,1-2i,-1,1+2i\). Thus

\[X_0=6,\ X_2=2-4i,\ X_4=-2,\ X_6=2+4i.\]

The sample energy is

\[2(1^2+2^2)=10.\]

The frequency-domain squared magnitudes sum to \(36+20+4+20=80\). Dividing by eight gives 10. Both the zeros and the energy check depend on the adopted transform convention. Reversing the exponent sign conjugates these coefficients and leaves their squared magnitudes unchanged.
