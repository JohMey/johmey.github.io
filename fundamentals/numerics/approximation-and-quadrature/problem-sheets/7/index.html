Project · How many sensors measure the mean around a ring?

Design, challenge and repair a quadrature for periodic temperature measurements. Distinguish a sampled mean, reconstruction of a continuous profile and error in a reference calculation.

Problem sheet 7 · Integrative project

Predict three instrument readings

A manufactured test field is \(T(\theta)=20+2\cos(5\theta)+0.8\cos(12\theta)\) °C. Determine its exact spatial mean. Predict measurements from rings with 5, 6 and 7 equally spaced sensors at zero offset. Calculate their mean using a finite geometric sum.

Work through the solution

The continuous mean is 20 °C because both cosines integrate to zero over a full turn. For mode \(k\) set \(z=e^{2\pi i k/N}\). When \(z\ne1\), the discrete average is

\[(1-z^N)/[N(1-z)]=0.\]

When \(z=1\), every term is one. Taking real parts leaves only harmonics divisible by the sensor count. Five sensors see a mean of 22, six give 20.8 and seven give 20. These results describe three different instruments observing exactly the same field. A 12-sensor instrument also measures 20.8, so agreement with a coarser grid can be misleading.

Build a diagnostic from a second run

Turn the six-sensor ring by 15°. Determine which part of the signal changes in its mean. Can the average of the two runs recover the continuous mean? Test the 12th harmonic with the rotatable sensor ring. Its test harmonic has amplitude 3 °C. Compare the sign reversal and rescale its mean offset by 0.8/3 to match this instrument.

Work through the solution

The twelfth harmonic changes sign since \(12(15^\circ)=180^\circ\). The fifth harmonic still averages to zero. The second mean is 19.2 °C; the two-run average is 20. The offset is a quarter of the old sample interval. A half-interval rotation of 30° would bring the twelfth harmonic through a full cycle and keep the erroneous mean. Choose offsets using the alias you intend to diagnose.

Use an interval integral for an independent calibration

A second sensor samples a reference profile \(u(x)=x^4\) along a straight rail with scaled coordinate \(0\le x\le1\). On one, two and four equal panels its trapezoidal values are 0.5, 0.28125 and 0.220703125. Build the first two extrapolation columns. Then change the exponent to one half in the extrapolation experiment. Explain where using the wrong error power would distort the calibration.

Work through the solution

Two first extrapolates are

\[(4\times0.28125-0.5)/3=0.2083333,\]
\[(4\times0.220703125-0.28125)/3=0.20052083.\]

Combining them with denominator 15 gives 0.2. The square-root profile lacks a bounded derivative at the endpoint, so a full even-power error expansion cannot be assumed. The observed convergence ratio is the diagnostic in this run.

State what the specification guarantees

The design must integrate any trigonometric polynomial with modes through 12 and also reconstruct it uniquely if asked. Find sufficient uniform sensor counts for each task and explain their difference.

Work through the solution

A 13-point ring integrates all these modes exactly because none of \(1,\ldots,12\) is divisible by 13. To recover all sine and cosine coefficients through mode 12 one needs \(2(12)+1=25\) real degrees of freedom. A 25-point uniform ring suffices. The mean is a single linear functional and can be identifiable when the full field is not.
