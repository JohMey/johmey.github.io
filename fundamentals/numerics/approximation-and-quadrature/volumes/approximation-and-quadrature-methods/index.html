Function approximation and numerical integration

Construct approximations from the information actually available. Derive their error and observe what changes when the function, data or sampling rule violates an assumption.

    Contents

    1. Approximating a resonance with a rational function

      A smooth response on a measured interval can be governed by a nearby pole. Derive a low-order Padé fit, construct a barycentric rational approximant from samples and check whether a predicted pole survives independent data.

    2. Integrating cosine interpolants on nested grids

      Chebyshev points turn polynomial interpolation into a cosine series. Derive the exact moments of each basis polynomial, explain aliasing and find a case where two consecutive quadrature grids confidently agree on the wrong answer.

    3. Integrate the oscillations analytically and sample the envelope

      A sensor model asks for many Fourier integrals of a slowly changing envelope. Direct trapezoids can return a full-scale signal where the true integral nearly cancels. Derive a Filon rule from exact oscillatory moments and find where it needs a different local model.

    4. Spend random samples where they reduce uncertainty

      A Monte Carlo estimate can be unbiased and still too noisy to be useful. Derive control variates, allocate a fixed budget across strata, and distinguish variance reduction from an underestimated standard error.

    5. How many neighbouring samples belong in a slope estimate?

      A wider local fit suppresses measurement noise, yet it can smooth away real changes in slope. Derive the competing errors, then use noisy synthetic data to find which differentiator wins at a chosen spacing.

    6. What can smoothness buy at a fixed polynomial degree?

      Prove density for every continuous signal, derive a faster rate for smooth signals and identify the persistent overshoot when a jump violates the assumptions.

    7. When should the denominator be fitted too?

      Develop a minimax certificate for rational approximants, find the pole of a measured response and identify the obstruction that appears when a trial denominator vanishes inside the interval.

    8. Measuring an area integral with finitely many samples

      Build deterministic multidimensional rules from moments and geometry. Compare tensor products, symmetric triangle rules and sparse combinations at a fixed number of evaluations.
