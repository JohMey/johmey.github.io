    • Function approximation and numerical integration

    How the course fits together

    1. Chapters 1–4

      Connect samples locally, choose polynomial nodes, then impose smooth joins to obtain splines. Integrating a reconstructed curve produces a quadrature rule with an error inherited from the approximation.

    2. Chapters 5–8

      Choose nodes to integrate moment conditions exactly or adapt intervals where estimates disagree. Divided differences make incremental interpolation possible, while minimax approximation controls the worst pointwise error.

    3. Chapters 9–12

      Represent functions by global frequencies or orthogonal projections. Endpoint regularity can limit convergence. Random quadrature replaces geometrical spacing with sampling and a statistical error estimate.

    Applied study

    Reconstruct a field from samples and estimate how interpolation changes its integral.

    What lies between two measurements?

    A probe records temperatures at equally spaced positions along a rod. To estimate the mean temperature, first decide what profile those samples represent. For neighbouring points \(x_j,x_{j+1}\) at distance \(h\), write \(x=x_j+sh\) with \(0\leq s\leq1\). The straight line matching both readings is

    \[(I_h f)(x)=(1-s)f(x_j)+sf(x_{j+1}).\]

    The weights are nonnegative and sum to one. This reproduces every affine function, keeps interpolated values between their neighbours, and amplifies bounded sample errors by at most a factor of one. A finer display grid can draw this line more densely. Recovering new curvature requires new source information.

    Use curvature to predict the sign and size of error

    For twice continuously differentiable \(f\), the interpolation remainder on one panel is \(f(x)-I_h f(x)=\tfrac12 f''(\xi)(x-x_j)(x-x_{j+1})\) for some \(\xi\) on that panel. The product is nonpositive and reaches magnitude \(h^2/4\) at the midpoint. Thus the maximum error is bounded by \(h^2\max|f''|/8\). For positive curvature the chord lies above the curve.

    Integrating the chord gives \(h[f(x_j)+f(x_{j+1})]/2\), the trapezoidal rule. Integrating the bound with the quadratic factor retained gives a panel integral error at most \(h^3\max|f''|/12\). On a fixed interval of length \(L\) there are \(L/h\) panels, so the composite error is bounded by \(Lh^2\max|f''|/12\). The pointwise and total error both decrease quadratically, for different reasons.

    Integrate a curved temperature profile

    Use the exact profile \(T(x)=300+20(x/L)^2\) K with \(L=1\) m. Four equal panels have \(h=0.25\) m. On the first panel the endpoints are 300 and 301.25 K. Their interpolated midpoint is 300.625 K, while the exact midpoint is 300.3125 K. The overestimate of 0.3125 K is the same on every panel because the curvature is constant.

    The exact integral is \(300+20/3=306.6667\) K·m on this unit-length rod. Here \(T''=40\) K/m² and the trapezoidal overestimate equals \(Lh^2 T''/12=0.20833\) K·m. Dividing the integral by length gives mean temperature. To obtain thermal energy above a reference temperature, additional material properties and cross-sectional area are needed.

    Problem class

    Choose the sensor spacing

    For the quadratic rod, select the minimum number of equal intervals so the midpoint error never exceeds 0.05 K. How many measurements are needed including both endpoints?

    Show the calculation

    With \(N\) intervals on a metre, the bound is \(40/(8N^2)=5/N^2\) K. Requiring \(5/N^2\leq0.05\) gives \(N\geq10\), hence 11 measurements. At constant curvature this bound is attained at the midpoints.

    Infer an integral from two estimates

    Four and eight panels give 306.875 and 306.71875 K·m. Assume \(Q_h=Q+c h^2\). Eliminate \(c\) and estimate \(Q\). Would adding sensors only to the display grid make this correction possible?

    Show the calculation

    Multiply \(Q_{h/2}=Q+c h^2/4\) by four and subtract \(Q_h\). This gives \(Q=(4Q_{h/2}-Q_h)/3=306.66667\) K·m. For the quadratic profile the error law is exact. Subdividing the same source chords on screen preserves their total area and supplies no second source approximation.
