Summing one Fourier mode

A smooth closed curve parameterised by angle provides a natural periodic integral. On \(N\) equally spaced points \(\theta_j=2\pi j/N\), write the trapezoidal estimate of a mean as \(T_N=N^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}f(\theta_j)\). The endpoint \(2\pi\) equals zero angle and is counted only once. For \(f=e^{ik\theta}\), put \(q=e^{2\pi i k/N}\). If \(q\ne1\) then \((1-q)(1+q+\cdots+q^{N-1})=1-q^N=0\). If \(q=1\) all \(N\) samples equal one.

For a Fourier series whose absolute convergence permits termwise summation, this gives

\[T_N=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}\widehat f_{\ell N},\]
\[T_N-\widehat f_0=\sum_{\ell\ne0}\widehat f_{\ell N}.\]

The continuous mean sees only the constant mode. The grid silently adds every integer multiple of its sample count to that mode. Fast decay of smooth Fourier coefficients usually makes this error small. A single deliberately aligned harmonic can defeat the rule.

Two rings of sensors

A ring carries the temperature \(20+3\cos(8\theta)\) °C. Eight sensors at zero angular offset all read 23 °C. The true mean is 20 °C. Nine equally spaced sensors give the exact mean for this harmonic. Moving the eight sensors through an offset \(\phi\) changes their mean to \(20+3\cos(8\phi)\) while the ring is unchanged.

A different signal \(2+\cos(3\theta)+0.2\cos(10\theta)\) on five sensors gives 2.2 because the tenth harmonic aliases to zero frequency. Increasing the number to ten gives the same wrong answer. Independent node offsets or a noncommensurate sample count can challenge an apparently converged result.

Tutorial on resolution

A discrete test gives the exact mean of \(\cos(7\theta)\) on eight equally spaced nodes. Can eight samples reconstruct that frequency uniquely?

Worked solution

Its mean vanishes, but samples of \(\cos(7\theta)\) coincide with those of \(\cos(\theta)\) on this grid. Accurate integration of the mean does not imply unique reconstruction.

Average two \(N\)-point rules offset by \(\pi/N\). Which aliases survive?

Worked solution

Together the rings form a \(2N\)-point uniform grid. Terms at odd multiples of \(N\) change sign between grids and cancel. Terms at even multiples survive.
