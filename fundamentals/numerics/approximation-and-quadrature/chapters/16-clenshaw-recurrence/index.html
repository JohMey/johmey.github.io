From cosines to a polynomial recurrence

Set \(x=\cos\theta\) and \(T_k(x)=\cos(k\theta)\). The angle-addition formula gives

\[T_{k+1}=2xT_k-T_{k-1},\]

with \(T_0=1,T_1=x\). Consider \(p=\sum_{k=0}^n c_k T_k\) with the constant coefficient included in full. We could generate all the polynomials forward. Clenshaw evaluation instead gathers their weighted coefficients from the high end, without converting to monomials.

Set \(b_{n+1}=b_{n+2}=0\) and \(b_k=c_k+2xb_{k+1}-b_{k+2}\) for \(k=n,\ldots,1\). Substitute \(c_k=b_k-2xb_{k+1}+b_{k+2}\) into the original sum. Every interior \(b_j\) multiplies \(T_j-2xT_{j-1}+T_{j-2}=0\). Only two boundary terms survive:

\[p(x)=c_0+xb_1-b_2.\]

The derivation also identifies a common convention error. If the source defines its zeroth coefficient with a half-weight then it must be halved once before using this formula.

Two useful arithmetic checks

For coefficients \((1,2,3)\) at \(x=0.5\), \(b_2=3\), \(b_1=2+2(0.5)3=5\). The answer is

\[1+0.5(5)-3=0.5.\]

Independently \(T_2(0.5)=-0.5\), giving \(1+1-1.5=0.5\).

On the calibration interval each \(|T_k|\le1\), so an isolated coefficient error of 0.001 changes the value by at most 0.001. At \(x=1.2\), \(T_{10}(1.2)=\cosh(10\operatorname{arcosh}(1.2))\) is around 252. The same tiny coefficient error now changes a prediction by roughly a quarter of a unit. Accurate summation does not make extrapolation reliable.

Tutorial on mapping a laboratory interval

A calibration covers \(10\le T\le50\) °C. Which coordinate should enter the recurrence and what does \(T=54\) correspond to?

Worked solution

Centre at 30 and divide by the half-width 20, so \(x=(T-30)/20\). At 54 °C this gives 1.2, already outside the bounded Chebyshev interval.

Give a computable uniform error bound if three stored coefficients have errors no larger than 0.002 each.

Worked solution

For \(|x|\le1\), the triangle inequality and \(|T_k|\le1\) give 0.006. Exact values may cancel at some positions; this bound does not rely on such cancellation.
