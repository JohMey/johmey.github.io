Fit incompatible sensors

Two dimensionless residuals are

\[r_1=x-2,\ r_2=2x-3.\]

Compute the exact least-squares state, both residuals and their dot product with the Jacobian. How many undamped Gauss–Newton steps suffice from zero?

Worked solution

The gradient is \((x-2)+2(2x-3)=5x-8\), giving \(x=1.6\). The residual is \((-0.4,0.2)^T\) and the dot product with \((1,2)^T\) is zero.

The residual is affine, so its linear model is exact everywhere. One step from zero produces 1.6 even though neither individual observation is exactly satisfied.

A stationary point with a poor model

For the scalar residual \(r=x^2-1\), evaluate the residual, gradient and curvature of \(\Phi=r^2/2\) at zero. Analyse the undamped Gauss–Newton linear least-squares subproblem there, and compare it with a trial step to \(x=0.1\).

Worked solution

At zero \(r=-1\), \(J=0\) and \(Jr=0\). Exact curvature \(J^2+rr''=-2\) identifies a strict local maximum.

The model \(r+Js=-1\) has the same size for every step, so cannot select a descent direction. Directly testing \(x=0.1\) gives \(\Phi=0.99^2/2=0.49005\), below 0.5. A small gradient alone cannot certify a minimum.

Locate the switching load

Use \(K=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&-1\\-1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(f=(t,2)^T\), \(u,w\ge0\), \(w=Ku-f\), \(u_iw_i=0\). Assume the first gap is closed. Determine the range of \(t\) for which that assumption holds and solve the case \(t=0\).

Worked solution

With \(u_1=0\), the open second gap has \(2u_2=2\), so \(u_2=1\). The first reaction is \(-1-t\), nonnegative precisely for \(t\le-1\). At the threshold the closed coordinate has zero reaction.

At \(t=0\) release the first gap. Solving \(3u_1-u_2=0\), \(-u_1+2u_2=2\) gives \((0.4,1.2)^T\) with both reactions zero. The positive gaps verify the new active set.

Locate and follow the two equilibria of a fold

Consider equilibria of the dimensionless evolution u′ = λ − u². For λ = 0.04 find both roots, classify their stability, and estimate the movement of the positive root when λ increases by 0.002. Then find out why stepping in λ fails near zero and construct a continuation step through the fold.

Worked solution

The equilibria are u = ±0.2. A small perturbation obeys δu′ ≈ −2u δu, so +0.2 attracts while −0.2 repels. Both are perfectly valid zeros of the steady residual. A solver’s ability to converge to either root has no direct implication for dynamical stability.

Differentiating u²−λ = 0 gives du/dλ = 1/(2u). At the positive root the predicted change is +0.005. The exact change is √0.042 − 0.2 ≈ 0.00494. At the negative root the sign reverses. As the fold approaches, sensitivity diverges even though the equilibrium curve itself is the smooth parabola λ = u².

At (u,λ) = (0,0) choose the tangent along increasing u. An arclength predictor of length 0.05 gives (0.05,0). Keep the correction normal to that tangent by imposing u = 0.05 together with the residual. The corrected point is (0.05,0.0025). The two-equation Jacobian remains invertible there and at the fold. A parameter sweep fixing λ below zero would have no real point to correct to.
