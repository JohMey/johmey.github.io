Tangents and Jacobians

Compute scalar, secant and coupled Newton corrections, and test what an incorrectly assembled derivative changes.

Use the secant on a cubic

For \(F(x)=x^3-3\), use the two starts \(x_0=1\) and \(x_1=2\). Find the next secant iterate, compare it with one Newton update from 2, and evaluate both residuals. Does one comparison prove an asymptotic convergence order?

Worked solution

The secant slope is \((5-(-2))/(2-1)=7\), giving \(x_2=2-5/7=9/7\) and residual \(729/343-3\simeq-0.875\). Newton uses slope 12, giving \(19/12\simeq1.58333\) and residual \(6859/1728-3\simeq0.970\).

The methods take corrections from different local models. One residual comparison on early iterates does not establish their asymptotic rates, which concern successive errors near the root.

Locate two coupled unknowns

Take \(F_1=x^2+y-3,\ F_2=x+y^2-3\). At \(x=y=1\), compute the Jacobian and full Newton update. Compare the residual after that update with the result of dropping both coupling terms from the Jacobian.

Worked solution

The residual is \((-1,-1)\) and \(J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Symmetry gives \(s_x=s_y=1/3\), hence \(x=y=4/3\). Both new residuals equal \(1/9\).

Dropping the off-diagonal entries instead gives \(s_x=s_y=1/2\), landing at 1.5 in both coordinates with residual \(0.75\) in each row. The uncoupled derivative overestimates the correction because each unknown changes both balances.

Roundoff in a derivative test

Estimate the derivative of \(F(x)=x^2-2\) at 1.5 using a forward quotient with positive step \(s\). Derive the exact truncation error and suppose arithmetic introduces absolute numerator error at most \(10^{-14}\). Choose \(s\) to minimise the sum of the two error bounds.

Worked solution

Subtracting the function values and dividing by \(s\) gives \(3+s\), so the exact truncation error is \(s\). Numerator noise contributes at most \(10^{-14}/s\). The sum \(s+10^{-14}/s\) is minimised by \(s^2=10^{-14}\), giving \(s=10^{-7}\) and a bound \(2\times10^{-7}\).

A perturbation smaller than this model’s optimum amplifies cancellation. The numerical optimum depends on evaluation scale and arithmetic, so use several step sizes when testing an unfamiliar Jacobian.

Decide when a reused slope is still informative

Solve \(x^2=9\) from \(x_0=4\) using the initial slope for the first two steps. Compare the second result with refreshing the slope at the first iterate. For an arbitrary positive frozen slope \(B\), find the range giving a strict local contraction at the positive root.

Worked solution

The initial slope is 8. The first iterate is \(4-7/8=25/8\). Its residual is \(49/64\). Reuse gives \(25/8-49/512=1551/512\approx3.02930\). Refreshing the slope to \(25/4\) gives \(25/8-(49/64)(4/25)=3.0025\).

At the root the error multiplier is \(1-6/B\). The strict inequality \(|1-6/B|<1\) requires \(B>3\). In particular \(B=8\) gives multiplier \(1/4\): factor reuse converges but does not retain Newton's local quadratic rate.
