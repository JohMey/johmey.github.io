A multiple root splits

Take \(p=(x-3)^2\). Add a constant perturbation \(\delta\) and determine all real roots for \(\delta=-0.0004,0,+0.0004\). Explain why the usual derivative-based sensitivity formula fails.

Worked solution

Solving \((x-3)^2=-\delta\) gives \(2.98\) and \(3.02\) for the negative perturbation, a double root 3 for zero, and no real roots for positive perturbation.

The derivative \(2(x-3)\) vanishes at 3, so dividing by this derivative is undefined. The displacement 0.02 is proportional to the square root of the coefficient error. It need not be small relative to the coefficient error itself.

Evaluate before deflating

For \(p=x^3-6x^2+11x-6\), use Horner evaluation at 2.1 to obtain \(p\), \(p'\), and the synthetic quotient upon division by \(x-2.1\). Check that the quotient and remainder reconstruct the original value at zero.

Worked solution

The coefficient sweep gives \(1,-3.9,2.81,-0.099\). The derivative sweep uses the old coefficients and gives \(0,1,-1.8,-0.97\), so \(p'(2.1)=-0.97\).

The quotient is \(x^2-3.9x+2.81\), with remainder \(-0.099\). At zero this gives \((-2.1)(2.81)-0.099=-6\). Treating the remainder as zero would change the constant term of the reconstructed polynomial.

Contract or reject

Apply interval Newton at the midpoint to \(f(x)=x^2-3\) on \([1.5,2]\). Then repeat on \([2,2.2]\). Compute both interval corrections and interpret their intersections.

Worked solution

In the first interval \(m=1.75\), \(f=1/16\), and \(D=[3,4]\). Subtracting the quotient gives \([1.75-1/48,1.75-1/64]\), approximately \([1.72916,1.73438]\) outward. Opposite endpoint signs and a positive derivative give exactly one root.

In the second interval \(m=2.1\), \(f=1.41\) and \(D=[4,4.4]\). The enclosure before intersection is \([1.7475,1.77955]\) rounded outward, entirely below 2. Its intersection is empty, excluding a root in the starting interval.
