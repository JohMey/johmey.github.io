Three readings of an unknown limit

Suppose \(x_n=r+cq^n\) with unknown \(r,c,q\). Consecutive differences are \(c(q-1)q^n\) and \(c(q-1)q^{n+1}\). Their ratio estimates \(q\). Substituting that ratio into the sum of the geometric tail gives Aitken's delta-squared estimate

\[\widehat r=x_n-\frac{(x_{n+1}-x_n)^2}{x_{n+2}-2x_{n+1}+x_n}.\]

For an exactly geometric sequence the denominator is \(cq^n(q-1)^2\) and the cancellation is exact. For a smooth fixed-point map \(g\) with \(|g'(r)|<1\), the leading error is locally geometric, with a quadratic remainder. Replacing \(x_n,x_{n+1},x_{n+2}\) by \(x,g(x),g(g(x))\), then restarting at the estimate, gives Steffensen's iteration. Each outer step costs two map evaluations.

Two computed limits

For \(g(x)=1+0.8(x-1)\) and \(x=0\), the three numbers are 0, 0.2 and 0.36. The denominator is \(-0.04\). The accelerated value is

\[0-0.04/(-0.04)=1.\]

A slowly contracting map can supply a very accurate extrapolation when the data are exact.

To solve \(x=\cos x\), begin at 0.5. The next two map evaluations are approximately 0.87758 and 0.63901. The accelerated value is about 0.73139. Its fixed-point residual is much smaller than the initial \(\cos(0.5)-0.5\), but plugging the result back into the original equation is essential: the geometric model is now only local.

Compare by number of map evaluations

Use the smooth map \(g(1+e)=1+qe+0.05e^2\) close to its fixed point. Plain substitution gets one update per function call. The accelerated curve receives two calls per update. Read the horizontal axis before deciding which method made better use of the budget.

Tutorial on stability of the transformation

For an exact linear map with \(q=0.999\) and error one, what order of magnitude is the second difference?

Worked solution

Its magnitude is \((1-q)^2=10^{-6}\), while the three original readings may be of order one. A measurement perturbation comparable to this denominator can completely change the extrapolation.

What should an implementation do at an exact fixed point where all three readings agree?

Worked solution

Return that fixed point after checking the residual. The delta-squared expression is then \(0/0\). Away from a certified fixed point, a small denominator warrants retaining a verified plain step or a different local model.
