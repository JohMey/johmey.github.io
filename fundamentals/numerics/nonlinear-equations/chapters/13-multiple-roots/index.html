Why a flat zero slows Newton's method

Factor a residual near a multiple root, recover the missing factor in a Newton step and distinguish a small residual from a small location error.

Expand around the actual root

Write \(f(x)=(x-r)^m g(x)\), with \(g\) smooth and \(g(r)\ne0\). Denote the current error by \(e=x-r\). Differentiation gives

\[f'=e^{m-1}(m g+eg').\]

Away from the root the quotient stays finite even when both function and derivative become very small. Substitution into Newton's update gives

\[e_{+}=e-\frac{e g}{m g+eg'}=(1-1/m)e+O(e^2).\]

If \(m=1\), the first-order error cancels. If \(m>1\), a fixed fraction survives. Multiplying the correction by the known multiplicity gives \(e_+=e^2g'/(mg+eg')\), restoring local quadratic convergence. A wrong value of \(m\) can send steps across the root or make them diverge.

A contact measurement

Near tangency a gap can behave like \(f=(x-2)^2\) in dimensionless coordinates. Starting from 3 produces 2.5, 2.25 and 2.125. The gap residual falls by a factor of four per step. The location error only halves. A stopping rule \(|f|<10^{-8}\) certifies merely \(|x-2|<10^{-4}\) in this exact model. The multiplicity-corrected step reaches 2 immediately.

Now take \(f=e^2(1+e)\) at \(e=0.1\). The residual and derivative are 0.011 and 0.23. An ordinary correction leaves error 0.05217. Twice the correction leaves 0.00435. The analytic denominator explains why this second update is no longer exact when \(g\) varies.

Tutorial on error certificates

A zero of \(f=e^4\) has residual \(10^{-12}\). How many decimal places of location accuracy follow from this observation alone?

Worked solution

The magnitude of the location error is \(10^{-3}\). Four powers have compressed the residual. Any inference based on a nonzero lower bound for \(|f'|\) fails across this root.

Why might a sign-changing bracket never isolate a double root?

Worked solution

The leading sign is the sign of \(g(r)\) on both sides. A sampler can step across the root without a sign change. Information about stationary points or a separate lower-bound argument is needed.
