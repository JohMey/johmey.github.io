Two equilibria share one control value

Use the scaled normal form \(F(x,p)=x^2+p=0\). The equilibrium curve is \(p=-x^2\). At \(p=-0.01\) two displacements, \(x=\pm0.1\), give the same load. Direct continuation in \(p\) uses \(dx/dp=-F_p/F_x=-1/(2x)\). The derivative diverges at the fold. Plotting \(p\) against \(x\), however, produces a smooth curve there.

Background · Backward error
\[F(\hat x)+\delta F=0\]

Specify how perturbations in data or equations are measured. A tiny perturbation making the computed value exact can coexist with a large solution error near a fold.

Add a geometric condition

At a known point \(y_0=(x_0,p_0)\), let \(t\) be a unit tangent perpendicular to \((2x_0,1)\). A predictor \(y_*=y_0+\Delta s\,t\) lies on the tangent. Correct it by solving \(F(x,p)=0\) together with \(t\cdot(y-y_*)=0\), which confines corrections to a line normal to the old tangent. At the fold the Jacobian of this pair is

\[J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\t_x&t_p\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det J=-t_x.\]

With the tangent aligned with the displacement axis, \(t_x\) is nonzero. The pair remains locally invertible where load control alone fails. This is a change in the computation's coordinate, not removal of the physical multiple equilibria at a fixed load.

A step that crosses the maximum

Start at \(x_0=-0.1,p_0=-0.01\) with increasing \(x\), so \(t=(1,0.2)/\sqrt{1.04}\). A step of length 0.2 predicts \(x_*\simeq0.0961,p_*\simeq0.0292\), which is off the curve and has positive load. The correction line satisfies

\[x+0.2p=x_*+0.2p_*.\]

Substitute \(p=-x^2\) to obtain \(x-0.2x^2\simeq0.1020\). The nearby root is about 0.1041 and the corrected load about −0.0108. Both endpoints have negative load despite crossing the maximum.

Test the predictor before trusting it

Choose the starting displacement and step length. Plot the tangent segment, the original curve and the selected nearby intersection of the correction line with the curve. A large step makes the tangent less faithful. The correction must stay on the intended local branch.

Tutorial on the experimental meaning

At \(p=-0.01\), a pressure measurement has uncertainty 0.001. Estimate its local displacement uncertainty and compare it near \(p=-0.0001\).

Worked solution

At \(|x|=0.1\), \(|dx/dp|=5\) and the linear estimate is 0.005. At \(|x|=0.01\), the formal factor is 50. The same pressure error now exceeds the distance to the fold, so the local linear approximation itself loses validity.

If the continuation algorithm traces both sides smoothly, are both equilibria dynamically stable?

Worked solution

A static residual gives equilibrium locations. Stability requires the linearisation of a specified evolution law or a second variation of the appropriate energy. Smooth numerical traversal gives no such certificate.
