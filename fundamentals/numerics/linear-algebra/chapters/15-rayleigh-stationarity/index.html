A frequency estimate from a trial shape

For \(Kq=\lambda Mq\) with symmetric \(K\) and positive definite mass \(M\), a trial vector \(v\) fixes a direction but leaves an amplitude free. Substituting \(q(t)=a(t)v\) in kinetic and elastic energy gives effective mass \(v^TMv\) and stiffness \(v^TKv\). Their ratio \(R(v)\) estimates the squared frequency. Rescaling \(v\) has no effect. For \(M=I\), differentiating \(v^TKv/(v^Tv)\) gives a gradient proportional to \(Kv-Rv\). Its zeros are precisely the eigenvectors.

Angular error and the residual

Take exact orthonormal modes \(u_1,u_2\) and a trial \(v=u_1\cos\theta+u_2\sin\theta\). Orthogonality removes all cross terms. Then

\[R(v)-\lambda_1=(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)\sin^2\theta,\]
\[\|Kv-R(v)v\|_2=|\lambda_2-\lambda_1|\,|\sin\theta\cos\theta|.\]

The eigenvalue error is second order in a small angular error. The residual is first order. For general dimension write \(v=\sum c_i u_i\) with unit norm. The squared residual is \(\sum |c_i|^2(\lambda_i-R)^2\), so at least one eigenvalue lies within one residual norm of \(R\). Identifying which mode requires more information about the spectrum.

Two measurements

For squared frequencies 4 and 9 in s⁻² and a 10-degree error in the first trial mode, \(R=4+5(0.03015)=4.1508\) s⁻². The estimated angular frequency is 2.0373 rad/s. The relative error in frequency is about half the relative error in its square at small errors.

For eigenvalues 4 and 4.01, a 45-degree mixture gives a residual of just 0.005. This very small number is consistent with a large uncertainty in shape. If the two eigenvalues were identical, every combination in the eigenspace would be an exact mode.

Tutorial on frequency estimates

For eigenvalues 2 and 10, a trial produces \(R=2.08\). Assuming the two-mode model, estimate its angular error.

Worked solution

The mixture fraction is

\[(2.08-2)/(10-2)=0.01.\]

Hence \(|\sin\theta|=0.1\), or about 5.74 degrees near the lower mode.

A trial gives \(R=5\) and residual zero. Does it prove that 5 is the lowest eigenvalue?

Worked solution

It proves that the trial vector is an exact eigenvector for 5. To certify the lowest eigenvalue one also needs a lower bound or a description of the remaining spectral subspace.
