One matrix for current balance

Give each resistor edge an arbitrary orientation. A row of the incidence matrix \(B\) has −1 at its tail and +1 at its head. The difference \(Bv\) measures an oriented voltage drop. With diagonal conductances \(G\), the dissipated power is

\[P=v^TB^TGBv.\]

Differentiating half this quadratic minus injected-current work \(j^Tv\) gives \(Lv=j\) with \(L=B^TGB\). Changing an edge orientation changes a sign twice and leaves \(L\) unchanged.

A constant voltage lies in the nullspace because \(B\mathbf1=0\). On a connected graph the converse follows from \(v^TLv=\sum_e g_e(\Delta v_e)^2\): zero energy forces equal voltage across every edge. The current injections must sum to zero. Set one reference voltage to zero or constrain the mean to remove this unobservable constant.

Compute a three-node measurement in two ways

Connect terminals A and C by a 6 Ω resistor. A second path passes through B with 2 Ω between A and B and 4 Ω between B and C. Inject 1 A at A, remove 1 A at C and ground C. Kirchhoff at B gives \((V_B-V_A)/2+V_B/4=0\), so \(V_B=2V_A/3\). At A, \(V_A/6+(V_A-V_B)/2=1\), giving \(V_A=3\) V and \(V_B=2\) V. Effective resistance is 3 Ω.

Each parallel path carries 0.5 A, and dissipates \(0.5^2(6)=1.5\) W. Total heat equals \(I(V_A-V_C)=3\) W. Opening the direct edge doubles the effective resistance. Opening an edge that bridges two otherwise disconnected subgraphs can instead make it impossible to pass any steady current between the terminals.

Tutorial on identifiability in a circuit

Can a single terminal-resistance reading isolate the two series resistor values?

Worked solution

It determines only their sum if the direct edge is known. Measuring \(V_B\) under the same known 1 A injection determines the division within that sum.

Why does minimising dissipation with prescribed terminal voltages yield Kirchhoff balance at interior nodes?

Worked solution

A variation of any free voltage contributes twice its net current departure to the first variation of total power. At the minimum all such coefficients vanish. Terminal voltages are fixed and their reaction currents are the measured injections.
