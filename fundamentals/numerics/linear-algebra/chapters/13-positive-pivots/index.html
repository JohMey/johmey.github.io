From positive energy to two triangular solves

If displacements \(x\) store elastic energy \(x^TAx/2\), then a fixed structure with no free modes has \(x^TAx>0\) for every nonzero \(x\). Equilibrium under load \(f\) minimises \(x^TAx/2-f^Tx\). Differentiation gives \(Ax=f\) for symmetric \(A\). Can the energy itself tell us which operations are safe in a solver?

Write the first coordinate as \(s\) and the remaining coordinates as \(z\). Expand the quadratic form using first entry \(a\), coupling column \(b\), and trailing block \(C\). The cross term is \(2s b^Tz\). Adding and subtracting \((b^Tz)^2/a\) gives

\[x^TAx=a(s+b^Tz/a)^2+z^T(C-bb^T/a)z.\]

Positivity with \(x=e_1\) proves \(a>0\). For any nonzero \(z\), choose \(s=-b^Tz/a\). The remaining term must then be positive, so the reduced matrix is positive definite. The argument repeats down to a scalar. Each completed square supplies one row of a triangular factor and one positive pivot. In matrix notation \(A=LL^T\), with \(L_{11}=\sqrt{a}\) and \(L_{2:n,1}=b/\sqrt{a}\). Solve \(Ly=f\) forward, then \(L^Tx=y\) backward. Neither inverse need be formed.

Worked equilibrium and a change of load

Use \(A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}4&2\\2&5\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) kN/m. The first diagonal factor is 2, the off-diagonal factor is 1, and the remaining pivot is \(5-1^2=4\). Thus \(L=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) in consistent numerical units. For \(f=(2,3)^T\) kN the intermediate result is

\[y=(1,1)^T.\]

Back substitution gives \(x=(0.25,0.5)^T\) m. Check the second row directly: \(2(0.25)+5(0.5)=3\).

Now apply \(f=(0,2)^T\) to the same structure. The intermediate vector changes to \((0,1)^T\) and the displacements to \((-0.25,0.5)^T\). The same two square roots serve both load cases. This matters for many load cases on one stiffness matrix.

The second pivot is \(1-c^2\). Under the antisymmetric load \((1,-1)^T\) the displacement is \((1,-1)^T/(1-c)\). A modest change in the input coupling can therefore have a large physical effect even with an exact factorisation.

Tutorial on pivots and supports

A free two-node spring has matrix \(k\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Where does the recurrence stop and which physical motion does it reveal?

Worked solution

Elimination gives the second pivot \(k-k^2/k=0\). Translating both endpoints by the same amount stores zero energy. Fixing one displacement removes that unconstrained motion.

For diagonal entries 9 and 4, what range of coupling \(b\) admits positive pivots? Interpret the endpoints.

Worked solution

The first pivot is 9. The second is \(4-b^2/9>0\), or \(|b|<6\). At equality the second completed square vanishes. There is a displacement with zero energy even though each diagonal term separately is positive.
