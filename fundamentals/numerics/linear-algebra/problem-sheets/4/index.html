Orthogonal factorizations

Use geometric reflections and singular directions to separate independent information from redundant measurements.

Build a reflection

A column has leading entries \(x=(3,4)^T\). Using the target \((-5,0)^T\), construct the reflector, apply it to \(y=(0,5)^T\), and check preservation of the angle between the columns.

Worked solution

Take \(v=x+5e_1=(8,4)^T\). Then

\[H=I-2vv^T/80=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-0.6&-0.8\\-0.8&0.6\end{smallmatrix}\right).\]

It sends \(x\) to \((-5,0)^T\) and \(y\) to \((-4,3)^T\).

Both lengths stay five. The original dot product is 20 and the transformed dot product is also 20, so the cosine is \(20/25=0.8\). Retaining the reflector vector suffices to apply the same transformation to another load.

Detect a missing direction

A measurement matrix has rows \((1,1)\) and \((2,2)\). Compute its singular values. For observations \(b=(3,6)^T\), find every exact solution and the one of smallest Euclidean norm.

Worked solution

The Gram matrix has both rows equal to \((5,5)\), so its eigenvalues are 10 and 0. The singular values are \(\sqrt{10}\) and zero. The null direction is \((1,-1)\).

The independent measurement requires \(x_1+x_2=3\). Write \(x=(1.5+t,1.5-t)^T\); its squared length is \(4.5+2t^2\), minimised at \(t=0\). Doubling a measurement supplies no independent information about \(t\).

Decide whether to truncate

Let \(A=\operatorname{diag}(4,0.02)\) and observe \(b=(4,0.03)^T\) with a possible second-component error of 0.02. Find the unregularised estimate, propagate the uncertainty, and compare with a truncated SVD retaining singular values above 0.1.

Worked solution

Direct inversion gives \((1,1.5)^T\); an observational error of 0.02 in the second component propagates to an uncertainty of one unit in \(x_2\). The interval of consistent values is \([0.5,2.5]\).

Truncating gives \((1,0)^T\) and residual norm 0.03. Truncation suppresses noise amplification but also discards the second-coordinate signal. The predicted second measurement is now outside the stated uncertainty range, which is evidence against this cutoff for these data.
