Matrix computations and spectral accuracy

Trace how algebraic structure, representation and numerical precision affect the solution of linear problems. Verify each conclusion with an independent diagnostic.

    Contents

    1. When stable eigenvalues allow large transient growth

      Two damped states exchange energy in only one direction. Its eigenvalues predict ultimate decay, while a short-time response can grow by a large factor. Compute the matrix exponential, its gain and a resolvent-based sensitivity check.

    2. How a random sketch finds a dominant subspace

      An instrument returns a matrix too large to factor densely but cheap enough to multiply by a few vectors. Build a low-rank approximation by probing its range, explain what a random draw can miss and design a residual check using fresh probes.

    3. Solve a singular network without inventing a ground current

      A floating resistor network has unique voltage drops but no absolute voltage reference. Derive its nullspace, test charge balance, and distinguish adding a computational gauge from changing the physical boundary conditions.

    4. Refitting a sensor as observations arrive or disappear

      A calibration line receives one new observation at a time. Update its least-squares factorisation using rotations, quantify the influence of a deleted observation and detect when removing a sample destroys identifiability.

    5. Which resistor can carry its current through another path?

      A short circuit diagram leads to a matrix that describes both voltage differences and sensitivity to losing an edge. Change one conductance and follow where the current goes.

    6. How a computer can lose a sensor direction

      Two sensors report almost the same quantity. Trace a perturbation through orthogonalisation, a Gram matrix and a rectangular SVD, then decide which computed digits the data support.

    7. Recovering a spectrum from three-term recurrences

      A large vibrating system supplies products with its stiffness operator. Derive the short recurrence implied by symmetry and use a residual and a moment test to interpret its small projected spectrum.
