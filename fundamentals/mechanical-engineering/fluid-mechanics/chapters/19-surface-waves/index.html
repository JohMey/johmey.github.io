Continue the surface motion down into the liquid

Use z positive upward, liquid occupying \(z<0\), and an undisturbed planar free surface at zero. Neglect viscosity and consider small-amplitude irrotational motion. The velocity is \(\nabla\phi\); incompressibility requires \(\nabla^2\phi=0\). A horizontal Fourier component \(e^{i(kx-\omega t)}\) with \(k>0\) therefore has vertical dependence \(e^{kz}\) in water deep compared with \(1/k\). The other Laplace solution grows without bound as \(z\to-\infty\) and is discarded. Let \(\eta\) denote surface elevation. Linearising the kinematic condition at z=0 gives \(\eta_t=\phi_z\), since the surface moves with the normal fluid velocity.

Translate curvature and elevation into surface pressure

For small slope the liquid surface pressure above atmospheric is \(-\gamma\eta_{xx}\), positive beneath a crest. Linear Bernoulli with the hydrostatic shift at the rising surface gives \(\phi_t+g\eta-(\gamma/\rho)\eta_{xx}=0\) at z=0. For amplitudes \(\Phi,\hat\eta\), the kinematic condition gives \(-i\omega\hat\eta=k\Phi\). Insert this in the dynamic condition to eliminate \(\Phi\):

\[\omega^2=gk+{\gamma\over\rho}k^3,\]
\[c_p={\omega\over k}=\sqrt{g/k+\gamma k/\rho},\]
\[c_g={d\omega\over dk}={g+3\gamma k^2/\rho\over2\omega}.\]

For long deep-water gravity waves \(c_g\simeq c_p/2\). In the opposite capillary limit \(c_g\simeq3c_p/2\). Thus tracking the envelope of a short group and timing an individual crest can give different speeds even with accurate instruments. Finite depth would multiply the right-hand side for \(\omega^2\) by \(\tanh(kH)\); deep-water long-wave asymptotics still require \(kH\gg1\).

Find the slowest crests and time a wave packet

For water take \(\rho=1000\) kg/m³ and \(\gamma=0.072\) N/m. Differentiating \(c_p^2\) with respect to k gives a stationary point at \(k_*=\sqrt{\rho g/\gamma}\), so \(\lambda_*=2\pi\sqrt{\gamma/(\rho g)}=17.0\) mm. Here gravity and capillarity contribute equally to \(\omega^2\); the minimum phase speed is 0.231 m/s. Also \(d(kc_p)/dk=c_p+k\,dc_p/dk=c_p\) at this point. Thus group and phase velocities coincide locally.

At 100 mm wavelength the predicted phase and group speeds are 0.401 and 0.212 m/s. At 10 mm they are 0.247 and 0.307 m/s. Over a 0.50 m path a narrow 10 mm packet envelope takes about 1.63 s, whereas timing a crest across the same distance would give 2.03 s if it stayed observable. Surface contamination changes effective restoring and damping behaviour, and dispersion broadens a packet with appreciable bandwidth.

Tutorial · recover surface tension from a measured frequency

A sinusoidal mode of wavelength 12.0 mm oscillates at 20.0 Hz in deep water of density 1000 kg/m³. Infer its surface tension using the full relation and estimate the error caused by discarding gravity. Which depth would make the deep-water approximation credible?

Solve for the constitutive parameter

With \(k=523.6\) m⁻¹ and \(\omega=125.66\) rad/s, \(\gamma=\rho(\omega^2-gk)/k^3\simeq0.0742\) N/m. Omitting gravity gives about 0.110 N/m, roughly 48% high. At depth 10 mm, \(kH=5.24\) and \(\tanh(kH)\) exceeds 0.9999, making the finite-depth correction very small in this model. Frequency, wavelength and depth should all be measured before attributing a discrepancy to surface chemistry.
