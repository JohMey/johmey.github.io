1. Split a laminar flow

Two long circular pipes of the same length connect common manifolds. Their radii are 2 mm and 3 mm and the fluid is the same. (a) Find the flow ratio. (b) Split a total of 6.0 mL/s. (c) If both radii are reduced by 10%, how does the required pressure drop change at fixed total flow?

Apply the common-pressure condition

At equal pressure drop the radius-fourth law gives

\[Q_2/Q_1=(3/2)^4=5.0625.\]

Thus \(Q_1=6/(1+5.0625)=0.990\) mL/s and \(Q_2=5.01\) mL/s. Reducing both radii scales each conductance by \(0.9^4\), so the common pressure drop must increase by \(1/0.9^4=1.52\). This assumes both flows remain developed and laminar and manifold losses remain negligible.

2. Compare pressure and wall driving

A 2 mm film of viscosity 0.10 Pa·s is bounded by a fixed lower plate and an upper plate moving at 0.40 m/s. A downstream pressure fall produces \(G=3000\) Pa/m. (a) Find the two contributions to \(Q/b\). (b) Find the midpoint speed. (c) What pressure gradient would produce zero net flux?

Superpose the profiles

The moving-wall and pressure terms are \(UH/2=4.0\times10^{-4}\) and \(GH^3/(12\mu)=2.0\times10^{-5}\) m²/s, totalling 0.00042 m²/s. At the middle, \(u=U/2+GH^2/(8\mu)=0.215\) m/s. To cancel wall transport one needs \(G=-6\mu U/H^2=-60\,000\) Pa/m. The negative sign represents a pressure rise in the direction of plate motion.

3. Design a reduced-scale tow test

A 2 m submerged object is towed at 1.5 m/s in water with \(\nu=10^{-6}\) m²/s. A geometrically similar 0.50 m model is available. (a) Find the required speed for equal Reynolds number in the same water. (b) For equal drag coefficient, compare model and prototype drag. (c) If surface waves dominate instead, find the speed for equal Froude number and the resulting Reynolds-number ratio.

Match a dimensionless balance

Prototype Reynolds number is \(3.0\times10^6\). At quarter length the required model speed is 6.0 m/s. Same density and drag coefficient imply \(F_m/F_p=(6/1.5)^2(0.5/2)^2=1\). Froude matching would require \(1.5\sqrt{0.25}=0.75\) m/s, giving \(Re_m/Re_p=(0.75/1.5)(0.5/2)=1/8\). The choice of matching rule changes the interpretation of the measured drag.

Which pressure difference establishes an accelerating uniform stream?

In the uniform core of a horizontal water test channel let \(u=0.60t\) in SI units for the first two seconds, with negligible transverse velocity and negligible viscous stresses in the core. Two pressure taps sampling the core at the same elevation are 1.50 m apart. Find the material acceleration and the pressure difference, then compare this result at \(t=2\) s with a steady core moving at the same instantaneous speed. Use \(\rho=998\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\).

Show the worked solution

Spatial gradients vanish, so the advective term is zero. During the ramp \(Du/Dt=0.60\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), hence

\[\partial_xp=-998(0.60)=-598.8\,\mathrm{Pa\,m^{-1}}.\]

Integrating downstream gives \(p_\mathrm{up}-p_\mathrm{down}=898.2\,\mathrm{Pa}\). Both cases have \(u=1.20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) at the instant of comparison. In the steady uniform ideal core the local derivative also vanishes, so the tap difference is zero. A calculation using speed alone could not distinguish them. Actual wall friction, if significant, would demand an additional pressure gradient.
