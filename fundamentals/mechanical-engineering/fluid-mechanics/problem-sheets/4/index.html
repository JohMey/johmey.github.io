Swirling flows and wall drag

Use a velocity distribution to infer local vorticity, pressure differences and the momentum lost to wall drag.

A rigidly spinning tank

Water in an open cylindrical tank of radius 0.15 m rotates as a solid body at 6 rad/s. Determine the circulation around the rim, local vorticity and wall-to-centre free-surface rise. Compare the vorticity with that of an ideal free-vortex exterior carrying the same circulation.

Reveal the worked solution

The rim speed is \(6(0.15)=0.90\,\mathrm{m/s}\) and circulation is

\[2\pi(0.15)(0.90)=0.848\,\mathrm{m^2/s}.\]

The vorticity is \(2\Omega=12\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\). Integrating the radial and vertical pressure balance gives

\[\Delta z=6^2(0.15)^2/(2g)=0.0413\,\mathrm m.\]

A free vortex of the same circulation has zero local vorticity outside its core. The circulation measures what the loop encloses.

A linear layer profile

An idealised velocity survey gives \(u/U=y/\delta\) to the layer edge and unity above. Derive displacement and momentum thicknesses. For \(U=8\,\mathrm{m/s}\), water density 1000 kg/m³, \(\delta=2\,\mathrm{mm}\) and plate width 0.15 m, estimate drag on the surveyed face to this station.

Reveal the worked solution

Integrating \(1-\eta\) gives

\[\delta^*=\delta/2=1.0\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

Integrating \(\eta(1-\eta)\) gives

\[\theta_m=\delta/6=0.333\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

Under constant external pressure and speed,

\[D=\rho U^2\theta_m b=3.20\,\mathrm N.\]

Only the one face to which the survey applies has been counted.

Infer a local shear stress

A measured zero-pressure-gradient momentum thickness follows \(\theta_m=0.0010\sqrt{x/(1\,\mathrm m)}\,\mathrm m\). Air density is 1.2 kg/m³ and external speed 15 m/s. At \(x=0.25\,\mathrm m\), find local wall shear and integrated drag per unit width. Why do these two quantities have different units?

Reveal the worked solution

At the station \(\theta_m=0.00050\,\mathrm m\) and \(d\theta_m/dx=0.0010\). Local shear is

\[\rho U^2d\theta_m/dx=0.270\,\mathrm{Pa}.\]

Drag per width is

\[\rho U^2\theta_m=0.135\,\mathrm{N/m}.\]

Local shear is force per area; integrating along plate length removes one inverse length.

Test a turbulent channel measurement

A water channel has half-height 15 mm and pressure gradient \(-80\) Pa/m. At a probe 5 mm from the lower wall, measurements give \(dU/dy=100\) s⁻¹ and \(\overline{u'v'}=-5.0\times10^{-4}\) m²/s². Use \(\rho=1000\) kg/m³ and \(\mu=0.0010\) Pa s. How well do the measurements close the momentum balance? What correlation would close it at the stated position?

Worked solution

The expected total stress is

\[80(0.015-0.005)=0.80\,\mathrm{Pa}.\]

The gradient gives 0.10 Pa and the measured covariance gives another 0.50 Pa. Together these account for 0.60 Pa. The shortfall is 0.20 Pa, or 25% of the pressure-based stress at the probe. The wall stress of 1.20 Pa is a different comparison value.

Holding the gradient fixed gives \(\overline{u'v'}=-(0.80-0.10)/1000=-7.0\times10^{-4}\) m²/s². Before changing a turbulence model, check the probe height, the averaging interval and whether the flow is fully developed. Changing the sign convention for just one term cannot resolve the shortfall.

Infer viscosity from the edge of a boundary layer

Using the baseline 99% thickness 35.84 mm at \(x=0.50\) m and speed 1 m/s, calibrate the coefficient of \(\delta=C\sqrt{\nu x/U}\) at \(\nu=10^{-4}\) m²/s. At the same station and speed a different run gives 51.03 mm. Infer its kinematic viscosity without measuring a wall gradient. Compare it with the input and identify one reason for a discrepancy.

Work through the solution

The baseline gives

\[C=0.03584/\sqrt{10^{-4}(0.50)}=5.07.\]

At common \(x,U\), the coefficient cancels in the ratio, so \(\nu_2=10^{-4}(51.03/35.84)^2=2.03\times10^{-4}\) m²/s. The laboratory input was \(2.00\times10^{-4}\) m²/s. The similarity argument describes an ideal laminar layer. Estimating its edge from a discretised velocity profile also depends on interpolation at 99% of the free-stream velocity.
