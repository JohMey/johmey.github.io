    Contents

    1. Gravity, capillarity and instability at a moving interface

      A flat interface can carry waves restored by gravity and surface tension. If its two liquids or gases move at different speeds, a band of those waves can grow. Derive the ideal threshold, identify the most vulnerable wavelength at onset and distinguish growth of an instability from advection of an otherwise stable disturbance.

    2. Settling spheres, Stokes drag and a wall correction

      A falling-sphere viscometer infers viscosity from terminal speed. Derive the drag on a no-slip sphere, distinguish creeping flow from merely steady motion and use radius, vessel width and orientation checks to decide what the speed experiment actually measures.

    3. Why shear disperses a tracer faster than molecular diffusion

      A short dye pulse in a straight tube first stretches into a filament and much later resembles a broadened one-dimensional cloud. Derive the long-time Taylor–Aris coefficient, predict the width of a measured pulse and identify the mixing time needed before fitting the familiar one-dimensional approximation.

    4. Estimate wall drag by integrating a boundary-layer profile

      A velocity probe can resolve the momentum missing from a boundary layer more easily than the wall shear itself. Derive an integral momentum relation, estimate drag using a polynomial profile and determine when a pressure gradient or a measurement too far from the wall changes the answer.

    5. How long does a moving wall take to set a fluid layer in motion?

      Start the top of a narrow gap suddenly and follow the velocity, volume flow and wall stresses as viscous diffusion builds a Couette profile.
