Fluid mechanics · Notation and assumptions

Read each defining equation with its assumptions and units, then follow the link to the argument that gives the equation its meaning.

Capillary pressure

\[\Delta p=\gamma(1/R_1+1/R_2)\]

Principal curvatures carry signs set by the chosen normal. A cylindrical interface contributes only one finite principal curvature.

Derivation · Curvature pressure, wetting and capillary rise

Froude number

\[\mathrm{Fr}=U/\sqrt{gh}\]

For shallow rectangular flow with gravity waves this compares material speed to wave speed. Critical depth occurs when the two are equal.

Derivation · Critical depth and the hydraulic jump

Head loss

\[h_L=\Delta p_{\rm loss}/(\rho g)\]

This is an irreversible loss expressed as a length. A static pressure change can also include reversible changes of speed or elevation.

Derivation · Bernoulli head, losses and pump duty

Material acceleration

\[D\mathbf u/Dt=\partial_t\mathbf u+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u\]

A fluid parcel can accelerate as it follows a steady but spatially varying velocity. A probe fixed in space measures only the local time history.

Derivation · Velocity fields, flow rates and conservation of mass

Reynolds number

\[\mathrm{Re}=\rho UL/\mu\]

Choose speed and length scales appropriate to the geometry. Matching this ratio compares inertial and viscous terms under the stated nondimensionalisation.

Derivation · Dynamic similarity, boundary layers and model tests

Vorticity

\[\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf u\]

Its units are reciprocal seconds. Circulation around a closed loop integrates the corresponding vorticity flux when the interior is regular.

Derivation · Vorticity, circulation and swirling flow
