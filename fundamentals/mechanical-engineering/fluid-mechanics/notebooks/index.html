Fluid mechanics · Computational notebooks

Change an input, run the Python cells and compare the result with the derivation. You can also download each notebook for Jupyter.

  • Locate the force on an inclined circular viewing window

    Two circular windows have the same area and centre depth. One is more steeply inclined. Predict whether the net force and its position both stay the same. The circular face is completely submerged and the opposite face is at atmospheric pressure.

    Open editable notebook · Download .ipynb · Chapter 2 · Hydrostatic pressure and forces on submerged surfaces

  • Watch a wetting front approach the capillary height

    Dip two clean glass tubes of different inner radii into the same wetting liquid. A microscope following only the first few millimetres of rise can give a different impression of which tube fills more readily than a photograph taken at equilibrium. Start with the force on one meniscus, derive a transient relation including gravity, and learn which measurements can distinguish a change of viscosity from a change of surface condition.

    Open editable notebook · Download .ipynb · Chapter 20

  • How long does a moving wall take to set a fluid layer in motion?

    Start the top of a narrow gap suddenly and follow the velocity, volume flow and wall stresses as viscous diffusion builds a Couette profile.

    Open editable notebook · Download .ipynb · Momentum, motion and transport in fluids
