Machine elements and contact mechanics · Notation and assumptions

Read each defining equation with its assumptions and units, then follow the link to the argument that gives the equation its meaning.

Contact ratio

\[\epsilon_\alpha=L_{\rm contact}/p_b\]

For a transverse involute pair compare the active path of contact with the base pitch. This determines how many pairs of teeth can share the load.

Derivation · How an involute gear pair keeps contact from one tooth to the next

Gear ratio

\[i=\omega_{\rm in}/\omega_{\rm out}\]

Specify the chosen convention and rotation signs. Reflecting an inertia to the input of an ideal reduction divides it by the squared speed ratio.

Derivation · Matching a gear ratio to load inertia and move time

Joint stiffness fraction

\[C=k_b/(k_b+k_m)\]

Before separation, bolt and clamped-member stiffnesses decide what fraction of a separating external force increases the bolt load.

Derivation · Preload, joint stiffness and separation

Lead angle

\[\tan\lambda=l/(2\pi r_m)\]

Lead is axial advance per revolution and can differ from the thread pitch. Friction and this angle together determine backdrivability.

Derivation · Deriving screw-drive torque, efficiency and backdrivability

Slip power

\[P_{\rm slip}=\tau|\omega_1-\omega_2|\]

Friction power during engagement depends on relative speed. Integrating it over engagement gives energy entering the thermal model.

Derivation · Friction clutches, braking torque and heat

Torsional stiffness

\[k_\theta=GJ/L\]

For a uniform elastic circular shaft use its polar second moment. A noncircular section generally needs a distinct torsion constant and warping analysis.

Derivation · Shaft reactions, twist and rotating bending
