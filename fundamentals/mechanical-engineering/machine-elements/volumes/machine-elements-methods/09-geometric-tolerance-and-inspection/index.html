A rectangular coordinate tolerance and a circular position tolerance accept different parts. Carry a hole requirement from its datum reference frame through its material condition to an actual inspection decision.

Construct the measurement before assigning error

A primary planar datum fitted by the specified simulator fixes normal translation and two tilts. A secondary side plane arrests one lateral translation and rotation about the primary normal. A tertiary end stop removes the final sliding freedom. Basic coordinates specify the intended hole axis in this frame. Datum order matters when the real surfaces are imperfect. A three-point seating scheme has different sensitivity to dirt or local form than a least-squares plane.

For a hole perpendicular to the primary plane in a thin plate, suppose tilt has been independently shown negligible. If the fitted centre departs from its basic coordinates by \(\Delta x,\Delta y\), the smallest circular zone centred on the basic axis that contains it has diameter \(p\). In a deep hole the entire derived axis must remain in the specified cylindrical zone, so a single face reading is insufficient.

\[p=2\sqrt{\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2}.\]

Why a larger internal feature can gain position tolerance

Suppose a mating design uses a hole between 10.00 and 10.20 mm and specifies a 0.20 mm diametral position tolerance at maximum material condition relative to fixed datums. For an internal feature maximum material is the smallest permissible diameter \(D_{MMC}\). Holding a fixed virtual-condition boundary at the basic location gives

\[D_{VC}=D_{MMC}-t_{MMC}=9.80\ \mathrm{mm},\]
\[t_{allowed}=t_{MMC}+D_{actual}-D_{MMC}.\]

A measured 10.12 mm hole gains 0.12 mm diametral allowance. For offsets (0.09,0.10) mm its measured position is 0.269 mm, within the 0.32 mm allowance. An otherwise identical 10.00 mm hole fails the 0.20 mm position requirement. The smaller hole still satisfies its size limit. Under a rule without the material-condition modifier, the position allowance in this example stays 0.20 mm for both.

What a functional gauge actually checks

A suitably located 9.80 mm pin simulates the worst-case internal boundary only with the specified datum simulators engaged. Passing that pin alone does not check the hole’s upper size limit or all separate requirements on shape. The example assumes ideal form unless a test explicitly evaluates form.

Exercises on inspection decisions

A 10.08 mm hole has offsets (0.11,0.09) mm. Can the part be accepted on nominal readings?

Worked solution

The diameter of its position zone is \(2\sqrt{0.11^2+0.09^2}=0.2843\) mm. The allowance is 0.28 mm. It narrowly fails on the stated values. With uncertainty comparable to 0.0043 mm, apply the drawing and inspection decision rule rather than rounding to two decimals before comparison.

A 30 mm long hole with nominal entrance centre is tilted by 0.3°. Where should a second measurement be taken?

Worked solution

At the opposite end the centre departs by \(30\tan(0.3°)=0.157\) mm. The corresponding diametral deviation is about 0.314 mm if the axis is constrained to its basic orientation. Two widely separated centre measurements reveal tilt that an entrance-only check cannot see.
