Resolve a torque into tooth force

Take straight external spur gears with a pitch diameter \(d\) and pressure angle \(\alpha\). At the pitch circle, transmission of torque \(T\) fixes \(F_t=2T/d\). The nominal normal force is \(F_n=F_t/\cos\alpha\). Use one conservative single-tooth load factor \(K\) for the example. Real cyclic mesh sharing, alignment, speed and surface finish require data specific to the teeth.

For root bending, replace the tooth by a cantilever section. Its bending stress scales as \(6F_t\ell/(bt^2)\) for lever arm \(\ell\), face width \(b\) and root thickness \(t\). A dimensionless shape factor \(Y\) collects the section geometry into a module-based estimate. For contact take elastic nonconforming line contact with reduced modulus \(E'\) and effective local radius \(R'\). Integrating the Hertz pressure \(p_0\sqrt{1-(x/a)^2}\) over width \(2a\) gives \(KF_n/b=\pi a p_0/2\). Compatibility with the local quadratic gap gives \(a^2=4KF_nR'/(\pi bE')\). Eliminating \(a\) gives

\[\sigma_F=\frac{KF_t}{bmY},\]
\[p_H=\sqrt{\frac{E'KF_n}{\pi bR'}}.\]

Follow one reducer through two life checks

Use \(T=80\) N m, \(d=80\) mm, \(m=2.5\) mm, \(b=16\) mm, \(Y=0.32\), \(\alpha=20°\), \(K=1.30\), \(E'=115\) GPa, and \(R'=8\) mm. The nominal tangential force is 2000 N. The model gives \(\sigma_F=203.1\) MPa and \(p_H=889.5\) MPa. The contact pressure is a compressive surface quantity and its pitting limit must come from a contact-fatigue test.

For an illustrative material pair take \(N_F=10^7(220\,\mathrm{MPa}/\sigma_F)^6\) and \(N_H=10^7(950\,\mathrm{MPa}/p_H)^{10}\). Spend 80% of cycles at full torque and 20% at half. Since root stress scales with \(T\) and contact pressure with \(\sqrt T\), damage per million cycles is \(0.1(203.1/220)^6[0.8+0.2/64]\simeq0.050\) for bending and \(0.1(889.5/950)^{10}[0.8+0.2/32]\simeq0.042\) for contact. In this assumed pair, root fatigue uses more of its life. A change of flank hardness can change which limit controls.

Where an industrial rating requires additional evidence

These two models explain scaling and identify required inputs. A standard rating requires the applicable tooth geometry, quality, dynamic and distribution factors, material condition, lubrication, life and reliability provisions. The illustrative constants are not a substitute for those inputs.

Size the face width for one duty and two failure mechanisms

Retain the pitch diameter, module, normal force, pressure angle and radii of the worked gear. A design brief specifies 20 million pinion load cycles, of which 80% are at 80 N m and 20% at 40 N m. It supplies an application load multiplier \(K_A=1.15\), a measured dynamic amplification \(K_v=1.10\), and face-distribution factors \(K_{bF}=1.18\) for root stress and \(K_{bH}=1.12\) for pressure. These illustrative factors replace the earlier single \(K\) and apply to load. Define \(K_F=K_AK_vK_{bF}=1.4927\) and \(K_H=K_AK_vK_{bH}=1.4168\). Root stress is proportional to \(K_F\). Contact pressure is proportional to \(\sqrt{K_H}\).

On the material side, take 10-million-cycle reference stresses of 260 MPa at the root and 1050 MPa in contact. The design data supply two independent strength multipliers for each mechanism. Root strength is \(S_F=260(0.95)(0.97)=239.59\) MPa, and flank strength is \(S_H=1050(0.98)(0.95)=977.55\) MPa. Use the laboratory exponents 6 and 10 against these corrected strengths. This completely defines the teaching model from measured loads to allowable cumulative damage \(D_{lim}=0.60\). The factors are design inputs, with no claim to implement a particular industrial standard.

At \(b_0=16\) mm the full-load root stress is 233.23 MPa and the full-load peak contact pressure is 928.64 MPa. The half-torque root stress is 116.62 MPa; the half-torque pressure is 656.64 MPa. Summing counted cycles divided by their mechanism-specific lives gives

\[D_F(b_0)=2(0.8+0.2/2^6)(233.23/239.59)^6=1.367,\]
\[D_H(b_0)=2(0.8+0.2/2^5)(928.64/977.55)^{10}=0.965 .\]

Both exceed the design limit despite the full-load stresses being below their reference strengths. At fixed distribution factors \(D_F\propto b^{-6}\) and \(D_H\propto b^{-5}\). The separate required widths are \(16(1.367/0.60)^{1/6}=18.35\) mm and \(16(0.965/0.60)^{1/5}=17.60\) mm. Choose a 20 mm available width. Its damages are 0.358 and 0.316. The margin is then traceable to one specified mission, one set of material data and one geometry. Check each gear separately where tooth counts and hence cycle counts differ.

A contact pattern test at 20 mm finds that the flank-distribution factor is 15% greater than assumed. Does the adopted width still meet the stated target? What width would restore it if all other inputs stayed fixed?

Worked solution

Pressure grows by \(\sqrt{1.15}\). Damage therefore grows by \(1.15^5\), taking the flank result from 0.316 to 0.636, just above 0.60. Required width becomes about \(20(0.636/0.60)^{1/5}=20.23\) mm. The original root result is unchanged under the problem’s assumption. A new width needs a new contact-pattern check if alignment changes.

Exercises on design changes

Double the face width while keeping load sharing uniform. What happens to each computed damage?

Worked solution

Root stress halves, reducing bending damage by \(2^6=64\). Contact stress falls by \(\sqrt2\), reducing contact damage by \(2^5=32\). A widened but poorly aligned mesh can lose much of this benefit.

Why might a gear with safe tooth bending still exhibit pitting after its lubricant changes?

Worked solution

The root check depends chiefly on tangential load and root section. Lubrication, film thickness, roughness and local sliding influence the durability of the contacting surfaces. A bending margin does not supply a contact life or a wear prediction.
