Keep coupon, component and nominal stress distinct

Take a quoted fully reversed specimen strength \(S'_f(N_*)\) at a stated reference life \(N_*\). A component with a different finish, stressed volume, environment or survival requirement has different statistics of crack initiation. Here use experimentally supplied finish and size factors \(k_a,k_b\) for this process and diameter. They act on allowable amplitude. Factors calibrated at one life need supporting data before use over another regime.

\[S_f(N_*)=k_a k_b S'_f(N_*),\]
\[K_f=1+q(K_t-1).\]

The elastic concentration \(K_t\) is the local-to-nominal peak from the geometry. Notch sensitivity \(q\) transfers the nominal alternating component to an effective fatigue amplitude. Its values zero and one recover no notch penalty and the full elastic concentration. Keep surface correction on the material side and stress concentration on the demand side. Applying the same notch debit to both counts it twice.

Move a load cycle to the reference mean

For a cycle with nominal maximum and minimum stresses, their half-difference is \(\sigma_a\) and their mean is \(\sigma_m\). With elastic local response, a specified tensile-mean Goodman approximation gives a fully reversed equivalent demand. Adopt zero benefit from compressive mean for this example. A separate maximum-stress check against yield must precede this calculation.

\[a_{eq}=\frac{K_f\sigma_a}{1-K_t\max(\sigma_m,0)/S_u},\]
\[N_f=N_*\left(\frac{S_f(N_*)}{a_{eq}}\right)^b .\]

Take \(S_u=650\) MPa, \(S'_f=280\) MPa at a million cycles, \(k_a=0.8\), \(k_b=0.88\), \(q=0.8\), \(K_t=1.65\) and exponent \(b=5\). A 100 MPa amplitude and 30 MPa mean give \(K_f=1.52\), component strength 197.1 MPa, and equivalent demand 164.5 MPa. The illustrative power law predicts about 2.47 million repetitions. It has no endurance knee. Extrapolation to small stresses would require additional material data.

What did the heat treatment change?

A surface-hardened shoulder can have hardness and residual stress gradients across the crack-initiation zone. A coupon from the core measures neither directly. Select data from the production condition, measure the local radius and identify any residual tensile mean before assigning corrections from a handbook.

Turn a measured record into cumulative damage

Rainflow counting pairs stress reversals to recover amplitudes, means and fractional counts. Analyze an explicitly finite record and retain its half cycles. A periodic mission should instead be closed consistently across its boundary. Linear accumulation then sets \(D=\sum_i n_i/N_i\) for this model. Sequence sensitivity and residual stress relaxation require independent checks.

Interactive experiment · Move a load cycle across the Goodman diagram

Set the mean and alternating nominal stress to place a single constant-amplitude cycle in the diagram. Its boundary corresponds to one million cycles in the illustrative material law above. Change the surface factor and elastic concentration separately to see which acts on the available strength and which on the applied demand. The unnotched comparison uses the same finish and size corrections.

Begin with a 30 MPa mean and a 100 MPa amplitude. Raising the surface factor from 0.80 to 0.90 multiplies predicted life by approximately 1.80. Increasing the mean stress lowers the admissible amplitude even when the stress range is unchanged. This interactive diagnostic treats one repeating cycle. The displayed law assumes an elastic local response, includes no endurance knee and needs a separate yield check.

OneSimulation experiment · Changing the measured finish factor changes damage from the same counted load record

Run the notebook with OneSimulation 0.2.0, NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib. Change the input data and compare your run with the recorded result.

"""Compare the effect of measured surface factors at one shoulder under a finite record."""
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from onesim.physics.solid_mechanics.fatigue.models.cycles.cycle_counting import fatigue_cycles

nominal = np.array([0,110,-40,85,-65,130,-55,80,0.0])  # MPa
cycles = list(fatigue_cycles(stress=nominal))
surface = np.linspace(0.65, 0.95, 61)
kt, notch_sensitivity, ultimate = 1.65, 0.8, 650.0  # ultimate in MPa
kf = 1+notch_sensitivity*(kt-1)
# Illustrative calibrated finite-life power law. Reference life: one million cycles.
def block_damage(ka):
    strength = 280*ka*0.88  # MPa at reference life, after size correction
    damage = 0.0
    for cycle in cycles:
        mean = kt*max(cycle.mean_stress, 0)
        alternating = kf*cycle.stress_amplitude/(1-mean/ultimate)
        damage += cycle.count/1e6*(alternating/strength)**5
    return damage

damage = np.array([block_damage(value) for value in surface])
print("counted cycle equivalents", sum(cycle.count for cycle in cycles))
for ka in [0.70, 0.90]:
    print(f"surface factor {ka:.2f}: damage per record = {block_damage(ka):.4e}")

surface factor 0.70: damage per record = 9.8463e-07

Exercises on fatigue evidence

Polish the previous shaft so that \(k_a\) increases from 0.80 to 0.90. How does the predicted life change at the same cycle and exponent?

Worked solution

At fixed mean correction and amplitude, \(N_f\) scales as \(k_a^5\). The multiplier is \((0.90/0.80)^5=1.80\). Changing \(K_t\) as well would require a separate geometry update.

Two records have equal peaks at 160 MPa. The first cycles between 0 and 160, the second between −160 and 160 MPa. Which has larger corrected demand here?

Worked solution

The first gives \(1.52(80)/(1-1.65(80)/650)=152.6\) MPa. The second gives \(1.52(160)=243.2\) MPa. A peak-only count would discard the dominant difference in range.
