Helical gears, mesh forces and bearing reactions

A motor supplies a known torque to a helical pinion. Select the free body carefully: torque fixes one component of the tooth load, the helix creates thrust, and the location of that thrust can alter bearing reactions even in a parallel-shaft transmission. Build the force diagram and reverse it from measured support loads.

Unroll a pitch cylinder and resolve the tooth normal

Imagine cutting the pitch cylinder and unwrapping it. The tooth trace makes helix angle \(\beta\) with the shaft axis. The component of tooth contact force lying in the unwrapped pitch surface points perpendicular to that trace. It has circumferential projection \(F_t\) and axial projection \(F_a\). The line of action also tilts out of the tangent plane through normal pressure angle \(\alpha_n\), creating a radial component. For a frictionless external mesh the magnitudes follow from two right triangles and torque balance:

\[F_t=\frac{2T}{d},\]
\[F_a=F_t\tan\beta,\]
\[F_r=\frac{F_t\tan\alpha_n}{\cos\beta},\]
\[F_n=\frac{F_t}{\cos\beta\cos\alpha_n}.\]

Projection of the transverse circular tooth pitch onto its normal section gives \(p_n=p_t\cos\beta\), so the pitch diameter of a standard unshifted gear is \(d=z m_n/\cos\beta\). Distinguish fixed diameter from fixed tooth count and normal module when changing helix angle. At fixed diameter and torque the tangential force stays fixed; changing the diameter changes it too. A spur gear emerges continuously as the helix angle tends to zero, because axial thrust vanishes and both pressure angles coincide.

For 100 N m, 100 mm reference diameter and 20 degrees for both helix and normal pressure angles, the tangential, axial and radial forces are respectively 2000, 728 and 775 N. Their vector magnitude is 2265 N. The direction of thrust depends on helix hand and torque direction. The two mating gears experience equal and opposite contact forces in common coordinates. Two separate diagrams drawn from opposite shaft ends can make consistent thrust arrows look identical on the paper.

Transport the mesh wrench to the shaft centreline

Let bearing A lie at coordinate zero and B at span \(L\). A lateral force acts a distance \(a\) from A. In each independent bending plane its reaction magnitudes for a simple support model are \(F(L-a)/L\) and \(Fa/L\), with directions opposite the applied force. The axial force acts at the pitch contact, a radius away from the centreline; moving it onto the shaft centre adds a couple of magnitude \(F_a d/2\). In a plane where the radius points vertically this couple acts in the vertical bending plane. Determine its sign from the cross product. The tangential force at that radius also contributes the transmitted torsional moment, balanced by the drive.

For \(a=60\) mm, \(L=150\) mm, the 2000 N tangential force gives 1200 and 800 N. Ignoring the thrust couple, the radial force would give 465 and 310 N. The 36.4 N m couple transfers 243 N between the two radial reactions without changing their sum. With the sign increasing B, the radial reactions become approximately 222 and 553 N. Dynamic load ratings use the resultant load on each bearing and a catalogue rule for its combination with axial force. Suppose A locates the shaft axially and B permits axial displacement. Then A reacts the full thrust in this idealisation. Two preloaded locating bearings require deformation compatibility, clearance and stiffness for a reliable split.

Measure a signature of mesh geometry

Make two steady tests at the same torque magnitude, reversing rotation and applied torque together. The separating radial component of an external mesh usually stays separating while thrust changes direction with the loaded flank. Tangential reactions reverse sign. Resolve measured reactions into a common coordinate frame and subtract bearing preload before comparing. A change in torque sign may also move load to a different bearing shoulder or traverse backlash. Examine displacement on each side of the reversal before attributing changes to helix geometry.

Use the involute-contact study to distinguish tooth contact ratio from the total steady mesh force.

Move the mesh along the shaft without changing the mesh forces

The experiment holds torque and pitch diameter fixed while shifting the mesh station. In each radial reaction the contribution of the lateral force varies linearly with station. The off-axis thrust contribution is instead a constant signed transfer between the supports for this geometry. Compare the two curves for support B. Their vertical separation is the thrust couple divided by span and stays fixed as the gear moves. The sum of A and B stays equal to the separating force at every station.

Open the live experiment

Near one end the smaller support reaction can change sign. In the signed vector convention this means the support must react in the opposite direction. A preloaded bearing can change its incremental force in this direction. A clearance support might instead unload until the opposite side takes up load. The simple-support drawing chooses neither actual contact path. Check axial location, allowable movement and measured preload before interpreting a predicted negative incremental load as a reversed material stress at one rolling contact.

Tutorial · Size from the normal module

An unshifted pinion has 30 teeth, normal module 3 mm and helix angle 25 degrees. At 90 N m find pitch diameter, tangential load and axial thrust.

Resolve geometry before applying torque

The transverse module is \(3/\cos25^\circ=3.31\) mm, giving diameter 99.3 mm. Consequently the tangential force is 1813 N and axial thrust 845 N. Using 90 mm as diameter would overestimate transmitted force. The torque check multiplies 1813 N by a 49.65 mm arm, recovering approximately 90 N m. The input power cannot be recovered without the rotation speed; steady torque does not by itself determine heating or time to a given number of bearing revolutions.

Tutorial · Find a missing transverse couple

Radial support reactions sum to 780 N in a 150 mm span. The mesh lies 60 mm from A, and B reacts 550 N. What signed applied couple is consistent with the departure from simple force sharing?

Subtract the force-only support moment

A force-only prediction for B is

\[780(60/150)=312\,\mathrm{N}.\]

The excess 238 N corresponds to a couple of magnitude 35.7 N m in the direction increasing B. With a 50 mm pitch radius that magnitude would imply 714 N axial force if off-axis thrust were its only source. Check the thrust bearing directly. Misaligned couplings or another overhung force can give the same two transverse reactions, so the inversion identifies a net moment rather than its unique origin.

Tutorial · Explain a life sensitivity

For an ideal ball bearing the reference rating model gives life in millions of revolutions as \((C/P)^3\). A catalogue combined-load calculation increases effective load from 1.00 to 1.20 kN at unchanged rating. Estimate the life ratio and explain what data the ratio omits.

Keep the bearing rating and the statistical meaning fixed

The ratio is \((1/1.20)^3=0.579\), a reduction of about 42 percent in the rating life. This is a population-based rating at a chosen reliability under specified conditions. Lubrication, contamination, preload, fit, load cycles and installation can change service results. Comparing against a test measured in hours also requires shaft speed. If speed doubles at the same effective load, the reference life in revolutions stays the same while the corresponding time halves.
