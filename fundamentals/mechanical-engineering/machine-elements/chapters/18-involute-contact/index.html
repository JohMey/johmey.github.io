How an involute gear pair keeps contact from one tooth to the next

Unwrap base pitch from a taut string

An involute is the curve traced by the end of a taut string as it unwinds from a base circle. At each point the string is normal to the involute and tangent to the base circle. The common normal of conjugate external involute teeth is therefore the straight line tangent to both base circles, the line of action. Successive teeth reach equivalent points on this line one base pitch apart. For module m, tooth count z and reference pressure angle \(\alpha\), the reference pitch radius is \(r=mz/2\) and the base radius is \(r_b=r\cos\alpha\). One tooth pitch on the base circle is

\[p_b={2\pi r_b\over z}=\pi m\cos\alpha.\]

The operating centre distance a can differ from the sum of the reference pitch radii. The new operating pressure angle satisfies

\[\cos\alpha_w=(r_{b1}+r_{b2})/a.\]

The two base circles and base pitch are unchanged because tooth geometry was cut before assembly. This explains how involute gears accommodate a moderate centre-distance shift while retaining their velocity ratio.

Background · Contact ratio
\[\epsilon_\alpha=L_{\rm contact}/p_b\]

For a transverse involute pair compare the active path of contact with the base pitch. This determines how many pairs of teeth can share the load.

Compute the interval on the line of action

At first and last contact one of the addendum circles intersects the common normal. In the right triangle from its centre to a tangent-foot point on the base circle and the addendum intersection, the distance along the tangent is \(\sqrt{r_a^2-r_b^2}\). The tangent feet of both base circles are separated along the normal by \(a\sin\alpha_w\). Combining the two triangle lengths gives

\[\ell=\sqrt{r_{a1}^2-r_{b1}^2}+\sqrt{r_{a2}^2-r_{b2}^2}-a\sin\alpha_w,\]
\[\epsilon_\alpha={\ell\over p_b}.\]

For \(1<\epsilon_\alpha<2\), the fraction of a mesh cycle in nominal two-pair contact is \(\epsilon_\alpha-1\), and the remainder has one pair. This is a geometric sharing opportunity. Actual pair loads depend on tooth compliance, profile modifications and errors. The formula assumes the usable involute extends across the predicted path without tip interference or undercut.

Count the overlap and change centre distance

Take a spur pair with 20 and 40 teeth, module 2 mm, reference pressure angle 20°, and addendum equal to one module on both gears. The reference radii are 20 and 40 mm and addendum radii are 22 and 42 mm. At the nominal 60 mm spacing the base pitch is 5.904 mm and path length is 9.655 mm. The contact ratio is 1.635, so about 63.5% of each cycle can have two tooth pairs in contact. A 600 rpm pinion produces \(20(600/60)=200\) tooth engagements per second and a mesh period of 5.0 ms.

Increase centre distance by 0.50 mm without recutting either gear. The operating angle rises to 21.26° and the new ratio becomes 1.395. The two-pair interval falls to about 1.97 ms per mesh period from 3.18 ms. Gear ratio stays 2:1. By contrast, at nominal centres shortening both addenda to \(0.8m\) changes the tangent intersection lengths and gives ratio 1.347. Measure centre distance and tooth height independently before attributing an apparent overlap reduction to a single manufacturing error.

Increase the centre distance until the ratio is about 1.50. Mark that point before decreasing tooth addendum. Read the fraction of time with a single contacting pair and explain why tooth-count speed ratio stays fixed.

Isolate the operating tangent length

Let \(S=\sqrt{r_{a1}^2-r_{b1}^2}+\sqrt{r_{a2}^2-r_{b2}^2}\). Since \(a\sin\alpha_w=\sqrt{a^2-(r_{b1}+r_{b2})^2}\), the limiting distance is \(\sqrt{(S-1.50p_b)^2+(r_{b1}+r_{b2})^2}\), about 60.28 mm. Differentiating gives \(d\epsilon_\alpha/da=-1/(p_b\sin\alpha_w)\), about −0.495 per mm at nominal spacing. A 0.135 loss predicts about 0.27 mm additional spacing, close because the tested change is small.
