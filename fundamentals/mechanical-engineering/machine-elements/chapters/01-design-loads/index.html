From first principles

Convert a repeated force history into stress and accumulated damage

Begin by isolating the moving part and balancing its forces. For a mass \(m\) accelerated upwards at \(a\), an ideal vertical lifting member supplies \(F=m(g+a)\) if its own inertia is negligible. In a straight prismatic member of area \(A\) loaded along its centroid, the nominal axial stress is \(F/A\). Record the largest and smallest stress during one repeated operating cycle. Their mean and half-range are

\[\sigma_m=\frac{F_{\max}+F_{\min}}{2A},\]
\[\sigma_a=\frac{F_{\max}-F_{\min}}{2A}.\]

Fatigue life is empirical: tests on the appropriate detail relate a specified stress amplitude and mean stress to a characteristic failure count \(N_i\). Under the linear damage-summation approximation, \(n_i\) repetitions of that level consume a fraction \(n_i/N_i\) of life. For one recurring duty block,

\[D_{\rm block}=\sum_i\frac{n_i}{N_i},\]
\[B_{\rm predicted}=\frac1{D_{\rm block}} .\]

The second relation uses a nominal failure threshold of one. It requires the same load block to repeat and assumes damage fractions add regardless of sequence. Peak stress and cumulative fatigue damage answer different questions about that operating history.

Derivation and applications

Convert a variable-amplitude block to a damage-equivalent stress

Two repeated machine duties can have different peak stresses yet accumulate the same fatigue damage in a specified model. Consider a fixed detail, a common stress definition and constant mean-stress conditions. Use an experimentally fitted single-slope life relation \(N(S)=N_r(S_r/S)^m\), where \(S\) is alternating stress amplitude, \(m>0\) is the fitted slope and the reference point is \((S_r,N_r)\). Assume the same environment and material condition as the fit. Palmgren–Miner summation gives the predicted damage of one load block with \(n_i\) cycles at each amplitude \(S_i\).

\[D_b=\sum_i\frac{n_i}{N(S_i)}=\frac{1}{N_rS_r^m}\sum_i n_i S_i^m .\]

Define a constant amplitude \(S_{\rm eq}\) acting for the same total number of cycles \(n=\sum_i n_i\). Equate its predicted damage to the variable-amplitude damage. The reference point cancels from this comparison because both predictions use the same life curve.

\[n S_{\rm eq}^m=\sum_i n_i S_i^m,\]
\[S_{\rm eq}=\left(\sum_i\frac{n_i}{n}S_i^m\right)^{1/m}.\]

A rare high stress can dominate this power average. For two levels the proportion of total damage due to the higher one follows by dividing its contribution by their sum. This fraction is independent of how often the entire block is repeated.

\[f_H=\frac{n_H S_H^m}{n_H S_H^m+n_L S_L^m}.\]

Changing sequence, residual stress, mean stress or a transition between slopes can invalidate the reduction; the equality here is an exact statement about the stated damage model.

Worked example

Identify which cycles dominate a conveyor duty

A block contains 900 cycles at 40.0 MPa and 100 at 80.0 MPa. For \(m=4\), the equivalent amplitude is

\[40[0.9+0.1(2^4)]^{1/4}=50.3\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

The high cycles provide \(1.6/(0.9+1.6)=0.640\) of damage, though they occupy one tenth of the block. Simply taking the arithmetic mean would give 44.0 MPa and understate predicted damage.

Second application

Determine a permissible high-cycle fraction

Another duty must stay below a damage-equivalent amplitude of 60.0 MPa. Let the available levels be 50.0 and 75.0 MPa with slope \(m=3\). Solving \(60^3=(1-f)50^3+f75^3\) gives

\[f=(60^3-50^3)/(75^3-50^3)=0.307.\]

At most about 30.7 percent of cycles can occupy the higher level under these assumptions. The result concerns cycle count; unequal shaft speeds would require converting operating durations first.

Follow the force through the machine

A machine specification may give lifting capacity in kilograms or motor power in kilowatts. Neither quantity by itself gives the tension in an individual link. Begin with the moving assembly and its connections. Isolate one body, mark every contact and decide which reactions the connection can transmit. A pin carries two force components in a planar model. A flexible cable carries tension along its length. A pair of guides may transmit a couple even though each individual contact carries only a normal force. These decisions determine which part experiences each load.

Consider a carriage raised by a vertical cable. Its cable attachment transfers tension through a flat link of net area \(A\). If upward is positive, the carriage balance reads \(F-mg=ma\), hence \(F=m(g+a)\). This also applies while the carriage is moving downwards and slowing down. Its upward acceleration increases tension. A speed record alone therefore conceals an important load case. Draw both the velocity and its slope before assigning signs. A cable solution with negative tension means that the cable would slacken and the assumed constraint has failed.

Separate a steady bias from an alternating load

Cut the link across its smallest section. With the load centred and bending negligible, integration of an approximately uniform axial stress gives

\[F=\sigma A.\]

The stress at the two turning points defines the mean stress and alternating amplitude for that cycle. An amplitude is measured from the mean, so a recorded peak-to-peak range must be divided by two.

\[\begin{aligned} \sigma_{\max,\min} &= \frac{F_{\max,\min}}{A},\\ \sigma_m &= \frac{\sigma_{\max}+\sigma_{\min}}{2},\\ \sigma_a &= \frac{\sigma_{\max}-\sigma_{\min}}{2}. \end{aligned}\]

A part loaded from zero to a maximum tensile stress has equal positive mean and alternating components. A fully reversed load with the same peak tensile stress has zero mean and twice that alternating amplitude. To assess fatigue, a material curve must describe the applicable mean stress, surface condition, stress concentration and environment. For yielding, first compare the largest local stress with the yield strength. Those two calculations use different features of the same history.

Background · Why can the surface of a steadily loaded rotating shaft see alternating stress?

A transverse force fixed in the laboratory creates a bending moment with a stationary direction. A particular material fibre at radius \(r\) rotates through the tensile and compressive sides. For linear bending of a circular shaft with second moment \(I\), that fibre samples

\[\sigma_x(t)=\frac{Mr}{I}\cos(\Omega t+\theta_0).\]

At 1500 rpm the fibre experiences 25 complete stress cycles per second, even while a force transducer on the housing sees a steady reading. A torque also fixed in magnitude gives a steady torsional component in the rotating material frame. Trace the history at one material point before combining a bending cycle with a torsional mean.

A lift with repeated starts

A carriage and its payload have a combined mass of 800 kg. In each trip the drive accelerates upwards at 2.0 m/s² and later decelerates while travelling upwards at 3.0 m/s². The cable passes without mechanical advantage to a link with net section 80 mm². Gravity is 9.81 m/s². Assume the cable remains taut, and treat the measured accelerations as representing the load on the carriage.

The maximum tension is \(800(9.81+2.0)=9448\,\mathrm N\) and the minimum during the stated loaded cycle is

\[800(9.81-3.0)=5448\,\mathrm N.\]

Dividing by area gives 118.1 and 68.1 MPa. Their mean is 93.1 MPa and their amplitude is 25.0 MPa. A stopped, suspended carriage still loads the link at 98.1 MPa. Unloading the carriage fully between trips would introduce another turning point. The original two-level description would no longer define every cycle experienced by the link.

Convert a repeated block into a fatigue estimate

Suppose tests at the applicable stress ratios give lives of \(8.0\times10^5\) cycles for ordinary loaded trips and \(1.2\times10^5\) for heavier trips. One representative block contains 200 ordinary and 30 heavy cycles. Palmgren–Miner averaging assigns the used fraction \(n_i/N_i\) to each level. If a block is repeated \(B\) times, its predicted accumulated damage is

\[D=B\sum_i n_i/N_i=B(200/800000+30/120000).\]

The conventional estimate \(D=1\) gives 2000 blocks. The smaller count of heavy cycles contributes half of the estimated damage. This calculation can help decide which trips to instrument. It assumes that sequence effects and interactions between levels can be neglected. Changing the order of the same cycles would leave this prediction unchanged. An overload that yields the link, changes its residual stress or damages a connection calls for evidence beyond this linear rule. Record the peak event separately when preparing the input for a component calculation.

Combine alternating stress with a tensile mean stress

A duty histogram can contain cycles with the same amplitude but different tensile mean stresses. Applying a fully reversed fatigue curve to every cycle ignores this distinction. A common preliminary engineering construction at a selected life is the modified Goodman line. Let \(S_f(N)\) be a component’s fully reversed fatigue strength at that life and \(S_u\) its tensile ultimate strength. Draw the line joining \((\sigma_m=0,\sigma_a=S_f)\) and \((\sigma_m=S_u,\sigma_a=0)\) on a mean–alternating stress diagram. Points on the line satisfy

\[\frac{\sigma_a}{S_f(N)}+\frac{\sigma_m}{S_u}=1,\]
\[\sigma_{a,\rm eq}=\frac{\sigma_a}{1-\sigma_m/S_u}.\]

The second expression gives the fully reversed amplitude assigned to the measured cycle by this interpolation. This is an empirical mean-stress correction, rather than a derivation of material behaviour from elasticity. Use component or specimen data appropriate to surface, size, loading mode, environment and stress concentration. Compressive mean stresses and regions near yield require their own treatment. If a local cycle produces plastic strain, an elastic stress-life calculation can be inappropriate.

If all stresses scale in proportion to an external load multiplier \(n_L\), the intersection of that load ray with the Goodman line gives

\[n_L=1/(\sigma_a/S_f+\sigma_m/S_u).\]

A fixed installation preload is different: it contributes to the mean but will not scale with a later service-load multiplier. Adding preload to a stress diagram and multiplying the whole state by a single factor would then misstate the available service-load increase.

Compare a raised preload with an increased cycle range

Consider a smooth tensile link with illustrative life-\(10^6\)-cycle strength \(S_f=180\,\mathrm{MPa}\), ultimate strength \(S_u=600\,\mathrm{MPa}\) and yield strength 360 MPa. Its measured stress cycles from 40 to 200 MPa. Mean and amplitude are 120 and 80 MPa; the Goodman sum is

\[80/180+120/600=0.644.\]

The model’s equivalent reversed amplitude is

\[80/(1-120/600)=100\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

The peak of 200 MPa is below yield. If the full cycle scales with service load, the fatigue-ray multiplier is 1.55, while first yield at the nominal peak gives

\[360/200=1.80.\]

Suppose instead that a 60 MPa assembly preload remains fixed and only the remainder scales. At the original operating point the service component then has a mean of 60 MPa and an amplitude of 80 MPa. Its fatigue boundary is \(n_L(80/180)+(60+60n_L)/600=1\), giving \(n_L=1.65\). The peak yield condition becomes \(60+140n_L=360\), or \(n_L=2.14\). Both multipliers differ from scaling everything because the preload has a different physical source. A further rise of fixed preload by 30 MPa at the original service range changes the equivalent reversed amplitude to \(80/(1-150/600)=106.7\,\mathrm{MPa}\). This comparison shows which load measurement must change if the aim is reducing fatigue demand rather than merely reducing the largest force in a test record.

The same diagram can represent a measured range with uncertainty. A sensor offset changes the reconstructed mean while leaving the alternating component unchanged. A gain calibration error generally changes both. Reporting only the highest gauge reading leaves these two uncertainty sources indistinguishable when estimating fatigue demand.

Allocate fluctuating and mean stress in a fatigue screen

Use a linear Goodman estimate \(\sigma_a/S_e+\sigma_m/S_u=1\) with \(S_u=600\) MPa. Also require the peak tensile stress \(\sigma_m+\sigma_a\) to remain below the 380 MPa yield scale. At each mean stress the smaller ordinate is the controlling screen.
