Sizing the leaves of a compliant translation guide

A pair of thin leaves can guide a carriage with very little sliding friction. Their stiffness, stress and parasitic motion stem from the same bent shape. Derive those links before choosing a thickness or stroke.

Enforce the root and platform boundary conditions

Consider two identical rectangular leaves connecting a fixed base to a rigid guided platform. Each leaf has length L, width b, thickness t and bending stiffness \(EI=Ebt^3/12\). Let the platform translate sideways by a small amount \(\delta\) without rotating. Ignore shear deformation, axial tension and clamp compliance in the linear bending solution. Along each unloaded span \(EIw''''=0\), so the transverse shape is a cubic polynomial. At x=0 displacement and slope vanish. At x=L impose \(w=\delta\) and \(w'=0\). With \(s=x/L\) the resulting shape and curvature are

\[w=\delta(3s^2-2s^3),\]
\[w''={6\delta\over L^2}(1-2s).\]

The curvature changes sign at midspan. Each leaf has opposite end moments and maximal bending strain at both ends. A root modeled as a free pin would remove an essential slope condition and give the wrong stiffness.

Find guide force, surface stress and secondary motion

The end shear magnitude is \(EI|w'''|=12EI|\delta|/L^3\) per leaf. Because both leaves impose the same displacement their forces add. At the outer fibre the strain magnitude is \((t/2)|w''|\). Hooke's law gives

\[F={24EI\over L^3}\delta,\]
\[|\sigma|_{\max}={3Et|\delta|\over L^2}.\]

For inextensible leaves free to shorten their projected span, the arclength exceeds the projection by approximately \(\tfrac12\int_0^L (w')^2 dx\). Substituting the cubic shape gives \(3\delta^2/(5L)\). This parasitic travel is second order and has the same direction for positive or negative displacement. Constraining that axial motion instead introduces an axial force and nonlinearity. At large stroke use an appropriate geometric model for both stress and stiffness.

A stiffness target can conflict with a stress target

Choose \(L=40\) mm, \(b=10\) mm, \(t=0.30\) mm and \(E=200\) GPa. For each leaf \(I=2.25\times10^{-14}\) m⁴. The two-leaf stiffness is 1688 N/m. At 1 mm stroke the required force is 1.69 N, peak stress is 112.5 MPa and the free projected shortening is 15 μm. This small axial travel can matter when the stage is used for optical alignment.

Doubling the leaf thickness to 0.60 mm multiplies stiffness by eight, so 13.5 N is needed at the same stroke. Peak stress doubles to 225 MPa because surface fibres are farther from the neutral axis. In contrast, doubling width doubles stiffness without changing surface stress at a specified displacement. At a specified force the comparison changes because the stiffer guide travels less. Always identify which quantity is prescribed before drawing a design conclusion.

Hold the requested stroke fixed and double the leaf thickness. Check the ratios of force, peak stress and shortening against their different powers of thickness. Then reduce stroke until the original peak stress is recovered.

Show the displacement-limited design

Solving the stress expression gives \(\delta=0.80\) mm. Force is \(1687.5(0.0008)=1.35\) N. The end moment magnitude is \(EI(6\delta/L^2)=0.0135\) N m. The leaf end shear is 0.675 N; multiplying it by half the span gives the same end-moment magnitude. The predicted projected shortening is \(3(0.0008)^2/[5(0.040)]=9.6\) μm. Local clamp stress concentrations require the actual corner geometry.
