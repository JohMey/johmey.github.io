Closing a flexure-guided fixture with a screw drive

A bench fixture moves a specimen with a two-leaf guide and a square-thread screw. Determine the guide stroke, the actuator torque and the positioning error that a remote scale can miss. The results describe a nominal small-displacement design.

Problem sheet 7 · Integrative project

1 · Determine the allowable stroke from surface stress

Two identical fixed-guided leaves have \(L=40\) mm, \(b=10\) mm, \(t=0.30\) mm and \(E=200\) GPa. Keep peak nominal bending stress at or below 90 MPa. Find the stroke, total guide force and parasitic axial shortening. Distinguish this guide force from the specimen load.

Relate leaf curvature to force and stroke

With fixed end slopes the cubic shape gives \(\sigma_{\max}=3Et\delta/L^2\), hence \(\delta=0.800\) mm. Since \(I=bt^3/12=2.25\times10^{-14}\) m⁴, the two leaves have \(24EI/L^3=1688\) N/m stiffness and require 1.35 N at that stroke. Their projected length change is \(3\delta^2/(5L)=9.6\) μm. The drive must supply the separately specified specimen load in addition to this restoring force.

Check peak bending stress at 0.80 mm stroke

2 · Size nominal torque and travel

At the end of the stroke the specimen pushes back with 150 N. The screw has mean diameter 16 mm, lead 4 mm, friction coefficient 0.12 and no bearing or collar loss. Calculate the total axial load, screw torque, number of revolutions and lowering-torque sign.

Unwrap the thread at its mean diameter

Total axial demand is 151.35 N. With \(t_\lambda=4/(\pi16)=0.07958\), the required torque is \((151.35)(0.008)(t_\lambda+0.12)/(1-0.12t_\lambda)=0.244\) N m. The stroke requires \(0.8/4=0.20\) revolutions or 72°. The external lowering torque is positive, about 0.0485 N m. Under the assumed friction a downward axial load alone does not maintain steady descent. The chapter study uses loads down to 200 N; use its 800 N reference torque and scale by \(151.35/800\) because the torque law is linear in load.

Check the raising-to-lowering torque relation at 4 mm lead

3 · Locate error at the specimen

The position scale is mounted 60 mm above the specimen contact axis. A changing pitch of 80 μrad accompanies closure. Estimate the longitudinal scale-to-contact position difference and compare it with a ±3 μm measurement requirement. Does the 9.6 μm flexure shortening necessarily add directly to this error?

Resolve error vectors on the measurement axis

The Abbe difference is \(60\ \mathrm{mm}\times80\times10^{-6}=4.8\) μm. A 3 μm bound demands an offset at most 37.5 mm at this pitch, or pitch at most 50 μrad at the original offset. The flexure's parasitic motion is along the leaf length while commanded translation is transverse. It adds directly only if the measurement axis has a component along that motion. A second scale or surface-tilt measurement can separate translation from rotation.

4 · Compare a two-start thread at equal pitch

Replace the screw by a two-start version at the same 4 mm pitch. Keep the mean diameter and friction. Predict revolutions, raising torque and change in backdrivability. What independent measurement would help near the transition?

Recompute the helix and friction terms

Lead is now 8 mm, so the motion takes 0.10 revolution. The tangent of the helix angle is 0.1592, giving a nominal raising torque of about 0.345 N m. Since 0.1592 exceeds friction 0.12, the lowering torque is negative. A holding drive must oppose backdriving under the sliding model. Static breakaway friction may differ, so measuring breakaway and running torque separately at known axial load distinguishes the two conditions.
