Resolve gear forces

An 18-tooth pinion of module 4 mm drives a 54-tooth external gear. Input torque is 90 N m at 900 rpm. Use a pressure angle of 20°. Find output speed, ideal output torque, both pitch diameters and the radial force on either shaft. What part of the bearing load would a torque-only analysis omit?

Draw the pitch circles and the force normal

Pitch diameters are 72 and 216 mm. The output turns at 300 rpm in the opposing direction, with ideal torque 270 N m. The tangential force is

\[90/0.036=2500\,\mathrm N.\]

The radial force is \(2500\tan20^\circ=910\,\mathrm N\), and the normal resultant is 2660 N. A drawing showing only torque misses both transverse forces feeding shaft bending and bearing reactions.

Find the least favourable fit

A shaft spans 50.028–50.040 mm, and its bore spans 50.005–50.016 mm. The combined radial compliance is 0.40 micrometres/MPa. The overlap is 30 mm and friction coefficient 0.10. Find pressure limits and minimum torque capacity. Is a demand of 160 N m supported by every allowed pair in this model?

Keep track of radius and diameter

Diametral interference ranges from \(28-16=12\) to \(40-5=35\) micrometres. Radial mismatches are 6 and 17.5 micrometres and pressures 15 and 43.75 MPa. With N and mm the limiting torque at minimum pressure is

\[\pi(0.10)(15)(30)(50^2)/2=176715\,\mathrm{N\,mm}=176.7\,\mathrm{N\,m}.\]

The smallest nominal capacity exceeds the demand by about 10%. This margin is sensitive to error in the friction coefficient.

Connect a gear stage to a friction joint

A compound reducer uses tooth pairs 20:50 and 24:48, with both meshes external, each 97% efficient. Its input runs at 1500 rpm and supplies 3 kW. Find output speed, direction and useful output torque. If a fit of radius 20 mm supports at most 6000 N resultant surface friction and also transmits a 4000 N axial force, can it transmit that torque?

Compare drive output with remaining surface capacity

The total reduction is \(2.5\times2=5\); the output turns at 300 rpm in the input direction. Useful output power is \(3000(0.97)^2=2823\,\mathrm W\), hence

\[T=2823/[2\pi(300)/60]=89.85\,\mathrm{N\,m}.\]

Remaining tangential force is \(\sqrt{6000^2-4000^2}=4472\,\mathrm N\), giving 89.44 N m. The joint falls just short even before an allowance for manufacturing and friction scatter.

Trace the power when both the sun and ring move

A lossless test gearbox has \(N_s=30,\ N_r=90\). The sun runs at \(+900\,\mathrm{rpm}\) with \(T_s=+8.00\,\mathrm{N\,m}\), and a speed-controlled ring runs at \(-120\,\mathrm{rpm}\). Determine the carrier speed, the other two torques and which ports supply or receive power.

Show the worked solution

The carrier speed is

\[[30(900)+90(-120)]/120=135\,\mathrm{rpm}.\]

The torque proportions are \(T_c=-8(120/30)=-32.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\) and \(T_r=+24.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). Multiplying each torque by its signed speed in rad/s gives

\[P_s=+754.0\,\mathrm W,\ P_r=-301.6\,\mathrm W,\ P_c=-452.4\,\mathrm W.\]

The sun supplies all the power, and both the ring drive and carrier load receive it. A positive torque on a member rotating negatively represents negative power to the gearset.

Can the available hub length carry the specified steady torque?

A pump hub must transmit 240 N m on a 50.0 mm shaft. Its parallel key has width 14.0 mm and usable hub-side flank height 3.80 mm. The mean shear allowable is 45 MPa and the bearing allowable 80 MPa. Find the minimum effective length and the maximum torque if only 30.0 mm of effective engagement fits.

Show the worked solution

The tangential force is

\[2(240000)/50=9600\,\mathrm N.\]

The shear requirement is

\[9600/(14\times45)=15.24\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

The bearing requirement is

\[9600/(3.80\times80)=31.58\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

The available 30.0 mm falls short. Inverting both checks at that length gives shear capacity \(45(14)(30)(25)=472500\,\mathrm{N\,mm}\) and bearing capacity \(80(3.80)(30)(25)=228000\,\mathrm{N\,mm}\). The limiting torque is 228 N m. A longer nominal key only helps if it increases effective flank engagement.
