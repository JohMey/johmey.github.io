A stopped flywheel

A flywheel of inertia 0.80 kg m² initially turns at 60 rad/s and receives a constant braking torque of 12 N m. Find stopping time, heat and total revolutions during braking. If the same stop is repeated every minute, what is the cycle-average heat load?

Reveal the worked solution

Angular deceleration is \(12/0.80=15\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}\), so stopping takes 4.0 s. Heat equals the initial kinetic energy \(0.5(0.80)(60)^2=1440\,\mathrm J\). Mean speed is 30 rad/s and travel is 120 rad, or 19.1 revolutions. One stop every 60 s averages 24 W despite the much larger power during a stop.

Two faces, two pressure assumptions

A clutch has two active faces, 30 and 90 mm inner and outer radii, axial clamp 800 N, and friction coefficient 0.25. Find torque capacities for uniform wear and uniform pressure. A drive requires 25 N m. How does the choice of model affect the result?

Reveal the worked solution

Uniform wear gives \(r_e=60\,\mathrm{mm}\) and torque \(2(0.25)(800)(0.060)=24.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). Uniform pressure gives \(r_e=(2/3)(90^3-30^3)/(90^2-30^2)=65\,\mathrm{mm}\), yielding 26.0 N m. Only the pressure model nominally reaches the required torque. Wear distribution and tolerances matter for such a small margin.

Infer a pressure gradient from a flow

A slider locally has oil-film thickness 0.15 mm, viscosity 0.040 Pa s, lower-surface speed 2.0 m/s and measured flow per width \(1.20\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}\). Find the pressure gradient and its sign. In a second region the gap is 0.10 mm at the same flow. Is the pressure still rising?

Reveal the worked solution

Use \(dp/dx=12\mu(Uh/2-q)/h^3\). The first dragged flow is \(1.50\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}\), giving \(+4.27\times10^6\,\mathrm{Pa/m}\). At the smaller gap the dragged flow is \(1.00\times10^{-4}\), giving \(-9.60\times10^6\,\mathrm{Pa/m}\). The pressure maximum lies between the two regimes in a smooth converging film.
