A tolerance band on preload

A joint has stiffnesses \(k_b=200\) and \(k_m=600\) kN/mm and is preloaded to \(18\pm2\) kN. The external separating force cycles from 2 to 12 kN. Find the minimum residual clamp, maximum bolt force and alternating stress for a stress area of 36 mm². Find the smallest separation load.

Use the appropriate end of the tolerance interval

The bolt load fraction is 0.25. The least residual clamp is

\[16-0.75(12)=7\,\mathrm{kN}.\]

The largest bolt force is

\[20+0.25(12)=23\,\mathrm{kN}.\]

For any fixed preload within the band, the stress amplitude is

\[0.25(12-2)1000/(2\times36)=34.7\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

The lowest preload separates first, at \(16/0.75=21.33\,\mathrm{kN}\), above the ordinary maximum.

Convert a load block into bearing hours

A ball bearing turns for 15 min at 800 rpm under radial load 3 kN, then 10 min at 1200 rpm under 5 kN. Find the equivalent radial load, the basic life in hours for \(C=22\,\mathrm{kN}\), and the static ratio for a separate stationary load \(P_0=8\,\mathrm{kN}\) and \(C_0=12\,\mathrm{kN}\).

Count revolutions before averaging

Each interval contains 12,000 revolutions. Thus \(P_{\rm eq}^3=(3^3+5^3)/2=76\,\mathrm{kN^3}\), hence \(P_{\rm eq}=4.236\,\mathrm{kN}\). Life is \(22^3/76=140.1\) million revolutions at cycle-average speed \(24000/25=960\,\mathrm{rpm}\), or about 2432 h. For the separate stationary event, \(C_0/P_0=1.50\). A stationary overload adds no revolutions but can indent the races.

Two bearing seats warm by different amounts

A 0.50 m steel shaft warms by 55 K while its aluminium housing between the support planes warms by 20 K. Use linear expansions 12 and 23 micrometres per metre per kelvin. Determine relative growth. Does 0.08 mm axial travel accommodate it? Estimate the force after that travel is exhausted if the equivalent axial stiffness is 110 kN/mm.

Compare travel with differential growth

The shaft lengthens by 0.330 mm and the housing by 0.230 mm. Thus one end must accommodate 0.100 mm relative to the locating end. The available travel leaves 0.020 mm incompatible. In the stated linear idealisation the extra axial force magnitude is \(110(0.020)=2.2\,\mathrm{kN}\). Changing the locating scheme or the operating clearances changes this reaction.

Infer a contact load from an optical footprint

An optical instrument resolves a circular contact radius of \(0.150\) mm, with a radius uncertainty of ±2%. A 10 mm radius sphere and a flat have effective modulus \(115\) GPa. Estimate load and peak pressure from the footprint. How do the two relative uncertainties differ if the elastic model and other inputs are exact? A numerical pressure map gives

\[M_2/F=9.00\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m^2}.\]

Is that result consistent with the optical radius?

Worked solution

Invert the Hertz law to obtain

\[F=4E^*a^3/(3R^*)=51.75\,\mathrm{N}.\]

The peak pressure is

\[2E^*a/(\pi R^*)=1.098\,\mathrm{GPa}.\]

The ratio of peak to mean pressure is \(3/2\), which also follows by integrating the assumed profile.

Since the inferred load scales as \(a^3\), its first-order relative uncertainty is about 6%. The exact upper factor is

\[1.02^3=1.061208.\]

Peak pressure scales linearly with \(a\), giving 2%. Treating the reconstructed force and measured area as independent error sources would count the same radius uncertainty twice. The numerical moment implies a radius of √(2.5 × 9.00 × 10⁻⁹) = 0.150 mm. Agreement of these independent radius estimates tests the shape assumption involved in converting a pressure moment into an edge position.

Measure contact-law exponents from two solutions

Fit power laws \(F\propto d^p\) and \(a\propto d^q\) locally from the two runs at 210 GPa. At \(d=25\) µm the normal force is 2060.2 N and the inferred contact radius 0.5033 mm. At 12.5 µm they are 714.5 N and 0.3481 mm. Derive both exponents, compare them with Hertz theory, and name an independent numerical check.

Work through the solution

Divide the two power laws to eliminate their unknown prefactors. Taking logarithms gives

\[p=\ln(714.5/2060.2)/\ln(1/2)=1.528,\]
\[q=\ln(0.3481/0.5033)/\ln(1/2)=0.532.\]

The elastic half-space predicts \(3/2\) and \(1/2\). Two observations fit two power laws but cannot tell whether the exponents vary with approach. Repeat with a finer contact mesh and a third approach. Check the force integral and the radius from the pressure distribution separately.
