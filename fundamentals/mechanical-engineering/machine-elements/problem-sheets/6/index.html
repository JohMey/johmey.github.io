One circular weld

A circular line weld of radius 25 mm and effective throat 3 mm carries an in-plane force of 2.0 kN whose line is offset 60 mm from its centre. Determine direct shear flow, moment-induced shear flow and largest resulting throat stress. Locate a point at which the components add.

Reveal the worked solution

Direct flow is \(2000/(2\pi25)=12.73\,\mathrm{N/mm}\). With \(J_L=2\pi r^3\), moment flow is \(M/(2\pi r^2)=2000(60)/(2\pi25^2)=30.56\,\mathrm{N/mm}\). At the point where the moment-induced tangent has the same direction as the applied force, the maximum is \((12.73+30.56)/3=14.43\,\mathrm{MPa}\); at the diametrically opposite point the flow components oppose.

Link process drift to yield

A clearance has mean 0.18 mm and independent contributing standard deviations 0.030 and 0.040 mm. Assuming a normal distribution, find its standard deviation and estimated fraction below zero. One process drifts so the mean clearance falls to 0.12 mm. What is the new fraction?

Reveal the worked solution

The combined standard deviation is \(\sqrt{0.03^2+0.04^2}=0.050\,\mathrm{mm}\). Initially zero lies 3.6 standard deviations below the mean, giving probability about 0.000159 or 0.0159%. After the shift it is only 2.4 standard deviations below the mean, giving about 0.00820 or 0.820%. An unchanged process spread can conceal a large change in assembly yield.

A shared supply

Two independent pumps each have mission survival probability 0.95 conditional on an operating shared power supply. Either pump alone handles the full demand. The supply survives the mission with probability 0.98. Find system survival with one and with two pumps. What upper bound holds even with many such pumps?

Reveal the worked solution

With one pump the survival probability is \(0.98(0.95)=0.931\). With both, survival conditional on the supply is \(1-(1-0.95)^2=0.9975\), giving total \(0.98(0.9975)=0.97755\). Increasing the number of independent pumps can push their conditional survival towards unity, but the system probability remains bounded by the single supply’s 0.98.

Follow power through a belt and its supporting shaft

A pulley of pitch radius 0.10 m transmits 4.00 kW at 1200 rpm. Its belt has 180° wrap and coefficient of friction 0.30. Treat the two spans as parallel and the pulley as overhung 60 mm outside a bearing. Find the minimum pretension, approximate root bending moment, and von Mises stress in a solid shaft of diameter 25 mm. Neglect keyway concentration and shaft weight.

Worked solution

The shaft speed is 125.66 rad/s. The torque is 31.83 N m, requiring T₁−T₂ = 318.3 N. At impending slip T₁/T₂ = exp(0.30π) = 2.566, so T₂ ≈ 203.3 N and T₁ ≈ 521.6 N. The mean span tension is about 362 N. This is the minimum common pretension in the elementary equal-extension belt model.

The net radial pull in this parallel-span geometry is their sum, approximately 725 N. Its 0.060 m lever arm gives 43.5 N m bending moment. Taking the difference of tensions here would confuse the torque-producing tangential resultant about the pulley axis with the transverse force on the shaft.

At the surface the nominal bending stress 32M/(πd³) is 28.4 MPa and the torsional shear 16T/(πd³) is 10.4 MPa. The combined equivalent stress is √(σ²+3τ²) ≈ 33.6 MPa. A shaft rotating under a stationary transverse load experiences alternating bending even when this static equivalent value is well below yield. A fatigue decision requires endurance, surface finish and notch information.
