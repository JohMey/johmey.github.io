A return spring at its working point

A close-coiled steel spring has wire diameter 3 mm, mean coil diameter 21 mm, seven active turns and \(G=79\,\mathrm{GPa}\). Find its rate, deflection and stored energy at 50 N. Calculate peak shear using the Wahl factor.

Reveal the worked solution

The rate is \(79000(3^4)/[8(21^3)(7)]=12.34\,\mathrm{N/mm}\), giving 4.05 mm deflection and \(50(0.00405)/2=0.101\,\mathrm J\). With \(C=7\), \(K_w=1.213\). Corrected shear is

\[1.213[8(50)(21)/(\pi3^3)]=120\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

Combine two different springs

Springs with rates 18 and 30 N/mm are installed with negligible preload. Determine equivalent rate and travel of each at total force 90 N when in series. Then put them in parallel and find force carried by each at a 3 mm common compression.

Reveal the worked solution

In series the same force acts in both. Their compressions are \(90/18=5.0\,\mathrm{mm}\) and \(90/30=3.0\,\mathrm{mm}\); effective rate is

\[90/8=11.25\,\mathrm{N/mm}.\]

In parallel the common deflection gives 54 and 90 N, a total 144 N and equivalent rate 48 N/mm. The active constraint is equal force in the first layout and equal travel in the second.

A reduction in wrap

A flat belt operates at 6 m/s with total span tension 600 N and friction coefficient 0.28. Estimate maximum power at a 180° wrap and again at a 120° wrap. Assume fixed sum of span tensions and negligible centrifugal tension.

Reveal the worked solution

At limiting ratio \(r=e^{\mu\beta}\), the tension difference is \(600(r-1)/(r+1)\). The respective ratios are 2.410 and 1.798, giving differences 248.1 and 171.1 N. The corresponding maximum powers are 1.49 and 1.03 kW. The force pressing the shaft can remain similar even as the available power limit falls.
