1. Select damping from a peak

A unity-static-gain second-order system has no zeros. Specify 10% overshoot and first peak at 1 s after a step. (a) Derive damping ratio and damped frequency. (b) Find the desired characteristic polynomial. (c) State which prediction fails if a numerator zero is added and the poles are held fixed.

Start with the logarithm of the overshoot

The positive logarithmic decrement to the first peak is \(-\ln(0.10)=2.303=\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\), giving \(\zeta=0.591\). The damped frequency is \(\omega_d=\pi\) rad/s, and the exponential decay rate is \(2.303\) s⁻¹. The poles are \(-2.303\pm3.142i\), so the monic polynomial is \(s^2+4.605s+15.17\). A zero changes the residues and the step shape. The poles still give the natural frequencies and decay rates, but first-peak time and overshoot need recalculation.

2. Three time constants and one gain

For \(L=g/[s(s+1)(s+3)]\) under unity negative feedback, (a) derive the closed-loop characteristic polynomial, (b) find the positive gain range for stability and the crossing frequency, and (c) compare the pole result with the open-loop frequency response at the crossing.

Locate the imaginary-axis pair

The polynomial is \(s^3+4s^2+3s+g\). The first column of the Routh array is \(1,4,(12-g)/4,g\); all signs agree for \(0\lt g\lt12\). At \(g=12\) factor the polynomial as \((s+4)(s^2+3)\). The crossing frequency is \(\sqrt3\) rad/s, with the third pole still at \(-4\). Substituting \(s=i\sqrt3\) in the open-loop denominator gives \(-12\), so \(L=-1\) at the same gain and frequency.

3. Delay on a positioning stage

With \(L=2/[s(1+s/2)]\), (a) find the gain crossover, (b) estimate the delay that would use all phase margin, and (c) evaluate the remaining margin after including 0.10 s delay. Count angles consistently.

Convert delay to a phase at crossover

Writing \(x=\omega_c^2\) gives \(x(1+x/4)=4\), so

\[\omega_c=\sqrt{-2+2\sqrt5}=1.572\,\mathrm{rad/s}.\]

The phase margin is \(90^\circ-\arctan(1.572/2)=51.8^\circ\), or 0.905 rad. A pure delay leaves the crossover magnitude fixed, so the first crossing occurs at about \(0.905/1.572=0.576\) s. A 0.10 s delay costs 0.157 rad or 9.0°, leaving approximately 42.8°. This calculation assumes the given loop is otherwise accurately modelled.

Assign a repeated pole and check a holding-force constraint

A second stage has \(m=1.0\,\mathrm{kg}\),

\[c=2.0\,\mathrm{N\,s\,m^{-1}},\]
\[k=6.0\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}.\]

Select the PID gains for poles at \(-2,-2,-5\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\). A constant \(+0.50\,\mathrm N\) force then acts on the stage while its position command is \(+0.020\,\mathrm m\). Determine the holding force and whether an actuator limited to \(\pm0.30\,\mathrm N\) can achieve zero steady position error.

Show the worked solution
\[(s+2)^2(s+5)=s^3+9s^2+24s+20.\]

Subtract the passive coefficients to obtain \(K_d=7.0\,\mathrm{N\,s\,m^{-1}}\),

\[K_p=18\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}},\]
\[K_i=20\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}s^{-1}}.\]

At rest the plant requires \(u=kr-d=6(0.020)-0.50=-0.38\,\mathrm N\), outside the range. Even with \(u=-0.30\,\mathrm N\), the equilibrium is

\[y=(u+d)/k=0.0333\,\mathrm m.\]

The pole assignment describes unsaturated dynamics. Tuning the three gains cannot provide the missing holding authority.

Explain why a stable three-lag plant can acquire two unstable poles

Three identical first-order lags with one-second time constants give \(L(s)=K/(1+s)^3\), using seconds as the time unit. Find the frequency at which the locus reaches the negative real axis and the corresponding critical positive gain. For \(K=27\), determine the unstable pole count and the signed Nyquist winding.

Show the worked solution

The loop phase is \(-3\arctan\omega\), so \(-\pi\) is reached at \(\omega=\tan(\pi/3)=\sqrt 3\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\). The magnitude there is \(K/(1+3)^{3/2}=K/8\). The critical gain is 8. At \(K=27\), solve \((s+1)^3=-27\). The three roots are \(-4\) and \(0.5\pm i(3\sqrt3/2)\), so \(Z=2\). Since the three open-loop poles all lie at \(-1\), \(P=0\) and \(N=P-Z=-2\), two net clockwise turns. At the critical gain a conjugate pair lies on the imaginary axis and the winding through the critical point is undefined.

Identify a controller from a finite-time observation

A zero-initial-state, first-order proportional-feedback loop has plant static gain 2. A unit step gives error 0.11331 at six fitted closed-loop time constants. The fitted time constant is 2.22222 s. Infer the controller gain and the original plant time constant, retaining the residual exponential at the sample time.

Work through the solution

The first-order error is

\[e(t)=e_\infty+(1-e_\infty)e^{-t/\tau_c}.\]

Thus \(e_\infty=(0.11331-e^{-6})/(1-e^{-6})=0.11111\) to rounding. With negative unity feedback, \(e_\infty=1/(1+2K_p)\) gives \(K_p\simeq4.00\). The closed-loop denominator gives \(\tau_c=\tau_p/(1+2K_p)\), so \(\tau_p\simeq20.0\) s. Treating the sampled error as the asymptotic value would slightly underestimate gain.
