Modelling a plant and interpreting its signals

Use balances, local linearisation and block diagrams to decide which parameters a commissioning test actually measures.

Problem sheet 1 · Chapters 1 and 2

1. A step on a heated plate2. A tank drained by gravity3. A slow sensor on a fast drive

1. A step on a heated plate

A plate initially in equilibrium at 22 °C receives a further 40 W. Its final temperature is 32 °C and it reaches 28.32 °C after 50 s. (a) Estimate the incremental static gain and time constant. (b) Recover heat capacity and heat-loss conductance in a one-capacity model. (c) Describe a second experiment that could expose a temperature-dependent loss.

Recover the physical coefficients

Write \(C\dot\theta+G\theta=\Delta P\) about the initial equilibrium. The 10 K final increment gives \(K=0.25\) K/W and \(G=4\) W/K. The recorded increment is 63.2% of the final value, giving \(\tau=50\) s and \(C=\tau G=200\) J/K. Repeat a small equal step about a higher initial temperature with the same airflow. A change in incremental gain indicates a changed local loss slope; a second slow exponential could instead indicate another stored-energy state. One measurement at 50 s cannot distinguish these hypotheses.

2. A tank drained by gravity

An open tank of cross-section \(A=0.50\) m² discharges \(q_\mathrm{out}=c\sqrt H\) with \(c=0.020\) \(\mathrm{m}^{5/2}/\mathrm{s}\) and level \(H\) in metres. (a) Find the inflow at a 1 m operating level. (b) Derive the incremental transfer function and predict the rise for a sustained additional 0.002 m³/s. (c) Check the new equilibrium exactly and identify the sign of the approximation error.

Linearise the outflow at the operating level

The balance is

\[A\dot H=q-c\sqrt H.\]

At \(H_0=1\) m, \(q_0=0.020\) m³/s. The slope of the outflow is 0.010 m²/s, so \(\delta H/\delta q=1/(0.50s+0.010)\) in the Laplace domain. Its time constant is 50 s and its final incremental prediction is 0.20 m. The exact new level is

\[(0.022/0.020)^2=1.21\,\mathrm{m}.\]

The local model underestimates the rise by 0.01 m because the outflow curve becomes flatter as the level rises.

3. A slow sensor on a fast drive

The transfer functions from torque to speed and from speed to the recorded signal are \(P=2/(1+0.5s)\) and \(H=1/(1+2s)\). (a) Determine the transfer from torque to the recording and its DC gain. (b) For a unit step derive the recording in time. (c) Would the dominant time visible in this record be a good plant-only identification?

Distinguish dynamics by tracing the signal

The recording has transfer \(PH=2/[(1+0.5s)(1+2s)]\) and DC gain 2. Partial fractions for a unit step give

\[y_m=2[1-\tfrac43 e^{-t/2}+\tfrac13e^{-2t}].\]

Its value and slope are zero at switch-on, whereas physical speed has initial slope 4 in the chosen units. The late transient is dominated by the sensor's 2 s time constant. An independently calibrated sensor model or a faster sensor separates its effect from the 0.5 s plant response.
