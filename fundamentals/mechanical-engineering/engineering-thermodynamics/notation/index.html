Biot number

\[\mathrm{Bi}=hL_c/k\]

Here h is a surface heat-transfer coefficient and k is conductivity. A small Biot number supports a nearly uniform specimen temperature for the specified boundary condition.

Derivation · Thermal diffusion and transient surface cooling

Entropy generation

\[\dot S_{\rm gen}=\dot S_{\rm system}-\sum\dot Q_j/T_j-\dot S_{\rm mass,in}+\dot S_{\rm mass,out}\]

Use boundary temperatures at which heat actually crosses. All flows, storage and units must refer to the same control volume.

Derivation · Entropy, irreversibility and useful-work loss

Exergy destruction

\[\dot X_{\rm dest}=T_0\dot S_{\rm gen}\]

The environmental reference temperature enters in kelvin. Irreversibility destroys useful-work potential even when the total energy balance closes.

Derivation · Useful work and exergy destruction

Heat-exchanger effectiveness

\[\epsilon=\dot Q/[C_{\min}(T_{h,i}-T_{c,i})]\]

Heat-capacity rate is mass flow times specific heat for a single-phase stream. The smaller rate limits the maximum possible sensible heat transfer.

Derivation · Heat-exchanger effectiveness and finite capacity rates

Specific enthalpy

\[h=u+pv\]

Specific quantities are per unit mass. Enthalpy accounts for internal energy and flow work when mass passes through a control surface.

Derivation · Open systems, enthalpy and steady-flow devices

Vapour quality

\[x=m_v/(m_v+m_l)\]

This mass fraction applies in a two-phase equilibrium region. Mole fraction, mass fraction and fraction of volume occupied by vapour can be very different.

Derivation · Phase equilibrium and steam quality
