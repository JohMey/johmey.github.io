Distinguish a defining relation from a predictive law

At an impermeable wall heat crosses the fluid immediately adjacent to the solid by molecular conduction. Define \(h\) by equating that flux to the observable temperature difference. In a tube use the bulk mean temperature from the enthalpy flux. On an external plate use the free-stream temperature.

\[q''=-k\,\partial_nT|_w=h(T_w-T_b),\]
\[Nu_L=hL/k,\]
\[Re_L=UL/\nu,\]
\[Pr=\nu/\alpha .\]

Nondimensionalising the advective and diffusive terms of the thermal energy equation produces \(Re\,Pr\). Geometry, boundary conditions, momentum transport and entrance development decide the actual dependence of Nusselt number. The exponents fitted to turbulent data do not follow from dimensional analysis alone.

A heated smooth tube with developed turbulent flow

For a smooth, sufficiently long, fully developed turbulent tube with moderate property changes, a common first estimate for heating the fluid is \(Nu_D=0.023 Re_D^{0.8}Pr^{0.4}\). Use it here within its usual range, roughly \(Re_D\gtrsim10000\) and \(0.7\lesssim Pr\lesssim160\). Check that the physical setting and temperature convention match before comparing correlations. For laminar fully developed circular flow the exact constant-wall-temperature and constant-wall-flux limits instead give 3.66 and 4.36, respectively.

At \(Re_D=30000\), \(Pr=5\), \(D=0.020\) m, \(k=0.60\) W/(m K) and imposed \(q''=9000\) W/m², the turbulent estimate gives \(Nu=167.1\), \(h=5013\) W/(m² K) and \(T_w-T_b=1.80\) K. That temperature rise is small enough that sensor offsets matter. The following notebook fits one prefactor to deliberately perturbed synthetic readings and tests whether the residual size is compatible with thermometer precision.

OneSimulation experiment · Measured wall superheat decreases with flow rate in a heated tube

Run the notebook with OneSimulation 0.2.0, NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib. Change the input data and compare your run with the recorded result.

"""Estimate a turbulent tube coefficient using synthetic wall temperature readings."""
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from onesim.numerics.nonlinear import LeastSquares

reynolds = np.array([12000., 18000., 26000., 36000., 50000.])
q, diameter, conductivity, prandtl = 9000.0, 0.02, 0.60, 5.0
def superheat(coefficient, re):
    nusselt = coefficient*re**0.8*prandtl**0.4
    return q*diameter/(conductivity*nusselt)

readings = superheat(0.025, reynolds)+np.array([0.04,-0.04,0.02,0.01,-0.03])
fit = LeastSquares().solve(lambda c: superheat(c[0], reynolds)-readings,
                           [0.023], bounds=([0.005],[0.08]))
assert fit.success
coefficient = fit.x[0]
print(f"fitted prefactor = {coefficient:.5f}")
print(f"RMS temperature residual = {np.sqrt(np.mean(fit.fun**2)):.4f} K")
print(f"predicted rise at Re=30000 = {superheat(coefficient, 30000):.3f} K")

Changing the flow geometry changes the correlation

For a smooth isothermal plate with a laminar external boundary layer starting at the leading edge and moderate Prandtl number, use the area-mean value \(\overline{Nu}_L=0.664 Re_L^{1/2}Pr^{1/3}\). A local correlation at the trailing edge has half the prefactor: local and average heat transfer answer different measurement questions. For air with \(\nu=1.6\times10^{-5}\) m²/s, \(Pr=0.71\), \(k=0.026\) W/(m K), \(L=0.20\) m and \(U=2\) m/s, the mean result is \(h=12.18\) W/(m² K). At 4 m/s it becomes 17.22 W/(m² K).

For a still fluid by a vertical plate the motion instead follows from thermal expansion. Buoyant acceleration is of order \(g\beta\Delta T\). Balancing it against thermal and momentum diffusion supplies \(Ra_L=g\beta|T_w-T_\infty|L^3/(\nu\alpha)\). A vertical isothermal plate correlation across laminar and turbulent regimes is

\[\overline{Nu}_L=\left[0.825+\frac{0.387Ra_L^{1/6}}{[1+(0.492/Pr)^{9/16}]^{8/27}}\right]^2 .\]

With the same length, \(\Delta T=20\) K, \(\beta=1/300\) K⁻¹ and \(\alpha=2.25\times10^{-5}\) m²/s, \(Ra_L=1.45\times10^7\) and \(h=4.51\) W/(m² K). Evaluate properties consistently, usually at a representative film temperature. If both buoyancy and forced motion matter, their orientation can aid or oppose the flow.

What changes when the wall temperature is unknown?

For imposed heat flux in free convection, the temperature difference appears both in \(h\) and in \(q''=h\Delta T\). Solve the scalar nonlinear balance with updated properties as warranted. Treating \(h\) as independent of wall temperature suppresses the coupling driving the motion.

Exercises on correlation selection

Double the pipe velocity with constant properties inside the turbulent range and unchanged imposed heat flux. How far does the wall rise above the fluid?

Worked solution

The coefficient rises by \(2^{0.8}=1.741\). The 1.80 K rise falls to about 1.03 K. A fixed wall-temperature experiment would observe the opposite dependent variable, an increased heat rate.

A stationary vertical plate in air has a known 20 K excess temperature. Its height is doubled. May the original coefficient simply be reused?

Worked solution

Rayleigh number grows eightfold. Reevaluate the stated correlation at that Rayleigh number and divide the new \(Nu\) by the doubled length when recovering \(h\). The heat transfer area also changes if width stays fixed.
