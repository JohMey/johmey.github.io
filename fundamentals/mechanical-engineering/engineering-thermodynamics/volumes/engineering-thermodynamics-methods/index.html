Thermal processes and transport in engineering

Close energy and entropy balances around real processes. Use measured states and heat flows to separate component performance from modelling assumptions.

    Contents

    1. Staged compression with an imperfect intercooler

      A laboratory compressed-air supply has two compressor stages and an intercooler. Equal pressure ratios minimise work only under particular assumptions. Derive the optimum, compare it with a single-stage machine and identify which temperature and power measurements reveal imperfect cooling or unequal efficiency.

    2. How a gravity-drained condensate film limits heat transfer

      Saturated vapour condenses on a cool vertical plate. A liquid film thickens as it drains, adding thermal resistance toward the bottom. Derive the local heat-transfer coefficient from coupled momentum and energy balances, then determine which observations would invalidate the smooth-film model.

    3. Radiative exchange in a grey enclosure

      A thin radiation shield does not need to absorb all incoming radiation to reduce heat transfer. Multiple reflections connect every surface to every other visible surface. Build the radiosity equations, recover the two-plate result and infer what can be learned from a measured shield temperature.

    4. Periodic heating as a measurement of thermal diffusivity

      Two buried thermometers see the same applied surface temperature oscillation with different amplitudes and phases. Derive how a solid filters that oscillation, recover diffusivity independently from both observations and identify when a rear boundary or a poor sensor contact changes the inference.

    5. What temperature does a probe in a hot gas measure?

      A small bead in a duct exchanges energy with both the gas and the duct walls. Calculate the indicated temperature from an energy balance, infer the gas temperature from the reading, and test how emissivity changes the measurement.

    6. Where does the temperature of a reacting gas come from?

      A heater in an ideal cycle can supply a prescribed amount of heat. A steady combustor converts chemical enthalpy into sensible enthalpy. Compute a state from atomic balances and show where the complete-conversion model begins to overpredict its temperature.

    7. How can a flow measurement determine a wall temperature?

      Move from a heat-transfer coefficient supplied as data to one inferred from geometry and flow. Compare a turbulent pipe, a laminar external boundary layer and a plate cooled by buoyancy.

    8. Why can a heated surface suddenly become much hotter?

      Trace the pool-boiling curve of a saturated liquid. Compare changing a heater’s temperature with changing its electrical power and estimate the hydrodynamic scale at which nucleate boiling becomes unstable.
