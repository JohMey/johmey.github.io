Read a boiling curve as a family of states

Plot heat flux \(q''\) against excess wall temperature \(\Delta T_e=T_w-T_{sat}(p)\) for a saturated liquid at fixed pressure. Natural convection carries small heating rates. When active cavities support vapour embryos, rising bubbles renew liquid at the wall. Nucleate boiling heats the liquid much more effectively and the slope is usually steep. At the critical heat flux vapour structures begin to impede liquid return. The transition branch can have negative slope. Farther along, a continuous vapour film separates liquid from the surface and heat crosses a poorly conducting layer.

A boiling curve depends on wetting, surface finish, pressure, orientation and heating history. Saturation temperature gives the equilibrium temperature at a given pressure. It is insufficient to determine the local wall superheat or boiling branch. A condensing film and a boiling boundary involve the same latent heat but different interfacial dynamics.

Balance capillary and buoyant lengths

At a gravity-disturbed interface a pressure difference of order \(\sigma/\ell\) competes with a hydrostatic difference \((\rho_l-\rho_v)g\ell\). Their equality gives capillary length \(\ell_c=[\sigma/(g(\rho_l-\rho_v))]^{1/2}\). A vapour inertial stress of order \(\rho_v u_v^2\) matching the same pressure sets a velocity scale. Multiplication by \(\rho_v h_{fg}\) converts that speed to a latent-heat flux scale. A hydrodynamic model for saturated pool boiling on a large horizontal upward-facing plate gives the empirical/geometric coefficient \(C\simeq0.131\):

\[q''_{CHF}=C h_{fg}\sqrt{\rho_v}\,[\sigma g(\rho_l-\rho_v)]^{1/4}.\]

For saturated water near atmospheric pressure take \(h_{fg}=2.257\) MJ/kg, \(\rho_l=958\), \(\rho_v=0.598\) kg/m³ and \(\sigma=0.0589\) N/m. The scale predicts about 1.11 MW/m². This order-of-magnitude estimate pertains to a specific large-surface model, not a universal limit for small heaters or forced flow.

Explain the jump using the heater energy balance

Write \(C_A dT_w/dt=q''_{in}-q''_{out}(T_w)\) for heat capacity per area \(C_A\). Perturb a steady point by \(\delta T\) at fixed input. Then \(C_A\,d\delta T/dt=-q'_{out}\delta T\). A positive local slope suppresses a perturbation and a negative slope amplifies it. Consequently an imposed-power sweep can jump past much of the transition branch. A temperature-controlled experiment can in principle visit states that constant-power control cannot stabilize.

Does a target heat flux determine a unique wall temperature?

A horizontal line on a nonmonotone boiling curve can intersect more than one branch. Surface state, control mode and the direction of the preceding sweep decide which intersections can be reached.

Follow a flowing liquid from subcooling into a two-phase tube

For a uniformly heated circular duct, wall area per axial length is \(\pi D\) and the mass flow is \(G\pi D^2/4\) with mass flux \(G\). Neglect pressure change, kinetic-energy change, loss to surroundings and inlet vapour. A differential steady energy balance yields \(dh/dz=4q''/(GD)\). Define the equilibrium quality \(x_{eq}=(h-h_f)/h_{fg}\) at the local pressure. Negative values measure subcooling. A positive value describes the mass vapour fraction when both phases are in local thermal equilibrium.

\[z_{sat}=\frac{GD c_{p,l}(T_{sat}-T_{in})}{4q''},\]
\[x_{eq}(z)=\frac{4q''(z-z_{sat})}{GD h_{fg}} .\]

Wall bubbles may form before the bulk reaches saturation and condense in the colder core. Downstream, bubbly or slug flow can give way to an annular liquid film with droplets in a fast vapour core. Dryout of that film and departure from nucleate boiling in a subcooled region are distinct routes to a sharp rise in wall temperature. Orientation, mass flux, pressure, quality and heating history influence the transitions.

Use boiling and momentum scales before choosing a closure

The boiling number \(Bo_q=q''/(Gh_{fg})\) measures vapour generation relative to mass transport. The liquid-only Froude number \(Fr_{lo}=G^2/(\rho_l^2gD)\) compares inertia and gravity. The liquid-only Weber number \(We_{lo}=G^2D/(\rho_l\sigma)\) compares inertia and capillarity. Confinement number \(Co=D^{-1}\sqrt{\sigma/[g(\rho_l-\rho_v)]}\) measures capillary length relative to diameter. These definitions specify their reference density. A regime map may instead use superficial velocities \(j_g=Gx/\rho_v\) and \(j_l=G(1-x)/\rho_l\). Enter that map with its own orientation, geometry and definitions.

For a vertical water tube take \(D=16\) mm, \(G=300\) kg/(m² s), \(q''=42\) kW/m², 8 K inlet subcooling and representative 1 MPa saturation data \(\rho_l=887\), \(\rho_v=5.15\) kg/m³, \(h_{fg}=2.015\) MJ/kg, \(c_{p,l}=4.4\) kJ/(kg K), \(\sigma=0.042\) N/m. Bulk saturation occurs at 1.006 m. At 3 m the predicted quality is only 0.03464, yet \(j_g=2.018\) m/s and \(j_l=0.327\) m/s. The much smaller vapour density explains why a small mass fraction can dominate volumetric transport. The reference groups are \(Bo_q=6.95\times10^{-5}\), \(Fr_{lo}=0.729\), \(We_{lo}=38.7\) and \(Co=0.138\).

For a local illustrative closure suppose a validated superposition fit at this station supplies \(h_{lo}=4500\) W/(m² K), convective enhancement \(F=1.35\), pool reference \(h_{nb}=6500\) W/(m² K) and nucleate suppression \(S=0.60\). Then \(h_{tp}=Fh_{lo}+Sh_{nb}=9975\) W/(m² K) and the required superheat is \(42000/9975=4.21\) K. Those coefficients are supplied station data. Extending them through flow-regime change would require a validated local closure. A single-phase fit cannot predict film dryout.

At what distance does the simple uniform-pressure model first predict quality 0.10? What additional evidence would justify calling that station a dryout location?

Worked solution

The quality slope is \(4q''/(GDh_{fg})=0.01737\) per metre. Adding \(0.10/0.01737\) m to the saturation length gives 6.76 m. Dryout needs evidence of film depletion, measured wall-temperature excursion or a correlation validated for this pressure, mass flux, tube and heated length. A quality of 0.10 alone provides none of these.

Exercises on energy and stability

A saturated pool removes 200 kW/m² solely by evaporation. What is the associated mass flux of water vapour?

Worked solution

The evaporation rate is \(q''/h_{fg}=200000/2257000=0.0886\) kg/(m² s). The fraction of area covered by vapour cannot be inferred from energy alone.

Near a transition point \(q'_{out}=-4000\) W/(m² K) and \(C_A=2000\) J/(m² K). Predict the initial growth time of a small temperature error at fixed power.

Worked solution

The linear growth exponent is \(-q'_{out}/C_A=2\) s⁻¹. An initial deviation grows by a factor \(e\) in 0.5 s until nonlinear changes of the heat-removal law become significant.
