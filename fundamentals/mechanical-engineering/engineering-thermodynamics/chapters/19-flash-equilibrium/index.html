Finding the vapour fraction of an equilibrium flash

A mixture enters a separator at given overall composition, pressure and temperature. Some components prefer the vapour more strongly than others. Derive a scalar equation for the fraction that evaporates and test for a two-phase solution before iterating.

Connect composition to the equilibrium ratios

Let \(F=L+V\) be the total molar feed and liquid and vapour flow rates. Overall component fractions are \(z_i\), liquid fractions \(x_i\) and vapour fractions \(y_i\). At equilibrium the chemical potential of each species agrees across phases. In an ideal gas over an ideal liquid this reduces to Raoult's law \(y_i p=x_i p_i^{\rm sat}(T)\), so \(K_i=y_i/x_i=p_i^{\rm sat}(T)/p\). A larger K enriches that species in the vapour. For a real mixture K can depend on phase composition and require an outer property iteration. Here use fixed positive K values to isolate the material balance.

Eliminate the component flows

Define \(\beta=V/F\). Component conservation gives

\[z_i=(1-\beta)x_i+\beta y_i=x_i[1+\beta(K_i-1)].\]

Thus \(x_i=z_i/[1+\beta(K_i-1)]\). For both compositions to sum to one, enforce \(\sum_i(y_i-x_i)=0\), giving the Rachford–Rice equation:

\[R(\beta)=\sum_i{z_i(K_i-1)\over1+\beta(K_i-1)}=0,\]
\[R'(\beta)=-\sum_i{z_i(K_i-1)^2\over[1+\beta(K_i-1)]^2}.\]

The denominators are positive for \(0\le\beta\le1\). With at least one non-unity K of positive feed fraction, R decreases strictly, so at most one physical root exists. A positive R(0) and a negative R(1) bracket two phases. If \(R(0)\le0\) the stable fixed-K split is all liquid; if \(R(1)\ge0\) it is all vapour, with equality corresponding to incipient phase appearance. When every K is one this balance cannot specify \(\beta\). A bracketed numerical method preserves the meaningful interval even near a phase boundary.

Perform a binary flash and change the feed composition

Choose \(K_A=2,\ K_B=0.5,\ z_A=z_B=0.5\). Then

\[R(0)=0.25,\ R(1)=-0.25.\]

Multiplying by the two denominators gives \(0.25-0.50\beta=0\), hence \(\beta=0.50\). Liquid fractions are \(x_A=1/3,\ x_B=2/3\), and vapour fractions are

\[y_A=2/3,\ y_B=1/3.\]

For 10 mol/s feed each outlet carries 5 mol/s. The A balance reads \(5(1/3)+5(2/3)=5\) mol/s.

Increase \(z_A\) to 0.60 at the same T, p and K. The physical root becomes \(\beta=0.80\). The phase compositions remain at the same two endpoints; their proportions change to accommodate the new feed. Indeed the overall A fraction is

\[0.20(1/3)+0.80(2/3)=0.60.\]

At \(z_A=0.75\) the feed lies beyond the vapour endpoint. Then R(1) is positive, and searching outside the interval would produce a vapour fraction above unity. Report a single vapour phase instead. An adiabatic flash at unknown temperature would require a further enthalpy balance.

Start from equal feed fractions and increase the fraction of A until the root leaves the physical interval. Predict which pure-phase endpoint is reached. Compare residual signs at both endpoints before interpreting the reported phase.

Tutorial · determine whether an iteration is warranted

At an operating point take \(K_A=3,\ K_B=0.6\). Compute the liquid and vapour A fractions when both phases coexist. For \(z_A=0.30\), calculate \(\beta\). Repeat the phase test for \(z_A=0.10\) and explain why normalized hypothetical phase compositions can hide a failed balance.

Solve phase closure and the lever rule

Vapour closure gives \(3x_A+0.6(1-x_A)=1\), hence \(x_A=1/6\), \(y_A=1/2\). For overall fraction 0.30,

\[\beta=(0.30-1/6)/(1/2-1/6)=0.40.\]

At \(z_A=0.10\),

\[R(0)=0.10(2)+0.90(-0.4)=-0.16.\]

The feed remains liquid. Renormalizing an unphysical trial vapour composition could make it sum to unity while violating the original component and equilibrium equations. Check the physical endpoint signs first.
