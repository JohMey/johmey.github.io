Reading a vapour-compression refrigerator from its four enthalpies

A cooling machine circulates refrigerant through a compressor, condenser, valve and evaporator. Follow one kilogram around the circuit and see why compressor efficiency and liquid subcooling change the coefficient of performance by different mechanisms.

Write separate steady-flow balances on each component

State 1 is low-pressure vapour at compressor inlet. State 2 is high-pressure vapour at the discharge. At 3 the condenser delivers high-pressure liquid, which throttles to the evaporator pressure at 4. Neglect velocity and height changes and assume insulated compressor and valve. For the valve the flow energy balance gives

\[h_4=h_3.\]

For the other devices write positive magnitudes for useful cooling, input work and rejected heat:

\[q_L=h_1-h_4,\]
\[w_c=h_2-h_1,\]
\[q_H=h_2-h_3,\]
\[{\rm COP}_R={q_L\over w_c}.\]

Summing the component balances gives

\[q_H=q_L+w_c.\]

The COP may exceed one because a refrigerator transports heat using work. The maximum for two specified external reservoir temperatures is \(T_L/(T_H-T_L)\) in kelvins. No particular refrigerant's enthalpy table is implied in the following illustrative property data. In a design calculation determine the properties at consistent pressure, temperature or quality from one validated refrigerant source.

Get the compressor exit and valve exit by different paths

At specified discharge pressure define \(h_{2s}\) by the same entropy as state 1. The isentropic efficiency of the compressor is \(\eta_c=(h_{2s}-h_1)/(h_2-h_1)\). Rearranging gives

\[h_2=h_1+(h_{2s}-h_1)/\eta_c.\]

At the valve exit, if \(h_f<h_4<h_g\) at evaporator pressure, the vapour mass fraction follows from \(h_4=(1-x_4)h_f+x_4h_g\). Entropy increases through the valve even though enthalpy is constant. Assigning a constant entropy there would alter both flash quality and refrigeration effect.

\[x_4={h_3-h_f\over h_g-h_f},\]
\[\dot m={\dot Q_L\over h_1-h_3},\]
\[\dot W_c=\dot m\,{h_{2s}-h_1\over\eta_c}.\]

Keeping evaporation and condensation pressures fixed isolates the role of each state change. In an installed system the pressures, compressor map, flow and heat exchangers interact. A subcooler with a separate electrical load must also be included when evaluating a whole-system COP.

Evaluate a baseline and a subcooled variant

Take \(h_f=200,\ h_g=h_1=400,\ h_{2s}=430,\ h_3=250\) kJ/kg with \(\eta_c=0.75\). The actual outlet has \(h_2=440\) kJ/kg, cooling per kilogram is 150 kJ/kg and compressor input is 40 kJ/kg. The evaporator inlet is 25% vapour by mass, and COP is 3.75. At 6.0 kW cooling demand the flow is 0.0400 kg/s, compressor input 1.60 kW and rejected heat 7.60 kW.

At the same pressure levels and compressor inlet state, lower \(h_3\) by 10 kJ/kg before throttling. Valve-exit quality becomes \(40/200=0.20\), and 160 kJ/kg can be absorbed in the evaporator. COP becomes 4.00. For the specified cooling duty the flow falls to 0.0375 kg/s and compressor power to 1.50 kW. By comparison raising compressor efficiency from 0.75 to 0.85 with the original condenser exit reduces the work per kilogram to 35.29 kJ/kg and gives COP 4.25 at the original mass flow. One change acts on refrigeration effect and the other on compression work.

At unchanged compressor efficiency reduce the liquid enthalpy by 20 kJ/kg. Predict how much flash vapour is avoided for each kilogram passing the valve. Then reduce compressor efficiency and determine which of the two changes affects inlet quality.

Tutorial · diagnose a measured power excess

A rig with the original inlet and condenser enthalpies delivers 5.4 kW cooling while the measured refrigerant-side shaft input is 1.8 kW. Infer mass flow, actual outlet enthalpy and compressor isentropic efficiency. An electrical measurement gives 2.0 kW. What additional efficiency does that measurement quantify?

Close the component boundaries

The evaporator gives \(\dot m=5.4/150=0.0360\) kg/s. The shaft input gives \(w_c=1.8/0.0360=50.0\) kJ/kg and \(h_2=450\) kJ/kg. Hence

\[\eta_c=30/50=0.60.\]

With 2.0 kW electrical input, the upstream motor/drive delivers \(1.8/2.0=0.90\) of that power to the shaft. Shaft-based COP is 3.0, electrical COP is 2.7. Comparing measured COP values requires identifying which boundary each power meter encloses.
