A vessel containing liquid and vapour

At a specified saturation pressure use \(v_f=0.0011,\ v_g=0.900\,\mathrm{m^3/kg}\),

\[h_f=505,\ h_{fg}=2200\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}.\]

A 0.180 m³ vessel contains 1.0 kg in equilibrium. Determine quality, vapour volume fraction and specific enthalpy. Does its liquid mass or vapour mass dominate?

Reveal the worked solution

Quality is

\[(0.180-0.0011)/(0.900-0.0011)=0.1990.\]

The vapour occupies \(0.1990(0.900)=0.1791\,\mathrm{m^3}\), approximately 99.5% of the volume. Enthalpy is

\[505+0.1990(2200)=942.8\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}.\]

The liquid still supplies about 80.1% of the mass.

Establish a condensate flow

An air stream at 100 kPa total pressure has vapour partial pressure 2.80 kPa. Cooling reduces this to 1.20 kPa through condensation. For a dry-air flow of 0.80 kg/s determine both humidity ratios, hourly condensate flow and relative humidity after reheating to a state with saturation pressure 3.00 kPa.

Reveal the worked solution

The ratios are \(0.622(2.8)/(100-2.8)=0.01792\) and \(0.622(1.2)/(100-1.2)=0.00755\) kg/kg dry air. Condensate flow is

\[0.80(0.01792-0.00755)3600=29.9\,\mathrm{kg/h}.\]

Reheating at fixed composition and total pressure preserves vapour partial pressure, giving a final relative humidity of \(1.20/3.00=40\%\).

Predict an outlet humidity

Two streams at the same pressure carry 0.30 and 0.70 kg/s of dry air with humidity ratios 0.006 and 0.016 kg/kg dry air. Mixed air leaves at 100 kPa and a temperature with saturation pressure 2.60 kPa. Find outlet humidity ratio, vapour pressure and relative humidity. Verify that the assumption of no condensation is consistent.

Reveal the worked solution

Dry-air flow is 1.00 kg/s and water flow \(0.30(0.006)+0.70(0.016)=0.0130\,\mathrm{kg/s}\). The humidity ratio is 0.0130. Solving \(w=0.622p_v/(p-p_v)\) gives

\[p_v=100(0.0130)/(0.622+0.0130)=2.047\,\mathrm{kPa}.\]

Relative humidity is 78.7%, below saturation at this outlet temperature.

Compare free and isothermal expansion of the same model gas

One mole of gas obeys the van der Waals law with \(a=0.180\,\mathrm{Pa\,m^6\,mol^{-2}}\),

\[b=5.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3\,mol^{-1}},\]
\[c_v=25.0\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}.\]

It expands from 0.0030 to \(0.0060\,\mathrm{m^3\,mol^{-1}}\), initially at 320 K. Find its final temperature for insulated expansion into vacuum. Then calculate the heat needed for reversible isothermal expansion between the same volumes. Use \(R=8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}\).

Show the worked solution

The potential-energy contribution is \(0.180(1/0.0030-1/0.0060)=30.0\,\mathrm J\) for the mole. Free expansion has \(\Delta U=0\), hence

\[T_2=320-30/25=318.8\,\mathrm K.\]

Along the isotherm,

\[\Delta U=30.0\,\mathrm J.\]

The reversible work done by the gas is \(\int p\,dv=RT\ln[(v_2-b)/(v_1-b)]-a(1/v_1-1/v_2)\). The first law gives

\[Q=\Delta U+W=8.314(320)\ln(0.00595/0.00295)\simeq1.87\,\mathrm{kJ}.\]

Here the reservoir supplies heat while the gas performs boundary work. Equal volume endpoints alone do not determine the heat transfer.
