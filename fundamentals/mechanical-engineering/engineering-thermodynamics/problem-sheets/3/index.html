A wall with two films

A flat panel of area 2.0 m² and thickness 40 mm has conductivity 0.080 W/(m K). Inside and outside air are at 22 and 2 °C; film coefficients are 8 and 20 W/(m² K). Find the heat rate and both surface temperatures. Which design change would halve the rate at fixed film coefficients?

Combine the three resistances

The films give 0.0625 and 0.0250 K/W. The solid contributes \(0.040/(0.080\times2)=0.250\,\mathrm{K/W}\). Hence

\[Q=20/0.3375=59.26\,\mathrm W.\]

The inside face is \(22-59.26(0.0625)=18.30\) °C, the outside face \(2+59.26(0.025)=3.48\) °C. Halving the rate requires 0.3375 K/W of extra resistance, or 54 mm more of this material over the same area. Doubling just the existing insulation gives a smaller reduction.

Time to reach a handling temperature

A metal sphere of radius 12 mm cools in air at 20 °C from 140 to 50 °C. Its conductivity is 45 W/(m K), volumetric heat capacity 3.7 MJ/(m³ K), and \(h=35\,\mathrm{W/(m^2 K)}\). Assess the uniform-temperature approximation, estimate cooling time and determine the new time if the initial temperature becomes 80 °C.

Select a length and integrate

For a sphere \(V/A=R/3=0.004\,\mathrm m\), giving \(\mathrm{Bi}=35(0.004)/45=0.00311\). The time constant is

\[3.7\times10^6(0.004)/35=422.9\,\mathrm s.\]

The original excess falls from 120 to 30 K, taking \(\tau\ln4=586.2\,\mathrm s\). With a 60 K initial excess it takes \(\tau\ln2=293.1\,\mathrm s\). The time constant is unchanged under the given constant-property conditions.

Reproduce a centreline temperature

Two thick slabs have half-thicknesses 10 and 25 mm and the same diffusivity \(8.0\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}\). Each starts at the same uniform temperature and suddenly acquires the same prescribed surface temperatures on both faces. If the thinner slab reaches a given centreline temperature after 18 s, find the corresponding Fourier number and the time for the thicker slab. What if surface temperatures are replaced by convective boundary conditions?

Match dimensionless time and boundary data

The thinner slab gives

\[\mathrm{Fo}=\alpha t/L^2=1.44.\]

Matching it gives

\[t_2=18(25/10)^2=112.5\,\mathrm s.\]

The surfaces and initial profile have the same dimensionless temperatures. For convective cooling the dimensionless temperature history also depends on \(hL/k\), so equal Fourier number alone is insufficient. With the same material, the thicker slab needs a film coefficient \(10/25\) times that of the thinner specimen to match Biot number.

How much skin friction is consistent with a cooling requirement?

An isothermal face of area \(0.20\,\mathrm{m^2}\) must lose 150 W to a \(Pr\simeq1\) stream. The allowable wall excess is 15 K,

\[c_p=1000\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}},\]
\[U=8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.\]

Estimate the required area-averaged coefficient and mean skin-friction force. The balance reports 0.110 N, of which 0.040 N is support pressure drag. Estimate the achievable heat rate.

Show the worked solution

The target coefficient is

\[150/(0.20\times15)=50\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}K^{-1}}.\]

The corresponding wall stress is \(hU/c_p=0.400\,\mathrm{Pa}\), or \(0.0800\,\mathrm N\) on this face. After subtracting support drag the measured skin force is \(0.0700\,\mathrm N\), giving \(\bar\tau=0.350\,\mathrm{Pa}\) and \(\bar h\simeq43.75\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}K^{-1}}\). At 15 K excess the predicted heat rate is \(131.3\,\mathrm W\), roughly 19 W below target. Total balance force would overestimate cooling because pressure drag lacks the wall-gradient relation used in the analogy.
