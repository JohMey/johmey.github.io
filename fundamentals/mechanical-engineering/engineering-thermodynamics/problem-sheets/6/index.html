A balanced counterflow exchanger

Both stream capacity rates are 1.5 kW/K and conductance \(UA=3.0\) kW/K. Inlets are at 80 and 20 °C. Determine effectiveness, heat rate, both outlets and the local temperature difference. Explain why both end differences agree.

Reveal the worked solution

At \(C_r=1\), \(NTU=2\) and \(\varepsilon=2/3\). The exchanger transfers \((2/3)(1.5)(60)=60\,\mathrm{kW}\). Hot outlet is 40 °C and cold outlet 60 °C. At each end the hot stream exceeds the adjacent cold stream by 20 K. Equal capacity rates produce equal profile slopes when followed along the same physical direction.

Infer a required exchanger size

A hot stream of capacity rate 4 kW/K cools from 100 to 75 °C. A counterflow cold stream enters at 25 °C with capacity rate 2 kW/K. Calculate its outlet, the terminal differences and required \(UA\). What is the effectiveness?

Reveal the worked solution

The duty is \(4(100-75)=100\,\mathrm{kW}\), giving cold outlet \(25+100/2=75^\circ\mathrm C\). Terminal differences are 25 and 50 K. Their logarithmic mean is \((50-25)/\ln2=36.07\,\mathrm K\), so \(UA=2.773\,\mathrm{kW/K}\). The maximum duty is \(2(100-25)=150\,\mathrm{kW}\); effectiveness is 0.667.

A shield in a black enclosure gap

Large black parallel walls face one another at 400 and 300 K. A thin black shield is inserted midway using negligible conducting supports. Find unshielded flux, shielded flux and equilibrium shield temperature. Does equal spacing enter the result?

Reveal the worked solution

The unshielded flux is

\[\sigma(400^4-300^4)=992\,\mathrm{W/m^2}.\]

Each black gap contributes the same unit denominator, halving the flux to 496 W/m². The shield satisfies \(T_s^4=(400^4+300^4)/2\), giving \(T_s=360.3\,\mathrm K\). Equal spacing is unnecessary for two effectively infinite parallel plates whose view factors are unity.

Close the energy and exergy balances of a heat exchanger

An insulated counterflow exchanger cools water from 90°C to 60°C at 1.0 kg/s. Another water stream enters at 20°C and leaves at 50°C. Both have cₚ = 4.18 kJ/(kg K). Find the cold mass flow, thermal conductance UA, effectiveness and rate of exergy destruction at an ambient temperature of 293.15 K. Ignore pressure losses.

Worked solution

The hot side releases 125.4 kW. Conservation of energy gives a cold mass flow of 1.0 kg/s because the two temperature changes have equal magnitude. For counterflow, the end temperature differences are 90−50 = 40 K and 60−20 = 40 K. Their logarithmic mean is 40 K by continuity, giving UA = 3.135 kW/K.

The minimum heat-capacity rate is 4.18 kW/K, and the maximum possible duty is 4.18 × 70 = 292.6 kW. The effectiveness is 0.4286. As a check, NTU = UA/Cmin = 0.75 and for balanced counterflow ε = NTU/(1+NTU) = 0.4286.

With absolute temperatures, the entropy creation rate is 4.18[ln(333.15/363.15)+ln(323.15/293.15)] kW/K, about 0.0469 kW/K. Multiplying by ambient temperature gives about 13.7 kW of exergy destruction. Insulation eliminates the external heat flux, but finite temperature differences inside the exchanger still produce entropy.

Recover an exchanger capacity rate from outlet data

For a steady counterflow exchanger, the baseline hot stream cools from 350 to 309.84 K and the cold stream warms from 290 to 314.20 K. Hot-side power loss is 0.2168 W. Infer the hot heat-capacity rate, estimate the cold rate assuming equal powers, and infer \(UA\) from the stated NTU of 1.5. Why is the inferred cold rate approximate?

Work through the solution

The hot balance gives \(C_h=\dot Q/(350-309.84)=0.00540\) W/K. Setting the cold gain equal gives \(C_c=0.2168/(314.20-290)=0.00896\) W/K. Their ratio is 0.603, close to the input 0.60. The smaller capacity rate is the hot one, so \(UA={\rm NTU}\,C_{\min}=0.00810\) W/K. The finite mesh yields a small mismatch between the two streamwise estimates of heat rate. Using the measured cold-side power instead would directly test that balance.
