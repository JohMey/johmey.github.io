A gas under a spring-loaded piston

A closed gas expands from 0.010 to 0.016 m³. The resisting absolute pressure rises linearly from 160 to 280 kPa. Its internal energy increases by 0.900 kJ. Compute the boundary work and the heat entering the gas. If the ambient pressure is 100 kPa, how much of the boundary work is available after displacing the atmosphere?

Work, heat and the atmosphere

The area under the straight pressure–volume line is the mean ordinate times the volume interval. With quasistatic motion,

\[W=(160+280)(0.006)/2=1.320\,\mathrm{kJ}.\]

The first law with work positive out gives

\[Q=\Delta U+W=2.220\,\mathrm{kJ}.\]

Displacing the atmosphere consumes \(100(0.006)=0.600\,\mathrm{kJ}\). The remaining 0.720 kJ raises the piston or compresses the spring. Subtract atmospheric work when assessing useful work, but use absolute pressure for the closed gas energy balance.

Is the exit speed consistent?

Air enters a steady insulated nozzle at 360 K and 30 m/s, and leaves at 325 K. Take constant specific heat 1.005 kJ/(kg K). Find the exit speed and the exit area for 0.24 kg/s if the exit density is 1.08 kg/m³. Explain what changes if the nozzle loses 3 kJ/kg as heat at the same temperatures.

Balance and continuity

Per unit mass,

\[V_2^2=V_1^2+2c_p(T_1-T_2)=900+2(1005)(35)=71250\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}.\]

Thus \(V_2=266.9\,\mathrm{m/s}\). The area is

\[\dot m/(\rho_2 V_2)=8.33\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}.\]

Losing 3000 J/kg reduces \(V_2^2\) by 6000, giving 255.4 m/s. Energy that leaves the control volume as heat is unavailable to accelerate the stream.

Filling an evacuated rigid receiver

An insulated, initially evacuated tank is filled from a large air line at 300 K. The final mass is 0.80 kg. Assume perfect gas, \(c_p=1.005\), \(c_v=0.718\) kJ/(kg K), negligible kinetic energy in the final tank, no shaft work. Find the final energy and temperature. Why would a closed, constant-volume calculation yield a different interpretation?

Integrate the unsteady balance

Each kilogram carries inlet enthalpy 301.5 kJ/kg. Integrating over filling gives \(U_2=m_2 h_{\rm in}=241.2\,\mathrm{kJ}\) with a common zero-energy convention. Hence

\[T_2=h_{\rm in}/c_v=419.9\,\mathrm K.\]

The temperature rise accompanies the flow work carried in with the gas. A closed balance that follows the entering parcel must explicitly include the work that pushes the parcel into the receiver.
