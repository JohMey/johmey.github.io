Crack driving force and fracture assessment

From first principles

Relate crack extension to the energy released by elastic compliance

Consider a slowly loaded linear elastic specimen of thickness \(B\), applied force \(P\), measured displacement \(\delta\) and crack coordinate \(a\). Write \(C(a)=\delta/P\). The projected area convention is \(dA=B\,da\). On a fixed crack the linear loading curve stores strain energy equal to the triangle below its force–displacement graph:

\[U=\tfrac12P\delta=\tfrac12P^2C(a).\]

For a dead force held constant while the crack extends, the external force performs additional work \(P\,d\delta\). Subtract the load potential \(P\delta\) from the stored energy and differentiate with respect to area to obtain the driving force for extension:

\[\Pi=U-P\delta=-\tfrac12P^2C,\]
\[G=-\left.\frac{d\Pi}{dA}\right|_{P}=\frac{P^2}{2B}\frac{dC}{da}.\]

The units are J/m². An increase of compliance can free energy to create new surfaces and to dissipate energy near the crack. A material resistance measured under matching conditions is needed to predict onset. In linear elastic mode I, the local intensity relation \(G=K_I^2/E'\) connects this global energy measure with the near-tip field.

Background · Stress intensity
\[K_I=Y\sigma\sqrt{\pi a}\]

Opening-mode intensity has units of stress times the square root of length. Geometry and the definition of crack length are part of the calibration.

Derivation and applications

How machine compliance changes the energy available to a crack

A tensile fixture can release stored elastic energy as a crack grows even when the grips do not move. To quantify this, treat a specimen of thickness \(B\) with crack coordinate \(a\) and measured opening compliance \(C(a)=\delta_s/P\). Use a single projected crack-area increment \(B\,da\) consistent with the stated convention. Let an elastic testing machine in series have constant compliance \(C_m\), and assume quasistatic linear elastic loading with negligible dissipative processes outside the crack tip. At fixed total grip opening \(\Delta\) the force must change as the specimen compliance changes:

\[\Delta=P(C+C_m),\]
\[U=\tfrac12P^2(C+C_m)=\frac{\Delta^2}{2(C+C_m)} .\]

No further external work enters at fixed \(\Delta\). Differentiate the stored energy with respect to projected area. Since \(C_m\) is independent of the crack coordinate, only the specimen derivative appears:

\[G=-\frac1B\left.\frac{dU}{da}\right|_\Delta=\frac{\Delta^2}{2B(C+C_m)^2}\frac{dC}{da}=\frac{P^2}{2B}C'(a).\]

At a given instantaneous force this has the familiar force-based value, but its variation during crack extension depends on compliance control. Take the idealized measured relation \(C=ca^3\) over a limited range. Logarithmic differentiation at fixed imposed opening gives

\[\frac{1}{G}\frac{dG}{da}=\frac2a-\frac{6C}{a(C+C_m)} .\]

For constant fracture resistance, a decreasing driving force after slight growth favours arrest. In this compliance model it occurs when \(C_m<2C\). This local comparison assumes a small crack extension within the calibrated range and a material whose fracture resistance is not changing appreciably.

Worked example

Estimate driving force from a coupon measurement

At \(a=40.0\) mm take \(C=2.00\) micrometres/N, \(C_m=1.00\) micrometre/N, \(B=10.0\) mm and \(\Delta=0.600\) mm. The force is \(200\) N, and the local cubic slope is \(C'=3C/a=1.50\times10^{-4}\) N⁻¹. Substitution yields \(G=300\) J/m². The calculated logarithmic slope is \(-50.0\) m⁻¹, predicting about a \(5\%\) decrease for an initial additional millimetre of extension.

A sharp crack changes the question

An uncracked tensile coupon can often be assessed by comparing nominal stress with a yield stress. A sharp pre-existing crack concentrates the stress into a small region near its tip. Within linear elastic fracture mechanics the leading opening-mode field has magnitude proportional to \(K_I/\sqrt{2\pi r}\) at distance \(r\) ahead of the tip. The unbounded mathematical stress signals the limits of the elastic tip description. A material toughness measured under comparable conditions instead sets a threshold for crack extension.

A common representation is \(K_I=Y\sigma\sqrt{\pi a}\), where \(a\) is the specified crack dimension, \(\sigma\) is a stated remote stress, and \(Y\) describes the geometry and loading. For a central crack in a sufficiently wide plate \(a\) is the half-length and \(Y\) approaches one. For a short edge crack in a wide plate \(a\) is its depth and a common approximation is \(Y\approx1.12\). Finite width, bending or a different crack shape changes the factor.

Relate compliance to an energy release rate

In a linear test specimen let load \(P\), opening displacement \(u\), and compliance \(C(a)=u/P\) refer to the same load point. At fixed load the stored elastic energy is \(P^2C/2\). The external load does work \(P\,du=P^2\,dC\) as the crack extends. The released potential energy is the difference, \(P^2 dC/2\). For a through crack with one advancing front and specimen thickness \(B\), its projected area increases by \(B\,da\), giving

\[G=\frac{P^2}{2B}\frac{dC}{da}=\frac{K_I^2}{E'},\]
\[E'=E\ {\rm (plane\ stress)},\]
\[E'=E/(1-\nu^2)\ {\rm (plane\ strain)}.\]

The compliance argument explains how a distant load–displacement measurement can identify energy available at a crack. Define the crack area consistently: a symmetric specimen in which two tips advance together changes projected area twice as quickly for the same increment of half-length.

Infer a critical flaw size

Take a wide plate with an edge crack of depth 3.0 mm, tensile remote stress 160 MPa and illustrative applicable toughness \(K_c=35\,\mathrm{MPa\sqrt m}\). The estimate \(K_I=1.12(160)\sqrt{\pi(0.003)}=17.4\,\mathrm{MPa\sqrt m}\) uses metres for crack depth. Holding geometry factor and stress fixed, the critical depth is \(a_c=[35/(1.12\times160)]^2/\pi=0.0121\,\mathrm m\), about 12.1 mm.

At twice the stress this depth falls by a factor of four, to approximately 3.0 mm. Before using that extrapolation, check remaining net section, applicability of the wide-plate geometry and whether plasticity near the crack is small compared with crack and ligament dimensions. Toughness depends on temperature, constraint and loading rate. An inspection interval would additionally require a crack-growth law, a load history and a detection limit. The calculated critical depth describes one load state and cannot by itself predict the number of cycles remaining.

Interpret a crack-length uncertainty

At fixed remote stress and geometry factor a small relative error in crack depth gives approximately half that relative error in stress intensity, because \(dK/K=da/(2a)\). A 0.3 mm uncertainty on a 3 mm measurement therefore changes this estimated intensity by about five percent.

Integrate stable fatigue growth up to inspection

Fracture toughness characterises an instability at a given load. Damage tolerance also asks how long a detected crack takes to reach an inspection or retirement size under repeated loading. For illustration, suppose tests for the service material and stress ratio support a Paris relation \(da/dN=C(\Delta K)^m\) in its intermediate stable growth range. Use crack depth \(a\) in metres and intensities in MPa√m. The coefficient \(C\) must correspond to those units, stress ratio and environment. This empirical law generally changes near threshold and near final instability.

For constant stress range \(\Delta\sigma\), fixed geometry factor \(Y\) and exponent \(m=3\), substitute \(\Delta K=Y\Delta\sigma\sqrt{\pi a}\). Crack advance per cycle then scales as \(a^{3/2}\); the same stress history grows a larger crack faster. Separate variables between measured depth \(a_i\) and removal depth \(a_f\). Integrating yields

\[N=\frac{2(a_i^{-1/2}-a_f^{-1/2})}{C(Y\Delta\sigma)^3\pi^{3/2}} .\]

This prediction decreases to zero as the two depths approach each other. It scales with the inverse cube of stress range at fixed bounds. If the geometry factor changes appreciably with crack depth, retain \(Y(a)\) inside the integral. The maximum stress in the cycle is needed separately to compare \(K_{\max}\) with applicable toughness, because it is the range that drives the adopted fatigue law.

Compare two inspection thresholds on the same plate

Consider a wide plate with \(Y=1.12\), \(\Delta\sigma=80\,\mathrm{MPa}\), \(\sigma_{\max}=140\,\mathrm{MPa}\), \(m=3\), and illustrative \(C=2.0\times10^{-12}\,(\mathrm{m/cycle})/(\mathrm{MPa}\sqrt{\mathrm m})^3\). Suppose the measured growth-law range includes depths between 2 and 8 mm for this loading. At 2 mm,

\[\Delta K=1.12(80)\sqrt{\pi(0.002)}=7.10\,\mathrm{MPa\sqrt m};\]

at 8 mm it doubles to 14.2. The growth rate at the upper limit is consequently eight times the lower-limit rate.

The integral denominator is \(2.0\times10^{-12}(89.6)^3\pi^{3/2}=8.01\times10^{-6}\) in the stated units, and the numerator is

\[2(22.36-11.18)=22.36\,\mathrm{m^{-1/2}}.\]

The estimated interval is \(2.79\times10^6\) cycles. If the smallest reliably detected crack were 4 mm, the available interval to 8 mm would fall to \(2(15.81-11.18)/(8.01\times10^{-6})=1.16\times10^6\) cycles. Improving early detection has a measurable value in this model. At retirement depth \(K_{\max}=24.9\,\mathrm{MPa\sqrt m}\); compare that independently with the applicable toughness. Uncertainty in detection, loading, environment and growth coefficients would enter an actual inspection decision rather than treating this calculated mean growth interval as a guaranteed service interval.

A useful sensitivity check is to increase the entire stress range by ten percent while keeping the inspection depths and coefficient fixed. The Paris estimate falls by \(1/1.1^3=0.751\), reducing the 2.79 million-cycle interval to about 2.10 million cycles. If the maximum stress also rises by ten percent, retirement intensity increases by the same ten percent to 27.4 MPa√m. Those two changes affect different checks and cannot be combined into a single linear safety multiplier. A record of both stress range and stress maximum is therefore more informative than a peak-only reading for planning inspections.

Both quantities can be recovered from the two measured stress extremes if the counting convention and gauge reference are retained.
