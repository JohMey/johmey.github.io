Calibrating a compression fixture through three independent readings

A teaching rig uses a hydraulic piston to compress a circular polymer pad. Its readouts are a hoop strain gauge on an annular calibration disk and a displacement encoder on the crosshead. Predict what each instrument reads and diagnose a deliberately inconsistent run.

1 · Infer the applied force from a radial strain

Fluid at gauge pressure p acts independently on the inner circumference of a thin calibration disk and on a frictionless piston of area 100 mm². The disk has inner radius 20 mm, outer radius 40 mm, free flat faces and modulus 70 GPa. An outer circumferential gauge reads +9.52 microstrain. Find the pressure, piston force and inner hoop stress. Explain why the piston area and the disk area enter different steps.

Recover pressure and close the disk force balance

The outer radial traction is zero. In plane stress, the strain there gives

\[\sigma_\theta(b)=E\epsilon_\theta=0.6664\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

In the axisymmetric disk \(\sigma_\theta(b)=2pa^2/(b^2-a^2)=2p/3\), so \(p\simeq1.00\) MPa. Piston thrust is \(pA_p=100\) N. Inner hoop stress is

\[p(a^2+b^2)/(b^2-a^2)=1.67\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

The disk senses pressure through its curved surface. Its wall geometry enters the gauge calibration, while the piston converts that common fluid pressure into an axial force.

Set the inner pressure to 1 MPa and check both boundary stresses

2 · Separate pad compression from fixture compliance

The pad has area 400 mm², initial thickness 20 mm, \(E=30\) MPa and \(\nu=0.40\). Its sides are constrained by a close-fitting rigid cylindrical sleeve. A steel tie bar of length 300 mm and axial stiffness product \(EA=2.00\) MN is in the load path. The encoder adds pad compression and bar elongation in magnitude. Predict crosshead displacement, then repeat for free lateral expansion.

Resolve both compliances at the same measured force

The compressive stress magnitude is

\[100/400=0.250\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

In the sleeve the effective modulus is \(M=E(1-\nu)/[(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)]=64.29\) MPa, so compression is 0.0778 mm. Bar elongation is

\[FL/(EA)=0.0150\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

The predicted crosshead motion is 0.0928 mm. Free sidewalls give 0.1667 mm pad compression and 0.1817 mm total. Each component carries 100 N. Adding its displacement, rather than averaging its modulus with the other component, implements the series load path.

3 · Interpret an anomalous displacement

With the same pressure reading the encoder now moves 0.125 mm. Subtract the calibrated bar extension and infer a pad modulus under the observed constraint. Could a pressure offset alone explain the readings if the strain gauge and the bar calibration are both trusted? Describe a second measurement that discriminates between partial lateral restraint and extra contact compliance.

Diagnose what the two signals identify

The inferred pad motion is \(0.125-0.015=0.110\) mm, giving apparent modulus \(0.250(20)/0.110=45.5\) MPa. This value lies between the free and ideal-sleeve results. A pressure change large enough to explain the excess motion under the ideal model would also change the hoop strain. Incomplete lateral restraint could increase true pad strain. A settling contact could instead add displacement to the encoder without changing the pad strain. A transverse mid-height gauge tests the sleeve assumption; a direct axial pad extensometer separates pad deformation from contact settlement. A model fit to the encoder alone cannot identify both effects.

4 · Change only the load level

Double the pressure within the same linear elastic idealization. Predict the outer strain, encoder displacement for the sound sleeved assembly, and the ratio of bar displacement to total motion. State an observation that would contradict the model.

Predict the second operating point

The outer strain doubles to about 19.0 microstrain, thrust reaches 200 N and total motion doubles to 0.1856 mm. The bar accounts for \(0.0150/0.0928=16.2\%\) at both loads. A changing fraction from direct extensometer measurements would point to a load-dependent stiffness, slip or contact seating. The proportional predictions hold while geometry, constraint and material response remain linear.
