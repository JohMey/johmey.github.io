A cantilever with a spring prop

A released cantilever tip displacement under the specified distributed load is 14 mm downward; the tip flexibility is 4 mm/kN. It rests on an initially unloaded spring of stiffness 0.50 kN/mm. Find the spring reaction and tip displacement. Repeat for a rigid support initially 5 mm below the undeformed tip.

Reveal the worked solution

For the spring, \(14-4R=R/0.50\), hence \(R=2.333\,\mathrm{kN}\) and tip movement \(R/k=4.667\,\mathrm{mm}\). With the rigid gapped support, the freely deflected beam would reach the support, so \(14-4R=5\) and \(R=2.25\,\mathrm{kN}\). In both cases the positive reaction verifies compressive contact.

A point load on a propped beam

A cantilever of length \(L=2.4\,\mathrm m\) carries a downward point load \(P=6\,\mathrm{kN}\) at midspan and has a prop at the far tip, fixed at its original height. By virtual work, find the released tip displacement in terms of \(EI\), then determine prop reaction and the fixed-end moment magnitude.

Reveal the worked solution

Use actual moment \(P(a-x)\) for \(x<a=L/2\) and zero elsewhere. With tip unit-load moment \(L-x\), integrating gives

\[\delta=P a^2(3L-a)/(6EI)=5PL^3/(48EI).\]

The tip flexibility is \(L^3/(3EI)\), giving \(R=5P/16=1.875\,\mathrm{kN}\). The external fixed-end moment magnitude is

\[Pa-RL=7.2-4.5=2.70\,\mathrm{kN\,m}.\]

Identify a nearly pure shear state

An aluminium surface has readings \(+50,+400,-50\) microstrain at zero, 45 and 90°. Use \(E=70\,\mathrm{GPa}\), \(\nu=0.33\) and plane stress. Find engineering shear strain, shear stress and the principal stresses. Why can a large 45° reading occur with small strains along both coordinate axes?

Reveal the worked solution

The shear strain is \(2(400)-50-(-50)=800\) microstrain, giving \(\tau=70000(0.0008)/(2.66)=21.05\,\mathrm{MPa}\). The normal stresses are \(\pm2.63\,\mathrm{MPa}\), and the mean normal stress is zero. The principal stresses are

\[\pm\sqrt{2.63^2+21.05^2}=\pm21.22\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

Shear changes the diagonal lengths of a material square even when its side lengths change little.

Find a mechanism before adding a diagonal spring

A joint is first held only by horizontal bars with combined axial stiffness 12 MN/m. Can it resist a 1.5 kN load directed upwards in a linear truss model? Add a bar running up and to the right at 45° from its fixed anchor to the joint. With axial stiffness 6 MN/m, find both displacements and the diagonal force.

Show the worked solution

With only horizontal bars the free stiffness matrix is \(\operatorname{diag}(12,0)\) MN/m, so no finite solution exists for a nonzero vertical load. The added diagonal contributes 3 MN/m to all four entries, giving rows \((15,3)\) and \((3,3)\). In kN and mm the load vector is \((0,1.5)\). The first row gives

\[u_y=-5u_x.\]

The second then gives

\[u_x=-0.125,\ u_y=0.625\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

Diagonal extension is \((0.625-0.125)/\sqrt2=0.3536\) mm and tension is 2.12 kN. The diagonal exerts 1.50 kN downwards and 1.50 kN leftwards on the joint. The horizontal bars exert 1.50 kN rightwards, so the joint balances the applied upward load.
