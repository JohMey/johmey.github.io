1. Change shaft speed at constant power

A smooth solid shaft of diameter 30 mm transmits 6 kW at 1200 rpm and carries a fixed bending moment of 80 N·m. (a) Find the torque and surface stresses. (b) Compute von Mises stress. (c) Repeat at 600 rpm at the same power and explain which stress changes.

Combine bending and torsion

At 1200 rpm the angular speed is \(40\pi\) rad/s and \(T=6000/(40\pi)=47.75\) N·m. At the tensile surface \(\sigma=32(80)/(\pi d^3)=30.18\) MPa and \(\tau=16(47.75)/(\pi d^3)=9.01\) MPa. Thus

\[\sigma_{\rm vm}=\sqrt{\sigma^2+3\tau^2}=33.98\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

Halving the speed doubles the torque and the shear to 18.01 MPa while bending stays fixed. The equivalent stress becomes about 43.4 MPa. Keeping power constant does not keep the torque demand constant.

2. Decide which plane controls a strut

A straight pin-ended column is 2.0 m long with \(E=200\) GPa, \(I_y=8.0\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m^4}\) and \(I_z=2.0\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^4}\). (a) Calculate both mode loads. (b) Add an ideal midspan lateral brace that halves only the effective length for bending about \(y\). Determine the controlling load. (c) Compare your result with a prediction multiplying the original capacity by four.

Compare both elastic modes

Using \(P=\pi^2EI/L^2\) gives 39.5 kN about \(y\) and 98.7 kN about \(z\); initially the first governs. After bracing, the ideal \(y\) load is 158 kN. The \(z\) load is still 98.7 kN and now governs. The ideal capacity therefore rises by a factor 2.5, because the weak mode switches. The brace must restrain displacement in the direction in which the original weak mode bends.

3. Interpret a lateral-displacement reading

A test column has initial sinusoidal bow 0.40 mm. At a compressive load of 12 kN the total lateral amplitude is 1.00 mm. (a) Infer its first-mode elastic critical load. (b) Estimate amplitude at 16 kN. (c) If the area is 300 mm² and \(Z=1200\) mm³, estimate the most compressive nominal stress at 16 kN.

Infer the stiffness loss and add the bending stress

From \(w_0/w=1-P/P_{\rm cr}\), the 12 kN reading gives

\[P_{\rm cr}=12/(1-0.4)=20\,\mathrm{kN}.\]

At 16 kN the predicted amplitude is

\[0.40/(1-16/20)=2.0\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

The direct stress is

\[16\,000/300=53.3\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

The additional moment is \(16\,000(2.0)=32\,000\) N·mm, giving \(M/Z=26.7\) MPa. At the more compressed fibre the two effects add, reaching about 80.0 MPa. The inference assumes an elastic member, a first-mode imperfection and small slopes.
