1. Move a wheel along a beam

A single 8 kN wheel loads a simply supported 4.0 m span 1.0 m from A. (a) Find the reactions and largest moment. (b) Place the wheel 2.0 m from A and repeat. (c) A section has \(Z=160\,000\) mm³. Compare the largest nominal stresses.

Work from reactions to stress

The first moment balance about A gives \(R_B=8(1)/4=2\) kN and \(R_A=6\) kN. Shear changes sign at the wheel, which is where \(M_{\max}=6(1)=6\) kN·m. At midspan the reactions are both 4 kN and \(M_{\max}=8\) kN·m. Convert moments to N·mm and divide by \(Z\). The extreme stresses are 37.5 and 50.0 MPa. Symmetry maximises the moment from one mobile wheel at midspan for this support arrangement.

2. Locate the maximum moment under a tapered load

A 3.0 m simple span carries downward load rising from zero at A to 6.0 kN/m at B. (a) Determine the reactions. (b) Obtain \(V(x)\) and \(M(x)\) on the open span. (c) Find the maximum and check the other end condition.

Integrate the triangular load

The resultant is 9 kN at 2 m from A, so \(R_B=6\) kN and \(R_A=3\) kN. In metres the intensity is \(2x\) kN/m. Integrating \(V'=-2x\) gives \(V=3-x^2\) kN. With \(M(0)=0\), \(M=3x-x^3/3\) kN·m. The positive shear vanishes at \(x=\sqrt3=1.732\) m, where \(M=2\sqrt3=3.46\) kN·m. At B, \(M(3)=9-9=0\) and the 6 kN support reaction exactly cancels the remaining negative shear.

3. Compare two displacements on a cantilever

A 0.90 m cantilever with \(EI=6000\,\mathrm{N\,m^2}\) carries a 240 N downward tip force. (a) Derive the end deflection by the unit-load method. (b) Compute the deflection at the halfway station using a fictitious load there. (c) Explain why linear interpolation from the tip is inaccurate.

Work the two virtual-load diagrams

For the tip reading multiply \(M=-P(L-x)\) by \(m=-(L-x)\) and integrate from 0 to \(L\). The displacement is

\[PL^3/(3EI)=9.72\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

For a unit load at \(a=L/2\), \(m=-(a-x)\) up to \(a\) and zero beyond. Thus

\[\delta(a)=P\int_0^a(L-x)(a-x)\,dx/(EI)=Pa^2(3L-a)/(6EI)=3.04\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

Half the tip reading would be 4.86 mm. The zero slope at the clamp and bending curvature throughout the beam give a nonlinear deflection shape.

Size a symmetric sandwich beam from its bending rigidity

A 30 mm wide beam has a 16 mm core of modulus 2 GPa and two 2 mm faces of modulus 70 GPa. For a 15 N m moment, locate the neutral axis, calculate curvature and maximum face stress, and determine the fraction of bending rigidity supplied by the core.

Show the worked solution

Symmetry of materials and geometry fixes the neutral axis halfway through the 20 mm total depth. Core \(I=30(16)^3/12=10240\,\mathrm{mm^4}\). Each face centroid is 9 mm from mid-depth and contributes \(30(2)^3/12+60(9)^2=4880\,\mathrm{mm^4}\). Rigidity is

\[2000(10240)+2(70000)(4880)=7.0368\times10^8\,\mathrm{N\,mm^2}.\]

Curvature is

\[15000/D=2.132\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{mm^{-1}}.\]

Outer face stress magnitude is \(70000(10)\kappa=14.9\,\mathrm{MPa}\). The core provides only \(20.48/703.68=2.91\%\) of bending rigidity. It still keeps the faces separated and can carry substantial transverse shear.
