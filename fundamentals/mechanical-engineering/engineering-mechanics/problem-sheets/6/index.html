How much of the cross-section has yielded?

A rectangular strip 12 mm wide and 30 mm deep has elastic–perfectly plastic yield stress 200 MPa. Determine the first-yield and plastic moments. At a curvature three times the yield curvature, find the depth of the elastic core and resulting moment.

Reveal the worked solution

With \(c=15\,\mathrm{mm}\),

\[M_p=b\sigma_Yc^2=540\,\mathrm{N\,m},\]
\[M_Y=2M_p/3=360\,\mathrm{N\,m}.\]

The elastic half-depth is \(a=c/3=5\,\mathrm{mm}\), giving a 10 mm deep core. The moment is

\[12(200)(15^2-5^2/3)=520\,\mathrm{N\,m}.\]

Yield has spread far inward for a comparatively small increase over the first-yield moment.

Changing the inspection threshold

A wide plate with a central through crack has applicable toughness 45 MPa√m at the service temperature. With remote tension 180 MPa and \(Y=1\), calculate critical half-length and full length. If inspection finds a full crack length of 16 mm, what remote stress gives the critical intensity?

Reveal the worked solution

The critical half-length is \(a_c=(45/180)^2/\pi=0.0199\,\mathrm m\), or 19.9 mm; the full length is 39.8 mm. The measured half-length is 8 mm, so

\[\sigma_c=45/\sqrt{\pi(0.008)}=284\,\mathrm{MPa}.\]

Both predictions assume small-scale yielding and adequate plate width. Using full length inside the square root would underpredict the critical stress.

Infer release rate from two readings

A specimen with one advancing crack front has thickness 10 mm. Its measured compliance increases from 0.0250 to 0.0270 mm/kN as crack length rises by 1.0 mm. Under a fixed load of 4 kN estimate release rate. For plane stress with \(E=70\,\mathrm{GPa}\), find the corresponding intensity.

Reveal the worked solution

The slope is

\[0.0020\,\mathrm{kN^{-1}}=2.0\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N^{-1}}.\]

Thus

\[G=4000^2(2.0\times10^{-6})/[2(0.010)]=1600\,\mathrm{J/m^2}.\]

Then

\[K=\sqrt{EG}=10.58\,\mathrm{MPa\sqrt m}.\]

This uses a local finite-difference slope, so significant curvature of the compliance data would call for measurements at closer crack lengths.

Separate load capacity from serviceability

A simply supported steel beam of span 2.00 m carries a 2.00 kN centre load. Its solid rectangular section is 40.0 mm wide by 80.0 mm deep. Take E = 200 GPa, allowable bending stress 120 MPa and a service deflection limit L/400. Find the admissible centre load. Would rotating the section by 90° change which limit governs?

Worked solution

Symmetry gives reactions P/2 and the peak bending moment PL/4. With the 80 mm side vertical the second moment of area is bh³/12 = 1.707 × 10⁻⁶ m⁴ and the elastic section modulus is I/(h/2) = 4.267 × 10⁻⁵ m³. Under the specified load the centre moment is 1000 N m and the extreme-fibre stress is 23.4 MPa.

Integrating the elastic curvature with zero displacement at the supports and zero slope at midspan gives δ = PL³/(48EI). At 2.00 kN this is 0.977 mm. The stress limit gives 10.24 kN and the deflection limit of 5.00 mm also gives 10.24 kN. Their coincidence is particular to these dimensions and limits.

On rotating the beam, the new second moment is one quarter of the original and the section modulus one half. The stress limit now allows 5.12 kN, while deflection allows only 2.56 kN. The material, mass and support reactions stayed the same. The calculation assumes elastic bending and effective lateral restraint; connection and local bearing capacities require separate data.
