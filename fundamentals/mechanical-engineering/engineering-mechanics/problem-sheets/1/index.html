1. Find a load from two readings

A 5 m platform with a pin at the left end and a roller at the right weighs 6 kN uniformly. An added crate makes the reactions 4.2 kN and 5.8 kN respectively. (a) Determine the crate weight. (b) Find its position measured from the left. (c) Slide it 0.50 m left. Predict both new readings and show how their sum and difference respond.

Work the two balances

The total vertical load is \(4.2+5.8=10.0\) kN, so the crate weighs 4.0 kN. Taking moments about the left support gives \(5.8(5)=6(2.5)+4x\); hence \(x=3.50\) m. Moving left removes \(4(0.50)=2.0\) kN·m from the clockwise load moment. The right reading falls by \(2.0/5=0.40\) kN to 5.4 kN, while the left rises to 4.6 kN. Total weight remains 10 kN. The right-minus-left difference decreases from 1.6 to 0.8 kN because one reading rises while the other falls.

2. Let two bars share one load

Rigid parallel plates are joined by a 0.40 m long steel bar (\(E=200\) GPa, \(A=100\) mm²) and an equal-length aluminium bar (\(E=70\) GPa, \(A=200\) mm²). The plates are guided to remain parallel. A centred 17 kN tensile load separates them. (a) Find each stiffness. (b) Determine the extension and forces. (c) Compare the two strains and two stresses.

Work the common-displacement condition

The stiffnesses \(EA/L\) are 50 MN/m and 35 MN/m. The common extension is \(17000/(85\times10^6)=0.200\) mm. Steel takes 10.0 kN and aluminium 7.0 kN, summing to 17 kN. Both strains are \(0.0002/0.40=0.00050\), but the stresses are 100 and 35 MPa. Equal length and equal displacement enforce equal strain. Different moduli turn equal strain into different stress, and different areas convert those stresses to forces.

3. Close a gap before loading a support

A stress-free 1.20 m steel bar points toward a rigid stop with a 0.18 mm gap. Its other end is fixed. Use \(A=150\) mm², \(E=200\) GPa and \(\alpha=12\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}\). (a) How much warming closes the gap? (b) Find the compressive force and stress after a uniform 30 K rise. (c) Interpret a calculation that uses all 30 K as a restrained rise.

Separate free and restrained stages

Free expansion closes the gap at \(0.00018/(1.20\alpha)=12.5\) K. The subsequent 17.5 K would add another 0.252 mm of expansion, which the stop prevents. Compatibility now requires elastic strain \(-\alpha(17.5)=-0.000210\). The resulting stress is −42.0 MPa and force −6.30 kN. Treating all 30 K as restrained would give −72 MPa and would incorrectly predict a reaction before contact. This piecewise solution assumes the bar remains straight and the stop carries compression.

Infer a relaxation model from a displacement-hold test

A 40 mm² specimen held at fixed strain 0.004 requires 48 N immediately, 24 N after a long hold and 32.83 N after 30 s. Infer both spring moduli and the branch viscosity. Predict the long-time strain when a fresh specimen instead carries 32 N.

Show the worked solution

The instantaneous and long-time stresses are 1.20 and 0.60 MPa. Dividing by strain gives \(E_\infty+E_1=300\) MPa and \(E_\infty=150\) MPa, hence \(E_1=150\) MPa. The excess force fraction after 30 s is \((32.83-24)/(48-24)=0.368\), so \(\tau_r\simeq30\) s and \(\eta=4500\) MPa s. At 32 N the maintained stress is 0.80 MPa. The strain starts at \(0.8/300=0.00267\) and tends to \(0.8/150=0.00533\). Its creep time is 60 s, so transferring the relaxation time directly to the force-hold test would predict the wrong rate.
