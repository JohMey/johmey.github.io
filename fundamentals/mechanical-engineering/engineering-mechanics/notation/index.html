Engineering mechanics · Notation and assumptions

Read each defining equation with its assumptions and units, then follow the link to the argument that gives the equation its meaning.

Axial strain

\[\varepsilon=du/dx\]

This is engineering strain for small displacement gradients in a straight bar. A measured extension is an integral over the gauge length.

Derivation · Axial stress, strain and compatibility

Euler load

\[P_{\rm cr}=\pi^2 EI/L_{\rm eff}^2\]

The ideal slender elastic column buckles in its lowest admissible mode. Support conditions determine effective length and change the result quadratically.

Derivation · Elastic stability of compression members

Second moment of area

\[I=\int_A y^2\,dA\]

Measure the transverse coordinate from the stated bending axis. This section property has units of length to the fourth power and enters the flexural rigidity.

Derivation · Elastic curves and virtual work

Shear flow

\[q=VQ/I\]

In elementary beam theory the first moment Q belongs to the cut portion of the section. Shear flow is force per length. Divide by the local width to obtain shear stress.

Derivation · Transverse shear and connections in built-up beams

Stress intensity

\[K_I=Y\sigma\sqrt{\pi a}\]

Opening-mode intensity has units of stress times the square root of length. Geometry and the definition of crack length are part of the calibration.

Derivation · Crack driving force and fracture assessment

Traction

\[\mathbf t(\mathbf n)=\boldsymbol\sigma\mathbf n\]

Specify the plane normal before projecting the force per area into normal and shear parts. A stress component is not a net force until integrated over an area.
