Which members carry a load through a truss?

A weighing frame can have perfect external force balance and still contain a failed load path. Follow the reactions into individual bars, cut across the middle of the frame, and carry the same reasoning into three dimensions.

Earn the two-force assumption

Ideal pins transmit forces through their centres. Apply loads at joints, neglect member self-weight or lump it at joints, and treat each initially straight bar as hinged at both ends. Moment balance on that bar requires its two end forces to be collinear. A single scalar \(N\) now describes each member. Positive \(N\) pulls away from the joint. For a bar joining positions \(x_i\) and \(x_j\), use \(e_{ij}=(x_j-x_i)/|x_j-x_i|\). Reversing the joint reverses its column direction.

\[\sum_{j}N_{ij}e_{ij}+R_i+P_i=0.\]

At an unloaded joint with two noncollinear bars their coefficients must both vanish. With three bars, two collinear and no applied load, project normal to their shared line to eliminate both and identify the third as a zero-force member. A brace classified this way may still resist the next load case or restrain buckling.

Read a two-panel bridge from both ends

Place bottom joints A, B, C at (0,0), (2,0), (4,0) m, and D, E at (1,1), (3,1). Connect AB, BC, AD, DB, BE, EC and DE. Pin A, put C on a vertical-reaction roller. Apply 6 kN down at D and 2 kN down at E. Taking moments about A gives \(4C_y=6(1)+2(3)\), hence \(C_y=3\) and \(A_y=5\) kN. At A, the vertical projection gives \(N_{AD}=-5\sqrt2\) kN, and horizontal equilibrium gives \(N_{AB}=5\) kN. At C the corresponding forces are \(N_{EC}=-3\sqrt2\) and \(N_{BC}=3\) kN.

Cut AB, DB and DE immediately left of B and take moments on the left fragment about B. AB and DB intersect there. The reaction at A contributes −10 kN m, the downward load at D contributes +6 kN m, and the horizontal force in DE contributes \(-N_{DE}(1\,\mathrm m)\). Thus \(N_{DE}=-4\) kN. Solving at D gives \(N_{DB}=-\sqrt2\) kN. At E the corresponding diagonal BE is in tension at \(+\sqrt2\) kN. The opposite signs are physically measurable under this asymmetric loading.

When a section cut can determine three forces

Three equilibrium equations suffice only if the three cut-member force lines supply independent force and moment conditions. If all three lines meet at one point, their moments about that point vanish together. Choose another cut or a second free body.

Lift the joint into three dimensions

Place three feet at equal azimuthal intervals on a circle of radius 1 m. Pin the apex 1 m above its centre. Measure the horizontal 0° direction toward foot 1. The directions from apex to feet are \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta,-1)/\sqrt2\). A downward load \(W\) and horizontal load \(H\) give \(N_2=N_3\) by symmetry. Taking horizontal and vertical projections yields

\[N_1=-\frac{\sqrt2}{3}(W+2H),\]
\[N_2=N_3=\frac{\sqrt2}{3}(H-W).\]

At \(W=6\) and \(H=3\) kN, all three bars are in compression. At \(H=6\) kN two members unload simultaneously. Pins capable of taking tension and unanchored compression-only feet then predict different admissible states. The notebook resolves all three joint equations and plots that boundary.

OneSimulation experiment · Two legs unload as the horizontal apex force reaches the vertical load

Run the notebook with OneSimulation 0.2.0, NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib. Change the input data and compare your run with the recorded result.

"""Resolve measured loads on a three-dimensional tripod into signed member forces."""
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from onesim.numerics.nonlinear import LeastSquares

angles = np.deg2rad([0, 120, 240])
# Columns point from the loaded apex toward a foot; positive force is tension.
directions = np.array([np.cos(angles), np.sin(angles), -np.ones(3)]) / np.sqrt(2)
horizontal = np.linspace(0, 6, 61)  # kN
forces = []
for load in horizontal:
    external = np.array([load, 0.0, -6.0])
    fit = LeastSquares().solve(lambda force: directions @ force + external, [-2.0]*3)
    assert np.linalg.norm(directions @ fit.x + external) < 1e-6
    forces.append(fit.x)
forces = np.array(forces)
print("H=3 kN: signed member forces [kN]", np.round(forces[30], 3))
print("H=6 kN: signed member forces [kN]", np.round(forces[-1], 3))

Exercises on force paths

Move both bridge loads to E while retaining their combined magnitude. Which support reaction and which top-chord force change?

Worked solution

The right reaction is \(8(3)/4=6\) kN and the left is 2 kN. Taking moments about B on the same left fragment gives \(-2(2)-N_{DE}=0\), so DE still carries −4 kN. An unchanged chord force does not imply unchanged diagonals.

Remove BE from the original frame. Does external equilibrium establish rigidity?

Worked solution

The pin and roller provide three independent reaction components. Five planar joints admit ten equilibrium rows against six members and three reactions. There cannot be full rank. The original load may no longer be supported without motion. For geometries satisfying the count, independence must still be checked.

What happens when the apex height tends to zero at fixed radius and vertical load?

Worked solution

Every vertical component of a member direction tends to zero. Maintaining finite upward resultant requires unbounded compression before the idealised matrix loses rank at zero height. Strength, deformation and buckling constrain the real frame much earlier.
