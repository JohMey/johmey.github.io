Stress, deformation and structural measurement

Start from balance, compatibility and material response. Work through structural predictions with alternative load or support conditions and assess the measurements needed to test them.

    Contents

    1. Torsion in a thin-walled two-cell section

      Two closed cells share one slender web. The applied couple gives only their combined torque, yet the common web can carry much less shear than either exterior wall. Derive the missing compatibility equation, calculate a twist and test which measurements distinguish the two circulations.

    2. Contact pressure between elastic cylinders

      Two polished cylindrical rollers touch along a narrow strip. Dividing load by a nominal full face area gives little indication of the actual pressure. Use the local shape of the gap and elastic half-space compliance to obtain the pressure distribution, and decide whether a measured contact width can determine material stiffness.

    3. Springback and residual stress after plastic bending

      After a rectangular strip has been bent beyond first yield, removing its end couple does not return it to its original curvature. Work out the loading and unloading paths fibre by fibre. Then explain why an unloaded strip can contain a substantial stress field with zero net force and zero net bending moment.

    4. Why an open channel twists under a transverse load

      A channel can twist even when a force passes through its centroid. Trace the longitudinal shear flow around its flanges, find the load line that produces bending without torsion, and design a loading experiment that separates the stiffness in bending from eccentricity relative to this shear centre.

    5. Where does a circular opening concentrate stress?

      Make a hole in a flat sheet pulled along one direction. Derive the conditions on the empty edge, compute the circumferential stress and check how quickly the disturbance decays as a strain gauge moves into the sheet.

    6. Which members carry a load through a truss?

      A weighing frame can have perfect external force balance and still contain a failed load path. Follow the reactions into individual bars, cut across the middle of the frame, and carry the same reasoning into three dimensions.

    7. Where does the neutral axis of an angle section lie?

      A downward tip load can bend an angle section sideways. Construct its full area-moment matrix and use measured strains to distinguish coupling of bending axes from an unintended applied torque.

    8. When does Euler’s column load cease to govern?

      A stocky strut may yield while its elastic eigenvalue still looks comfortable. Separate a bifurcation of a straight member, stress amplification under eccentric loading and loss of tangent stiffness in a yielded section.
