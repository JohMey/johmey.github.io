Why an open channel twists under a transverse load

A channel can twist even when a force passes through its centroid. Trace the longitudinal shear flow around its flanges, find the load line that produces bending without torsion, and design a loading experiment that separates the stiffness in bending from eccentricity relative to this shear centre.

Find the internal horizontal force in each flange

Use a thin channel made of a vertical web of midline height \(h\) and two horizontal flanges of midline width \(b\) projecting to the same side. Take uniform thickness \(t\ll h,b\), ignore corner radii and flange through-thickness bending, and consider a vertical shear resultant \(V\). Symmetry places the shear centre on the horizontal axis through mid-height but does not place it on the web or centroid. Set the web midline at \(x=0\) and both flanges at positive \(x\).

For bending about the horizontal centroidal axis, the thin-wall second moment is

\[I_x\simeq th^3/12+2bt(h/2)^2.\]

The usual beam shear-flow relation \(q=VQ/I_x\) follows from balancing the longitudinal stress change of a cut-off strip over an incremental beam length, using \(dM/dz=V\) with a consistent beam-axis orientation. At a free flange edge the cut-off area vanishes and the flow is zero. A distance \(s\) inwards from that free edge, \(Q\simeq t s h/2\). The flow increases linearly toward the web.

Integrating the flow over one flange gives a horizontal resultant magnitude \(F_f=Vthb^2/(4I_x)\). Top and bottom flange forces are opposing, so they contribute zero net horizontal force but a couple \(F_fh\) about the web line. The web carries the net vertical shear and its contribution has zero moment about its own midline in this thin-wall representation. Balance the internal couple with the moment of the external vertical force about the web. The shear centre is on the side opposite the flanges, at

\[x_{\rm sc}=-e,\]
\[e=\frac{th^2b^2}{4I_x}=\frac{3b^2}{h+6b}.\]

The sign describes the stated channel opening direction. Reflecting the channel reverses the sign in a fixed apparatus. The result is independent of uniform thickness in this thin-wall limit because both shear flow first moment and bending second moment scale with thickness. It does not imply that the torsional stiffness or stress is independent of thickness.

Measure a load line outside the material

Take \(h=60\) mm, \(b=30\) mm and \(t=2\) mm. The shear centre lies 11.25 mm behind the web. The thin-wall centroid is at \(x_c=b^2/(h+2b)=7.50\) mm on the opening side. A vertical load of 100 N applied through the centroid therefore introduces a torsional moment magnitude \(100(0.01875)=1.875\) N m about the shear centre. A cantilever channel clamped against warping can show both Saint-Venant twist and additional restrained-warping stresses, especially near the clamp.

Mount an adjustable loading arm across the tip and apply several known vertical forces at signed positions \(x\). In a sufficiently linear regime the measured twist under fixed load is proportional to \(x-x_{\rm sc}\). Fit its zero crossing; this eliminates the need to know absolute torsional rigidity in the first estimate. A force applied through an external bracket can reach a line outside the actual material. Ensure the bracket supplies a force and no unintended direct couple, and take readings away from local load introduction.

Find the same shear centre with a signed load-position sweep

Keep the vertical force fixed and move its line of application laterally while recording twist at a section a known distance from a clamp. In a member with constant torsional compliance in the test range, the slope of twist versus lateral position contains both the force and compliance. Its zero crossing locates the shear centre without requiring a separate torsional stiffness measurement. The second curve in the model shows the moment computed about the web. Its zero crossing is set by the coordinate origin. Only the other crossing predicts a vanishing twist of the section in the simple beam idealisation.

Open the live experiment

Repeat the sweep with the force reversed and the same attachment bracket. A constant apparatus torque shifts the two inferred zero positions in opposite directions because the applied torque per unit position changes sign. Their mean can remove a load-independent offset. A bracket whose compliance changes asymmetrically under reversal requires a separate correction. Measure twist with two gauges spanning a known width so that common vertical translation cancels. If the two gauges are far apart along the beam instead of across the same section, differing bending slopes can contaminate the apparent twist and shift the crossing.

Tutorial · Recover the shear-centre location from two angular readings

At fixed load an adjustable fixture gives twist minus 0.10 degrees when the load lies at \(x=-15.0\) mm and plus 0.30 degrees at \(x=0\). Infer the shear-centre coordinate and predict twist when the force passes through the centroid of the example.

Use signed interpolation and check the geometric result

The slope is \(0.40/15=0.0267\) degrees per millimetre. The zero crossing is \(-15.0+0.10/0.0267=-11.25\) mm, matching the predicted shear centre. At \(x=7.50\) mm the lever arm is 18.75 mm and the predicted twist 0.500 degrees. This inference assumes no independent angular offset. Reversing load lifts out an offset unchanged by force sign, while moving the bracket with no force checks alignment.

Tutorial · Predict the effect of making the flanges shorter

At unchanged web height and thickness, halve flange width to 15 mm. Predict both centroid and shear-centre location. Is their separation simply half its old value?

Recompute both first moments

The centroid is \(225/(60+30)=2.50\) mm, while \(e=3(225)/(60+90)=4.50\) mm. Their distance becomes 7.00 mm instead of 18.75 mm. Both formulas contain a squared flange width and a changing perimeter or second moment, so halving width does not simply halve the eccentricity. In the formal limit of vanishing flange width channel torsional resistance also becomes very small, requiring careful control of imperfection and fixture compliance.

Tutorial · Distinguish eccentric bending from torsional warping

A short channel shows nonzero mean twist even at the predicted load line and opposite longitudinal strains on the free flange tips near its clamp. Could changing shear modulus alone move the predicted shear centre? Suggest two specific measurement changes.

Test the assumptions that control the zero crossing

For this homogeneous thin elastic channel the centre follows force and moment equilibrium of bending shear and ideal geometry; changing one common shear modulus principally changes torsional compliance rather than moving that ideal geometric point. Compare two specimen lengths and measure twist slopes at several interior stations to identify restrained end warping. Reverse channel orientation while keeping the apparatus fixed to separate a channel-related eccentricity from a fixed bracket couple. If the discrepancy follows the specimen, measure actual corner radii, wall thickness asymmetry and local distortion and use a suitable open-section beam or shell model before attributing every measured longitudinal strain to pure flexure.
