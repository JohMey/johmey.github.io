Where does the neutral axis of an angle section lie?

A downward tip load can bend an angle section sideways. Construct its full area-moment matrix and use measured strains to distinguish coupling of bending axes from an unintended applied torque.

Balance two bending moments with one stress plane

Consider a straight, homogeneous prismatic beam in small-deflection Euler–Bernoulli bending. Coordinates \(x,y\) lie in the section, with their origin at the area centroid. Plane sections remain plane and zero axial resultant eliminates the constant part of the stress. Write \(\sigma=a x+b y\). Fix signs by defining \(M_x=\int y\sigma\,dA\) and \(M_y=-\int x\sigma\,dA\). These signs follow from the cross product of position with axial traction. Then

\[\begin{bmatrix}I_{yy}&I_{xy}\\I_{xy}&I_{xx}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a\\b\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-M_y\\M_x\end{bmatrix},\quad I_{xy}=\int_Axy\,dA.\]

Rotating coordinates to an orthogonal eigenbasis diagonalises this matrix. Its positive determinant is the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to the coordinates. Under pure \(M_x\), \(a/b=-I_{xy}/I_{yy}\), so the neutral line \(y=-(a/b)x\) generally slopes. A moment aligned with a principal axis recovers ordinary uniaxial bending.

Build the section before inverting the moments

Use an 80 by 10 mm horizontal rectangle and a 10 by 60 mm upright above its left edge. The centroid measured from the lower left corner is (25,20) mm. Translating each rectangle to the centroid gives \(I_{xx}=606667\), \(I_{yy}=851667\), and \(I_{xy}=-420000\) in mm⁴. For instance, the horizontal part contributes \(800(40-25)(5-20)=-180000\) mm⁴ to the product. Adding independent values of \(bh^3/12\) would omit these offsets.

Take \(M_x=1\) kN m and \(M_y=0\). With moments in N mm, the result is \(a=1.2343\) and \(b=2.5029\) MPa/mm. The neutral line has slope −0.4932. At the horizontal flange corner (80,10) the predicted longitudinal tension is 42.86 MPa. The two principal area moments are approximately 291667 and 1166667 mm⁴. These are useful geometry checks because a rigid rotation cannot change them.

If the tip load misses the shear centre

An eccentric transverse force can supply both a bending moment and a torque. The formula above predicts longitudinal bending stress in a section away from load introduction. A separate twist measurement or a loading couple with no net transverse force helps isolate the source of a sideways signal.

Exercises on signed strain readings

A strain gauge is glued at (0,0) mm. What signal should it give for steel with \(E=200\) GPa?

Worked solution

Its stress is \(1.2343(-25)+2.5029(-20)=-80.9\) MPa. Its axial strain is −405 microstrain. A wrong choice of corner origin would falsely predict zero.

Can two axial gauges recover both bending moments?

Worked solution

Write their two rows as \((x_i,y_i)\) relative to the centroid and solve for \(a,b\) from \(E\epsilon_i\). Recovery requires \(x_1 y_2-x_2 y_1\ne0\). Two gauges on the same centroidal line give proportional information even if they lie on opposite sides of the section.
