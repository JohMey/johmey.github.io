Compare slenderness against the material stress scale

For a pin-ended straight column with effective length \(L_e\), radius of gyration \(r=\sqrt{I/A}\) and uniform modulus \(E\), the homogeneous transverse balance admits a nonzero mode when \(P_E=\pi^2EI/L_e^2\). The corresponding average stress falls as the inverse square of \(\lambda=L_e/r\). If this value exceeds the proportional limit, part of the constitutive relation assumed by the eigenproblem is already invalid.

One engineering interpolation for intermediate steel columns is a Johnson parabola starting at yield stress \(S_y\). Write \(\sigma_J=S_y-c\lambda^2\) and require value and first derivative to match Euler’s curve at the join. Eliminating \(c\) and the joining slenderness gives

\[c=\frac{S_y^2}{4\pi^2 E},\]
\[\lambda_c=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi^2E}{S_y}}.\]

For \(E=200000\) MPa and \(S_y=250\) MPa the join is 125.7. At \(\lambda=70\) the interpolation predicts 211.2 MPa whereas elastic Euler predicts 402.8 MPa. The parabola is a material/design model. Measured residual stress, end restraint and a prescribed column curve can change the permissible design load.

Why a tangent modulus appears

Linearise a measured compressive material curve about the current stress. An incremental strain changes stress by \(E_t d\epsilon\). Under uniform continued loading, replacing \(E\) by \(E_t(\sigma)\) in the incremental bending resistance leads to an implicit tangent-modulus estimate. Unloading fibres may respond with a different modulus. Check which constitutive path the test actually follows.

Derive the secant amplification under eccentric load

Apply equal end eccentricities \(e\) on the same side of an ideal pin-ended member of length \(L\). A section at lateral deflection \(v\) sees moment magnitude \(P(e+v)\). Taking positive \(v\) toward the load line gives \(EI v''=-P(e+v)\). Set \(k^2=P/EI\), use symmetry \(v'(0)=0\) at midspan, and enforce \(v(L/2)=0\) at a pin. Thus \(e+v(x)=e\cos(kx)/\cos(kL/2)\). At the outer fibre of distance \(c_f\),

\[\sigma_{\max}=p\left[1+\frac{e c_f}{r^2}\sec\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{p/p_E}\right)\right],\]
\[p=P/A,\]
\[p_E=P_E/A .\]

For \(\lambda=100\) and \(ec_f/r^2=0.20\), \(p_E=197.39\) MPa. Solving the monotone pre-buckling branch for first yield at 250 MPa gives \(p=138.94\) MPa. The formula locates first fibre yield while the member remains nominally elastic. Post-yield redistribution needs an updated material and section model.

Combine an initial bow, locked-in stress and incremental stiffness

Make the constitutive assumption explicit with four equal-area longitudinal fibres at \(y=\pm a\) and \(\pm a/3\). Use compression positive. At zero external load, both outer fibres have residual compression +60 MPa and both inner fibres have residual tension −60 MPa. The initial resultant force and bending moment vanish, but subsequent yielding starts at different applied loads. Take \(E=200000\) MPa, compressive knee \(S_y=250\) MPa and continued-loading tangent \(E_t=4000\) MPa. Plane sections remain plane. The numerical model below linearises lateral response around the uniformly compressed reference path.

For axial strain increment \(d\epsilon_0\) and curvature increment \(d\kappa\), every fibre sees \(d\sigma_i=E_i(d\epsilon_0+y_i d\kappa)\). Summing force and moment gives the tangent matrix with \(A_t=\sum E_i A_i\), \(B_t=\sum E_i A_i y_i\) and \(D_t=\sum E_i A_i y_i^2\). Holding incremental axial force fixed and eliminating \(d\epsilon_0\) yields effective bending rigidity \(D_b=D_t-B_t^2/A_t\). Here equal opposite fibres have the same tangent on the straight reference path, so \(B_t=0\). The outer and inner fibre pairs contribute 90% and 10% of geometric \(I\), respectively.

The outer pair first reaches 250 MPa when \(E\epsilon_0=190\) MPa. The inner fibres then carry 130 MPa and the mean applied stress is 190 MPa. Before this point \(D_b/I=200000\) MPa. Afterwards \(D_b/I=0.9(4000)+0.1(200000)=23600\) MPa until the inner pair reaches its knee at mean stress 251.2 MPa. With \(a=12\) mm, \(r=8.944\) mm and \(L/r=32\), the elastic and second-stage tangent eigenstresses \(p_t=\pi^2D_b/(AL^2)\) are 1927.66 and 227.46 MPa. The latter lies inside its assumed reference-load interval.

Now prescribe a small initial half-sine bow of amplitude \(w_0=0.050\) mm, without load eccentricity. Approximate the additional lateral displacement by the same half-sine. At midspan the moment is \(P w\) where \(w\) denotes the total amplitude, including the initial bow. Differentiating equilibrium gives \(D_b(\pi/L)^2dw=w\,dP+P\,dw\). Thus the amplification law for a branch of constant tangent is

\[(p_t-p)dw=w\,dp,\]
\[w(p)=w(p_a)\frac{p_t-p_a}{p_t-p}.\]

Carry the actual displacement across each change in stiffness. At \(p=190\) MPa the first branch gives \(w=0.05547\) mm. On the second branch \(w(210)=0.05547(227.46-190)/(227.46-210)=0.1190\) mm. Replacing \(E\) by \(E_t\) at zero load would lose the prior history and use the wrong fraction of yielded material.

Where the tangent estimate stops being reliable

An initial bow makes yielding start at slightly different loads on individual fibres and vary along the span. This small-imperfection model evaluates tangents on the symmetric reference path and loses accuracy near its asymptote. For a finite imperfection, update fibres and unloading moduli station by station and follow the equilibrium path. The calculated 227.46 MPa is a tangent-reference threshold, not a measured collapse strength.

Keeping all reference data, estimate deflection at 220 MPa and identify which measurement best tests this model.

Worked solution

The second branch gives \(w=0.05547(37.46)/(7.46)=0.278\) mm. Opposed axial strain gauges at midspan, a lateral displacement reading and the initial unloaded shape separate compression, curvature and imperfection. Divergence from the predicted growth can reveal nonuniform yielding before the tangent asymptote.

Exercises on competing limits

At the join \(\lambda_c\), what fraction of yield stress do both laws give?

Worked solution

Substitute the expression for \(\lambda_c\) into either branch. Both give \(S_y/2=125\) MPa and have the same negative slope, a direct check on the interpolation.

A 400 mm² pin-ended specimen has the geometry of the eccentric example. What maximum force first produces a nominally yielded fibre? What if the eccentricity doubles?

Worked solution

The first force is \(138.94\times400=55.58\) kN. In the secant bracket the coefficient increases from 0.2 to 0.4. Holding \(p\) fixed raises peak stress, so the new admissible root is lower. Re-solve before using it as a load rating.
