Machine dynamics · Notation and assumptions

Read each defining equation with its assumptions and units, then follow the link to the argument that gives the equation its meaning.

Damped frequency

\[\omega_d=\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\]

This free-decay oscillation frequency exists in the underdamped regime. A resonance peak in a driven response uses a different frequency condition.

Derivation · Harmonic forcing, phase and dissipated power

Damping ratio

\[\zeta=c/(2\sqrt{km})\]

This compares a viscous damper with critical damping for a single linear oscillator. It cannot represent Coulomb friction at every amplitude.

Derivation · Free vibration, energy loss and ring-down data

Force impulse

\[J=\int F\,dt\]

An impulse changes linear momentum over an event. A coefficient of restitution supplies a separate relative-velocity condition for an impact.

Derivation · Impulse, angular momentum and impact

Generalised inertia

\[M(q)=2T/\dot q^2\]

This scalar definition applies to one independent moving coordinate with purely quadratic kinetic energy. Changing the coordinate changes the numerical inertia.

Derivation · Coordinates, energy and equivalent inertia

Modal participation

\[Q_j=\phi_j^\mathsf T f\]

A mode is excited according to its overlap with the force. Always state how its mode vector and modal mass were normalised.

Derivation · Modal participation and absorber tuning

Rayleigh quotient

\[R(u)=u^\mathsf TK u/(u^\mathsf TM u)\]

For symmetric stiffness and positive mass this estimates squared angular frequency. A kinematically admissible displacement is required for a useful structural estimate.

Derivation · Estimating a cantilever frequency from energy
