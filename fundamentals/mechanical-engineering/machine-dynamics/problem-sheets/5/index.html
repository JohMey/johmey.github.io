Add two angular unbalances

A 10 g mass at radius 30 mm and angle 0° shares a plane with an 8 g mass at radius 50 mm and angle 90°. Find the mass and angular position to add at radius 40 mm. Compute the original force amplitude at 1200 rpm.

Reveal the worked solution

The unbalance is \((300,400)\,\mathrm{g\,mm}\) with magnitude 500 g mm and angle 53.13°. The correction opposes it at 233.13°, and its mass is

\[500/40=12.5\,\mathrm g.\]

In SI units the force amplitude is

\[0.0005(2\pi1200/60)^2=7.90\,\mathrm N.\]

Correct an overhung imbalance

An imbalance of 600 g mm at angle 0° lies 50 mm beyond plane B. A and B are 200 mm apart. Find both correction vectors, then give the required added masses at respective radii of 25 and 50 mm. Why do the corrections point in different directions?

Reveal the worked solution

With A as origin, \(200U_B=-250(600)\) gives \(U_B=-750\,\mathrm{g\,mm}\). Force balance gives \(U_A=+150\,\mathrm{g\,mm}\). Add 15 g at 180° in B and 6 g at 0° in A. A correction between the planes cannot reproduce the larger moment arm of the overhang without an opposing correction to recover the required resultant.

Roll a ring down a slope

A thin hoop of mass 4 kg rolls without slipping down a 12° slope. Derive its acceleration, the friction force magnitude and minimum coefficient of static friction. Assume no rolling resistance.

Reveal the worked solution

The energy-coordinate effective mass is

\[m+I/R^2=2m.\]

Gravity supplies \(mg\sin12^\circ\), so

\[a=g\sin12^\circ/2=1.020\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.\]

Translational balance requires uphill friction \(mg\sin12^\circ-ma=4.08\,\mathrm N\). With normal load \(mg\cos12^\circ\), the minimum coefficient is

\[\tan12^\circ/2=0.106.\]

The friction supplies torque while its instantaneous contact point is at rest.

Balance the steering moment of a two-flywheel instrument

Two coaxial flywheels spin oppositely at 2400 rpm while their frame turns at 0.30 rad/s about a perpendicular axis. Their polar inertias are 0.080 and 0.060 kg m². Find the residual couple. What speed of the smaller wheel cancels the couple if the larger wheel retains its speed?

Show the worked solution

The signed net angular momentum magnitude is

\[(0.080-0.060)(2400)(2\pi/60)=5.03\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}.\]

The moment is \(0.30(5.03)=1.51\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). Its direction follows the turn rate crossed into the larger wheel’s spin. Cancelling angular momentum requires \(0.060n_{\rm small}=0.080(2400)\), giving 3200 rpm in the opposing sense. Equal speeds alone cancel only if the polar inertias also match. Changes of either spin speed during the turn add separate axial torques.
