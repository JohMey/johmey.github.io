A sensor changes the resonance it is meant to measure

An accelerometer is attached to a slender cantilever near a periodically driven machine. Use an energy model to estimate the shifted mode, predict how large the forced vibration becomes, and choose a second measurement that distinguishes mass loading from damping.

Problem sheet 7 · Integrative project

1 · Identify the modal stiffness and effective mass2 · Update the model before interpreting the spectrum3 · Convert an operating speed into vibration amplitude4 · Use a ring-down as an independent observation

1 · Identify the modal stiffness and effective mass

A clamped beam has \(L=0.50\) m, \(EI=10\) N m² and distributed mass 0.40 kg. Take tip displacement as generalized coordinate and use \(\phi(s)=s^2(3-s)/2\), \(s=x/L\). Calculate effective stiffness, mass and the bare frequency. Why does inserting the entire beam mass as a lump at the tip fail?

Integrate the assumed shape

The integrals are \(\int_0^1(\phi'')^2 ds=3\) and \(\int_0^1\phi^2 ds=33/140\). Hence \(k=3EI/L^3=240\) N/m, \(m_{\rm eff}=(33/140)(0.40)=0.0943\) kg and \(f=\sqrt{k/m_{\rm eff}}/(2\pi)=8.03\) Hz. Each distributed mass contribution scales with the square of its local velocity relative to the tip. Concentrating all 0.40 kg at the tip would predict only 3.90 Hz.

2 · Update the model before interpreting the spectrum

Mount a 30 g sensor first at the tip and then halfway along the beam. Predict both frequencies. At which position will the accelerometer signal be smaller for the same modal tip amplitude?

Compute two different mass participations

At the tip the full sensor mass participates, giving \(m_{\rm eff}=0.1243\) kg and 6.99 Hz. At half-span \(\phi=0.3125\), so the correction to effective mass is \(0.030(0.3125)^2=0.00293\) kg and the predicted frequency is 7.91 Hz. Half-span acceleration at the same frequency and modal amplitude is only 31.25% of the tip acceleration. In a real run, moving the sensor also shifts the resonance, so account for the changed response when comparing raw accelerometer amplitudes.

Set attached mass to 0.030 kg and compare half-span with the tip

3 · Convert an operating speed into vibration amplitude

Leave the sensor at the tip. A measured once-per-revolution harmonic supplies 0.20 N peak generalized force. Use modal damping ratio 0.040. Calculate the tip response amplitude at 420 rpm and at 300 rpm using the loaded frequency. Monitor displacement as well as acceleration.

Evaluate the frequency response

Peak displacement is \((F_0/k)/\sqrt{(1-r^2)^2+(2\zeta r)^2}\) with \(r=f_{\rm force}/f_n\). At 420 rpm the forcing is 7.00 Hz, essentially on the 6.99 Hz mode. The predicted amplitude is 0.0104 m. At 300 rpm the forcing is 5.00 Hz and the prediction drops to 0.00169 m. At 7 Hz the 10.4 mm amplitude corresponds to about 20.1 m/s² acceleration. The large amplification warrants checking the assumed linear spring and damping against amplitude-dependent measurements.

4 · Use a ring-down as an independent observation

After removing harmonic forcing, two successive positive displacement peaks are 8.0 and 6.2 mm. Estimate damping from their ratio. How could you tell whether shifting a peak after moving the sensor resulted primarily from damping or inertia?

Use logarithmic decrement before comparing resonances

The decrement is \(\delta=\ln(8.0/6.2)=0.255\), hence \(\zeta=\delta/\sqrt{4\pi^2+\delta^2}=0.0405\). At that damping the damped free frequency is \(f_d=f_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\), only about 0.08% below the undamped frequency. The roughly 13% increase predicted when moving this sensor from tip to half-span is much larger. Measure decay ratios and peak periods in both configurations and compare the mass-weighted mode prediction with the period shift.
