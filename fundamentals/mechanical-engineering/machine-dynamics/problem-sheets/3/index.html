Isolation and shock

Separate floor motion from mount travel, then distinguish peak response during and after an impact.

Problem sheet 3 · Chapters 5 and 6

1. Choose an isolation frequency2. Compare impacts with the same impulse3. Check whether a stop is struck during a pulse

1. Choose an isolation frequency

A 100 kg platform must have \(|X/Y|<1\) for floor excitation at all frequencies from 8 Hz upward. (a) Find the upper limit on its natural frequency. (b) Choose 5.0 Hz and calculate \(k\). (c) At 10 Hz and \(\zeta=0.20\), find absolute and relative amplitudes for a 0.50 mm base motion.

Apply the displacement transfer function

For the specified inequality \(f_n<8/\sqrt2=5.66\) Hz. At 5.0 Hz the stiffness is \(100(2\pi5)^2=98.7\) kN/m. At \(r=2\) and \(2\zeta r=0.8\), the common denominator magnitude is

\[\sqrt{9+0.64}=3.105.\]

The absolute amplitude is \(0.50\sqrt{1.64}/3.105=0.206\) mm, while relative amplitude is

\[0.50(4)/3.105=0.644\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

Size the allowable mount travel using the latter value.

2. Compare impacts with the same impulse

An undamped 5 kg component has \(\omega_n=40\) rad/s. Two rectangular forces each provide 1.0 N·s. Pulse A lasts 0.010 s and pulse B 0.100 s. (a) Calculate each force. (b) Calculate the two residual amplitudes. (c) Compare them with the ideal impulse limit.

Keep impulse fixed while changing duration

The forces are 100 and 10.0 N. The ideal limit is

\[J/(m\omega_n)=5.0\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

Applying the finite-duration correction \(|\sin z/z|\) with \(z=0.20\) and 2.0 yields 4.97 and 2.27 mm. The longer force partly acts during a phase when the oscillator moves back. Its smaller residual response does not follow from impulse alone.

3. Check whether a stop is struck during a pulse

A 10 kg mass on a 1000 N/m spring starts at rest. A 5 N constant force lasts exactly one undamped period. A travel stop lies 8 mm from equilibrium in the force direction. (a) Find the period. (b) Find the predicted motion at half and full period without the stop. (c) Does a zero residual response prove safe clearance?

Inspect the complete history

The angular frequency is 10 rad/s, giving a period of 0.628 s. During the force \(x=5(1-\cos10t)/1000\) m. At half a period this is 10.0 mm, with zero velocity. At the full period both displacement and velocity vanish. The motion crosses the 8 mm stop beforehand at \(\cos10t=-0.6\), or \(t=0.221\) s. The real motion after contact must be recalculated with the additional force. A post-event sample would miss the predicted interference.
