1. Accelerate a conveyor

A roller of inertia 0.080 kg·m² and radius 0.12 m drives an effective translating belt mass of 30 kg without slip. A constant resisting belt force of 40 N acts. (a) Find the roller-referred inertia. (b) Find the torque for belt acceleration 0.60 m/s². (c) At belt speed 1.20 m/s, check the input power against friction and the kinetic-energy increase.

Reduce and check the drive

The inertia is \(0.080+30(0.12)^2=0.512\) kg·m². Angular acceleration is \(0.60/0.12=5.0\) rad/s² and the resistance torque is \(40(0.12)=4.80\) N·m. The total torque is

\[4.80+0.512(5)=7.36\,\mathrm{N\,m}.\]

At 10 rad/s this supplies 73.6 W. Resistance consumes 48.0 W and kinetic energy increases at \(0.512(10)(5)=25.6\) W, giving the same total.

2. Identify an unknown damper

A 12 kg instrument rings down from 6 mm to 3 mm over exactly three full cycles taking 0.75 s. (a) Infer \(\omega_d\) and envelope rate \(\alpha\). (b) Obtain \(k,c,\zeta\). (c) Predict when the envelope will reach 1.5 mm and state one measurement that could falsify the viscous model.

Fit the measured peaks

The period is 0.25 s, so \(\omega_d=25.13\) rad/s. The envelope satisfies \(\alpha=\ln(6/3)/0.75=0.924\) s⁻¹. Thus \(c=2m\alpha=22.2\) N·s/m, \(k=m(\omega_d^2+\alpha^2)=7.59\) kN/m and \(\zeta=0.0367\). Another 0.75 s halves the envelope again, so 1.5 mm is expected 1.50 s after the first peak. If successive same-sign peak ratios change substantially above the noise floor, a constant viscous damper does not explain the trace by itself.

3. Identify an effective mass with an added mass

A nearly undamped mount oscillates at 5.00 Hz. A compact added mass of 4.50 kg lowers the frequency to 4.00 Hz. (a) Infer the original effective mass. (b) Find the spring stiffness. (c) Estimate the static additional sag and identify the assumption needed to compare with a ruler.

Solve the two frequency equations

If the stiffness does not change, \((5/4)^2=(m+4.50)/m\), giving \(m=8.00\) kg. Therefore \(k=8(2\pi5)^2=7.90\) kN/m. The extra weight gives sag \(4.5(9.81)/7896=5.59\) mm for a vertical mount. This comparison requires the measured displacement and added mass to participate in the same coordinate and the spring to remain linear over the extra static travel.

Identify a dry guide from three same-side peaks

A slide with stiffness 1.20 kN/m has successive positive maxima at 21, 15 and 9 mm before sticking occurs. Find the kinetic friction and predict its following negative turning point. Would it restart from there if static friction can supply 8 N?

Show the worked solution

The constant full-cycle decrement is 6 mm, hence

\[F_c=k(0.006)/4=1.80\,\mathrm{N}.\]

The half-cycle decrement is 3 mm, so a release from +9 mm reaches −6 mm. There the spring force is \(1200(0.006)=7.2\) N, within the 8 N static limit. It remains at −6 mm. The 9 mm positive peak can start this last half-cycle because its 10.8 N spring force exceeds the same static bound.
