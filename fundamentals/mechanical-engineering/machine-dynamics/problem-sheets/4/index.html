A point on a rotating arm

Point A on a planar body has velocity (0.8, 0.1) m/s and acceleration (1, −2) m/s². Point B is 0.30 m to the right and 0.10 m above A. The body rotates counterclockwise at 4 rad/s with clockwise angular acceleration 3 rad/s². Find the velocity and acceleration of B. Which term survives at constant angular speed?

Reveal the worked solution

The relative velocity is \(4(-0.10,0.30)=(-0.4,1.2)\), hence \(v_B=(0.4,1.3)\,\mathrm{m/s}\). The relative tangential acceleration is \((-3)(-0.10,0.30)=(0.3,-0.9)\); the centripetal term is \(-16(0.30,0.10)=(-4.8,-1.6)\). Adding A gives \(a_B=(-3.5,-4.5)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\). Even with zero angular acceleration the centripetal term remains.

Observe a collision

On a horizontal line a 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s collides with a stationary 1 kg cart. The restitution coefficient is 0.60. Find their final velocities, impulse on the target and energy lost. State what allows linear momentum conservation here.

Reveal the worked solution

Horizontal external impulse is negligible. Thus

\[2v_1+v_2=6,\]
\[v_2-v_1=0.60(3)=1.8.\]

Solving gives

\[v_1=1.4,\ v_2=3.2\,\mathrm{m/s}.\]

Target impulse is 3.2 N s. Initial energy is 9 J and final energy \(1.4^2+3.2^2/2=7.08\,\mathrm J\), giving loss 1.92 J.

A particle sticks to a pinned bar

A 0.20 kg clay mass strikes perpendicular to a 0.50 m slender bar at 4 m/s and sticks to its free end. The bar mass is 1.2 kg and its other end is pinned. Find final angular speed and energy lost. Why is angular momentum about the pin the convenient balance?

Reveal the worked solution

The incoming angular momentum is \(0.20(4)(0.50)=0.40\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}\). Combined inertia about the pin is

\[1.2(0.50)^2/3+0.20(0.50)^2=0.150\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}.\]

Thus \(\omega=2.667\,\mathrm{rad/s}\). Final energy is \(0.150(2.667)^2/2=0.533\,\mathrm J\), versus 1.60 J initially. The ideal pin impulse has no moment about the pin, although it can change total linear momentum.

Predict the reaction moment from a tilted mass arrangement

Two 0.25 kg trim masses sit at \((0.12,0,0.08)\) m and its negative relative to a shaft centre. The shaft rotates steadily about positive \(z\) at 30 rad/s. Find \(I_{xz}\), angular momentum, required external moment on the rotor and the moment at twice the speed.

Show the worked solution

Here \(I_{xz}=-2(0.25)(0.12)(0.08)=-0.0048\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\), and \(I_{zz}=2(0.25)(0.12)^2=0.0072\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\). Therefore \(H=(-0.144,0,0.216)\) kg m²/s. The torque \((0,0,30)\times H=(0,-4.32,0)\) N m. Doubling speed doubles angular momentum and its rotation rate, so the required moment magnitude grows fourfold to 17.28 N m. The frame feels the equal and opposite moment.
