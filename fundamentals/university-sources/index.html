Electrodynamics

  • Calculate a coil field without an assumed magnetic path

    University of Cambridge · David Tong, Electromagnetism · §3.2.3 Biot-Savart Law

  • Recover the same fields from different potentials

    The induction chapter used flux and circuit motion. Potentials provide a second construction of the fields and explain which changes leave observations invariant.

    Problem sheet 2 · Test a proposed change of potentials

    University of Cambridge · David Tong, Electromagnetism · §3.2.2 Gauge Transformations

  • Which observer can see a purely magnetic field?

    The plane-wave calculation measured fields and energy flux in one laboratory. Transforming the fields connects measurements made by observers in relative motion.

    Problem sheet 3 · Choose a frame for electric-dominated crossed fields

    University of Cambridge · David Tong, Electromagnetism · §5.3.2 The Electromagnetic Tensor

Waves and optics

  • Read a frequency from moving wavefronts

    The course related propagation speed to wavelength and period at a stationary point. A moving source or receiver required a separate connection between wavefront spacing and measured frequency.

    Problem sheet 1 · Recover a source speed from two microphones

    University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign · Physics 406, Acoustical Physics of Music · Lecture III, part 2 · Doppler effect for a moving source and observer

  • Hear the initial shape in the harmonics of a string

    Travelling pulses and standing frequencies were established. Projecting the release shape onto the normal modes predicts how much of each frequency a pluck excites.

    Problem sheet 1 · Predict which note disappears for an off-centre pluck

    Massachusetts Institute of Technology · 8.03SC, The Physics of Waves · §6.2.1 The String with Fixed Ends; §6.2.4 Plucking a String

  • Follow field amplitude through the critical angle

    Ray refraction located an image. Matching wave amplitudes at the interface also determines power transmission and the penetration of an evanescent field.

    Problem sheet 2 · Distinguish transmission amplitude, flux and penetration

    Delft University of Technology · Interactive Optics · Basic Electromagnetic and Wave Optics, “Properties of the Fresnel Coefficients” and “Total Internal Reflection and Evanescent Waves”

  • When does a beam propagate without diffracting?

    The beam chapter followed light through free space. Applying boundary conditions on both sides of a slab gives the allowed propagation constants and modal cutoff.

    Problem sheet 6 · Choose a slab width for one transverse TE mode

    Massachusetts Institute of Technology · Haus and Melcher, Electromagnetic Fields and Energy · §13.5 Dielectric Waveguides: Optical Fibers, Figures 13.5.1–13.5.4

Thermodynamics and statistical physics

  • Convert an isobaric calorimeter reading into an isochoric one

    Expansion, compressibility and heat capacities appeared separately. Their thermodynamic relation explains how a constant-pressure calorimetry result translates to fixed volume.

    Problem sheet 1 · Test a calorimeter conversion near zero expansion

    University of Edinburgh · Junior Honours Thermodynamics · Topic 9, “How to find the difference in heat capacities, CP − CV”

  • Choose the equilibrium pressure inside a two-phase region

    The coexistence slope described how a phase boundary moves. Equal free energies determine the actual coexistence pressure and the mixture fractions on an isotherm.

    Problem sheet 2 · Correct a trial coexistence pressure from an area residual

    University of Cambridge · David Tong, Statistical Physics · §5.1.1 Phase Equilibrium (Maxwell construction)

  • Predict particle-number fluctuations in an open subsystem

    Energy fluctuations were derived for a system in thermal contact. Allowing particle exchange connects number fluctuations to the chemical-potential response.

    Problem sheet 3 · Infer a chemical potential difference from a fluctuating surface

    University of Cambridge · David Tong, Statistical Physics · §1.4.1 Grand Canonical Ensemble and §1.4.2 Grand Canonical Potential

  • Test the single-frequency model before introducing a spectrum

    Problem sheet 5 · Discriminate two low-temperature laws with a ratio

    Problem sheet 1 · Fold a wave vector into the first zone

    University of Cambridge · David Tong, Solid State Physics · Chapter 2, Band Structure, §2.2.2 (construction of the reciprocal lattice)

  • Count thermally generated vacancies

    The lattice calculation prescribed a defect. Balancing its formation enthalpy with configurational entropy predicts how many vacancies are present at equilibrium.

    Problem sheet 1 · Infer a defect formation enthalpy from two temperatures

    Massachusetts Institute of Technology · 3.46 Photonic Materials and Devices · Notes on Defects in Solids, §3 Point Defect Equilibria

  • Open a gap by doubling a chain’s period

    The existing band examples imposed a periodic potential. A dimerizing chain explains how the electrons themselves can favour a distortion that opens a gap.

    Problem sheet 2 · Recover alternating hoppings from spectroscopy

    University of Cambridge · David Tong, Solid State Physics · Chapter 4, Phonons, §4.1.3 Peierls Transition

Engineering mechanics

  • Creep and relaxation put different constraints on the same bar

    The elastic bar calculation gave its immediate extension. Creep and relaxation require following the same material under different loading constraints over time.

    Problem sheet 1 · Infer a relaxation model from a displacement-hold test

  • Find curvature and stress in a bonded two-material beam

    The bending derivation assumed one material. A bonded section needs a stiffness-weighted neutral axis before the stresses in its two layers can be calculated.

    Problem sheet 2 · Size a symmetric sandwich beam from its bending rigidity

    MIT · 1.050 Solid Mechanics · Chapter 9, §9.5 Stresses within a Composite Beam

  • Assemble a displacement model of a redundant braced joint

    The force method established compatibility. Assembling a truss from individual bars connects compatibility to nodal displacements, support reactions and member forces.

    Problem sheet 5 · Find a mechanism before adding a diagonal spring

    MIT · 3.11 Mechanics of Materials · Trusses, “Matrix analysis of trusses”

Machine dynamics

  • Distinguish sliding-friction decay from a viscous ring-down

    The ring-down example described nonexponential decay. Integrating sliding friction over each half-cycle predicts both the lost amplitude and where a body can stop.

    Problem sheet 1 · Identify a dry guide from three same-side peaks

    IIT Kharagpur · Aircraft Structural Dynamics · Module 4, Lecture 3, Free Vibration of Damped System (Contd.), “Coulomb Damping”

  • Construct angular momentum when the rotation axis is not principal

    The planar model used a single moment of inertia. Rotation about an arbitrary axis requires the inertia tensor to recover the direction of angular momentum.

    Problem sheet 4 · Predict the reaction moment from a tilted mass arrangement

    MIT · 16.07 Dynamics · Lecture L26, 3D Rigid Body Dynamics: The Inertia Tensor

  • A balanced rotor can load its bearings during a turn

    The balance calculation removed eccentric forces. Turning the spin axis introduces a separate bearing load even on a perfectly balanced rotor.

    Problem sheet 5 · Balance the steering moment of a two-flywheel instrument

    MIT · 16.07 Dynamics · Lecture L30, 3D Rigid Body Dynamics: Tops and Gyroscopes, steady precession

Machine elements and contact mechanics

  • Why a single universal joint produces a twice-per-revolution speed fluctuation

    The shaft calculations assumed aligned axes. A universal joint introduces a periodic speed variation whose amplitude depends on shaft angle.

    Problem sheet 1 · Set a joint-angle limit from the driven-shaft speed tolerance

    Jönköping University · Applied Mechanics · §10.20 Shaft couplings, “The Hooke joint” and “Two Hooke joints”

  • Recover the size of a contact from its pressure field

    The applied study already integrated the pressure to obtain a force. The course needed the second-moment derivation to explain how its numerical mesh estimates contact radius and how an optical footprint can check that estimate.

    Problem sheet 2 · Infer a contact load from an optical footprint

    Heidelberg University · Continuum Mechanics · §5.8 · Contact of two elastic spheres (Hertz solution)

  • Find the three speeds and housing reaction of a planetary gearset

    Fixed-axis gears established the tooth-count ratio. A moving carrier adds an independent speed and makes the ring reaction relevant to the torque balance.

    Problem sheet 3 · Trace the power when both the sun and ring move

    Jönköping University · Applied Mechanics · §10.8 Planetary gears, “Seen from the carrier: the Willis equation” and “Torques and the forces on the teeth”

  • Size the working flanks and shear section of a parallel key

    The interference-fit model transmitted torque by friction. A keyed connection requires separate checks of shear and bearing pressure over its engaged length.

    Problem sheet 3 · Can the available hub length carry the specified steady torque?

    Jönköping University · Applied Mechanics · §10.18 Shaft–hub joints, “The parallel key”

Bradley, Numerische Mathematik · §§1.1–1.5 · Gleitkomma-Arithmetik · RWTH Aachen · A. Reusken, Numerische Analysis I · §§2.2.1 and 2.3 · Zahlendarstellungen, Stabilität eines Algorithmus
