When does heating a solid produce stress?

Separate thermal strain from elastic strain and use the support conditions to find displacement and stress.

Prerequisites Axial force balance and Hooke’s law.

Distinguish thermal strain from elastic strain

A steel rod warms by 50 K. If it can expand freely, it grows longer. If its ends are held the same distance apart, it pushes on its supports. The temperature change is identical. How do the supports turn heating into force?

Model a straight, slender rod with uniform cross-section \(A\), small axial strains, free lateral surfaces and no axial body force. Assume constant Young’s modulus \(E\) and linear expansion coefficient \(\alpha\). Let \(\Delta T(x)\) be the temperature rise from the initially stress-free state. A free material element would extend by the fraction \(\alpha\Delta T\). An axial stress \(\sigma\) adds elastic strain \(\sigma/E\). With tension positive, the displacement \(u\) therefore satisfies

\[\frac{du}{dx}=\frac{\sigma}{E}+\alpha\Delta T(x).\]

For this one-way thermal coupling, the temperature field is given by a measurement or a heat balance. The small deformation has negligible influence on heating. It is the thermal strain, rather than temperature alone, that enters the length balance.

Let the supports determine the force

Cut the rod at two nearby cross-sections. Force balance gives \(d(A\sigma)/dx=0\), so the axial stress is constant here even if \(\Delta T\) varies along the length. Integrate the strain from one end to the other to obtain

\[u(L)-u(0)=\frac{\sigma L}{E}+\alpha\int_0^L\Delta T(x)\,dx.\]

If one end is free of traction, the axial force and stress vanish. The second term alone gives the extension. If both ends are fixed, the extension on the left is zero. The stress becomes

\[\sigma=-E\alpha\overline{\Delta T},\]

where the bar denotes the length-average temperature rise. The compressive elastic strain cancels the average thermal strain.

Hold a heated rod at its original length

Take \(L=100\) mm, \(A=20\) mm², \(E=200\) GPa, \(\alpha=12\times10^{-6}\) K⁻¹ and a uniform 50 K rise. The free thermal strain is \(600\times10^{-6}\). The corresponding free extension is

\[0.100\times600\times10^{-6}=60\,\mu\mathrm m.\]

If both ends hold their initial positions, the elastic strain is \(-600\times10^{-6}\). Thus \(\sigma=-120\) MPa and \(N=A\sigma=-2400\) N. Each end support carries a 2.40 kN reaction in the axial direction. This is a pre-buckling elastic estimate. Checking a slender compressed member for buckling requires its bending stiffness and lateral support conditions.

Tutorial exercises

Heat only one end

The temperature rise now varies linearly from 0 K at \(x=0\) to 50 K at \(x=L\). Keep both ends fixed. Find the stress, and the local total strain at each end.

Show the solution

The average rise is 25 K, so \(\sigma=-E\alpha(25\,\mathrm K)=-60\) MPa everywhere. The elastic strain is \(-300\times10^{-6}\). Add local thermal strain to get \(-300\times10^{-6}\) at the cold end and \(+300\times10^{-6}\) at the hot end. These varying strains integrate to zero end-to-end extension.

Allow an initial gap

Fix the left end and leave a 30 µm gap between the right end and a rigid stop. Heat the original rod uniformly. At what temperature rise does contact begin, and what force acts at a 50 K rise?

Show the solution

Before contact \(\sigma=0\), so \(\alpha L\Delta T=30\) µm gives \(\Delta T=25\) K. At 50 K the free extension would be 60 µm. Contact limits it to 30 µm, requiring elastic strain \((30-60)\,\mu\mathrm{m}/(100\,\mathrm{mm})=-300\times10^{-6}\). The stress is \(-60\) MPa and the compressive force has magnitude 1.20 kN.

Optional numerical comparison

A copper-beryllium micromechanical device illustrates the same support dependence in three dimensions. The OneSimulation model heats the part at \(10^8\) W/m³ and loses heat to air at 298 K with \(h=10\) W/(m²·K). Its nearly uniform temperature is 323.673 K. In the matched-board case compare displacement with \(\mathbf u=\alpha\Delta T\,\mathbf x\) and stress with the stress-free prediction. In the rigid-board case compare the largest displacement across meshes. The reported fine-mesh value is 45.41 nm. Only the bonded faces follow the board, so this restraint differs from fixing both ends of a one-dimensional rod. Thermal expansion of a micromechanical device.
