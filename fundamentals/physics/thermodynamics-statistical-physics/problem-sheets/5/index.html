Two black surfaces

Two large parallel black surfaces at 350 K and 300 K face one another across vacuum. Calculate the net radiative flux. Linearize it about 325 K and assess the error. Use \(\sigma=5.6704\times10^{-8}\) W m⁻² K⁻⁴.

Show the transfer calculation

Net flux is

\[\sigma(350^4-300^4)=391.6\,\mathrm{W/m^2}.\]

The linear estimate \(4\sigma(325)^3(50)=389.3\) W/m² is about \(0.6\%\) low. For temperatures \(T_m\pm\Delta T/2\), expanding the fourth powers gives \(4T_m^3\Delta T+T_m(\Delta T)^3\). The omitted positive term explains the bias and shows how the relative error scales as \((\Delta T/T_m)^2\).

The missing calorimeter coefficient

Low-temperature molar measurements on a metal give \(C/T=4.00\) mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² at 2.00 K and \(C/T=10.0\) mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² at 4.00 K. Using \(C=\gamma T+\beta T^3\), extract both coefficients and the atomic Debye temperature. Take \(12\pi^4R/5=1944\) J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Show the separation of contributions

A plot of \(C/T\) against \(T^2\) has slope \(\beta=(10-4)/(16-4)=0.500\) mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻⁴ and intercept \(\gamma=4-0.5(4)=2.00\) mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻². From \(\beta=1944/\Theta_D^3\) obtain

\[\Theta_D=(1944/0.000500)^{1/3}=157\,\mathrm{K}.\]

The electronic term dominates below \(\sqrt{\gamma/\beta}=2.00\) K, provided the same low-temperature normal state persists.

Change the number of spatial dimensions

An idealized large sheet supports one acoustic branch with linear dispersion \(\omega=vk\) in two dimensions. Derive the low-temperature power of the thermal energy and heat capacity. Explain why a freely suspended membrane with a flexural branch may behave differently.

Show the mode count

The two-dimensional reciprocal-space annulus has area \(2\pi k\,dk\), hence \(g(\omega)\propto\omega\) for the linear branch. Integrating \(g(\omega)\hbar\omega/[e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT}-1]\) with \(x=\hbar\omega/k_BT\) gives

\[U_{\rm th}\propto T^3,\]
\[C\propto T^2.\]

A quadratic flexural branch instead gives \(k\,dk\) proportional to \(d\omega\), so the density of states is approximately constant and its contribution is proportional to \(T\) in heat capacity. The temperature exponent diagnoses both dimensionality and dispersion.

Discriminate two low-temperature laws with a ratio

A mole of insulating crystal is measured at fixed volume at 40 K and 20 K. The recorded lattice heat capacities are 0.800 and 0.101 J/(mol K). Compare their ratio with a Debye low-temperature prediction and with an Einstein model of \(\Theta_E=240\) K. Explain what conclusion these two readings support.

Worked solution

The measured ratio \(C_V(20)/C_V(40)=0.12625\) is close to the low-temperature acoustic prediction \((20/40)^3=0.125\). Applying the Einstein formula with \(x=6\) and \(12\) gives 2.237 and 0.02207 J/(mol K), a ratio of 0.00987. Even if one rescales its amplitude to match at 40 K, this proposed Einstein frequency predicts too sharp a fall.

The two measurements support an acoustic-mode contribution over this temperature interval. To establish the asymptotic exponent, take additional low-temperature measurements and examine \(C_V/T^3\), including uncertainty in both the calorimetry and the subtraction of electronic or background heat capacity. The ratio test alone supplies neither the whole phonon spectrum nor a unique microscopic model.
