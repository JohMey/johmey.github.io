Change the gas volume

At fixed temperature an ideal dimerization \(2A\rightleftharpoons A_2\) has \(K=2.00\). At total pressure \(p^\circ\) find the bound-unit fraction and dimer mole fraction. Immediately expand isothermally until all original partial pressures have halved. Find the instantaneous reaction quotient and the preferred direction.

Show the composition and direction

With bound-unit fraction \(\alpha\), equilibrium gives

\[1-\alpha=(1+4Kp/p^\circ)^{-1/2}=1/3.\]

Hence \(\alpha=2/3\) and the dimer mole fraction \(\alpha/(2-\alpha)=1/2\). Because \(Q=a_{A_2}/a_A^2\), halving both partial pressures doubles \(Q\) from 2 to 4 before chemical readjustment. Thus \(\Delta_rG=RT\ln(Q/K)>0\), favouring dissociation. Continuing mechanical readjustment depends on whether volume or pressure is subsequently held fixed.

Two equal heat capacities

Two insulated blocks each have \(C=200\) J/K and start at 320 and 280 K. Find the common temperature, total entropy gain and initial entropy-production rate for conductance 1.50 W/K. What is their temperature difference after 50.0 s?

Show the balance and rate

The final temperature is 300 K. Entropy gain is

\[200\ln[300^2/(320\times280)]=0.891\,\mathrm{J/K}.\]

Initial current is \(1.50(40)=60.0\) W and initial entropy production is \(60(1/280-1/320)=0.0268\) W/K. With equal capacities \(\tau=C/(2G)=66.7\) s. The difference after 50.0 s is

\[40e^{-0.75}=18.9\,\mathrm{K}.\]

The final entropy gain depends only on endpoints, while initial rate and time history depend on conductance.

Determine whether a drift is heat leakage

A trace from two strongly insulated bodies has equal capacities of 150 J/K. Initially the measured temperatures are 330 and 290 K. After the difference has vanished both read 309 K. Estimate the apparent net heat exchange with the surroundings. Can increasing internal contact conductance explain it? Give an independent instrumental diagnosis.

Show the energy inventory

For equal capacities the initial arithmetic mean is 310 K and must stay fixed under pure internal heat exchange. Final readings imply \(\Delta U=150(309-330)+150(309-290)=-300\) J. Finite contact conductance can change the approach rate, but its internal current cancels in the total energy balance. A common thermometer drift of \(-1\) K between beginning and end would create the same apparent loss. Calibration against an independent temperature reference, or an enclosure heat-flux measurement, distinguishes these possibilities.

Extract work from a finite hot body

A body of constant heat capacity 1.00 kJ/K cools from 500 K to 300 K. A large reservoir stays at 300 K. Find the maximum work recoverable, the heat delivered to the reservoir and the entropy generated by direct thermal contact. Then predict how much of the available work remains if cooling stops at 400 K.

Worked solution

For a reversible sequence of small engines the instantaneous efficiency is 1 − T₀/T, where T is the body's current temperature. The body supplies −C dT on cooling, so Wₘₐₓ = C[(Tᵢ − T₀) − T₀ ln(Tᵢ/T₀)] = 46.75 kJ. Applying the initial Carnot efficiency to the entire 200 kJ would overestimate the work because the hot-side temperature falls.

The remaining 153.25 kJ enters the reservoir. Its entropy gain is 153.25/300 = 0.5108 kJ/K, equal to the magnitude of C ln(300/500) lost by the body. With direct contact, the reservoir instead receives all 200 kJ and total entropy production is 0.6667 − 0.5108 = 0.1558 kJ/K. Multiplication by 300 K recovers the 46.75 kJ of lost work.

At 400 K the body still has C[100 − 300 ln(4/3)] = 13.70 kJ available relative to the reservoir. The reversible work already extracted is therefore 33.05 kJ. Additivity of these two stages provides a check on both the logarithm and the choice of reference temperature.

Identify a heat capacity and a bridge conductance

Two insulated blocks start at 360 and 300 K. The cooler block has heat capacity 1500 J/K. The capacity-weighted equilibrium temperature is 320 K, and a fitted temperature difference decays by a factor \(e\) in 125 s. Infer the unknown hot-block heat capacity and the conductance. Predict the two observables after doubling that conductance in the downloadable model.

Work through the solution

Energy conservation gives \(C_h(360-320)=1500(320-300)\), hence \(C_h=750\) J/K. Subtracting the two block balances gives

\[1/\tau=G(1/C_h+1/C_c).\]

Therefore \(G=[125(1/750+1/1500)]^{-1}=4.00\) W/K. Doubling \(G\) to 8.00 W/K gives 62.5 s and leaves the final temperature at 320 K. Equilibrium data alone would identify the heat-capacity ratio while leaving the conductance undetermined.
