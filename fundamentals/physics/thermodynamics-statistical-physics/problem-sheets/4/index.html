Compare gases in a molecular leak

Helium and argon are separately admitted at equal number density and temperature to the same tiny hole in a thin wall. With atomic masses 4.00 and 40.0 u, find their number-flux and mass-flux ratios. Determine which species is enriched in the emerging beam from an equimolar mixture.

Show the flux weighting

The outgoing number flux is \(J=n\sqrt{k_BT/(2\pi m)}\), so

\[J_{\rm He}/J_{\rm Ar}=\sqrt{40/4}=3.16.\]

Multiplying by particle mass reverses the trend, giving helium-to-argon mass flux ratio \(0.316\). In an equimolar mixture the predicted helium number fraction among emerging atoms is

\[3.16/(1+3.16)=0.760.\]

This conclusion uses the collisionless aperture regime. A bulk hydrodynamic flow would entrain both components differently.

Use variance without assuming a point release

The standard deviation of a dye profile grows from 0.300 to 0.500 mm in 200 s. The centroid simultaneously shifts by 0.400 mm. Infer the diffusivity and mean drift speed, then predict both observables after another 200 s under constant conditions.

Show the moment calculation

Variance grows from \(0.0900\) to \(0.250\) mm², giving \(D=(0.250-0.0900)/(2\times200)=4.00\times10^{-4}\) mm²/s. The mean drift is

\[0.400/200=0.00200\,\mathrm{mm/s}.\]

After another 200 s the variance is \(0.410\) mm², or standard deviation \(0.640\) mm. The centroid is displaced by a total \(0.800\) mm from its starting point. A second moment about a fixed origin would also contain the squared centroid displacement and overestimate diffusion.

Choose a faster concentration test

A closed 3.00 mm channel supports initial concentration modulations proportional to \(\cos(\pi x/L)\) and \(\cos(3\pi x/L)\). For \(D=5.0\times10^{-10}\) m²/s find both half-decay times. Explain why an apparently similar measurement using a sinusoid could violate the boundary condition.

Show the mode selection

No-flux ends require \(\partial_xc=0\) at \(0,L\), satisfied by the cosines. Substitution into diffusion gives

\[t_{1/2}=L^2\ln2/(D n^2\pi^2).\]

The fundamental takes \(1264\) s and the third harmonic \(140\) s. The shorter scale drives a larger flux gradient. A simple \(\sin(n\pi x/L)\) instead has nonzero end slopes, corresponding to an exchange of material at the walls. A measured decay cannot be used with the same boundary model unless the prepared mode actually satisfies it.
