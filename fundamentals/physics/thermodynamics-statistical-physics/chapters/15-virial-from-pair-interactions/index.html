How a pair potential changes the gas pressure

A low-density gas can measure its own intermolecular forces. At a prescribed temperature the first deviation from ideal behaviour is a volume. Its sign tells us whether overlap exclusion or attraction makes the larger contribution.

Separate independent pairs from correlated pairs

Let \(N\) classical particles occupy volume \(V\), with a total potential energy equal to a sum of short-range pair potentials \(u(r_{ij})\). Momentum integrals give the ideal-gas factors. In the remaining positional integral write each pair's Boltzmann weight as \(e^{-\beta u}=1+f(r)\). The auxiliary function \(f=e^{-\beta u}-1\) vanishes where particles do not interact. In a large vessel the positional integral with one \(f\) is \(V^{N-1}\int f(\boldsymbol r)\,d^3r\). One particle of the pair may be placed anywhere and its neighbour's relative coordinate is sampled against \(f\). There are \(N(N-1)/2\) possible unordered pairs.

In the low-density expansion of \(\ln Z\), independent repetitions of separate pairs cancel against products from the logarithm. The first connected correction is \(N(N-1)\int f/(2V)\). This is a density expansion of the free energy per particle. The total correction need not be small for a macroscopic sample. Differentiating with \(p=k_BT(\partial\ln Z/\partial V)_{T,N}\) supplies a minus sign from the reciprocal volume. In the thermodynamic limit with number density \(\rho=N/V\),

\[\frac{p}{\rho k_BT}=1+B_2(T)\rho+O(\rho^2),\]
\[B_2=-\tfrac12\int f\,d^3r=-2\pi\int_0^\infty f(r)r^2\,dr.\]

An impenetrable core gives \(f=-1\). An attractive annulus gives \(f>0\). The sign of \(B_2\) follows from their integrated volumes, including the spherical shell factor \(r^2\).

Integrate a square well

Set \(u=\infty\) for \(r<d\), \(u=-\epsilon\) for \(d<r<\lambda d\), and \(u=0\) outside. The two nonzero contributions are constants integrated over spherical volumes. With \(B_{\rm core}=2\pi d^3/3\), obtain

\[B_2=B_{\rm core}\{1-(\lambda^3-1)[e^{\epsilon/(k_BT)}-1]\}.\]

A sphere of diameter \(d\) has actual volume \(\pi d^3/6\). The hard-core coefficient is four times that volume. The forbidden positions concern the centre of a second particle, and the factor of one half avoids counting an interacting pair twice. For \(\lambda=1.5\) and \(k_BT/\epsilon=2\), the ratio \(B_2/B_{\rm core}=1-2.375(e^{0.5}-1)\simeq-0.541\). A dilute sample with \(\rho B_{\rm core}=0.02\) consequently has \(p/(\rho k_BT)\simeq0.9892\) at this order.

Locate the temperature at which the slope vanishes

Set \(B_2=0\) and solve for the Boyle temperature. The result is \(k_BT_B/\epsilon=1/\ln[1+1/(\lambda^3-1)]\), or 2.846 for the preceding range. Vary the width of the well. A larger attraction volume can offset the hard core even when the temperature is high. At \(T_B\) the pressure curve has zero first density correction. Three-particle contributions can still produce curvature. At very low temperatures pair binding can become strong enough that even a superficially low density fails to justify truncation.

Tutorial on inferring the range

In a square-well model the measured Boyle temperature is

\[k_BT_B/\epsilon=2.\]

Determine \(\lambda\), then predict the sign of the pressure correction when the temperature is doubled.

Work through the solution

Rearrangement gives \(\lambda^3=1+1/(e^{1/2}-1)=2.5415\), hence \(\lambda\simeq1.365\). Doubling the temperature decreases the attractive Boltzmann factor and leaves the hard-core term fixed. Substitution at reduced temperature 4 gives

\[B_2/B_{\rm core}\simeq1-1.5415(0.2840)=0.562.\]

At sufficiently low density the pressure exceeds the ideal prediction.

Can a negative \(B_2\) at one temperature fix how much of the molecular attraction comes from its depth and how much from its range?

Work through the solution

The coefficient in the square-well model depends on the product \((\lambda^3-1)[e^{\epsilon/k_BT}-1]\). Many combinations of width and depth give the same product. Measurements at two temperatures can constrain both parameters if the core diameter is independently known and the model is appropriate. Additional temperatures expose inadequacies in the assumed well shape.

Take two measured compressibility factors \(Z=0.9950,0.9900\) at \(\rho B_{\rm core}=0.010,0.020\) at the same temperature. Estimate \(B_2/B_{\rm core}\) and check consistency of the truncation with these two measurements.

Work through the solution

The slopes from the ideal limit are \((Z-1)/(\rho B_{\rm core})=-0.50\) for both points. Their common value agrees with a leading pair correction and shows no detectable curvature at the stated precision. It provides no bound on higher-order terms outside that measured density interval.
