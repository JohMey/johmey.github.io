Measure an interaction by its low-energy scattering length

A cloud of cold, distinguishable atoms can scatter as if it had a size much greater than the range of its mutual force. Derive that length by matching a radial wave on the two sides of a simple potential well.

Replace a trajectory by a flux

For a localized central interaction an incident plane wave at large distance can be written as the sum of its incoming state and an outgoing spherical wave of amplitude \(f(\theta)\). The spherical-wave intensity falls as \(1/r^2\). Multiplying its radial probability current by the detector area \(r^2d\Omega\) and dividing by the incident current leaves \(d\sigma/d\Omega=|f|^2\). This ratio is a geometrical area per unit solid angle even though the motion does not supply a definite classical impact parameter.

A partial wave with angular momentum \(\ell\) feels the centrifugal term \(\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)/(2\mu r^2)\). When \(kR\ll1\) for an interaction of range \(R\), higher partial waves usually reach the interior only weakly. In the absence of higher-wave resonances keep the \(\ell=0\) wave. Far outside, its radial part is proportional to \(\sin(kr+\delta_0)\). The phase shift measures how the interaction changes the oscillation relative to the free solution.

Match the zero-energy solutions

Choose \(V=-V_0\) for \(r<R\) and zero outside. At zero collision energy the exterior radial equation is \(u''=0\), hence \(u_{\rm out}=A(r-a_s)\). The extrapolated intercept \(a_s\) defines the scattering length. Inside the well the regular solution is \(B\sin(Kr)\), \(K=\sqrt{2\mu V_0}/\hbar\). Continuity of \(u,u'\) at \(R\) allows their amplitude to cancel from the logarithmic derivative:

\[K\cot(KR)=\frac{1}{R-a_s},\]
\[\frac{a_s}{R}=1-\frac{\tan x}{x},\]
\[x=KR .\]

In the weak-well limit \(\tan x=x+x^3/3+\cdots\), so \(a_s\simeq-Rx^2/3\). At \(x=\pi/2\) a state reaches the bound-state threshold; the exterior zero-energy slope vanishes relative to its boundary value. The extrapolated intercept diverges. Its sign on either side of this crossing records the phase of the wave, rather than the sign of a physical radius.

Keep the finite-energy bound on the cross section

Expanding \(\sin(kr+\delta_0)\) at small \(k\) gives \(k\cot\delta_0\simeq-1/a_s\), neglecting effective-range terms. The outgoing-wave amplitude and the integrated cross section of the isotropic component are

\[f_0=\frac{1}{k\cot\delta_0-ik},\]
\[\sigma_0\simeq\frac{4\pi a_s^2}{1+k^2a_s^2}.\]

Far from resonance with \(|ka_s|\ll1\), \(\sigma_0\simeq4\pi a_s^2\). At fixed nonzero \(k\), increasing \(|a_s|\) instead takes \(\sigma_0\) towards \(4\pi/k^2\). Keeping the imaginary term from outgoing flux is essential in this regime. A finite range adds terms of order \(k^2\) to the real denominator. Identical atoms require exchange symmetrization and can have different allowed partial waves.

For \(x=\pi/4\), \(a_s/R=1-4/\pi=-0.2732\). With \(kR=0.10\) the cross section is about \(0.937R^2\). At \(x=\pi\), \(a_s=R\), and the same incoming wave gives \(12.44R^2\). Thus increasing attraction does not produce a monotone core size. It changes a phase accumulated inside the well.

Tutorial on an ambiguous area

A low-energy measurement at \(kR=0.10\) finds \(\sigma=2\pi R^2\). Infer \(|a_s|/R\) and identify what measurement is still missing.

Work through the solution

Writing \(b=|a_s|/R\), the equation \(2\pi=4\pi b^2/(1+0.01b^2)\) gives \(b=1/\sqrt{1.99}=0.709\). The sign disappears in this cross section. A measurement sensitive to the scattering phase, or independent information about a near-threshold bound state, is needed to determine it.

A measured low-energy cross section equals half the unitarity value \(4\pi/k^2\) at nonzero \(k\). Infer the magnitude of the scattering length. Can this observation establish which side of a resonance the depth lies on?

Work through the solution

Set \(k^2a_s^2/(1+k^2a_s^2)=1/2\), giving \(|a_s|=1/k\). The total cross section depends on \(a_s^2\) at this order. A phase-sensitive measurement of the scattering amplitude or independent knowledge of a bound state can resolve its sign. This inversion also presumes effective-range corrections are small.

Obtain the leading scattering length for a shallow well and predict how the zero-energy cross section changes when the well depth doubles.

Work through the solution

Expand \(\tan x=x+x^3/3+\cdots\), giving \(a_s/R\simeq-x^2/3\). Since \(x^2=2mV_0R^2/\hbar^2\), the scattering length scales linearly and negatively with well depth. In the weak-scattering limit \(\sigma\simeq4\pi a_s^2\), so doubling a sufficiently shallow depth quadruples the cross section.
