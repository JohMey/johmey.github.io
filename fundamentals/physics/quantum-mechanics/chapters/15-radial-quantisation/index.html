Why the Coulomb radial series terminates

The hydrogen ground state admits a simple exponential guess. To understand whole spectral series, derive the radial recurrence and follow the two boundary conditions that force quantized energies.

Reduce the relative motion

A Coulomb attraction \(V=-\kappa/r\) depends only on the separation of the electron and the positive centre. Separating the centre of mass leaves the reduced mass \(\mu\). Write the relative wavefunction as \(u(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)/r\), with a normalized angular function. The \(r^2\) in the volume element cancels the inverse square of the radial denominator, so normalization is

\[\int_0^\infty |u|^2 dr=1.\]

The radial equation is

\[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}u''+\left[\frac{\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)}{2\mu r^2}-\frac{\kappa}{r}\right]u=Eu .\]

For a bound state define \(k=\sqrt{-2\mu E}/\hbar\) and \(\rho=2kr\). At large radius the constant binding energy gives a decaying factor \(e^{-\rho/2}\). At small radius the centrifugal term yields powers \(\rho^{\ell+1}\) and \(\rho^{-\ell}\). The regular Coulomb problem selects the first power. For \(\ell=0\) this enforces the regular three-dimensional boundary condition \(u(0)=0\). These two endpoint behaviours suggest factoring \(u=\rho^{\ell+1}e^{-\rho/2}v(\rho)\).

Let normalizability select the energy

Differentiate the product twice and cancel the constant and inverse-square terms with the radial equation. The remaining polynomial equation involves only the dimensionless attraction \(\lambda=\mu\kappa/(\hbar^2 k)\):

\[\rho v''+(2\ell+2-\rho)v'+(\lambda-\ell-1)v=0 .\]

Insert \(v=\sum_{j=0}^\infty a_j\rho^j\) and equate equal powers. The derivative term proportional to \(a_{j+1}\) contains both the factor \(j+1\) and the angular factor \(j+2\ell+2\).

\[a_{j+1}=\frac{j+\ell+1-\lambda}{(j+1)(j+2\ell+2)}a_j .\]

A generic regular series has large-order coefficient ratio approaching \(1/j\); its growing exponential component eventually overwhelms the front factor \(e^{-\rho/2}\). For the regular Coulomb solution the coefficient of that growing component vanishes when the series terminates. A nonnegative integer \(n_r\) makes the numerator zero at \(j=n_r\). Thus \(\lambda=n_r+\ell+1=n\), and

\[E_n=-\frac{\mu\kappa^2}{2\hbar^2n^2}.\]

Here \(n_r\) counts radial nodes in the open interval. Angular nodes and radial nodes enter the same energy label in this particular potential. Spin, fine structure, finite nuclear size and external fields are absent from this calculation.

Distinguish two equal energies spatially

For \(n=2,\ell=0\), the recurrence gives

\[a_1/a_0=-1/2.\]

With \(a=\hbar^2/(\mu\kappa)\), this state has \(\rho=r/a\), so its radial probability vanishes at \(r=2a\). For \(n=2,\ell=1\), the numerator vanishes already at \(j=0\). Here the radial probability is proportional to \(r^4e^{-r/a}\), with a maximum at \(4a\) and no internal radial zero. The two preparations can yield the same spectroscopic energy but different radial histograms.

For an ideal hydrogenic ion with nuclear charge twice as large and the same reduced mass, \(\kappa\) doubles and \(a\) halves. Every radial feature just discussed moves to half its old radius. Each binding energy magnitude increases by four. For a Rydberg energy of 13.6 eV at charge one, the \(n=3\) to \(n=2\) photon has energy \(13.6(1/4-1/9)=1.89\) eV, rising to 7.56 eV for charge two.

Tutorial on a third radial state

For \(n=3,\ell=1\), obtain the first coefficient ratio, the radial node and its radius in units of \(a\).

Work through the solution

The recurrence at \(j=0\) gives

\[(2-3)/(2+2)=-1/4.\]

The next numerator vanishes, so \(v=a_0(1-\rho/4)\). The node is \(\rho=4\). Because \(k=1/(3a)\), the physical radius is

\[r=\rho/(2k)=6a.\]

Compute the ratio of the most probable 2p radius to its expectation value. Why can those two characteristic radii differ for the same orbital?

Work through the solution

With \(x=Zr/a_0\) the radial density is \(x^4e^{-x}/24\). Its derivative vanishes at \(x=4\), whereas the mean is

\[\int_0^\infty x^5e^{-x}dx/24=5.\]

Thus the ratio is \(4/5\). The long tail shifts the mean above the mode. A local spatial probability density would be a different distribution again because it lacks the integrated spherical shell factor.

For an ideal Coulomb centre with \(Z=2\), predict the ratio of the \(n=3\) to \(n=2\) binding energies and the photon energy released from level three to level two in units of the hydrogen Rydberg. Neglect changes in reduced mass.

Work through the solution

Binding magnitudes are \(Z^2\mathrm{Ry}/n^2\). Their ratio is \(4/9\), and the positive emitted energy is

\[4\mathrm{Ry}(1/4-1/9)=5\mathrm{Ry}/9.\]

The allowed orbital changes would also need to satisfy a radiative selection rule before an actual emission line appears.
