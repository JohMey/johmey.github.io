From first principles

Turn a preparation ensemble into a density matrix

Suppose a preparation delivers normalized state \(|\psi_j\rangle\) with classical probability \(w_j\), where the weights sum to one. The Born probabilities for a measurement outcome with projector \(\Pi\) must be averaged over the unrecorded preparation. Use the identity \(\langle\psi_j|\Pi|\psi_j\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|\Pi)\) and linearity of the trace:

\[P(\Pi)=\sum_jw_j\langle\psi_j|\Pi|\psi_j\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\Pi),\]
\[\rho=\sum_jw_j|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j| .\]

In an energy basis the diagonal entries determine energy populations. To measure coherence, project onto \(|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\). Multiplication and unit trace give

\[P(+)=\tfrac12(\rho_{00}+\rho_{11}+\rho_{01}+\rho_{10})=\tfrac12+\operatorname{Re}\rho_{01}.\]

Thus equal energy populations can correspond either to perfectly coherent superposition or to an incoherent mixture. For unitary individual evolutions \(\rho(t)=U\rho(0)U^\dagger\); random relative phases across the ensemble can suppress the average off-diagonal entry while preserving both populations. Tomography in a further phase-shifted basis accesses the imaginary component.

Background · Density operator
\[\rho=\sum_j p_j|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|\]

A physical state is positive and has trace one. Different statistical preparations can give identical density operators and measurement predictions.

Derivation and applications

Separate reversible phase dispersion from irreversible decay with an echo

An ensemble of spins can lose average phase coherence even when every individual spin evolves unitarily. Start each spin in an equal-amplitude superposition. In a frame rotating at the reference frequency, assume each spin has a time-independent angular detuning \(\delta\) drawn from a Gaussian with zero mean and standard deviation \(\sigma\). Ignore energy relaxation during the measurement. Under free evolution, the two components accumulate a phase difference \(\delta t\). The normalized transverse coherence is the characteristic function of the detuning distribution:

\[C(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\exp[-\delta^2/(2\sigma^2)]e^{-i\delta t}.\]

Completing the square in the Gaussian exponent or Fourier transforming the normalized Gaussian gives

\[C_{\rm free}(t)=e^{-\sigma^2t^2/2},\]
\[P_{+x}(t)=\tfrac12[1+\operatorname{Re}C_{\rm free}(t)].\]

Now apply an ideal instantaneous \(\pi\) rotation about \(x\) at time \(\tau\). It exchanges the two levels, reversing the sign of subsequent relative phase accumulation in the toggling frame. An individual spin accumulates \(\delta\tau-\delta(t-\tau)\) before readout at \(t\ge\tau\). Gaussian averaging yields

\[|C_{\rm echo}(t)|=e^{-\sigma^2(2\tau-t)^2/2}.\]

At \(t=2\tau\) the static phase dispersion refocuses for every detuning. A separate independent exponential homogeneous decay factor \(e^{-t/T_2}\) would survive in the echo amplitude. Detunings changing appreciably between the two halves also spoil the cancellation. The echo therefore measures which part of an apparent decay remembers its earlier phase.

Worked example

Predict recovery in a broadened sample

Change the inputs and investigate this derivation.

Take \(\sigma/(2\pi)=1.00\) kHz. At \(t=0.500\) ms the unrefocused coherence is \(e^{-\pi^2/2}=0.00719\), so

\[P_{+x}=0.5036.\]

With a \(\pi\) pulse at \(0.250\) ms, the same static ensemble instead has ideal echo magnitude one at \(0.500\) ms. The two experiments distinguish rapid loss of ensemble contrast from loss of the individual phase information.

Second application

Infer a homogeneous decay and a timing tolerance

Change the inputs and investigate this derivation.

Suppose echoes at \(2.00\) and \(4.00\) ms have normalized magnitudes \(0.800\) and \(0.640\). Their ratio is \(0.800\), giving \(T_2=-2.00\,\mathrm{ms}/\ln0.800=8.96\) ms. If the readout is displaced by \(0.100\) ms from the true echo, static broadening with the previous \(\sigma\) reduces its contrast by another factor \(e^{-(2\pi\,0.100)^2/2}=0.821\). Timing and homogeneous decay enter separately in this inference.

Two preparations with identical energy populations

Prepare a two-level system with states \(|0\rangle\) and \(|1\rangle\). One source always prepares \((|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\). Another prepares \(|0\rangle\) or \(|1\rangle\) at random with equal probability. Measuring in the displayed basis gives identical frequencies for the two sources. After projection onto \(|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\), however, the first source always gives plus and the second gives plus on half the trials. An adequate description must store more than the populations seen in one basis.

Define \(\rho=\sum_jp_j|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|\) for a preparation ensemble. Probabilities follow from \(P(Q)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho Q)\) for a projector \(Q\). The trace is one, Hermiticity makes expectation values real and positivity ensures probabilities are nonnegative. The first preparation has all matrix entries equal to \(1/2\). The second has diagonal entries \(1/2\) and off-diagonal entries zero. Although a density matrix can be decomposed into ensembles in multiple ways, every measurement prediction depends only on the matrix itself.

Coherence can be lost to an unobserved system

Consider a pure joint state \((|0\rangle|e_0\rangle+|1\rangle|e_1\rangle)/\sqrt2\) of system and environment. Average over any complete environment basis, equivalently take the partial trace. The system retains equal populations, and its off-diagonal element is \(\rho_{01}=\langle e_1|e_0\rangle/2\). If the environment states become orthogonal, they carry enough information to distinguish the two alternatives and system-only interference disappears. The combined system can still be pure.

A simple dephasing model multiplies off-diagonal elements by \(e^{-t/T_2}\), leaving populations fixed. In a rotating frame that removes the known level splitting, initial plus preparation then gives

\[P_+(t)=\frac{1+e^{-t/T_2}}2,\]
\[\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho^2=\frac{1+e^{-2t/T_2}}2 .\]

The first expression follows by projecting onto plus; the second is the sum of diagonal entries of \(\rho^2\). Purity ranges from one for a pure state to one half for the completely mixed two-level state. Population relaxation would change the diagonal entries too. Measuring both the energy basis and the interference basis helps decide whether the simpler model is adequate.

An interference record

Following a calibrated plus preparation, a measurement in the rotating frame gives \(P_+=0.700\) after 30.0 μs. The contrast is \(2P_+-1=0.400\), giving \(T_2=-30.0/\ln(0.400)=32.7\) μs. The inferred purity is

\[(1+0.400^2)/2=0.580.\]

At 60.0 μs an exponential model gives contrast \(0.160\) and \(P_+=0.580\). A different next value can signal nonexponential noise or a misestimated coherent phase.

A single interference quadrature cannot distinguish reduced visibility from uncompensated precession. To test the rotating frame assumption, vary the final projection phase and fit the full fringe. Its phase reports the coherent rotation; its amplitude reports visibility. Combining this with an energy-basis measurement determines the full Bloch vector and makes the purity estimate meaningful even when populations drift.

Purity estimates from finite counts can also be misleading when each fitted probability is treated independently. The reconstructed Bloch vector must have length at most one for a physical density operator. Longer vectors indicate sampling fluctuations or inconsistent calibrations. Increasing repetitions narrows counting uncertainty, while rotating a known reference state helps diagnose systematic errors among measurement axes.

Energy relaxation places a bound on phase memory

Pure dephasing leaves populations fixed. A two-level system coupled to a cold environment can also lose energy by relaxing from excited state \(|1\rangle\) to ground state \(|0\rangle\). Suppose the probability for an initially excited system to survive time \(t\) is \(s=e^{-t/T_1}\). A simple system–environment evolution sends \(|1,E_0\rangle\) to \(\sqrt s|1,E_0\rangle+\sqrt{1-s}|0,E_1\rangle\) and leaves \(|0,E_0\rangle\) unchanged. The two environment states represent absence or presence of a released excitation and are orthogonal.

For an initial superposition only its unrelaxed excited amplitude shares the original environment state with the ground amplitude. Partial tracing consequently multiplies the off-diagonal system element by \(\sqrt s\), while the excited-state probability is multiplied by \(s\). Additional independent Markovian phase noise can multiply the off-diagonal element by \(e^{-t/T_\phi}\). In a rotating frame the resulting evolution is

\[\rho_{11}(t)=e^{-t/T_1}\rho_{11}(0),\]
\[\rho_{01}(t)=e^{-t/T_2}\rho_{01}(0),\]
\[\frac1{T_2}=\frac1{2T_1}+\frac1{T_\phi}.\]

Thus even a perfectly stable transition frequency cannot make coherence last indefinitely when population relaxes. In this two-level model \(T_2\le2T_1\), with equality if there is no additional pure dephasing. At finite bath temperature upward jumps also occur and populations approach a nonzero equilibrium excitation. A multilevel environment or nonexponential noise can require a different fitting model.

Separate two decay mechanisms using independent preparations

First prepare the excited state and measure population decay; suppose it gives \(T_1=40.0\) μs. Prepare \(|+\rangle\) independently and fit fringe contrast to obtain \(T_2=50.0\) μs. The pure-dephasing rate is \(1/50.0-1/80.0=0.00750\) μs⁻¹, corresponding to \(T_\phi=133\) μs. At \(t=40.0\) μs after plus preparation, the excited population is \(e^{-1}/2=0.184\), and the real coherence in the calibrated rotating frame is

\[e^{-0.8}/2=0.225.\]

Then

\[P_+=1/2+\mathrm{Re}\rho_{01}=0.725.\]

The reconstructed purity is \(\rho_{00}^2+\rho_{11}^2+2|\rho_{01}|^2=0.801\) to rounding. As time grows, coherence vanishes but the cold-bath model returns the system to the pure ground state, so purity ultimately rises back to one. This differs qualitatively from pure dephasing of an initial plus state, whose limiting purity is one half. An independently measured \(T_2>2T_1\) would call for checking the data and assumptions about temperature, state assignment, fit functions and time-dependent noise before assigning an unphysical negative pure-dephasing rate.

The two independent preparations can be checked against a single density matrix at an additional time. Starting in plus, a general measurement along a transverse direction gives a cosine fringe whose offset is one half regardless of the energy populations, while its amplitude measures the transverse coherence. A separate energy-basis measurement supplies the population imbalance. Together they determine the full Bloch-vector length, which should never exceed one. If independently fitted transverse and longitudinal decay curves imply a greater length at some time, the combined fitted model cannot represent a physical quantum state there even if each measured curve looks plausible in isolation.

Checking positivity across the fitted interval also constrains long extrapolations from records that cover only a small fraction of a relaxation time.
