Quantum mechanics · Notation and assumptions

Read each defining equation with its assumptions and units, then follow the link to the argument that gives the equation its meaning.

Commutator

\[[A,B]=AB-BA\]

Order matters. The expectation of the commutator controls a lower bound on the product of uncertainties when the state lies in the relevant operator domains.

Derivation · Operators and the quantum harmonic oscillator

Density operator

\[\rho=\sum_j p_j|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|\]

A physical state is positive and has trace one. Different statistical preparations can give identical density operators and measurement predictions.

Derivation · Density matrices and loss of phase

Probability current

\[j=(\hbar/m)\operatorname{Im}(\psi^*\partial_x\psi)\]

This expression assumes a scalar particle with no vector potential. Transmission probabilities require outgoing relative to incoming current, even when their wave numbers differ.

Derivation · Scattering and tunnelling in one dimension

Scattering length

\[k\cot\delta_0=-1/a+O(k^2)\]

This sets the low-energy s-wave scattering scale for a short-range potential. Its sign carries phase information, and its size can greatly exceed the interaction range.

Derivation · Measure an interaction by its low-energy scattering length

State normalisation

\[\int |\psi(x)|^2\,dx=1\]

For a normalisable one-dimensional state, squared modulus is probability per length. Stationary incoming scattering states require a flux or continuum normalisation.

Derivation · Wave packets and probability current

Variational energy

\[E[\psi]=\langle\psi|H|\psi\rangle/\langle\psi|\psi\rangle\]

For an admissible nonzero trial state and a Hamiltonian bounded below, this is an upper bound on the lowest energy. A good energy can hide a less accurate state.
