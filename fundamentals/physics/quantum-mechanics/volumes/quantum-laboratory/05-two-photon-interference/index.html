Read wave-packet overlap from a coincidence dip

Send one photon into each of two input ports of a balanced lossless beam splitter. Two alternatives lead to one click at each output. Derive the coincidence probability when their wave packets overlap only partially, then determine how a timing scan separates pulse duration from distinguishability in another degree of freedom.

Track the two indistinguishable alternatives

Choose input creation operators \(a^\dagger,b^\dagger\) and outputs \(c^\dagger,d^\dagger\). A possible symmetric unitary convention maps \(a^\dagger\) to \((c^\dagger+i d^\dagger)/\sqrt2\) and \(b^\dagger\) to \((i c^\dagger+d^\dagger)/\sqrt2\). For input \(a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle\), expand the product. The two one-in-each-output terms have coefficients \(1/2\) and \(i^2/2=-1/2\). They cancel for otherwise identical photons. The same-port outputs remain and their squared probabilities add to one after using the \(\sqrt2\) normalisation of a two-photon mode. The photons do not interact with each other; bosonic amplitudes for indistinguishable output records interfere.

Allow the internal wave packets to differ

Let the photons carry normalised internal states \(f,g\), which may include arrival time, polarisation and spectrum. In the coincidence sector the two possible output states are proportional to \(c^\dagger_f d^\dagger_g|0\rangle\) and \(c^\dagger_g d^\dagger_f|0\rangle\). Their overlap is \(\langle f|g\rangle \langle g|f\rangle=|\langle f|g\rangle|^2\). Squaring the difference of their amplitudes gives

\[P_{\rm coinc}=\tfrac12(1-|\langle f|g\rangle|^2).\]

Orthogonal internal states give one half, the value obtained by adding alternative paths as independent probabilities. A common overall optical phase of either photon leaves the modulus unchanged. Delay matters because it changes the overlap in time, even without a locked optical phase between the two sources.

Background · Can a polariser restore a missing coincidence dip?

Prepare one horizontally polarised and one vertically polarised photon in otherwise identical temporal and spatial modes. Their internal overlap is zero, giving the distinguishable coincidence probability of one half at a balanced splitter. Put identical 45° polarisers in the two input arms. Each photon passes with probability one half. Conditional on both passing, their polarisation states now agree.

\[P(\text{two pass})=\frac14,\]
\[P(\text{coincidence}\mid\text{two pass, zero delay})=0.\]

At large delay the conditional coincidence probability returns to one half, so the coincidence probability per incident pair after the polarisers is one eighth. The dip can regain ideal visibility while the available event rate falls. Reporting the transmission or raw count rate along with visibility distinguishes filtering from an improvement in the source brightness.

Compute a dip with a specified width convention

Take a transform-limited Gaussian amplitude proportional to \(e^{-t^2/(4\sigma^2)}\). Its detection-time probability has standard deviation \(\sigma\). Delay one photon by \(\tau\). Completing the square in the overlap integral gives amplitude overlap \(e^{-\tau^2/(8\sigma^2)}\) and squared overlap \(e^{-\tau^2/(4\sigma^2)}\). If both photons also have linear polarisations separated by \(\alpha\), multiply the squared overlap by \(\cos^2\alpha\). Thus the dip has full width at half depth \(4\sigma\sqrt{\ln2}\), independent of \(\alpha\) as long as a dip remains measurable.

For \(\sigma=80\) fs and \(\alpha=30^\circ\), the coincidence probability is 0.125 at zero delay and approaches 0.5 at long delay. The half-depth level is 0.3125 at \(|\tau|\simeq133\) fs, giving full width about 266 fs. A width measured at probability 0.25 instead would not in general represent half depth. Quote the baseline, minimum and width convention if comparing different measurements.

Decide what a coincidence counter can identify

Adjust pulse duration and relative polarisation. Study peak-to-baseline depth before using the width to infer duration. A reduced visibility might equally arise from unobserved spectral distinguishability, multi-photon contamination or uncorrelated background counts. Blocking each input arm provides an independent accidental-count estimate. The ideal probability plotted assumes exactly one photon in each input and observation of both outputs irrespective of arrival time within the accepted window. A narrow coincidence window makes detection conditioning part of the model.

Tutorial

Orthogonal linear polarisations yield no dip even at zero delay. Does a slower detector that cannot resolve polarisation automatically restore it?

Work through the solution

No. The outgoing internal states remain orthogonal. Summing over an unobserved label is equivalent to tracing it out and removes the interference term. Deliberate projection of both polarisations onto a common state is another experiment with a different success probability.

For a lossless beam splitter of intensity reflectance \(R\) and transmittance \(T=1-R\), predict the coincidence probabilities for perfectly distinguishable and identical photons.

Work through the solution

The distinguishable alternatives give \(R^2+T^2\). For identical packets the amplitudes subtract, giving \((R-T)^2\). A perfectly indistinguishable pair need not give a zero minimum at an unbalanced splitter.

What delay has squared temporal overlap one half if the individual photon intensity has 100 fs rms width?

Work through the solution

Set \(\tau^2/(4\sigma^2)=\ln2\), giving \(|\tau|=2(100)\sqrt{\ln2}=166.5\) fs. The measured full width spans both signs, about 333 fs.
