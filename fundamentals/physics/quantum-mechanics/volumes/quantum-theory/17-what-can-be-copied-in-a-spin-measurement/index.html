Which properties of an unknown spin can an instrument copy?

A device calibrated with spin up and down can write two perfectly distinguishable records. Asking what happens to a superposition reveals a limit on information extraction and explains why state tomography needs an ensemble.

Apply linearity to a calibrated copying apparatus

Let a blank qubit be prepared in \(|0\rangle\). A unitary device might obey \(U|0,0\rangle=|0,0\rangle\) and \(U|1,0\rangle=|1,1\rangle\). Both rules are realised by a controlled NOT on the blank. Linearity determines the device on any superposition of these inputs. For \(|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\) the output is

\[U|+,0\rangle=(|0,0\rangle+|1,1\rangle)/\sqrt2 .\]

The desired two-copy state would be \(|+,+\rangle=(|0,0\rangle+|0,1\rangle+|1,0\rangle+|1,1\rangle)/2\). Measure both qubits along \(z\). The actual output has zero probability of unequal results. The desired output has probability one half of unequal results. These are experimentally distinguishable preparations.

Derive the obstruction without assuming a particular basis

Suppose an apparatus with fixed blank and fixed prepared environment clones each of two pure states \(|\psi\rangle\) and \(|\phi\rangle\). After the operation any residual machine states may depend on the input. Because the two output copies are pure, they factor from the machine in each branch. Taking the magnitude of the inner product before and after unitary evolution gives

\[s=s^2\,|\langle M_\psi|M_\phi\rangle|,\]
\[s=|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle| .\]

The machine overlap is at most one, so inputs with \(0<s<1\) cannot satisfy this equality. Orthogonal states form the special class for which exact, deterministic copying is possible. For \(|0\rangle\) and \(|+\rangle\), \(s=1/\sqrt2\) but two copies have overlap \(1/2\) even before multiplying by the machine overlap. Additional hidden apparatus states cannot fix this discrepancy.

Recall · Pure, correlated and mixed outputs

Partial trace of the actual two-qubit entangled output gives each single qubit \(\rho=I/2\). That result matches two fair \(z\) histograms, yet the pair has perfect \(z\) correlation. Single-axis marginals never certify that a pair consists of independent copies.

Relate disturbance to an observable record

A measurement trying to leave each state intact while recording its identity would have outputs \(|\psi\rangle|m_\psi\rangle\) and \(|\phi\rangle|m_\phi\rangle\). Inner-product preservation gives \(\langle\psi|\phi\rangle=\langle\psi|\phi\rangle\langle m_\psi|m_\phi\rangle\). If the inputs overlap, a nondisturbing measurement of both must leave the same record up to phase. This argument assumes exact preservation. Weak or approximate measurements can provide partial information at the cost of a disturbance.

With independently prepared copies, spin tomography estimates different components on different trials. If \(N\) separate \(x\) measurements have expectation \(r_x\), then \(\hat r_x=(n_+-n_-)/N\) has variance \((1-r_x^2)/N\). At \(r_x=0\) one hundred trials give standard error 0.10. Copying a single unknown preparation could not produce these independent statistics.

2 · Distinguish a classical record from an unknown state

Work through the solution

A classical bit is encoded in two distinguished orthogonal states. Controlled NOT copies that alphabet. Rotating the incoming state to an unknown basis introduces nonorthogonal alternatives relative to that alphabet, for which the same guarantee fails.

3 · How many new preparations are needed?

Work through the solution

To estimate a spin component near zero to one standard error of 0.02 requires \(N\simeq1/0.02^2=2500\) independent preparations for that axis. Splitting the same budget among three axes increases each component's error. Repeating a projective measurement on one qubit measures the prepared post-measurement state.
