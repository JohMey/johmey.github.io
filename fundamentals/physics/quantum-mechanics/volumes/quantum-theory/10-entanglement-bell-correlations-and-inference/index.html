Distinguishing entanglement from ordinary shared randomness

Two distant analysers sometimes agree even when individual outcomes look random. Derive which correlations shared classical information can produce, compute the quantum prediction and identify what a finite table of coincidence counts can establish.

Build the full probability table for two quantum systems

Each of two separated laboratories receives one two-state system from a joint preparation. For orthonormal local bases the joint basis vectors are \(|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle\). A pure state is a complex matrix of coefficients \(C_{ij}\) with \(\sum_{ij}|C_{ij}|^2=1\). If \(C_{ij}=a_i b_j\) the state is a product, and all local measurements have independent probabilities. A sum of product terms is always possible but may not factorise into one product. Singular-value decomposition \(C=UDV^\dagger\) gives the Schmidt form \(\sum_k s_k|u_k\rangle|v_k\rangle\), with orthonormal local bases and \(s_k\ge0,\ \sum s_k^2=1\). For two qubits two nonzero Schmidt numbers imply entanglement of the pure state.

Measure only the first system and sum over a complete set of results on the second. Its state is \(\rho_A=CC^\dagger\), with eigenvalues \(s_k^2\). For a globally pure state the local purity is therefore

\[\operatorname{tr}\rho_A^2=\sum s_k^4;\]

it is one only for a product. If the global state is mixed, reduced-state impurity alone does not certify entanglement. A fifty-fifty classical mixture of \(|01\rangle\) and \(|10\rangle\) already has completely random local measurement results along \(z\) and perfect opposition along that axis. A second incompatible measurement arrangement is needed.

Take the singlet \(|\Psi^-\rangle=(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)/\sqrt2\). The reduced state of either system is \(I/2\). Both local means vanish for any analyser direction. Direct calculation gives \(\langle\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j\rangle=-\delta_{ij}\), or equivalently use rotational invariance and the action of each equal Pauli pair on the singlet. Measuring eigenvalues \(s,t=\pm1\) along \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b\) uses product projector \((I+s\mathbf a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\otimes(I+t\mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/4\), giving

\[P(s,t\mid\mathbf a,\mathbf b)=\frac{1-st\,\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b}{4},\]
\[E(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)=\sum_{s,t}stP=-\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b.\]

Aligned analysers give complete anticorrelation; orthogonal axes give independent equiprobable results; opposite axes give complete agreement. The marginal \(\sum_tP(s,t)=1/2\) is independent of the remote axis. Conditioning on its outcome can sharpen predictions for the other system, but that conditional dataset requires the remote recorded information. For linear photon polarisation analysers the corresponding Bloch angle is twice the polariser angle, a detail that changes physical dial settings even though the probability algebra is the same.

Derive a four-setting inequality from local response functions

Suppose each emitted pair carries information \(\lambda\) drawn from a common distribution independent of the experimenters' analyser choices. A local shared-randomness model supplies outcomes \(A,A'\in\{\pm1\}\) at the first location and \(B,B'\in\{\pm1\}\) at the second, depending on the local choice and on \(\lambda\). Local probabilistic response can be treated by including additional random variables in this construction or by bounding their means between minus and plus one. Only one setting is actually selected at each location per run. The model nevertheless assigns responses that can be averaged under the same \(\lambda\) distribution for all four possible pairs.

For each assignment \(A(B+B')+A'(B-B')=\pm2\), since exactly one bracket vanishes and the other is \(\pm2\). Averaging and using the triangle inequality yields the CHSH bound

\[|S|=|E(a,b)+E(a,b')+E(a',b)-E(a',b')|\le2 .\]

This algebra does not assert that arbitrary measured quantum observables have pre-existing joint outcomes for incompatible settings. It specifies what a particular class of local shared-variable descriptions predicts. It also relies on comparing the same underlying source ensemble in all four settings. Outcome-dependent selection, untracked setting-dependent losses or a changing preparation can invalidate the link between the measured correlations and the inequality. Randomising settings and recording the acquisition and treatment of every event are physical parts of a meaningful experiment.

For the singlet choose \(\mathbf a=z,\ \mathbf a'=x,\ \mathbf b=(z+x)/\sqrt2,\ \mathbf b'=(z-x)/\sqrt2\). The first three correlations are \(-1/\sqrt2\), the last \(+1/\sqrt2\), giving \(S=-2\sqrt2\). Neither local outcome becomes predictable. What exceeds the bound is a weighted combination of correlations observed in four independently chosen arrangements. A product state will never exceed it, nor will a classical probabilistic mixture of product states, since the correlations of separable preparations admit such local models.

Derive a quantum upper bound and a visibility threshold

Let \(A,A',B,B'\) now denote Hermitian operators with squared value identity at their respective locations. Construct \(\mathcal B=A\otimes(B+B')+A'\otimes(B-B')\). Multiplying out, using the squared identities and retaining cross terms gives

\[\mathcal B^2=4I-[A,A']\otimes[B,B'].\]

Each commutator has operator norm at most two, so \(\|\mathcal B\|\le2\sqrt2\). The singlet settings above reach this bound. If the two candidate observables commute at either end the cross term vanishes and this construction allows no violation. Thus the upper bound and the need for local incompatibility follow from the same operator product.

Mix the singlet with uniformly random pairs,

\[\rho_v=v|\Psi^-\rangle\langle\Psi^-|+(1-v)I/4.\]

Every product correlation becomes \(-v\,\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b\); the tested CHSH settings violate only for \(v>1/\sqrt2\). Entanglement itself persists farther into the noise. Transposing just the second subsystem of the singlet projector gives eigenvalues \(+1/2,+1/2,+1/2,-1/2\). The smallest partial-transpose eigenvalue of the mixture is therefore \((1-3v)/4\). Every separable state's partial transpose is positive, since ordinary transposition preserves positivity on each product factor. Thus \(v>1/3\) certifies entanglement even where this CHSH experiment no longer violates. This argument establishes a sufficient witness from the negative eigenvalue without assuming failure of one witness proves separability.

Report correlation uncertainty using the counted events

For one arrangement write \(N_{++},N_{+-},N_{-+},N_{--}\) and \(N\) their sum. Estimate \(E=(N_{\rm same}-N_{\rm opposite})/N\). Each recorded product outcome is \(\pm1\) with variance \(1-E^2\); for independent trials a plug-in standard error is \(\sqrt{(1-\hat E^2)/N}\). Separate correctly randomised runs can produce independent estimation errors in the four setting groups. The variance of their signed sum then adds. Marginal balance and full tables offer checks hidden by reporting only one aggregate number. Two tables with the same \(E\) can have quite different marginal responses or efficiency problems.

Suppose each setting has 400 accepted pairs and same-result counts are \(60,64,56,340\), respectively. The correlations are \(-0.70,-0.68,-0.72,+0.70\). Thus \(S=-2.80\) and the independent-count plug-in deviation is about 0.071. That comparison is conditional on the sample construction and the independent-trial model. A full experimental Bell test also treats losses, timing, source memory and randomness of settings. A simulation drawing directly from the four Born distributions verifies the arithmetic of the prediction; agreement of its simulated \(S\) is not an independent laboratory test of the assumptions.

Use a product-basis measurement to estimate a two-qubit witness

A witness is an observable whose expectation is constrained for every separable preparation but can cross that limit for some entangled states. Consider \(W=I/2-|\Psi^-\rangle\langle\Psi^-|\). To understand the half, maximise squared singlet overlap over a product input \(|a\rangle|b\rangle\). Contracting the first spin leaves an unnormalised vector on the second of norm \(1/\sqrt2\), because the singlet's reduced state is \(I/2\). Cauchy–Schwarz then bounds squared product overlap by \(1/2\), with equality for appropriately opposite spinors. Any mixture of product states obeys the same bound by linearity. A measured negative \(\langle W\rangle\) therefore certifies entanglement without requiring the whole state to be pure or exactly a white-noise mixture.

The singlet projector equals \([I-\sum_i\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_i]/4\), so only three equal-axis correlation settings are needed to measure this witness. Suppose independent experiments yield

\[E_{xx}=-0.55,\ E_{yy}=-0.63,\ E_{zz}=-0.74.\]

The singlet fraction is \(F_s=(1+1.92)/4=0.730\) and \(\langle W\rangle=-0.230\). If each correlation has standard uncertainty 0.04, independent random errors give a singlet-fraction uncertainty \(\sqrt3(0.04)/4=0.0173\). Misoriented axes can bias multiple settings together, so independent statistical errors alone are not a calibration error budget. This test does not require verifying an exact isotropic visibility parameter; its bound holds across the entire separable set provided the measurements implement the stated Pauli observables.

A witness is tailored to a target. The maximally entangled \((|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\) has zero singlet overlap, giving positive \(\langle W\rangle\) rather than a violation. A known local rotation converts that state to the singlet and would make the same physical analyser scheme sensitive. Without such preparation knowledge, one can measure the full three-by-three correlation tensor and independently choose optimal axes for a separate validation run. Choosing axes to maximise a noisy estimate and then quoting naïve significance on the same data biases the result upward. Calibration, model selection and independent validation serve different roles even in an elementary two-qubit experiment.

Find the best CHSH settings from a measured correlation tensor

For a general two-qubit state define the real \(3\times3\) matrix \(T_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}[\rho(\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j)]\). Its product correlation along unit directions is \(\mathbf a^TT\mathbf b\), irrespective of the local Bloch vectors. The CHSH expression becomes

\[S=\mathbf a^TT(\mathbf b+\mathbf b')+\mathbf a'^TT(\mathbf b-\mathbf b').\]

The sum and difference of two unit vectors are perpendicular. Write them as \(2\cos\theta\,\mathbf u\) and \(2\sin\theta\,\mathbf v\) for orthonormal \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\) and \(0\le\theta\le\pi/2\). For fixed \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\), choose \(\mathbf a\) parallel to \(T\mathbf u\) and \(\mathbf a'\) parallel to \(T\mathbf v\) when these are nonzero. The remaining scalar maximisation gives \(2\sqrt{\|T\mathbf u\|^2+\|T\mathbf v\|^2}\).

The maximum of this sum of quadratic forms over orthonormal \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\) is the sum of the two largest eigenvalues of \(T^TT\). One can see it by expanding both unit vectors in the eigenbasis: each squared projection weight summed over \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\) lies in \([0,1]\), their sum is two, and the largest possible weighted sum fills the two largest eigenvalues. Thus \(S_{\max}=2\sqrt{s_1^2+s_2^2}\) with \(s_i\) the singular values of \(T\). A specific state permits a violation by projective spin measurements exactly when \(s_1^2+s_2^2>1\). If the singular values are near-degenerate, optimal axes may vary markedly under noise even though the best attainable \(S\) changes little.

For a concrete physical Bell-diagonal mixture take \(\rho=0.75|\Psi^-\rangle\langle\Psi^-|+0.15|\Psi^+\rangle\langle\Psi^+|+0.10|\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+|\), where \(\Psi^\pm=(01\pm10)/\sqrt2\), \(\Phi^+=(00+11)/\sqrt2\) in ket shorthand. The respective correlation triples are \((-1,-1,-1),(1,1,-1),(1,-1,1)\). Their weighted sum is \((-0.50,-0.70,-0.80)\), giving best CHSH magnitude \(2\sqrt{0.8^2+0.7^2}=2.126\). If a particular apparatus sampled only the \(x,y\) plane its best value would be \(2\sqrt{0.5^2+0.7^2}=1.720\). The state and source are identical in both calculations; changing which axes are accessible changes what the experiment can demonstrate. A fixed set of settings optimised for an isotropic singlet might similarly underestimate an anisotropic source.

Derive the noise threshold for separability in a symmetric family

The negative partial-transpose eigenvalue proved entanglement for \(v>1/3\) in the white-noise singlet family. For this special family one can also construct a separable preparation exactly at that boundary. Average product states whose Bloch vectors point in opposite directions:

\[\rho_{\rm avg}=\int (I+\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\otimes(I-\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\,d\Omega/(16\pi).\]

Symmetry gives \(\langle n_i\rangle=0\) and \(\langle n_i n_j\rangle=\delta_{ij}/3\). Expanding yields \(\rho_{\rm avg}=[I\otimes I-\sum_i\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_i/3]/4\), precisely the Werner singlet mixture with \(v=1/3\). Even a discrete uniform selection of the six signed Cartesian axes has the same first and second moments here. Mixture with \(I/4\), itself separable, then constructs every \(0\le v\le1/3\).

The full picture of this specific preparation family is therefore quantitative. Below and including one third it has a separable construction. Above one third it is entangled. Up to the CHSH threshold \(1/\sqrt2\), its correlations still fail to exceed 2 in the test optimised over two spin directions per side. The gain from demanding a stronger operational certificate is a stricter threshold and potentially more demanding statistics. Failing a CHSH test in that intermediate interval does not erase the entanglement witness already derived. Conversely witnessing entanglement with trusted calibrated Pauli devices is a different experimental claim from testing a class of local causal models with explicitly addressed device and sampling assumptions.

For \(v=0.50\), the singlet fraction is \((1+3v)/4=0.625\) and the smallest partial-transpose eigenvalue is \(-0.125\). The maximal CHSH value is \(2\sqrt2v=1.414\). No improvement in count precision can move these ideal mean correlations across 2 without changing the state or measurement scenario. At \(v=0.72\) the maximal value is about 2.036, technically above the threshold but small enough that repeat count and systematic uncertainties matter substantially. This comparison distinguishes identifiability from statistical power: one experiment cannot average its way through an exact theoretical bound, while a narrow permitted violation can become detectable with better data and calibration.

Compute remote conditional states while preserving no signalling

After a local measurement with operators \(M_a\) on system A, the unnormalised conditional state of B is \(\tilde\rho_{B|a}=\operatorname{tr}_A[(M_a\otimes I)\rho_{AB}(M_a^\dagger\otimes I)]\). Its trace is the probability of the local outcome; dividing by that trace gives the conditional state. Summing over all outcomes and using \(\sum_a M_a^\dagger M_a=I\) under the partial trace returns \(\rho_B\). The result holds for any complete local instrument, including a nonprojective measurement with forgotten outcomes. A remote observer lacking the classical label sees exactly the same state as before. Conditioning becomes operational only when the two records can be compared.

For the singlet, if A measures direction \(\mathbf a\) sharply and obtains \(+1\), B's conditional Bloch vector is \(-\mathbf a\); outcome \(-1\) gives \(+\mathbf a\). In the white-noise mixture those vectors shrink to \(\mp v\mathbf a\), while each outcome remains equally probable. If the remote partner groups trials by A's announced result and measures along a fixed \(\mathbf b\), it recovers the same conditional probabilities as the original correlation formula. Without that grouping the two opposite vectors average to zero for every \(\mathbf a\). This calculation distinguishes a physical method for remotely preparing an ensemble conditional on communication from a method for sending a chosen bit by merely varying the local measurement angle.

A delayed readout changes none of these predicted coincidences because local operators \(M_a\otimes I\) and \(I\otimes M_b\) commute at the level of this separated systems model. The order in which a data analyst applies conditional probability does change which denominator appears in a finite table. In a test with imperfect detectors, report whether a conditional rate divides by all local triggers, all heralds or only coincidences. Setting-dependent missing events can make a conditional table look no-signalling or strongly correlated for purely sampling reasons. A transparent acquisition protocol preserves the correspondence between the Born joint probabilities being predicted and the ensemble included in their statistical test.

Build an entangled pair by conditioning two independent sources

Entanglement is a property of a joint preparation, not a permanent label attached to an individual particle. A four-qubit example illustrates how changing which outcomes are retained can create new conditional pair correlations. Prepare \(A,B\) in \(|\Phi^+\rangle=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\) and independently prepare \(C,D\) in the same Bell state. Before any further measurement the reduced preparation of \(A,D\) is \(I/4\) because the two sources are independent and each outer qubit is individually maximally mixed. A separate central apparatus receives \(B,C\) and measures in the Bell basis. The correct prediction after this operation depends on whether its recorded result is available.

Expand the initial state in the order \(A,D;B,C\), then use the identity expressing products \(|00\rangle,|11\rangle\) as sums and differences of \(\Phi^\pm\) and \(|01\rangle,|10\rangle\) as sums and differences of \(\Psi^\pm\). Regrouping gives

\[|\Phi^+\rangle_{AB}|\Phi^+\rangle_{CD}=\tfrac12\sum_{Q\in\{\Phi^+,\Phi^-,\Psi^+,\Psi^-\}} |Q\rangle_{AD}|Q\rangle_{BC}\]

for the consistent real Bell basis used here. The norm stays one because the four central labels are orthogonal. Each central result occurs with probability one quarter. Given a particular result \(Q\), the two outer systems are jointly in \(Q\), even though they came from independent sources and did not directly interact. Ignoring the central result leaves an equally weighted Bell mixture \(I/4\), the same outer reduced state as before. Conditioning, or applying a known local Pauli correction using the central label, is necessary to expose a consistent set of Bell correlations in the outer records.

For example, central outcomes \(\Phi^+\) and \(\Phi^-\) both predict equal outer \(z\) outcomes, whereas \(\Psi^+\) and \(\Psi^-\) predict opposite. In the \(x\) basis their signs follow a different pattern. A central device reporting only the parity of its \(z\) outcomes distinguishes the \(\Phi\) from the \(\Psi\) subspace but cannot distinguish their relative phases. Conditioned on even parity alone the outer state is \((|\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+|+|\Phi^-\rangle\langle\Phi^-|)/2\), identical to the classical mixture of \(|00\rangle\) and \(|11\rangle\). The very same perfect \(z\) correlation therefore has different implications for a resolved Bell label and a mere parity label. What the central device physically measures is essential background for interpreting the outer data.

Quantify how central-label mistakes change outer Bell contrast

Suppose the central Bell outcome is reported correctly with probability \(q\), and otherwise is replaced by each of the three wrong labels with equal probability. The outer parties apply Pauli corrections based on the reported label to target \(\Phi^+\). Derive the effective visibility and threshold for a two-setting Bell violation.

Derive and check the result

Because every correct label yields the target and wrong Pauli corrections map it to the other three orthogonal Bell states, the corrected outer ensemble is Bell diagonal with target weight \(q\) and equal wrong weights \((1-q)/3\). Comparing with \(v|\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+|+(1-v)I/4\) gives \(q=(1+3v)/4\), so \(v=(4q-1)/3\). In the useful regime \(q\ge1/4\) the maximal CHSH value is \(2\sqrt2(4q-1)/3\), above two when \(q>(1+3/\sqrt2)/4=0.7803\). At \(q=0.85\) the visibility is 0.80 and the best value is about 2.263. Central efficiency and central label fidelity affect different things: if losses are independent, lower efficiency reduces the number of heralded events, while wrong labels reduce conditional contrast. In a realistic inference, setting dependence of losses or an imperfect outer measurement must also be checked before assigning all observed contrast loss to central reporting errors.

Compare local entropy, global entropy and shared classical information

For a density matrix \(\rho\) with eigenvalues \(p_i\), define von Neumann entropy in bits by \(S(\rho)=-\sum_i p_i\log_2p_i\), interpreting the zero-eigenvalue term by continuity. For a pure bipartite state the two reduced spectra agree by Schmidt decomposition, so both reduced entropies equal the binary or multi-outcome entropy of the squared Schmidt coefficients. This is an operationally meaningful measure of entanglement for a pure bipartite preparation. For a mixed state, local entropy also contains ordinary uncertainty due to forgotten preparation records and cannot by itself quantify entanglement.

A singlet has \(S(AB)=0,\ S(A)=S(B)=1\). The equally weighted unentangled anticorrelation mixture \([|01\rangle\langle01|+|10\rangle\langle10|]/2\) has \(S(AB)=1\) and the same local entropies. Completely uncorrelated white noise \(I/4\) gives \(S(AB)=2\) and still the same local values. Their quantum mutual informations \(S(A)+S(B)-S(AB)\) are respectively 2, 1 and 0 bits. The two bits for a singlet do not mean each party receives two freely readable classical bits in a single spin measurement. With aligned local measurements the observed binary records share one bit, while the full quantum mutual information is a property of the joint density operator across all possible probes.

Infer entanglement entropy and contrast for a pure pair

A calibrated pure source has squared Schmidt coefficients \(0.90,0.10\). Find local entropy and compare the strongest CHSH value in its Schmidt basis with that of an incoherent mixture of its two Schmidt product states. Which part of the global description changes after completely dephasing one subsystem in the Schmidt basis?

Derive and check the result

The pure-pair entanglement entropy is the binary value \(-0.9\log_2(0.9)-0.1\log_2(0.1)=0.469\) bits. Write \(\cos^2\theta=0.9\), giving \(\sin2\theta=2\sqrt{0.09}=0.60\), and the best CHSH value \(2\sqrt{1+0.60^2}=2.332\). Complete dephasing removes off-diagonal coherence between the two global Schmidt products and leaves their same classical weights. The marginals and local entropies stay exactly the same. The global entropy rises from zero to 0.469 bits; the correlation tensor has only its longitudinal component of unit magnitude and hence best CHSH value two. Comparing these examples shows precisely which physical information is lost when a preparation record or coherent relative phase becomes inaccessible, and why an unchanged local entropy can coexist with a changed experimentally accessible joint correlation.

Distinguish a singlet from classical anticorrelation

Compare the singlet with an equally weighted mixture of \(|01\rangle,|10\rangle\). Compute \(E(z,z),E(x,x)\) and \(E(z,x)\) for both. Which measurement distinguishes them?

Work through the solution

The singlet gives \(-1,-1,0\) respectively. In the mixture each prepared product has perfect \(z\) opposition, but individual states measured along \(x\) have zero mean. Product outcomes factor on each component, so \(E(x,x)=0\) and \(E(z,x)=0\). Measuring just \(E(z,z)\) cannot distinguish the two; \(E(x,x)\) does. Distinguishing two named hypotheses with one extra setting does not establish a CHSH violation against all local shared-randomness models. The hypotheses and tested claim determine the required data.

Determine a partially entangled state's best planar settings

Let \(|\psi\rangle=\cos\theta|00\rangle+\sin\theta|11\rangle\). Use first-side settings \(z,x\) and second-side \(\mathbf b,\mathbf b'=(\pm\sin\alpha,0,\cos\alpha)\), with the plus sign assigned to \(\mathbf b\). Optimise \(S\).

Work through the solution

The correlation tensor in the \(x,z\) plane has entries \(T_{zz}=1,\ T_{xx}=\sin2\theta\) and vanishing cross terms. Therefore \(S=2[\cos\alpha+\sin2\theta\,\sin\alpha]\). Maximisation gives \(\tan\alpha=\sin2\theta\) and \(S_{\max}=2\sqrt{1+\sin^22\theta}\) for these directions. At \(\theta=\pi/12\) it is \(\sqrt5=2.236\), at a product endpoint it is 2, and at the maximally entangled midpoint \(2\sqrt2\). The optimising dial settings depend on the state; a poorly chosen apparatus can miss an available violation.

Estimate the needed pair count for a visibility-limited test

Take \(v=0.80\) at the ideal singlet CHSH settings and assume equal independent counts per setting. Estimate the number per setting for a nominal five-standard-error separation from 2. What limits this calculation?

Work through the solution

The predicted excess is \(2\sqrt2v-2=0.2627\). Each product mean has magnitude \(v/\sqrt2\), so

\[\operatorname{sd}S=2\sqrt{(1-v^2/2)/N}=1.649/\sqrt N.\]

Requiring the excess to exceed five such deviations gives \(N\gtrsim986\) pairs per setting. This is a planning estimate using Gaussian independent-sample errors and known visibility. It is not a complete finite-sample hypothesis test and gives no protection against systematic errors or outcome-dependent postselection. Report the four realised counts along with acquisition, losses and uncertainty rather than presenting the planned count as guaranteed evidence.
