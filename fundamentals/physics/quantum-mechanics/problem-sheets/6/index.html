Tomography of an imperfect preparation

For independently repeated preparations the probabilities for positive Pauli \(x,y,z\) outcomes are \(0.70,0.40,0.60\). Determine the Bloch vector, density matrix and purity. What would dephasing in the \(z\) basis do to each probability at long times?

Show the reconstruction

Each Bloch component is \(2P_+-1\), giving \((0.40,-0.20,0.20)\). From \(\rho=(I+\mathbf r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/2\), diagonal entries are \(0.60,0.40\) and the upper off-diagonal entry is \((r_x-ir_y)/2=0.20+0.10i\). Purity is \((1+|\mathbf r|^2)/2=(1+0.24)/2=0.62\). Pure \(z\) dephasing removes the two transverse components while preserving populations, so the final positive probabilities are \(0.50,0.50,0.60\). The nonzero \(y\) component would be missed by measuring only one transverse basis.

Split a square-well doublet

For an electron in a 3.00 nm square well compare the \((2,1)\) and \((1,2)\) energies. Stretch the \(x\) side to 3.30 nm while holding \(y\) fixed and compute both levels. Take \(\hbar^2/(2m_e)=0.03810\) eV nm².

Show the directional energy shifts

In the square both have \(E=0.03810\pi^2(5/9)=0.209\) eV. After stretching, the \((2,1)\) level has \(E=0.03810\pi^2(4/3.30^2+1/3.00^2)=0.180\) eV. For \((1,2)\) the factor is \(1/3.30^2+4/3.00^2\), yielding \(0.202\) eV. Modes with more rapid oscillations along the stretched axis lose more gradient energy. Uniform scaling of both dimensions would preserve the degeneracy.

Estimate the area from a level count

A spinless two-dimensional Dirichlet well has about 2000 levels up to \(k=50.0\) nm⁻¹. Use the leading counting law to estimate area. If an independent perimeter measurement gives 11.5 nm, apply the first boundary correction and discuss the precision of the inferred shape.

Show the asymptotic inversion

Using \(N\simeq Ak^2/(4\pi)\) gives \(A\simeq4\pi(2000)/2500=10.05\) nm². With \(N\simeq Ak^2/(4\pi)-Pk/(4\pi)\) the inferred area increases by \(P/k=0.230\) nm² to about \(10.28\) nm². Low-order terms and fluctuations from integer level counts limit precision at finite \(k\). Neither area nor perimeter fixes the full boundary, and even distinct shapes with identical spectra are possible. The estimate uses each spatial state once; including a separate spin degeneracy would double a counted list.

Recover a state from three spin measurements

Three independently prepared ensembles give probabilities for spin-up of 0.80 along x, 0.50 along y and 0.90 along z. Reconstruct the density matrix, decide whether the state is pure, and predict the result of an x measurement after a z measurement whose result is discarded. Repeat the purity check if the z probability was 0.95.

Worked solution

For each Pauli measurement the mean is p(+) − p(−) = 2p(+) − 1. The Bloch vector is (0.6, 0, 0.8). In the z basis the diagonal entries are 0.9 and 0.1 and both off-diagonal entries equal 0.3. Its trace is one and its determinant vanishes. Thus its eigenvalues are one and zero. A state vector is (√0.9, √0.1) up to global phase.

An unread projective z measurement removes the off-diagonal terms. The z probabilities stay fixed, while the subsequent x-up probability becomes 0.50. The new purity is 0.9² + 0.1² = 0.82. Selecting the z-up subset instead would leave a pure z-up state, which also gives 0.50 in x. Testing z again distinguishes selection from mixing.

With z-up probability 0.95 the reconstructed Bloch length squared would become 0.6² + 0.9² = 1.17. The nominal matrix has a negative eigenvalue. Finite-sample fluctuations or calibration differences can produce such estimates. A physical reconstruction fits a positive matrix to the original counts. The three quoted probabilities alone do not specify their statistical uncertainty.
