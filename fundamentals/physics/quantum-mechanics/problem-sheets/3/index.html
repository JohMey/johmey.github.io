Diagnose an avoided crossing

Two uncoupled levels are tuned symmetrically about zero energy. Their observed minimum gap is 0.80 meV. Find the coupling magnitude, then the exact positive eigenvalue at a bare separation of 2.0 meV and its second-order estimate. Identify the observable that tests whether an additional common energy shift is present.

Extract the coupling at zero detuning

The minimum splitting is \(2|v|\), hence \(|v|=0.40\) meV. At bare energies \(\pm1.0\) meV the upper eigenvalue is

\[\sqrt{1.0^2+0.40^2}=1.077\,\mathrm{meV}.\]

The perturbative result is

\[1.0+(0.40)^2/2.0=1.080\,\mathrm{meV}.\]

Coupling alone preserves the sum of the two energies; measuring the midpoint as well as their difference can reveal a diagonal shift common to the two states.

Distinguish an incoherent source

A pair source produces a singlet with probability 0.70 and an equal classical mixture of opposite \(z\) spins otherwise. Predict \(E(z,z)\) and \(E(x,x)\), then the expected fraction of opposite \(x\) outcomes. Can either one-particle \(z\) histogram reveal the admixture?

Average over preparations

Both preparations always give opposite \(z\) outcomes, hence \(E(z,z)=-1\). The singlet gives \(E(x,x)=-1\) and the incoherent mixture gives zero. Their weighted correlation is \(-0.70\), so opposite \(x\) outcomes occur with probability \((1+0.70)/2=0.85\). Both one-particle marginals are evenly split in the \(z\) basis for each preparation. Detecting the admixture therefore requires appropriate joint measurements.

Mix a pair of distant levels weakly

An unperturbed lower state has energy 0 and an upper state 50 meV. A perturbation has zero diagonal elements and off-diagonal magnitude 2.0 meV. Estimate the lower-state energy shift and the probability that a measurement of the original energy on the perturbed ground state finds the upper state. How do both estimates change if the perturbation doubles?

Calculate mixing before squaring it

The first-order diagonal energy shift is zero. The lower state has a first-order admixture of upper state of magnitude \(2/50=0.040\), giving upper-outcome probability about 0.0016. The lower energy shifts by \(-2^2/50=-0.080\) meV at second order. Doubling coupling doubles the amplitude, hence quadruples the probability to approximately 0.0064 and the downward shift to approximately 0.320 meV. Their common quadratic scaling holds only while the mixing ratio remains small.

Set the speed of a control ramp

A coupled pair has minimum splitting \(0.400\,\mathrm{meV}\). Set a constant detuning speed that keeps \(\eta(0)\leq0.0200\). Estimate the shortest time for a \(2.00\,\mathrm{meV}\) ramp at this speed. How must the speed change if the minimum gap is halved?

Determine the limiting speed and ramp duration

The coupling is half the minimum splitting, \(g=0.200\,\mathrm{meV}\). Solving the centre criterion gives

\[|\dot\Delta|\leq8(0.200)^2(0.0200)/0.6582=0.00972\,\mathrm{meV/ps}.\]

A constant-speed ramp through \(2.00\,\mathrm{meV}\) therefore takes at least \(206\,\mathrm{ps}\). Halving the gap halves \(g\) and reduces the allowed speed to a quarter, \(0.00243\,\mathrm{meV/ps}\). These numbers set a local adiabaticity margin. An exact transition probability would require specifying the start, end and switching of the sweep.
