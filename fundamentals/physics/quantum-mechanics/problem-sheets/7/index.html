Measuring two phases with one interferometer

An ideal coherent interferometer has two paths for a charged spin-half particle. One loop encloses a magnetic flux. A controlled field direction can also rotate the spin adiabatically in one arm. Use independent calibrations of the flux and spin phase to predict what the recombiner measures. Assume identical path intensities, perfect coherence and matched dynamical phases.

Calibrate the magnetic flux from a ring spectrum

A separate narrow ring made for the same particle has \(E_n/E_L=(n-\varphi)^2\) with signed flux \(\varphi=q\Phi/h\). Near zero flux, the \(n=0\) energy is \(0.0625E_L\). An independent current measurement on that branch gives

\[I/(qh/mL^2)=-0.25.\]

Find the flux and the nearest excited energy. Why are both observations useful?

Work through the solution

The energy gives \(|\varphi|=0.25\). The signed current is \(n-\varphi=-\varphi\) on this branch, so \(\varphi=+0.25\). The nearest excited branch is \(n=1\) at \(0.5625E_L\). The excitation gap is \(0.50E_L\). Energy alone leaves the sign undetermined.

Read off the energy branches and the signed ring current

Calibrate the spin phase by reversing a loop

Prepare the aligned spin state and carry the field direction once around a positive latitude at cone angle \(60^\circ\). Find the geometric phase for each traversal direction. With no magnetic flux, predict the bright-port probabilities at reference shifts \(\chi=0\) and \(\chi=+\pi/2\) for each direction.

Work through the solution

The solid angle is \(2\pi(1-\cos60^\circ)=\pi\), hence the forward phase is \(-\pi/2\) and the reverse phase \(+\pi/2\). In \(P=[1+\cos(\chi+\gamma)]/2\), zero reference phase gives one half for either traversal. A reference shift \(+\pi/2\) gives one for the forward loop and zero for its reverse. The second setting reveals the sign.

Reverse the latitude and read the two bright-port probabilities

Predict the combined measurement

In the interferometer the paths enclose the flux once with an orientation giving magnetic phase \(q\Phi/\hbar=2\pi\varphi\). At zero reference shift use the preceding calibrations to predict the bright-port probability with the forward spin loop and with its reversal. Then reverse the magnetic flux without changing the spin protocol.

Work through the solution

The magnetic phase is \(+\pi/2\), so the forward total is zero and the port is bright with probability one. The reverse spin loop gives total phase \(\pi\) and zero probability. Reversing the flux swaps those outcomes because the total phases become \(-\pi\) and zero. The two individual phases have different physical origins but their amplitudes multiply on the same path.

Check an imperfect observation

At the combined bright and dark settings an experiment instead records probabilities 0.91 and 0.09. Fit a visibility \(V\) in \(P=[1+V\cos\phi]/2\). Can these two points alone determine whether the loss was caused by dephasing or unequal path powers? Propose one independent observation.

Work through the solution

Their difference is \(V=0.82\) and their sum equals one, consistent with the balanced offset in the model. An incoherent admixture and unequal path amplitudes can both reduce the observed visibility. Measuring each arm intensity with the other arm blocked tests imbalance. Scanning the reference phase through a full period additionally checks whether a phase offset displaced the nominal extrema.
