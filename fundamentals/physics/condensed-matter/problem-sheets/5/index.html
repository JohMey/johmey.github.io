1. Independent moments at two temperatures

A two-state sample has \(M_\mathrm{sat}=2.0\times10^4\) A/m and \(\mu=\mu_B\). (a) Find the density of moments. (b) At 2 K find the field at which \(M=M_\mathrm{sat}/2\), using \(\mu_B/k_B=0.672\) K/T. (c) Repeat at 8 K and identify what could break this scaling.

Invert the hyperbolic tangent

The density is \(M_\mathrm{sat}/\mu_B=2.16\times10^{27}\) m⁻³. The required argument is \(\operatorname{artanh}(1/2)=0.5493\), so the fields are \(0.5493(2)/0.672=1.63\) T and 6.54 T. Interacting moments, additional low-energy levels or a background linear susceptibility can spoil scaling with \(B/T\).

2. A Curie tail on a constant background

A sample has \(\chi(10\,\mathrm K)=5.0\times10^{-4}\) and \(\chi(30\,\mathrm K)=3.0\times10^{-4}\). (a) Fit \(\chi=\chi_0+C/T\). (b) Predict its value at 20 K. (c) Would two points alone distinguish this fit from cooperative Curie–Weiss behaviour?

Plot against inverse temperature

The slope is \(C=(2.0\times10^{-4})/(1/10-1/30)=3.0\times10^{-3}\) K, and \(\chi_0=2.0\times10^{-4}\). The prediction at 20 K is \(3.5\times10^{-4}\). Two measured points determine two constants but cannot validate the functional form. Measure intermediate and lower temperatures and test field dependence in the weak-field regime.

3. Curvature and a spontaneous moment

For \(f(m)=\frac12 t m^2+\frac14 m^4-hm\) in scaled units, (a) find stable equilibria at \(h=0\) for \(t=1\) and \(t=-1\). (b) Compute the small-field slope on each stable branch. (c) Find the zero-field barrier height below the transition and state why this is insufficient to predict a real sample’s coercive field.

Inspect the stationary equation and its derivative

The equation is

\[tm+m^3=h.\]

At \(t=1\) the stable solution is zero with slope \(dm/dh=1/(t+3m^2)=1\). At \(t=-1\), \(m=\pm1\) are stable and have slope \(1/2\); zero has negative curvature. The difference between \(f(0)=0\) and either minimum \(f(\pm1)=-1/4\) is 0.25 per unit volume in the chosen scales. Domain-wall motion, nucleation and defects can provide reversal routes absent from the homogeneous model.
