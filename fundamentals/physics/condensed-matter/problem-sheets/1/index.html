1. Two peaks in a one-dimensional specimen

Successive reciprocal-lattice orders occur at 12.57 and 25.13 nm⁻¹. The corrected integrated intensity of the second order is nine times that of the first. (a) Find the cell length. (b) For sites at \(0,a/2\) with real positive amplitudes \(f_A>f_B\), determine their ratio. (c) What changes if the crystal is translated by one quarter of a cell?

Separate positions, amplitudes and phases

The spacing gives \(a=2\pi/12.56\simeq0.500\) nm. Even orders have amplitude \(f_A+f_B\) and odd ones \(f_A-f_B\), so \((f_A+f_B)/(f_A-f_B)=3\), hence \(f_A/f_B=2\). Translation adds the phase \(e^{iG_h a/4}\) to every amplitude for order \(h\). Peaks and intensities stay fixed. The ratio calculation assumes that instrumental and form-factor differences between orders have already been corrected.

2. Sound speed in two isotopes

A long monoatomic chain has sound speed 4000 m/s, spacing 0.25 nm and site mass \(5.0\times 10^{-26}\) kg. (a) Find its spring constant. (b) Find its edge frequency in cycles per second. (c) If every mass increases by 10% while the spring constant and lattice spacing are fixed, predict the two fractional changes.

Carry the angular frequency through to the final step

From \(v_s=a\sqrt{K/m}\), \(K=m(v_s/a)^2=12.8\) N/m. The edge angular frequency is \(2v_s/a=3.20\times10^{13}\) s⁻¹, giving 5.09 THz after division by \(2\pi\). Both the sound speed and edge frequency scale as \(m^{-1/2}\); both fall by \(1-1/\sqrt{1.1}=4.65\%\).

3. A diatomic inverse measurement

A diatomic chain with equal nearest-neighbour springs shows angular frequencies \(2.0\times10^{13}\) and \(3.0\times10^{13}\) s⁻¹ at the zone boundary. (a) Infer the heavy-to-light mass ratio. (b) Predict the optical frequency at the zone centre. (c) Explain whether these two data determine the absolute masses and stiffness.

Use the boundary decoupling

The smaller squared frequency is \(2K/M\) and the larger is \(2K/m\), hence

\[M/m=(3/2)^2=2.25.\]

At the centre \(\omega_\mathrm{op}^2=2K(1/m+1/M)\), the sum of the two squared edge frequencies. This gives \(3.61\times10^{13}\) s⁻¹. Multiplying both masses and the spring stiffness by the same factor leaves all frequencies invariant. One independently known mass or stiffness resolves that ambiguity.

Fold a wave vector into the first zone

A rectangular layer has primitive lengths \(0.400\) and \(0.250\,\mathrm{nm}\) along perpendicular axes. A momentum-resolved measurement reports \(\mathbf k=(9.00,-14.00)\,\mathrm{nm^{-1}}\). Determine the first-zone boundaries, fold the point, and give one real-space measurement for which the two labels yield the same phase.

Construct the zone and check a translation

The reciprocal periods are

\[2\pi/0.400=15.708,\]
\[2\pi/0.250=25.133\,\mathrm{nm^{-1}}.\]

The first zone extends from \(-7.854\) to \(7.854\) along \(x\) and from \(-12.566\) to \(12.566\,\mathrm{nm^{-1}}\) along \(y\). Subtract one \(x\) period and add one \(y\) period to get

\[\mathbf k'=(-6.708,11.133)\,\mathrm{nm^{-1}}.\]

Over one translation along \(x\) the phase difference is \((k_x-k'_x)(0.400\,\mathrm{nm})=2\pi\). A phase comparison between equivalent lattice sites therefore cannot distinguish these labels.

Infer a defect formation enthalpy from two temperatures

Equilibrated vacancy fractions are \(x_1=1.00\times10^{-5}\) at \(800\,\mathrm K\) and \(x_2=1.00\times10^{-4}\) at \(1000\,\mathrm K\). Suppose \(g_f=h_f-Ts_f\) with constant \(h_f,s_f\). Eliminate the unknown entropy to infer \(h_f\). Why would repeating the experiment on immediately quenched specimens require a different interpretation?

Eliminate the intercept and interpret the slope

Taking log odds gives

\[\ln[x/(1-x)]=s_f/k_B-h_f/(k_BT).\]

Subtract the first measurement from the second. The result is

\[h_f=k_B\ln\{[x_2/(1-x_2)]/[x_1/(1-x_1)]\}/(1/T_1-1/T_2).\]

The log ratio is \(2.3027\) and the inverse-temperature difference \(0.000250\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}\), yielding \(0.794\,\mathrm{eV}\) per vacancy. After a quench, a measured fraction can retain memory of an earlier temperature. Fitting it against the final measurement temperature assumes equilibration that may not have occurred.
