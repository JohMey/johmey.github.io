Inferring a chain termination from bulk bands and end-site data

A fabricated one-dimensional array has two sites per primitive cell, real positive nearest-neighbour hoppings and a reproducible way of cleaving its left edge. Use energy and intensity measurements to decide which link is exposed. Check which conclusions survive when the array contains only a few cells.

Problem sheet 7 · Integrative project

Extract the unordered bonds from a bulk energy scan

The upper band has energy \(1.60\,\mathrm{eV}\) at \(k=0\) and \(0.40\,\mathrm{eV}\) at \(ka=\pi\), measured from the symmetry centre. Find the unordered hopping strengths, the direct gap and the energy at \(ka=\pi/2\). Explain what the bulk measurements leave ambiguous.

Work through the solution

The two extrema give \(t_1+t_2=1.60\) and \(|t_1-t_2|=0.40\) eV. The positive hoppings are 1.00 and 0.60 eV. The full direct gap is 0.80 eV and the intermediate energy is

\[\sqrt{1^2+0.6^2}=1.166\,\mathrm{eV}.\]

The characteristic equation is unchanged when the hoppings are exchanged, so even a perfect positive-energy measurement over the full Brillouin zone cannot label the first physical bond.

Swap the two bonds and compare the two bulk energy scans

Use a boundary measurement to label the bonds

The leftmost orbital is \(A_1\). In an odd chain ending on an A site, local spectroscopy near zero energy gives relative intensities \(1.00,0.36,0.130\) on the first three A sites. Determine \(t_1/t_2\), label the hoppings and predict the first intensity outside the measured set.

Work through the solution

At zero energy the neighbouring B-site equations impose \(A_{j+1}=-(t_1/t_2)A_j\). Intensities fall by \(r^2\), giving \(r=0.60\). Therefore the first physical link \(A_1B_1\) is the weaker 0.60 eV hopping. The fourth A site has relative intensity \(0.36^3=0.04666\). The signs of successive amplitudes alternate although the intensities carry no sign.

Check the odd-chain end state, including its finite normalisation

Estimate finite-size contamination

Take \(M=8\) A sites and seven B sites. What fraction of the normalised state lies at \(A_1\)? How much is at \(A_8\)? Repeat those endpoint predictions after exchanging the intra-cell and intercell hoppings while preserving the cut.

Work through the solution

With ratio \(r=0.60\), finite normalisation gives

\[P_1=(1-r^2)/(1-r^{2M})=0.64018.\]

Eight A sites contain seven steps from one end to the other. The far-end probability is

\[P_8=P_1r^{14}=0.000502.\]

Replacing \(r\) by \(1/r\) reflects the normalised geometric sequence. The two endpoint probabilities exchange, while the bulk energy spectra from stage one coincide. In a short sample near \(r=1\), finite-size effects would become much larger.

Test whether a zero-energy claim depends on the model

Add on-site energy \(+\Delta\) to every A site and \(-\Delta\) to every B site, with \(\Delta=0.10\,\mathrm{eV}\). Given the original odd-chain state has amplitude only on A, find its new energy. Obtain the new bulk gap and say which observables still identify the weaker boundary bond.

Work through the solution

The diagonal perturbation acts as \(+\Delta\) times the original state. The off-diagonal Hamiltonian still annihilates that state because the B-site cancellations survive. Its energy consequently becomes \(+0.10\,\mathrm{eV}\) with unchanged profile. The bulk edges move to \(\pm\sqrt{(t_1-t_2)^2+\Delta^2}\), giving a gap \(2\sqrt{0.4^2+0.1^2}=0.825\,\mathrm{eV}\). Exact zero energy depended on the off-diagonal symmetry. The measured decay ratio and the specified termination still identify the hopping order for this uniform sublattice shift.
