1. Conductivity and Hall data

A film has \(R_H=-6.25\times10^{-4}\) m³/C and \(\sigma=160\) S/m. Assume one isotropic carrier band and \(e=1.60\times10^{-19}\) C. (a) Determine carrier sign and density. (b) Determine the mobility and drift speed at 50 V/m. (c) Explain what becomes ambiguous if a second band is populated.

Recover the two independent quantities

The sign indicates electrons in this model; \(n=1/(e|R_H|)=1.00\times10^{22}\) m⁻³. The mobility is \(\sigma |R_H|=0.100\) m²/(V·s), giving drift speed 5.0 m/s opposite the electric field. With two carrier types the conductivity is a mobility-weighted sum while the Hall numerator contains a signed mobility-squared combination. The two observations alone cannot identify four concentrations and mobilities.

2. Compensation and the mass-action law

At a given temperature \(n_i=10^{10}\) cm⁻³. (a) Solve neutrality for net donor density \(D=1.5\times10^{10}\) cm⁻³. (b) Reverse the sign of \(D\) and recompute. (c) If \(N_c=N_v\), infer the shift of the chemical potential from its intrinsic value in units of \(k_BT\).

Use the positive concentration root

For positive \(D\), \(\sqrt{D^2+4n_i^2}=2.5\times10^{10}\) cm⁻³, giving \(n=2.0\times10^{10}\) and \(p=0.50\times10^{10}\) cm⁻³. Reversing the compensation interchanges the two concentrations. From \(n/n_i=e^{(\mu-\mu_i)/k_BT}\) the shifts are \(+\ln2\) and \(-\ln2\), respectively. In this example dropping minority carriers would overestimate the imbalance needed to obtain a given majority concentration.

3. Does the computed gap survive two controls?

Read the optical band-edge data. (a) Calculate the relative gap for (13,1) from the displayed edges. (b) Give an absolute-frequency prediction for a uniform (13,13) cell of length 2 mm. (c) Propose one numerical and one physical test to distinguish a real narrow gap from a mesh artefact.

Compare scale, contrast and discretisation

The relative gap is \(2(0.2566-0.1509)/(0.2566+0.1509)=0.519\), subject to the rounded edges. In a uniform cell \(f=c/(2na)\simeq20.8\) GHz at the folded crossing, with no gap. Refine the mesh until the separation is insensitive to further refinement at the claimed precision. Then reduce contrast to zero at fixed geometry: the splitting must close. An independent layer-matrix computation at the same nonzero contrast can check the two edges without spatial meshing.

Reconstruct the edges of a photonic gap

A periodic dielectric has a midgap reduced frequency \(f_{\rm mid}a/c=0.20375\) and relative width 51.89%. Recover the two edges and compare them with the high-contrast run. A geometrically similar cell has period 2 mm. Estimate the corresponding frequencies in GHz using \(c=3.00\times10^8\) m/s.

Work through the solution

Writing the edges \(x_-,x_+\) in reduced units gives

\[(x_++x_-)/2=0.20375,\]
\[x_+-x_-=0.5189(0.20375).\]

Hence \(x_\pm=0.20375(1\pm0.5189/2)\), or 0.1509 and 0.2566. Multiplying by \(c/a=150\) GHz gives about 22.6 and 38.5 GHz. The same reduced spectrum can occupy different frequency bands in hertz when all lengths scale together and the permittivities are frequency-independent.
