Quantising the orbit and finding the zero Landau level

In a magnetic field the two kinetic momentum components cannot be specified simultaneously. In a conical band their commutator turns the two sublattice equations into coupled oscillator equations. Their spectra differ by one state, leaving an unpaired level at zero energy.

Construct the oscillator from kinetic momentum

In one valley of a gapless monolayer use the long-wavelength Hamiltonian \(H=v_F(\sigma_x\Pi_x+\sigma_y\Pi_y)\), with Pauli matrices acting on sublattices. For an electron of charge \(-e\),

\[\boldsymbol\Pi=\boldsymbol p+e\boldsymbol A.\]

In a uniform perpendicular field \(B>0\), applying \([p_j,A_k]=-i\hbar\partial_j A_k\) gives

\[[\Pi_x,\Pi_y]=-ie\hbar B.\]

Introduce \(a=(\Pi_x-i\Pi_y)/\sqrt{2e\hbar B}\). Its adjoint uses the opposite sign, giving \([a,a^\dagger]=1\). The magnetic length \(\ell_B=\sqrt{\hbar/(eB)}\) sets the spatial size of the oscillator.

\[H=v_F\sqrt{2e\hbar B}\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\a^\dagger&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad H^2=2e\hbar Bv_F^2\begin{pmatrix}aa^\dagger&0\\0&a^\dagger a\end{pmatrix}.\]

In the B component the squared Hamiltonian has oscillator numbers \(0,1,2,\ldots\). In the A component \(aa^\dagger=a^\dagger a+1\), so its sequence starts at one. For integer \(n\ge1\) combine \(|n-1\rangle\) on A with \(\pm|n\rangle\) on B in equal magnitudes. Acting with \(H\) confirms energies \(E_{n,\pm}=\pm v_F\sqrt{2e\hbar Bn}\). For \(n=0\), the B-only spinor with \(|0\rangle\) is annihilated by \(a\). Its energy vanishes. Valley and field conventions can exchange which sublattice carries that state.

Count independent centres at the same energy

Choose gauge \(A_y=Bx\) and retain translation along \(y\). Quantisation gives

\[k_y=2\pi j/L_y.\]

The kinetic momentum \(\Pi_y=\hbar k_y+eBx\) places the orbit centre at \(x_0=-\hbar k_y/(eB)\). Successive centres differ by \(2\pi\ell_B^2/L_y\). Counting centres in a width \(L_x\) gives \(L_xL_y/(2\pi\ell_B^2)\), or \(eB/h\) states per area for each spin and valley, apart from edge corrections. This degeneracy is independent of level index. Spin splitting, lattice effects and interactions are set aside here.

At \(B=1\,\mathrm T\), \(v_F=10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}\), \(\ell_B=25.7\,\mathrm{nm}\) and the first positive level is \(36.3\,\mathrm{meV}\) above zero. The next sits at \(51.3\,\mathrm{meV}\). The spacing between the first two positive levels is consequently only 15.0 meV. At 4 T all these energies double while the number of orbit centres per unit area quadruples. A parabolic band would instead give equally spaced levels at fixed field.

Identify the measurable dependence

Compare the first few positive levels as field changes. Dividing \(E_n\) by \(\sqrt{Bn}\) should return one material-dependent coefficient. An optical measurement sees transitions between occupied and available states, subject to matrix-element selection rules. A measured transition energy therefore need not coincide with the energy of an individual level. At zero field this discrete-level construction loses its normalisable magnetic oscillator; the limiting energies on the graph describe the approach from finite field.

Tutorial on two spectral lines

At \(B=2\,\mathrm T\) an ideal sample has a zero-to-first level spacing of \(48\,\mathrm{meV}\). Infer its velocity relative to \(10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}\). Predict the first-to-second positive-level spacing at \(8\,\mathrm T\).

Work through the solution

The reference first level at 2 T is \(36.3\sqrt2=51.3\,\mathrm{meV}\), so \(v_F\simeq0.935\times10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}\). Raising the field by a factor of four doubles all energies. The first level at 8 T is 96 meV, and \(E_2-E_1=96(\sqrt2-1)=39.8\,\mathrm{meV}\). This inference assumes an ungapped cone without interaction shifts.

At \(B=2\,\mathrm T\), estimate the number of orbit centres in area \(1\,\mu\mathrm m^2\) for a single level. Give both the per-flavour count and the full spin–valley count.

Work through the solution

Use \(eBA/h\), with \(A=10^{-12}\,\mathrm m^2\). This gives roughly 484 centres per flavour and 1934 across four flavours. The numbers are approximate because a finite sample admits edge corrections, whereas the area formula is a bulk degeneracy density.

At high positive level index show how the level spacing regains the semiclassical cyclotron frequency \(eB/m_c\).

Work through the solution

Write \(E_n^2=2e\hbar B v_F^2 n\). At large \(n\) the difference \(E_{n+1}-E_n\simeq dE/dn=e\hbar Bv_F^2/E_n\). Dividing by \(\hbar\) gives \(eB/m_c\) with \(m_c=E_n/v_F^2\). This connection uses many occupied levels, while the unpaired zero state is outside the semiclassical limit.
