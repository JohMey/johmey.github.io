Separate frozen flux from equilibrium screening

For mobile carriers of mass \(m\), charge \(q\) and number density \(n_s\), a simple inertial equation \(m\dot{\mathbf v}=q\mathbf E\) gives

\[\partial_t\mathbf j_s=n_s q^2\mathbf E/m.\]

This first London equation explains dissipation-free acceleration by an electric field in the ideal response. Take its curl and use Faraday's law: \(\partial_t[\nabla\times\mathbf j_s+(n_s q^2/m)\mathbf B]=0\) at fixed uniform \(n_s\). The expression in brackets is time independent. This derivative equation by itself allows an arbitrary initial curl–field combination, so it does not force a previously existing static induction to leave a conductor.

In a simply connected, vortex-free equilibrium superconductor the second London relation supplies the missing condition,

\[\nabla\times\mathbf j_s=-(n_sq^2/m)\mathbf B.\]

A condensate phase argument gives the same curl relation locally: mechanical pair momentum is \(\hbar\nabla\varphi-q\mathbf A\); on a patch without a vortex \(\nabla\times\nabla\varphi=0\). With \(\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf j_s\) and \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0\), take the curl once more to obtain

\[\nabla^2\mathbf B=\mathbf B/\lambda_L^2,\]
\[\lambda_L^2=m/(\mu_0 n_s q^2).\]

The pair of signs yields exponential decay, not sinusoidal oscillation into a bulk specimen.

The effective mass, charge and density must describe the same carriers. For Cooper pairs of mass about \(2m_e\), charge magnitude \(2e\), and pair density \(n_{\rm electron}/2\), the combination \(n_s q^2/m\) agrees with the expression in terms of participating electron density, charge \(e\) and mass \(m_e\). Mixing a pair charge with an electron density and electron mass overpredicts the stiffness by a factor of four. A material effective mass or nonlocal response may alter an inferred density, so a penetration-depth measurement fixes the stiffness combination rather than each microscopic parameter separately.

Solve a finite slab in a parallel applied field

A slab fills \(-d/2<x<d/2\) and a uniform applied induction \(B_a\hat z\) is imposed parallel to both faces. Assume the Meissner regime, negligible demagnetising fields, and a thickness large compared with microscopic dimensions needed for continuum electrodynamics. The even London solution is

\[B_z(x)=B_a\cosh(x/\lambda_L)/\cosh[d/(2\lambda_L)].\]

Its current is \(j_y=-(1/\mu_0)\partial_xB_z\), of opposite signs on the two faces. The mean induction is

\[\frac{\bar B}{B_a}=\frac{2\lambda_L}{d}\tanh\frac{d}{2\lambda_L}.\]

In a thick slab the centre field is exponentially small while the average falls only as \(2\lambda_L/d\), because a surface layer from each face still contains field. A probe at the centre and one measuring total flux through the cross section thus report different thickness dependence. In a thin slab the average is \(1-d^2/(12\lambda_L^2)+\cdots\), giving weak screening of a parallel field. This is not the perpendicular-field screening problem for a thin film, where fields outside the sample and lateral geometry lead to another length scale.

For \(\lambda_L=100\,{\rm nm}\), \(d=400\,{\rm nm}\), the centre ratio is \(\operatorname{sech}2=0.266\) and the mean ratio is

\[\tanh2/2=0.482.\]

With \(B_a=0.50\,{\rm mT}\) the current magnitude just inside a face is

\[B_a\tanh2/(\mu_0\lambda_L)=3.84\times10^9\,{\rm A/m^2}.\]

It flows only over a thin scale. If a material's critical current or critical field is exceeded, this linear vortex-free solution ceases to describe the experiment. Driven hysteresis or trapped vortices can then make the same external field produce different internal profiles after different preparation histories.

Connect kinetic energy to a sheet inductance

A thin uniform strip of length \(l\), width \(w\), thickness \(d\ll\lambda_L\) carries a current nearly uniformly through its thickness in a suitable transport geometry. The carrier kinetic energy per volume is

\[mn_s v^2/2=m j_s^2/(2n_s q^2)=\mu_0\lambda_L^2j_s^2/2.\]

Integrating over volume and using \(j_s=I/(wd)\) gives \(E_{\rm kin}=L_k I^2/2\) with \(L_k=\mu_0\lambda_L^2 l/(wd)\). Thus the sheet kinetic inductance is \(L_{k,\square}=\mu_0\lambda_L^2/d\), and the number of squares is \(l/w\). It is distinct from geometric inductance storing energy in the magnetic field around the wire.

For \(d=20\,{\rm nm}\), \(\lambda_L=100\,{\rm nm}\) the sheet value is about \(0.628\,{\rm pH}\) per square. A strip containing two thousand squares gives \(1.26\,{\rm nH}\) of kinetic inductance. Its resonance with a capacitor probes the sum of this inductance and geometric or stray contributions. If the superfluid density falls while the effective mass stays approximately fixed, \(\lambda_L^2\) increases and the resonant frequency tends to decrease. Measuring several lengths and widths can distinguish a distributed kinetic contribution from an approximately constant lead contribution. At frequencies approaching the gap scale a purely nondissipative London description is insufficient.

Determine what a thickness series can identify

A thin-film measurement sometimes fits the sheet kinetic inductance at a single thickness and quotes a bulk condensate density. In the uniform-film limit the measured product is \(L_{k,\square}d=\mu_0\lambda_L^2\). A thickness calibration error therefore rescales the inferred stiffness even if the resonance frequency is measured extremely accurately. For the \(20\,{\rm nm}\) example, a systematically inactive layer of \(2.0\,{\rm nm}\) leaves only \(18\,{\rm nm}\) contributing. Substituting the nominal thickness into a stiffness inference underestimates the conducting carrier density by ten percent if the other model assumptions hold. A series of films and an independent estimate of the active thickness can test this interpretation.

Compare with the parallel-field slab calculation by changing the ratio of thickness to penetration depth in the live profile. At a ratio of one, the centre and surface inductions differ only modestly. At a ratio of ten the interior is nearly field free while a substantial fraction of the averaged flux still resides near the two faces. The programme graphs \(B/B_a\) across the physical thickness and reports both centre and mean, which are different experimental weightings of the same field. Compare those numbers with an independently measured kinetic inductance before attributing a temperature shift entirely to an altered carrier density. Higher kinetic inductance can also reflect an increased effective mass or a reduced active cross section.

Compare parallel screening with a perpendicular-field measurement

For a wide film in perpendicular field the magnetic flux lines bend around the edges, and the one-dimensional parallel-field cosh solution no longer applies. Sample width, aspect ratio and the field outside the superconducting material enter the response. Rotate the same thin film through ninety degrees and an apparent change in susceptibility may therefore be largely geometrical. Verify orientation and demagnetising conditions before combining the two datasets into one estimate of penetration depth.

Tutorial on comparing probes

A thick slab shows a centre field less than one percent of the applied parallel field. What lower bound on \(d/\lambda_L\) follows from the local London solution?

Work through the solution

Require \(\cosh[d/(2\lambda_L)]>100\), hence \(d/\lambda_L>2\operatorname{arcosh}(100)\approx10.60\). The mean field at this threshold would still be about \(2/10.60=0.189\) of the applied field. A centre measurement at one percent therefore does not imply a one-percent cross-section average.

If the superfluid charge stiffness \(n_sq^2/m\) falls by half, how do penetration depth and sheet kinetic inductance change for the same film?

Work through the solution

The penetration depth increases by \(\sqrt2\), and the sheet kinetic inductance doubles because it scales as \(\lambda_L^2\). A resonance in which kinetic inductance is only one quarter of total inductance will shift less than the \(1/\sqrt2\) expected if all inductance doubled. The initial circuit partition must be estimated.

A ring retains flux after cooling in a field. Does this disprove the equilibrium slab result?

Work through the solution

The slab derivation assumed simple connectivity, a vortex-free state and prescribed fields on two faces. A ring permits nontrivial phase winding and fluxoid quantisation. A multiply connected circuit can maintain trapped flux even with local London screening in its material. Compare with a simply connected specimen under controlled zero-field cooling and field-cooling protocols before interpreting the ring as a direct slab test.
