Displace an electron fluid against its background

Consider a wide slab with carrier number density \(n\), band mass \(m^*\) and immobile neutralising positive background. Displace the electron cloud uniformly through a small distance \(u\) normal to the faces. The opposite surface charges have magnitude \(ne u\) per area and generate a restoring field \(E=ne u/(\epsilon_0\epsilon_\infty)\) inside. The factor \(\epsilon_\infty\) accounts for faster electronic degrees of freedom excluded from the carrier model.

\[m^*\ddot u=-\frac{ne^2}{\epsilon_0\epsilon_\infty}u,\]
\[\omega_p^2=\frac{ne^2}{m^*\epsilon_0\epsilon_\infty}.\]

In a homogeneous bulk the same long-wavelength mode follows from continuity, carrier momentum and Gauss's law for a longitudinal density fluctuation. A longitudinal self-sustaining oscillation occurs at a zero of the dielectric function. With damping time \(\tau\) the relative permittivity in this model is \(\epsilon(\omega)=\epsilon_\infty[1-\omega_p^2/(\omega(\omega+i/\tau))]\) for \(e^{-i\omega t}\). Electron energy loss is sensitive to \(-\mathrm{Im}(1/\epsilon)\), while transmission also depends on interfaces.

For \(n=10^{24}\) m⁻³, \(m^*=0.20m_e\) and \(\epsilon_\infty=12\), the model gives \(\omega_p\simeq3.64\times10^{13}\) rad/s, or 5.80 THz. Four times the density doubles this frequency if mass and background screening are unchanged.

Solve an effective electron–hole relative-coordinate problem

In a three-dimensional direct-gap semiconductor a photon can create a conduction electron and a valence hole. Separate their centre of mass from their relative coordinate. Near parabolic band edges the relative mass obeys \(\mu^{-1}=1/m_e^*+1/m_h^*\). Approximate screening by a constant \(\epsilon_r\) over the length and time scales of the pair. The relative Hamiltonian is then hydrogenic.

\[H_{\rm rel}=-\frac{\hbar^2\nabla^2}{2\mu}-\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon_r r},\]
\[E_n=E_g-\frac{R^*}{n^2}.\]

Minimising the expectation for an exponential radius \(a\) gives kinetic energy \(\hbar^2/(2\mu a^2)\) and potential \(-e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon_r a)\). The minimum yields \(a^*=(\epsilon_r m_e/\mu)a_0\) and \(R^*=(\mu/m_e)13.606\,{\rm eV}/\epsilon_r^2\). With \(m_e^*=0.20m_e\), \(m_h^*=0.50m_e\), \(\epsilon_r=10\), these give \(\mu=0.1429m_e\), \(a^*=3.70\) nm and \(R^*=19.4\) meV. The most strongly bound line lies this distance below the unbound continuum. A large radius relative to the unit cell supports the effective-mass model. A sub-gap peak alone can also come from a defect.

Let a single carrier relax a lattice coordinate

Take a local displacement \(Q_i\), stiffness \(K\), and local occupation \(n_i\). Each site's lattice energy and coupling are \(KQ_i^2/2-g Q_i n_i\). At a fixed occupation distribution minimise over each displacement. In the adiabatic classical distortion model,

\[Q_i=g n_i/K,\]
\[E_{\rm relax}=-\frac{g^2}{2K}\sum_i n_i^2 .\]

A carrier with all its weight on one site gains \(g^2/(2K)\). With uniform weight across \(N\) sites the gain is smaller by \(N\). Hopping competes with this localisation energy. For \(g=2.0\) eV/nm and \(K=20\) eV/nm² the one-site relaxation is 0.10 eV and the optimum displacement 0.10 nm. Compare with the bandwidth before concluding that a small polaron will form. Quantised phonons, tunnelling of the distortion and long-range polar coupling are additional physics.

A mode increasing from 5.8 to 8.2 THz as the carrier density doubles follows the plasmon square-root trend. The simple isolated exciton model has no such density law. At high density screening or many-body effects can also modify the exciton spectrum.

3 · Test the localisation argument

Work through the solution

For equal occupation of two sites the distortion energy gain is half the one-site gain. A two-site bonding state can lower the electronic kinetic energy by a hopping energy \(t\). If \(t\) is large compared with the difference in relaxation energies, localisation cannot be inferred from the static distortion gain alone.
