Project · Measure a cavity and identify two different loss mechanisms

A narrow optical resonance can identify a cavity's round-trip phase and total decay. Use a time trace, a spectrum and a change of coupling to distinguish information that a single measurement cannot supply.

Establish what resonates

In a one-dimensional ideal cavity of length \(L\) in vacuum the tangential electric field vanishes at two perfectly conducting end planes. Derive the allowed wavelengths and the frequency spacing. For \(L=0.15\) m calculate the spacing. Which assumption lets the spacing remain constant across the spectrum?

Work through the solution

A standing mode is proportional to \(\sin(kz)\) with \(kL=m\pi\). Thus \(\lambda_m=2L/m\) and \(f_m=mc/(2L)\). Adjacent modes stay about 0.9993 GHz apart. A frequency-independent phase speed and frequency-independent boundary phases have been assumed. Mirror phase dispersion or a dispersive fill changes the spacing.

Reconcile a ring-down trace with the spectral line

For a mode at \(f_0=10\) GHz the stored energy falls by \(1/e\) in 0.2 μs after the drive is removed. Compute the energy decay rate, quality factor and expected full power linewidth. Compare energy and field-amplitude time constants in the cavity experiment.

Work through the solution

Write \(U=U_0e^{-\kappa t}\). The rate is \(5.0\times10^6\) s⁻¹. The field amplitude falls as \(e^{-\kappa t/2}\), with time constant 0.4 μs. The driven response has denominator \((\kappa/2)^2+(\omega-\omega_0)^2\). The full width at half maximum in ordinary frequency is \(\kappa/(2\pi)=0.796\) MHz, giving \(Q\simeq12566\). These expressions agree with \(Q=\omega_0U/P_\mathrm{loss}\).

Infer loss rates from a second measurement

The initial linewidth includes an intrinsic energy decay rate \(\kappa_i\) and output-coupling rate \(\kappa_e\). A calibrated modification halves \(\kappa_e\) while keeping \(\kappa_i\) fixed, lengthening the energy decay time to 0.25 μs. Find each rate and the fraction of stored energy that could leave through the port before the change.

Work through the solution

Originally \(\kappa_i+\kappa_e=5\times10^6\) s⁻¹. After the change \(\kappa_i+\kappa_e/2=4\times10^6\) s⁻¹. Subtraction gives \(\kappa_e=2\times10^6\) and \(\kappa_i=3\times10^6\) s⁻¹. The initial out-coupled fraction is

\[\kappa_e/(\kappa_i+\kappa_e)=0.4.\]

A lone linewidth constrains only the sum. The second experiment supplies independent information.

Challenge the assumption of a single stored mode

An observed photodetector signal oscillates on top of an exponential envelope. Describe a cause consistent with a linear cavity and an experiment that distinguishes it from a defective exponential integrator.

Work through the solution

If two modes are populated, the detector records \(|a_1e^{-i\omega_1t}+a_2e^{-i\omega_2t}|^2\). The cross term oscillates at their difference frequency. Narrow the excitation band or spatially select one mode and repeat. A numerical time-step test can identify solver error, but changing the prepared physical field tests the single-mode assumption directly.
