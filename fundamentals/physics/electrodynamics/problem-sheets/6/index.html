A reflected step

A line of characteristic impedance 75 Ω and propagation speed \(2.00\times10^8\) m/s carries a 1.20 V step. A 0.360 V downward return step appears 480 ns after launch. Find the distance, reflection coefficient, load resistance and total first voltage at the load.

Show the fault reconstruction

The wave made a round trip, so the mismatch is \(vt/2=48.0\) m away. The signed ratio is

\[\Gamma=-0.360/1.20=-0.300.\]

Invert the reflection law to get \(R_L=75(1+\Gamma)/(1-\Gamma)=40.4\) Ω. Voltage at the load rises to \(1.20(1-0.300)=0.840\) V on first arrival. Its total current, \((1.20+0.360)/75=20.8\) mA, gives the same resistance.

Weigh the electric pull

Horizontal vacuum plates of area 40.0 cm² are separated by 0.500 mm. Find the voltage producing 2.00 mN of attraction. If disconnected and separated quasistatically to 1.00 mm, determine mechanical work supplied and final voltage.

Show the two controlled stages

Pressure is \(F/A=0.500\) Pa and \(E=\sqrt{2p/\epsilon_0}=3.36\times10^5\) V/m. The supply must provide \(V=Ed=168\) V. After disconnection the field stays fixed for broad plates because charge and area are fixed. The opposing force remains 2.00 mN, so the applied mechanical work is \(F\Delta d=1.00\) μJ. The final voltage is \(336\) V. The increased electrical field volume accounts for the stored energy.

Check a field-energy inventory

A uniform 50 Ω lossless cable has propagation speed \(2.00\times10^8\) m/s. A rectangular 2.0 V forward pulse is 100 ns long. Find its length, the total travelling energy and its separate electric and magnetic parts. Where does the energy go at an ideal open termination?

Show the travelling inventory

The pulse occupies \(v\Delta t=20.0\) m. Its power is \(V^2/Z_0=0.080\) W and energy is \(8.0\) nJ. The distributed parameters follow from \(Z_0\) and \(v\), giving \(C'=1/(vZ_0)=100\) pF/m and \(L'=Z_0/v=250\) nH/m. Electric energy \(\tfrac12 C'\ell V^2\) is \(4.0\) nJ and magnetic energy is equal since \(I=V/Z_0\). At an open circuit the voltage reflection coefficient is \(+1\) and current cancels at the end. The reflected pulse ultimately carries back all 8.0 nJ; instantaneous electric and magnetic shares can change during overlap.

Account for the energy of a charging capacitor

Two circular plates of radius 5.00 cm, separated by 1.00 mm of vacuum, are charged by a constant current of 1.00 mA. At the instant their voltage is 10.0 V, find the displacement current, magnetic field at the plate rim, increase in electric energy per second and direction of electromagnetic energy flow. Neglect fringe fields in the gap.

Worked solution

The field between the plates is V/d = 10⁴ V/m. The capacitance is ε₀πR²/d = 69.5 pF. Since dV/dt = I/C, the electric flux changes at precisely the rate needed to give ε₀ dΦ/dt = I through the gap. Thus a circular Ampèrian path returns the same result whichever spanning surface one chooses.

Inside the disk only the fraction r²/R² of the changing flux is enclosed. At the rim B = μ₀I/(2πR) = 4.00 nT. The magnetic field is azimuthal. With the displacement current along the electric field, E × B points radially inward. The Poynting flux at the rim has magnitude EB/μ₀ = 31.8 W/m².

The side area is 2πRd = 3.14 × 10⁻⁴ m², giving 0.0100 W inward. The derivative of ½CV² is CV dV/dt = VI = 0.0100 W. Both calculations close the same energy balance. The uniform-field approximation describes the interior and integrated flux well for d much less than R. It cannot resolve the local fringe-field pattern right at the metal edges.

Can impedance identify a dielectric by itself?

A coaxial line with fixed radii and relative permittivity 2.25 measures 47.4537 Ω. A second sample measures 44.0381 Ω. Assuming concentric geometry and the same permeability, infer its relative permittivity. Use the laboratory’s offset-conductor run to construct a second possible explanation. Which additional measurement distinguishes these two explanations?

Work through the solution

For a homogeneous concentric TEM line \(Z_0=\sqrt{L'/C'}\) and \(C'\) scales with permittivity while \(L'\) is fixed. Thus

\[\epsilon_{r,2}=2.25(47.4537/44.0381)^2=2.613.\]

An unchanged dielectric with a 0.40 mm offset gives the same impedance in the numerical study. Wave speed distinguishes them. Geometry cancels in \(L'C'=\mu\epsilon\) for a homogeneous TEM line. A dielectric change would reduce the speed by a factor \(\sqrt{2.25/2.613}=0.928\), while the offset alone leaves it unchanged.
