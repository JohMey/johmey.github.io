Metal penetration and guide cutoff

Distinguish magnetic diffusion from waveguide cutoff by calculating their frequency and length dependence.

Problem sheet 5 · Chapters 9 and 10

Invert a penetration measurement

At 400 Hz the tangential magnetic amplitude falls to \(1/e\) over 4.00 mm in a homogeneous nonmagnetic half-space. Infer conductivity, predict the phase lag at 6.00 mm, and find the frequency that halves the penetration depth.

Show the diffusion calculation

The measured length is \(\delta\). Inverting \(\delta^2=1/(\pi f\mu_0\sigma)\) gives \(\sigma=3.96\times10^7\) S/m. Depth divided by \(\delta\) is the phase lag in radians, giving 1.50 rad with amplitude ratio \(e^{-1.5}=0.223\). Since \(\delta\) scales with \(f^{-1/2}\), a 1.60 kHz drive gives 2.00 mm. A second nearby boundary or frequency-dependent permeability would spoil this inference.

A microwave pulse between walls

An empty rectangular guide has width 30.0 mm and height 12.0 mm. Find the first two cutoffs. At 8.00 GHz determine the group velocity and envelope transit time over 3.00 m. Use \(c=3.00\times10^8\) m/s.

Show the allowed band

The first cutoff \(c/(2a)\) is 5.00 GHz. Among the next candidates \(c/a=10.0\) GHz and \(c/(2b)=12.5\) GHz, the former is lower. Thus only TE\(_{10}\) propagates at 8 GHz. Its group velocity is \(c\sqrt{1-(5/8)^2}=2.34\times10^8\) m/s and transit takes 12.8 ns. The free-space value would be 10.0 ns. This applies to a narrowband excitation with negligible end-reflection delays.

Two decaying fields

Use the same guide below cutoff at 4.00 GHz. Derive its amplitude decay length, compute the amplitude ratio after 30.0 mm and explain how increasing frequency distinguishes this measured decay from conductor skin effect.

Show the field comparison

Subcutoff \(\beta=i\alpha\) with \(\alpha=(2\pi/c)\sqrt{f_c^2-f^2}=62.8\) m⁻¹. Its amplitude decay length is \(15.9\) mm, giving \(e^{-62.8(0.030)}=0.152\) after 30 mm in a long uniform guide. Raising frequency towards cutoff increases this length, whereas a good conductor's skin depth decreases. An ideal evanescent mode in a semi-infinite empty guide does not dissipate power. Metal penetration with finite conductivity does dissipate power through \(\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf E\).
