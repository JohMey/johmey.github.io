Reconcile two field instruments

A normally incident plane wave in vacuum gives a measured RMS magnetic field of 12 nT. Find the RMS electric field and mean intensity. An ideal absorber covers 25 cm²; determine its mean heating rate and explain how a standing wave would complicate the inference from a single magnetic probe.

Reconstruct the Poynting flux

For a forward plane wave \(E_{\rm rms}=cB_{\rm rms}=3.60\) V/m and \(\langle S\rangle=E_{\rm rms}^2/Z_{\rm vac}=0.0344\) W/m². The area is 0.0025 m², so mean power is \(86\) µW. A standing wave can have a magnetic antinode with substantial stored energy even though oppositely directed powers cancel. Then a measurement of the magnetic field alone fails to determine the net power delivered.

Infer total dipole radiation

A far-zone probe oriented correctly at \(r=8.0\) m and \(\theta=45°\) from an electric dipole axis reads 1.20 µW/m². Infer the equatorial intensity at the same distance, total radiated power and the reading at 16 m and 90°. State a test for an unwanted reflected wave.

Integrate the angular pattern

At 45° the factor \(\sin^2\theta\) is one half. The equatorial intensity is therefore 2.40 µW/m². Total power follows from \(P=(8\pi/3)r^2S_{\rm eq}\), giving 1.29 mW. Doubling radius at the equator gives 0.600 µW/m². A radial scan at fixed angle should make \(r^2S\) constant. Reproducible oscillations over fractions of a wavelength can reveal interference with a reflection.

Which quantity is fixed as frequency changes?

A very short current element of effective length \(\ell\) has \(\dot p=I\ell\), with sinusoidal current of peak amplitude \(I_0\). Express dipole radiation power in terms of current amplitude. Double the frequency at fixed current and then at fixed dipole moment. Compare and interpret the two power ratios.

Trace the frequency dependence

For a sinusoid \(p_0=I_0\ell/\omega\). Inserting this into \(P=\omega^4p_0^2/(12\pi\epsilon_0c^3)\) gives

\[P=\omega^2I_0^2\ell^2/(12\pi\epsilon_0c^3).\]

At fixed current it grows fourfold, while at fixed dipole moment it grows sixteenfold. If the original power were 0.50 mW, the outcomes would be 2.0 and 8.0 mW. At the higher frequency the same current displaces less charge per half-cycle. This explains why the two experiments impose different source conditions.

Choose a frame for electric-dominated crossed fields

In a laboratory \(E_x=12.0\) MV/m and \(B_z=0.0200\) T. Decide which field can vanish in an inertial frame. Determine the velocity of that frame and the magnitude of the remaining field. Use \(c=3.00\times10^8\) m/s.

Transform the magnetic component first

Here \(cB=6.00\) MV/m, less than \(E\). Set \(\mathbf u=c^2\mathbf E\times\mathbf B/E^2\) so

\[\mathbf u\times\mathbf E/c^2=\mathbf B.\]

The velocity is \(1.50\times10^8\) m/s along negative \(y\). The remaining electric field is \(\sqrt{E^2-c^2B^2}=10.4\) MV/m, consistent with invariance of \(E^2-c^2B^2\). Eliminating the electric field instead would require

\[E/B=6.00\times10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}.\]
