The axis and the midplane

Put charges \(+2.00\) nC and \(-2.00\) nC at \(z=+3.0\) mm and \(-3.0\) mm. Obtain the leading voltage at \(z=0.120\) m and the electric field at the same radial distance in the midplane. Explain which observable remains on the midplane.

Show the dipole calculation

The separation is \(6.0\) mm, giving \(p=1.20\times10^{-11}\) C m along positive \(z\). The axial potential is

\[kp/r^2=7.49\,\mathrm{V}.\]

The midplane potential vanishes exactly by symmetry. There the leading field is \(-kp\,\mathbf e_z/r^3\), or \(62.4\) V/m towards negative \(z\). Although tangent derivatives in the midplane vanish, the voltage changes in the perpendicular direction. The small source ratio \(0.003/0.12=0.025\) makes the neglected relative finite-size corrections of order \(0.025^2\).

Measure the dielectric constant

A sphere of radius 3.0 mm in a field of 20.0 kV/m creates an additional axial voltage of 1.20 V at 15.0 mm. Determine its relative permittivity and predict the interior field. State one geometry change that tests this interpretation.

Show the inversion

The dipole coefficient is \(B=\Delta V r^2=2.70\times10^{-4}\) V m². Thus

\[s=B/(a^3E_0)=0.500.\]

From \(s=(\epsilon_r-1)/(\epsilon_r+2)\) obtain

\[\epsilon_r=(1+2s)/(1-s)=4.00.\]

The uniform interior field is \(3E_0/(\epsilon_r+2)=10.0\) kV/m. At twice the axial distance the extra voltage should fall to \(0.300\) V. Measuring the extra voltage requires subtracting the no-sphere reading, which increases in magnitude away from the origin.

A nearly neutral inclusion

A polarized sphere has induced moment \(3.0\times10^{-13}\) C m and a residual free charge of \(+1.0\) pC. Find the axial distance where charge and dipole produce equal potential magnitudes. For two probes placed at opposite axial positions, derive combinations that isolate the two contributions.

Show the sum and difference

Equating \(kQ/r\) and \(kp/r^2\) gives \(r=p/Q=0.30\) m, assuming the probes are outside the sphere. After subtracting imposed background voltage, the two readings are \(V_\pm=kQ/r\pm kp/r^2\). Their average isolates the monopole while their half difference isolates the dipole. At the crossover each isolated term has magnitude \(0.0300\) V. An even quadrupole would also enter the average, so repeat at a second radius to distinguish its faster \(r^{-3}\) potential decay.
