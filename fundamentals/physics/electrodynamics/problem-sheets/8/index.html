Project · Trace energy from a wave to the material supporting it

A plane wave illuminates a transparent dielectric and then a metal surface. Use field boundary conditions, flux, mechanical reaction and dissipation to account for the same energy in different descriptions.

Problem sheet 8 · Integrative project

Start with field amplitudes

A normally incident 300 V/m peak plane wave arrives from vacuum at a lossless nonmagnetic glass of refractive index 1.5. Assume a semi-infinite medium. Derive the reflected and transmitted field amplitudes by matching tangential electric and magnetic fields. Check the net cycle-averaged power.

Work through the solution

Tangential continuity gives \(1+r=t\) and \(1-r=1.5t\), where the minus sign follows from the reversed magnetic field of the reflected wave. Hence \(r=-0.2\), \(t=0.8\). The reflected electric amplitude is \(-60\) V/m and the transmitted amplitude 240 V/m. Reflectance is \(r^2=0.04\) and power transmittance is

\[1.5t^2=0.96.\]

Squaring the transmitted electric amplitude ratio alone would omit the wave impedance.

Locate stored energy on the incident side

Use the incident and reflected energy experiment. Predict the two boundary contributions at phase zero with \(r=-0.2\). Explain how standing structure is compatible with a constant time-averaged net flux.

Work through the solution

At the boundary the normalised electric part is \((1+r)^2=0.64\) and the magnetic part \((1-r)^2=1.44\). The product \(EH\) has cancelling mixed terms between opposite propagation directions. Averaging over a period leaves the incident flux times \(1-|r|^2\), independent of position in the homogeneous incident region.

Replace the glass by a good conductor

For \(\sigma=5.8\times10^7\) S/m, \(\mu=\mu_0\) and 100 kHz, derive the skin depth and compare with the penetration experiment. At what depth is only one per cent of the surface Joule power density left?

Work through the solution

\(\delta=1/\sqrt{\pi f\mu\sigma}\approx0.209\) mm. Field amplitude decays as \(e^{-z/\delta}\), so time-averaged local Joule heating proportional to \(|E|^2\) decays as \(e^{-2z/\delta}\). Setting this to 0.01 gives \(z=\delta\ln(100)/2\approx0.481\) mm. Confusing intensity with amplitude would double the inferred depth.

Check the global heat budget and a mechanical limit

If the known internal surface field has peak amplitude \(E_s\), integrate the Joule heating over a thick conductor. Separately consider an ideal lossless mirror with incident time-averaged intensity \(I\). How can its heat absorption vanish while it experiences a force?

Work through the solution

The heat per unit area and time is

\[(\sigma |E_s|^2/2)\int_0^\infty e^{-2z/\delta}dz=\sigma |E_s|^2\delta/4.\]

This equals the inward mean Poynting flux in the good-conductor limit, using \(1/Z=(1-i)\sigma\delta/2\) for the stated phasor convention. At a perfect mirror the momentum flux reverses, giving pressure \(2I/c\). A stationary mirror can exchange momentum without doing mechanical work.
