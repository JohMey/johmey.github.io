The electric field of a rotating magnet

Use charge balance and a surface matching calculation to predict a voltage around a rotating magnetic body, then close the mechanical and electrical power accounts.

Specify the moving material and the electrical constraint

Consider an electrically isolated conducting sphere of radius a, with prescribed uniform axial magnetisation M, rotating slowly at angular speed Ω about that axis. Work to first order in Ωa/c. Neglect deformation and the field produced by the small convective electric current. The magnetostatic interior field is uniform, B₀=2μ₀M/3. Uniform rotation about the magnetisation axis leaves that magnetic field stationary in the laboratory.

At a material point with cylindrical radius s the velocity is Ωs eφ. For a highly conducting body in stationary open circuit, mobile charges rearrange until the local motional force vanishes. This gives a laboratory electric field even though the magnetic field is steady.

\[\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B_0=0,\]
\[E_s=-\Omega B_0s,\]
\[V_{\rm in}={\Omega B_0\over2}s^2+C.\]

Gauss's law then gives the total volume charge density −2ε₀ΩB₀. Its volume integral is −8πε₀ΩB₀a³/3. Since the sphere was neutral, the surface must carry the opposite total charge. In a magnetic material the assignment between free and bound charge needs a constitutive convention. The equations here determine the total charge that produces E.

Recall · A moving conductor need not be equipotential in the laboratory

Static equipotential reasoning assumes charges are at rest in the chosen frame. A charge carried with a rotating conductor feels q(E+v×B). In open circuit it is this total force that vanishes. Stationary voltmeter leads and brushes therefore matter when defining the measured voltage.

Solve the exterior problem using neutrality

At r=a, sin²θ=2[1−P₂(cosθ)]/3. The surface potential is therefore a constant plus a quadrupole. A neutral isolated sphere has no exterior monopole, so the constant spherical harmonic must vanish when V tends to zero at infinity. This fixes C=−ΩB₀a²/3 and determines the exterior field uniquely.

\[V_{\rm out}=-{\Omega B_0a^5\over3r^3}P_2(\cos\theta).\]

Differentiate radially on each side. The interior radial field at the surface is −ΩB₀a sin²θ, while the exterior radial field is −ΩB₀aP₂. The normal jump gives the total surface charge.

\[\sigma(\theta)=\epsilon_0\Omega B_0a\left[{2\over3}-{5\over3}P_2(\cos\theta)\right].\]

Integrating the constant term over the sphere cancels the volume charge exactly, since the angular integral of P₂ vanishes. The tangential derivative of the continuous potential also matches. These are independent checks on the sign and coefficient.

For a=0.02 m, B₀=0.30 T and Ω=100 s⁻¹, the potential difference between equator and pole is ΩB₀a²/2=6.0 mV. A laboratory voltmeter with stationary leads and sliding contacts measures that difference in the ideal open-circuit model. Grounding the sphere through a stationary brush at a specified latitude fixes the potential at that contact. This changes the constant harmonic and can supply a net charge.

Track the different constraint in an insulator

An insulating magnet cannot freely redistribute charge until E+v×B vanishes. To leading order, a moving magnetisation contributes electric polarisation P=v×M/c². For axial rotation this points radially outward with magnitude ΩMs/c². Its bound volume charge is −2ΩM/c², and its bound surface charge is ΩMa sin²θ/c². Integrating them cancels the net charge. Solve Poisson's equation with these specified sources to determine the field. A particular interior potential is ΩMr²/(3ε₀c²). Write the remaining interior quadrupole as D r²P₂ and the exterior one as B₂P₂/r³. Continuity of potential gives B₂=D a⁵. The quadrupole part of the radial field jump is 5DaP₂. The prescribed surface charge has quadrupole part −2ΩMaP₂/(3c²), so 5Da=−2ΩMa/(3ε₀c²). Solving for D and substituting B₂ gives the exterior potential below.

\[V_{\rm out,ins}=-{2\Omega Ma^5\over15\epsilon_0c^2r^3}P_2(\cos\theta).\]

For the same M and Ω, the exterior quadrupole is three fifths of the conducting result. The distinction is a physical charge-mobility constraint. Rotation of the magnetic pattern alone cannot decide the voltage. The derivation assumes the prescribed magnetisation and ignores additional electric susceptibility of the insulating material.

Account for a working generator

Connecting a load permits a current and changes the open-circuit field. In the related conducting-disc model with imposed uniform B, an outward radial current I experiences a braking torque dτz=−IBs ds for positive axial B. Integration from axis to rim gives the braking torque −IBa²/2. The driver supplies the opposite torque. Multiplying that driving torque by Ω yields mechanical input power IΩBa²/2=Iℰ. The driver supplies the electrical power.

Remember · The magnetic force on each charge is perpendicular to its full velocity

Write a carrier velocity as the material velocity plus drift. The magnetic force transfers momentum between moving lattice and drifting carriers. Its dot product with the full carrier velocity is zero. Mechanical constraints, electric fields and collisions perform the work appearing in the machine-level energy balance. Follow both carrier and body when resolving that balance.

Test a hypothetical magnetic source consistently

In source-free vacuum define F=E+icB. The curl equations combine into i∂tF=c curl F and div F=0. Multiplying F by a constant phase rotates the electric and magnetic fields into one another while preserving the source-free equations. Ordinary electric charges select which combination couples to matter.

A symmetric hypothetical source theory can be written by assigning a magnetic charge the same units as electric charge. Then div(cB)=ρm/ε₀ and curl E=−∂tB−Jm/(ε₀c), while the electric equations retain their usual SI form. Taking a divergence of the modified Faraday equation gives ∂tρm+div Jm=0. The magnetic source convention has to be declared before using a force or energy formula. No magnetic monopole is assumed in the preceding sphere calculation.

Exercises on the observed voltage

1. Reverse Ω while leaving the magnetisation fixed. Which measured quantities change sign?

Work through the solution

V, E and the leading induced electric charges change sign. The leading magnetostatic B stays fixed. Reversing both Ω and M leaves the electric signal unchanged.

2. A sensor observes the quadrupole at r=2a and at r=4a along the axis. Find the potential and field ratios.

Work through the solution

The potential varies as r⁻³, so the farther reading is one eighth. The radial field varies as r⁻⁴, so its magnitude is one sixteenth. A monopole contamination would decay more slowly and spoil these ratios.

3. In the disc model let I=0.20 A and use the numerical values above. Calculate torque and power.

Work through the solution

τ=12 μN m. Mechanical power is 1.2 mW, equal to I times 6.0 mV. Contact and resistive losses must be included before comparing with a real load.
