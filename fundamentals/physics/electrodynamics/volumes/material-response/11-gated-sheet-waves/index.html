Why a nearby gate changes the speed law of a two-dimensional plasma

A current pulse excites a travelling density modulation in a thin conducting sheet. Move a metal gate towards the sheet and the shape of the dispersion curve changes. Rebuild the restoring electric field from the surface charge before using any oscillator analogy.

Solve the electrostatic problem for one Fourier mode

Place a two-dimensional electron sheet at \(z=0\) in a homogeneous dielectric of absolute permittivity \(\epsilon\). The equilibrium positive background is fixed. Let the electron number perturbation be \(\delta n\,e^{iqx-i\omega t}\) with \(q>0\), so the perturbation of charge per area is \(\sigma=-e\,\delta n\). Away from the sheet the potential solves \((\partial_z^2-q^2)\phi=0\). Decay on both sides selects \(A e^{-q|z|}\). Across zero, the normal field discontinuity gives \(2\epsilon q A=\sigma\). Consequently the bare sheet has \(\phi(0)=\sigma/(2\epsilon q)\). The inverse power of \(q\) reflects the field extending a distance of order \(1/q\) away from the sheet.

Ground a parallel, perfectly conducting plane at \(z=d\). Place an image of opposite surface charge at \(2d\) to enforce zero potential at the conductor. The transverse exponential suppresses the contribution that travels from this image to the physical sheet. Linear superposition therefore gives

\[\phi(0)=\frac{-e\,\delta n}{2\epsilon q}(1-e^{-2qd}).\]

This result assumes that charge is free to flow into the gate from a ground reservoir and that the dielectric is homogeneous throughout the gap. A floating finite plate would require an additional global charge condition. The zero mode also needs its own electrostatic reference and is excluded from this derivation.

Combine continuity with the acceleration equation

Writing \(u\) for the sheet electron velocity along \(x\), number conservation gives \(-i\omega\delta n+iqn_0u=0\). The electric force on a single electron is \(-eE_x=+ieq\phi(0)\), and the acceleration equation is \(-i\omega m^*u=ieq\phi(0)\). Solving for \(u\), substituting the potential and cancelling the common density perturbation gives

\[\omega^2(q)=\frac{n_0e^2q}{2\epsilon m^*}(1-e^{-2qd}).\]

Check dimensions using \(n_0\) as a number per square metre. A three-dimensional density in this formula would leave a spurious inverse length. In an ungated plane the frequency scales as \(\sqrt q\). Its group velocity \(d\omega/dq\) is half the phase velocity. When \(qd\) is small, the exponential has first-order difference \(2qd\) and the field is largely confined in the gate gap. Then \(\omega\simeq s q\) with \(s=\sqrt{n_0e^2d/(\epsilon m^*)}\). The field and inertia now behave like a distributed capacitance and kinetic inductance. Both waves are collective charge oscillations even though one has the same long-wavelength dispersion exponent as sound.

Put numbers on the length-scale assumption

Use sheet density \(2.0\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}\), effective mass \(0.067m_e\) and relative permittivity 12.9. For a 5.0 micrometre density wavelength, \(q=2\pi/\lambda=1.257\times10^6\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\). A 50 nm gate separation gives \(qd=0.0628\). Taking a square root after evaluating the electrostatics gives 342.4 GHz without the gate and 117.6 GHz with it. The small-\(qd\) linear speed \(6.07\times10^5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) would predict roughly 121.4 GHz. The few-percent difference can be seen well before the field has ceased to be substantially gated.

The experiment lets you vary the distance and the selected wavelength. Compare the full dispersion with its gate-screened tangent. The horizontal coordinate is inverse micrometres for the angular wavenumber. Predict where \(qd=1\) lies before interpreting a bend. Check the reported selected frequency by converting \(q\) to an ordinary wavelength with the \(2\pi\) included.

Infer a parameter and test whether it is identifiable

A low-wavenumber velocity measurement determines \(n_0d/(\epsilon m^*)\). Doubling \(n_0\) and halving \(d\) gives the same leading speed, so one such measurement cannot separate density and spacing. At sufficiently large \(qd\), \(\omega^2/q\) becomes independent of \(d\) and determines \(n_0/(\epsilon m^*)\). Combining low and high wavenumber patterns can therefore identify \(d\) separately. In a real sheet, collisions, scattering, electron pressure and a nonlocal conductivity can invalidate the simple acceleration law. An inferred gate dimension is credible only if a model fitted in one regime predicts data in the other.

Work through the solution

Differentiate \(\ln\omega\) with respect to \(\ln q\). The answer is \(v_g/v_p=\tfrac12[1+2qd/(\exp(2qd)-1)]\). It tends to one for a closely screened long wave and to one half when the gate is far from the evanescent field.

A measured long-wave speed increases by ten percent with unchanged density and permittivity. Under the ideal gate model, how much must the spacing have changed?

Work through the solution

Since \(s^2\propto d\), the inferred spacing increases by \(1.10^2-1=0.21\), or 21 percent. A simultaneous change of effective mass would invalidate this inference. Check the large-\(q\) coefficient independently.

Work through the solution

The moving surface charge supplies a dynamic constitutive response with its own inertia. A constant bulk permittivity alone omits that degree of freedom. Conversely our use of Laplace's equation neglects propagation delay. If the mode approaches the dielectric light line one must keep the retarded Maxwell fields and solve for both charge motion and electromagnetic propagation.
