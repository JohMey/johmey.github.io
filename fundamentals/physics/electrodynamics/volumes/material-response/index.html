Fields and the response of matter

Connect electric and magnetic measurements to relaxation times, carrier motion, thermal fluctuations and collective order. Use frequency, temperature and geometry to distinguish competing explanations.

    Contents

    1. Measure how quickly a dielectric follows an electric field

      Derive the time and frequency responses of a relaxing polarisation, locate the loss maximum and decide whether a measured sample contains more than one relaxation process.

    2. Identify mobile charge with a transverse field

      Derive a Hall-voltage measurement from transverse force balance, reverse current and field to reject offsets and explore what fails when two carrier types conduct.

    3. Measure equilibrium field fluctuations with a capacitor

      Derive an electrical noise measurement in two ways, determine what fraction reaches a finite-bandwidth detector and use agreement to calibrate an experiment.

    4. When does a polar crystal change its preferred state?

      Derive stable and unstable dielectric responses from a free-energy landscape. Separate a reversible small-signal capacitance from a switching experiment.

    5. Distinguish magnetic screening from electrical diffusion

      Compare the frequency dependence, phase and thickness dependence of field penetration in a normal metal and a superconducting slab.

    6. When a periodic dielectric has no propagating Bloch wave

      Derive a band gap using two lossless transparent materials and distinguish evanescent Bloch propagation from absorption of optical power.

    7. Why rotating a layered dielectric changes a capacitance measurement

      Derive the two principal permittivities of a fine laminate, rotate the constitutive tensor and distinguish the applied electrode condition from an assumed field direction.

    8. How a magnetic field rotates the polarisation of transmitted light

      Resolve a linearly polarised wave into circular eigenmodes, infer a refractive-index difference from an analyser and distinguish nonreciprocal rotation from a simple change of reference axes.

    9. Can a flat interface guide light without mirrors?

      Send light along a plane boundary between a dielectric and a metal. Seek a wave that propagates parallel to the interface while decaying away on both sides. The signs in the magnetic and electric boundary conditions determine whether any such wave can exist.

    10. Infer charge discreteness from fluctuations in a steady photocurrent

      A detector can deliver a perfectly steady mean current over a long interval while its charge per short exposure fluctuates. Derive both a counting prediction and its spectral version, then use a bandwidth-limited measurement to separate partition fluctuations from thermal and readout noise.

    11. Why a nearby gate changes the speed law of a two-dimensional plasma

      A current pulse excites a travelling density modulation in a thin conducting sheet. Move a metal gate towards the sheet and the shape of the dispersion curve changes. Rebuild the restoring electric field from the surface charge before using any oscillator analogy.

    12. Can a capacitance measurement reveal the fraction of embedded particles?

      A sample contains well separated dielectric spheres in an insulating matrix. Its capacitance changes when the particle fraction changes. Derive a dilute estimate from one sphere and a self-consistent approximation from a local-field model. Use the inverse calculation to discover when the fraction becomes poorly determined.

    13. Can two ohmic layers imitate a relaxing dielectric?

      Model a film and its contact layer in series. Derive the frequency scale of interfacial charge accumulation and decide which features of a measured spectrum can arise from geometry alone.

    14. From molecular dipoles to a dielectric susceptibility

      Derive induced and orientational polarisation separately, then determine when temperature or local fields invalidate a constant dielectric coefficient.
