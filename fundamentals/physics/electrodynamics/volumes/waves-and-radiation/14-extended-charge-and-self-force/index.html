Resolve the self-force before taking a point-charge limit

A finite spherical charge distribution introduces a propagation time. Follow its memory kernel to electromagnetic inertia, radiative recoil and the limits of a truncated equation.

Give the charge a specified size and mechanical support

Take a thin spherical shell of radius a carrying fixed uniform total charge q. Prescribe rigid translational motion at speed much smaller than c, and linearise the self-field in that velocity. Mechanical stresses maintain the spherical distribution. Rotation, relativistic contraction and deformable internal charge modes lie outside this model. A finite radius is essential because two different surface elements communicate after a finite delay.

In the linearised retarded-field integral, the instantaneous longitudinal Coulomb pair forces cancel in the net force when using Coulomb gauge. The transverse inertial kernel contains the angular projector I−nn. Spherical averaging gives 2I/3. The resulting vector force is expressible as a pair-distance integral of the retarded acceleration.

\[\mathbf F_{\rm self}(t)=-{2\over3c^2}\int\!\!\int{dq\,dq'\over4\pi\epsilon_0 R}\,\mathbf a(t-R/c).\]

This is the linear, rigid, spherically symmetric kernel. An independently incident electromagnetic field belongs to the applied force. A deformable shell would require an additional internal response model. The delayed time appears inside the acceleration. Pulling it out before averaging would retain electromagnetic inertia but remove the radiative correction. Here R is the distance between two surface points, evaluated in the unperturbed shell geometry.

Evaluate the pair distribution geometrically

Fix one point on the shell. A second point at polar angle θ from it is separated by R=2a sin(θ/2). The fraction of surface area in the annulus is sinθ dθ/2. Since R dR=a² sinθ dθ, the normalised distribution of pair distances is R dR/(2a²) on 0≤R≤2a. This cancels the kernel's inverse distance. Integrating the acceleration over the remaining retarded-time interval gives the exact linear delay formula for this model.

\[\mathbf F_{\rm self}(t)={q^2\over12\pi\epsilon_0a^2c}\left[\mathbf v(t-2a/c)-\mathbf v(t)\right].\]
Recall · A delay equation needs a history

A second-order particle equation is normally supplied with position and velocity at one instant. The shell equation samples velocity over the preceding interval of length 2a/c. A compatible history and the incoming electromagnetic field are additional information. Replacing that history by extra arbitrary derivatives changes the initial-value problem.

Separate inertia from the first dissipative correction

If the acceleration varies on a time scale T much longer than 2a/c, Taylor-expand the delayed velocity. The first term is proportional to acceleration and the second to its derivative.

\[\mathbf F_{\rm self}=-m_{\rm em}\mathbf a+{q^2\over6\pi\epsilon_0c^3}\dot{\mathbf a}+O(q^2a\ddot{\mathbf a}/(\epsilon_0c^4)),\]
\[m_{\rm em}={q^2\over6\pi\epsilon_0ac^2}.\]

The electrostatic shell energy is U=q²/(8πε₀a), so this electromagnetic inertial coefficient is 4U/(3c²). The coefficient refers to the electromagnetic part of a constrained shell. Covariant energy-momentum accounting also includes the stresses that maintain its shape. Identifying this isolated coefficient with the complete rest mass would omit those stresses.

Move the inertial term to the left of m_bare a=F_ext+F_self and define the measured inertial mass m=m_bare+m_em. The remaining leading local term is the Abraham–Lorentz force mτ dot a, with τ=q²/(6πε₀mc³). In the limit a→0 at fixed q the electromagnetic inertia diverges. Holding the measured mass fixed then requires a singular adjustment of the mechanical parameter. The finite-shell model therefore does not justify an unrestricted point limit.

Check the mechanical power and the stored near field

Dot the recoil term with velocity and use the product rule. The first term is a total derivative. The second is the Larmor power with a minus sign.

\[m\tau\dot{\mathbf a}\cdot\mathbf v={d\over dt}(m\tau\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf v)-m\tau a^2.\]

Defining the near-field energy contribution E_S=−mτa·v gives d(K+E_S)/dt=F_ext·v−P_rad. Over a pulse whose endpoint acceleration vanishes, the integrated near-field change is zero. At an intermediate instant it can be nonzero, so mechanical power loss and radiated power need not agree instant by instant.

Remember · A derivative expansion has a frequency range

For harmonic velocity v∝e⁻ⁱωt, the exact delay factor is eⁱω(2a/c)−1. Its Taylor expansion is controlled by |ω|2a/c. Solving the truncated differential equation can create modes outside that range. Check the frequency of a proposed solution against the approximation that produced the equation.

Reduce the order on the physical slow branch

The truncated force-free equation a=τ dot a admits an exponentially growing acceleration. Its time scale is τ, while the local expansion was intended to describe slowly varying applied forcing. On the perturbative branch replace a on the small correction's right-hand side by F_ext/m. To first order in τ this gives m a=F_ext+τ dF_ext/dt, with a total derivative along the leading trajectory.

For a harmonic restoring force F_ext=−mω₀²x, order reduction gives ẍ+τω₀²ẋ+ω₀²x=0. The amplitude decay rate is τω₀²/2. The average Larmor power divided by oscillator energy gives the same energy decay rate τω₀². This comparison tests both the sign and the factor two between amplitude and energy.

Exercises on limits and energy

1. For an electron, τ≈6.27×10⁻²⁴ s. Estimate the amplitude-decay time for ω₀=10¹⁵ s⁻¹ within the classical oscillator model.

Work through the solution

The amplitude rate is 3.135×10⁶ s⁻¹ and the time is 3.19×10⁻⁷ s. The small parameter τω₀ is 6.27×10⁻⁹. A specific atomic transition additionally requires quantum state and coupling information.

2. Evaluate the ratio of the exact shell memory factor to its first-order inertial approximation when ω2a/c=0.1. Which next term captures loss?

Work through the solution

The exact factor is cos0.1−1+i sin0.1. Relative to i0.1 it is approximately 0.998334+i0.049958. The real negative term −(ω2a/c)²/2 in the force factor gives the first dissipative correction. Keeping only the linear imaginary term gives reactive inertia.

3. A force has compact support and the acceleration vanishes at both endpoints. Show that net radiated energy equals external work minus the change in kinetic energy.

Work through the solution

Integrate the balance for K+E_S. The endpoint values of E_S vanish, leaving W_ext−ΔK=∫P_rad dt. Truncating the interval before acceleration vanishes would require its near-field endpoint contribution.
