Where current and energy go in a charging capacitor

A wire carries current to the centre of a plate, yet charge appears across its face and the energy increases in the gap. Following that current over differently chosen surfaces gives a spatial test of charge conservation and an independent power budget.

A circulation must give one answer for every spanning surface

Surround the charging lead by a loop. A flat surface inside the lead intercepts conduction current; a surface stretched into the insulating gap can avoid the lead altogether. Applying the magnetostatic law to both would assign different circulation to the same loop. Charge continuity and Gauss's law instead give

\[\nabla\cdot(\mathbf J+\epsilon_0\partial_t\mathbf E)=-\partial_t\rho+\partial_t\rho=0 .\]

Consequently the flux of this sum through any two surfaces with the same boundary agrees. Ampère–Maxwell equates the circulation of \(\mathbf B/\mu_0\) to that common enclosed current in vacuum. In a material with identified free current the macroscopic balance uses \(\mathbf H,\mathbf J_f,\partial_t\mathbf D\), incorporating polarisation. The divergence identity verifies consistency; the magnetic field generated by the additional term is a physical prediction of Maxwell's equation.

Resolve the charging current across the plate

Take circular metal plates of radius \(a\), separation \(d\ll a\), connected at their centres. Work at times long compared with \(a/c\) and neglect small edge and lead corrections. In the gap \(E_z=V/d\) is approximately uniform with sign chosen from positive to negative plate. \(Q=\epsilon_0\pi a^2 E_z\), so \(\partial_tD_z=I/(\pi a^2)\) where \(I=\dot Q\). A circle of radius \(r<a\) spanning the gap intercepts displacement current \(I r^2/a^2\). Azimuthal symmetry gives

\[B_\phi(r)=\frac{\mu_0 I r}{2\pi a^2}\quad (r<a),\]
\[B_\phi(r)\simeq\frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}\quad (r>a).\]

The exterior expression assumes the return conductor lies farther away and retains the leading large-plate approximation. Magnetic field is continuous at the idealised rim. Now cut a ring of radius \(r\) into the positive plate. The portion of supplied current needed to charge its central disk is \(Ir^2/a^2\); the rest crosses that ring outwards as conduction current. Thus \(2\pi r K_r=I(1-r^2/a^2)\). Its apparent \(1/r\) singularity at the centre is rounded off by the finite contact patch. Distributed charging is compatible with current entering through one point.

Locate the power flow in the gap

The Poynting vector in the gap is

\[\mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H=-E_zH_\phi\hat{\mathbf r}.\]

For increasing field it points radially inward. Integrate the inward flux through the cylindrical side of a smaller pillbox of radius \(r<a\) and thickness \(d\):

\[P_{\rm in}(r)=2\pi r d E_z H_\phi(r)=VI\,\frac{r^2}{a^2}\]
\[\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\epsilon_0}{2}E_z^2\pi r^2d\right).\]

Thus energy crosses the side before being stored throughout that subvolume. At \(r=a\) the result equals the terminal power \(VI\). This derivation uses the leading quasistatic fields. A rapidly changing current also produces induction fields and time-varying magnetic energy; in that regime the full Poynting theorem includes them. During discharging, \(\dot Q\) reverses and the direction of radial power flow reverses.

Predict two independent instrument readings

Sample midway to the rim

Let \(a=20\) mm, \(d=0.50\) mm and \(I=4.0\) mA. At \(r=10\) mm a gap circle encloses 1.0 mA displacement current. Its magnetic field is 20.0 nT. The outward conduction current crossing the same radius in the positive metal is 3.0 mA, corresponding to \(K_r=0.0477\) A/m. The gap reading and the plate reading describe different surfaces; their sum recovers the lead current in this construction.

Record a finite charge ramp

The ideal capacitance here is 22.3 pF. A constant \(I=4.0\) mA for 20.0 ns raises the voltage by 3.60 V from zero. The source supplies \(\int VI\,dt=C V_{\rm final}^2/2=144\) pJ. At the half-radius pillbox only one quarter of this energy appears, 36.0 pJ. A real pulse has finite rise and fall times. The simple uniform-field solution is accurate away from abrupt switching transients when the spectral content is well below \(c/a\).

Measure a displaced current and a radial conduction current separately

Problem sheet · challenge a misleading measurement

Determine the sign during reversal

The capacitor field still points from the positive to the negative plate, but its magnitude is decreasing. Which of \(B_\phi,S_r,E_z\) changes sign relative to an increasing field with the same instantaneous voltage?

Work through the solution

The voltage and \(E_z\) keep their signs. \(I=C\dot V\) reverses, so the azimuthal magnetic field reverses. Hence radial energy flow reverses and leaves the pillbox. A measurement of voltage alone does not determine the direction of energy transport.

What changes if the centre contact is replaced by a rim feed?

Assume a uniform annular feed deposits charge symmetrically at the outer edge. Predict the signed metal current crossing a circle of radius \(r<a\) if positive is radially outward.

Work through the solution

All charge accumulating inside the test circle must now enter from larger radii. The inward flux is \(Ir^2/a^2\), so \(2\pi rK_r=-Ir^2/a^2\). The gap displacement current for the same uniform charging history is unchanged. The external lead and return geometry determines the remainder of the global magnetic field configuration.
