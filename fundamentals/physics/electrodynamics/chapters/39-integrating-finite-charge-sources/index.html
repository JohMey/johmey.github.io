A ring, a disk and an electric-field maximum in empty space

Gauss’s law gives the flux through a closed surface. For a finite circular source the field varies across that surface. Adding the fields of actual charge elements predicts both the near field and a surprising axial maximum when the centre is removed.

Decide what symmetry eliminates

A uniformly charged ring of radius \(s\) lies in the \(xy\) plane. A detector on the \(z\) axis is a distance \(R=\sqrt{s^2+z^2}\) from every charge element. Contributions transverse to the axis cancel in diametrically opposite pairs. The potential adds as a scalar, giving \(dV=dq/(4\pi\epsilon_0 R)\) for the whole ring charge \(dq\). Choose zero potential at infinity. Differentiating with respect to detector position gives

\[dE_z=z\,dq/(4\pi\epsilon_0R^3).\]

The same factor \(z/R\) appears if each Coulomb force is projected directly onto the axis. At the ring centre the potential is finite and the field is zero.

Construct an annulus with uniform surface density \(\sigma\), inner radius \(a\) and outer radius \(b\). A radial interval \(ds\) contributes \(dq=2\pi\sigma s\,ds\); forgetting \(s\) would assign equal charge to equal widths at the centre and rim. Integrating and differentiating yields

\[V(z)=\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}\left[\sqrt{z^2+b^2}-\sqrt{z^2+a^2}\right],\]
\[E_z(z)=\frac{\sigma z}{2\epsilon_0}\left[\frac1{\sqrt{z^2+a^2}}-\frac1{\sqrt{z^2+b^2}}\right].\]

The potential is even in \(z\) and the field odd. When \(a>0\) every point on the central axis is in empty space and both functions are smooth there. This smoothness changes in the disk limit.

Investigate two noncommuting limits

Set \(a=0\) before moving the probe to the plane. The potential then contains \(-|z|\) and the field contains \(\operatorname{sgn}(z)\). Thus \(E_z(0^+)-E_z(0^-)=\sigma/\epsilon_0\), just the charge-sheet jump. By contrast, taking \(z\to0\) first at any positive hole radius gives zero field. The order of shrinking the hole and approaching its centre determines whether the probe sits on material. A grid that places its probe exactly at \(z=0\) for both geometries can conceal the difference.

At large positive \(z\), Taylor-expand the two square roots before subtracting. The \(z\) terms cancel. Since \(Q=\pi\sigma(b^2-a^2)\),

\[V(z)=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0z}\left[1-\frac{a^2+b^2}{4z^2}+O(b^4/z^4)\right],\]
\[E_z=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0z^2}\left[1-\frac{3(a^2+b^2)}{4z^2}+\cdots\right].\]

The axial dipole term is absent by reflection symmetry. For very large \(z\) a numerical difference between almost identical square roots loses precision. Multiply by the conjugate to evaluate \(V=\sigma(b^2-a^2)/[2\epsilon_0(\sqrt{z^2+b^2}+\sqrt{z^2+a^2})]\).

Find an extremum without violating the vacuum field equations

For a nonzero hole the field grows linearly near the origin and decreases as an inverse square in the far field. Differentiate \(z/\sqrt{z^2+s^2}\) to get \(s^2/(z^2+s^2)^{3/2}\). Equating the two edge contributions gives

\[z_{\max}^2=\frac{b^2a^{4/3}-a^2b^{4/3}}{b^{4/3}-a^{4/3}} .\]

This is a maximum of one component along one line. No interior maximum of the electrostatic potential is implied. A lateral displacement would break the ring-pair cancellation and requires integration over the azimuthal coordinate. On axis the field also equals the difference between the fields of two disks of common density.

Plan two field measurements

Locate a peak in a centimetre-scale annulus

Choose \(a=10\) mm, \(b=20\) mm and \(\sigma=1.0\) nC/m². The maximum occurs at \(z=0.4934b=9.87\) mm. At this point \(2\epsilon_0 E_z/\sigma=0.25992\), so \(E_z=14.7\) V/m. A point charge approximation would incorrectly predict that the field increases indefinitely as the detector approaches the hole. The missing charge near the centre matters even though the whole source has nonzero net charge.

Turn a desired error into a measurement distance

For the same radius ratio the leading relative correction to the monopole field is \(0.9375(b/z)^2\). To keep this correction near 1%, choose \(z/b\simeq9.68\), about 0.194 m here. Substituting this distance in the exact expression checks the term neglected in the estimate. For a radial charge distribution with changing sign the monopole could cancel altogether, invalidating the inference.

Problem sheet · what can an axial probe tell you?

Locate the peak of a narrow ring

Shrink the annulus to a narrow ring of fixed total charge and radius \(a\). Find the field maximum directly and compare the answer with the picture above.

Work through the solution

The ring field is proportional to \(z/(z^2+a^2)^{3/2}\). Its derivative is proportional to \((a^2-2z^2)/(z^2+a^2)^{5/2}\). Thus the maximum on the positive axis occurs at \(a/\sqrt2\). Keeping total charge fixed requires the surface density to grow as the width shrinks.

Compute with a nonuniform surface density

Suppose the density increases linearly from zero at the centre of a disk to \(\sigma_0\) at its outer radius \(b\). Describe an integration rule and give two independent checks that do not require an analytic field formula.

Work through the solution

Divide the radius into cells with midpoint \(s_j\). Assign each cell its exact area \(\pi(s_{j,+}^2-s_{j,-}^2)\), multiply by \(\sigma_0s_j/b\), and add the axial ring field. The summed source charge must converge to \(2\pi\sigma_0 b^2/3\). At large \(z\) the computed field times \(4\pi\epsilon_0z^2\) approaches this same charge. At each fixed positive \(z\), double the cell count and check convergence separately from the far-field comparison.
