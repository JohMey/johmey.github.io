When a circuit element must be treated as a field

Can a short pair of conductors be called a capacitor? At low frequencies it stores energy almost electrostatically. As propagation time becomes important, the same open conductors support standing waves and their input current no longer grows proportionally to frequency.

Build a transmission line from small field volumes

Consider a uniform two-conductor line supporting a transverse electromagnetic wave. Let \(V(z,t)\) measure the transverse potential difference and \(I(z,t)\) the current toward \(+z\). Denote its inductance and capacitance per unit length by \(L'\) and \(C'\). For the moment assume ideal conductors, a linear lossless dielectric and negligible radiation. A segment \(dz\) stores charge \(C'V\,dz\). Charge balance gives

\[I(z)-I(z+dz)=C' dz\,\partial_t V.\]

Its linked magnetic flux is \(L'I\,dz\); Faraday's law gives the voltage drop \(V(z)-V(z+dz)=L' dz\,\partial_t I\). Division by \(dz\) yields

\[\partial_z I=-C'\partial_t V,\]
\[\partial_z V=-L'\partial_t I .\]

Differentiate the second relation with respect to \(z\) and substitute the first. The result is

\[\partial_z^2 V=L'C'\partial_t^2V,\]

with speed \(u=1/\sqrt{L'C'}\). An outgoing wave \(F(t-z/u)\) satisfies

\[V/I=Z_0=\sqrt{L'/C'}.\]

The electric and magnetic field energies per length are \(C'V^2/2\) and \(L'I^2/2\), and they are equal for this travelling wave. Multiplying the two equations by \(V\) and \(I\) also gives a one-dimensional conservation law:

\[\partial_z(VI)=-\partial_t(C'V^2/2+L'I^2/2).\]

Derive the input admittance of an open end

Use \(e^{-i\omega t}\) time dependence, and let the far end \(z=\ell\) be open so \(I(\ell)=0\). Then \(\partial_zV(\ell)=0\). The solution is

\[V(z)=V_L\cos k(\ell-z),\]

where \(k=\omega/u\). Since \(\partial_zV=i\omega L'I\), the current is \(-i V_L\sin k(\ell-z)/Z_0\). At the input,

\[Y_{\rm in}=-\frac{i}{Z_0}\tan(k\ell),\]
\[Y_{\rm lumped}=-i\omega C'\ell,\]
\[\frac{Y_{\rm in}}{Y_{\rm lumped}}=\frac{\tan x}{x},\]
\[x=\frac{\omega\ell}{u} .\]

At small \(x\),

\[\tan x/x=1+x^2/3+O(x^4).\]

Thus a circuit approximation can be assigned an error estimate. Near \(x=\pi/2\) the input voltage approaches a node and the ideal input admittance diverges. Losses round the pole in a real line. Beyond this frequency even the sign can change, although the far end remains open.

Two decisions about model size

A 15 cm open lead

Take \(u=2.00\times10^8\) m/s and \(Z_0=50.0\,\Omega\). Then \(C'=1/(Z_0u)=100\) pF/m and the lumped capacitance is 15.0 pF. At 20.0 MHz, \(x=0.09425\); the exact admittance exceeds the capacitive prediction by 0.297%. At 200 MHz, \(x=0.9425\), and the excess is about 46.0%. A component measured on an open fixture can therefore appear to acquire substantial extra capacitance at high frequency.

A time trace on the same lead

The one-way flight time is \(0.75\) ns. A step launched down the line first sees the characteristic impedance. Information about the open end returns only after \(1.50\) ns. At an open end an incident positive voltage wave is reflected with the same sign. The current waves cancel there, enforcing zero terminal current, while the terminal voltage doubles at their first overlap. For a source matched to \(Z_0\), the returning wave is absorbed at the source. This time-domain argument provides a check on the origin of the standing-wave solution.

A second circuit limit from magnetic energy

In the slow-variation limit, linked flux in two fixed coils is linear in both currents. For a reciprocal medium,

\[\lambda_1=L_1I_1+MI_2,\]
\[\lambda_2=MI_1+L_2I_2.\]

Integrate the supplied power \(I_1\dot\lambda_1+I_2\dot\lambda_2\) to obtain

\[U=(L_1I_1^2+2MI_1I_2+L_2I_2^2)/2.\]

Completing the square gives

\[2U=L_1(I_1+MI_2/L_1)^2+(L_2-M^2/L_1)I_2^2.\]

A passive magnetic energy requires

\[|M|\leq\sqrt{L_1L_2}.\]

The sign of \(M\) depends on terminal orientations. This bound is an independent way to check a measured or numerically inferred circuit model.

Measure the range of a lumped capacitor model

The line has speed 2.00 × 10⁸ m/s and impedance 50 Ω. For a 15 cm open section its first quarter-wave pole is 333 MHz. At 60% of that frequency the distributed admittance exceeds the lumped value by 46%. Move the open end and compare the two curves.

Check a claimed mutual inductance

Work through the solution

Choose \(I_1=-MI_2/L_1=-1.75I_2\). The surviving coefficient is \(9-49/4=-3.25\) mH, giving a negative stored energy for every nonzero \(I_2\). A passive reciprocal model with these self-inductances can support at most 6 mH of mutual inductance in magnitude.

Field momentum, momentum exchange and the quasistatic limits · Magnetism, induction and field dynamics

A system can have static electric and magnetic fields with nonzero local electromagnetic momentum density. A circuit can also be electrically small while either induction or displacement current remains indispensable. Both puzzles become clearer when one follows the relevant conservation law and compares the scales of the discarded terms.
