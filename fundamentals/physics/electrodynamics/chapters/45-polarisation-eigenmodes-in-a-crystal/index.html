Two polarisations moving through a uniaxial crystal

A crystal can send two pulses in different directions even when their wavefronts have the same normal. The distinction emerges by applying the divergence condition to electric displacement and measuring energy flow with the Poynting vector.

A field transverse to the wavevector need not be the electric field

Let a transparent, nonmagnetic uniaxial medium have \(\boldsymbol\epsilon/\epsilon_0=\operatorname{diag}(n_o^2,n_o^2,n_e^2)\). The optic axis is \(z\); dispersion over the bandwidth under study is negligible. For a harmonic plane wave with wavevector in the \(xz\) plane, write \(\mathbf k=(k_x,0,k_z)\). Source-free Maxwell equations require \(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf D=0\), \(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf B=0\),

\[\mathbf k\times\mathbf E=\omega\mathbf B,\]
\[\mathbf k\times\mathbf H=-\omega\mathbf D.\]

Eliminating the magnetic field gives

\[\mathbf k\times(\mathbf k\times\mathbf E)+k_0^2(\boldsymbol\epsilon/\epsilon_0)\mathbf E=0,\]
\[k_0=\omega/c.\]

An electric field along \(y\) satisfies this system with \(k^2=k_0^2n_o^2\). This ordinary mode has transverse \(\mathbf E,\mathbf D\) and energy travels parallel to \(\mathbf k\). A second mode has \(\mathbf E\) in the \(xz\) plane; \(\mathbf D\) is transverse but generally not parallel to \(\mathbf E\).

Find the phase index and the direction of energy separately

The remaining two Cartesian equations form a matrix with diagonal \(k_0^2 n_o^2-k_z^2,\ k_0^2 n_e^2-k_x^2\) and off-diagonal elements \(k_xk_z\). Its determinant vanishes for nontrivial fields. Expanding cancels the fourth-order cross term and gives

\[\frac{k_x^2}{n_e^2}+\frac{k_z^2}{n_o^2}=k_0^2,\]
\[\frac1{n_{\rm ph}(\theta)^2}=\frac{\sin^2\theta}{n_e^2}+\frac{\cos^2\theta}{n_o^2},\]

where \(\theta\) is the angle from the optic axis to the wavevector. Along the axis both modes have the same index; perpendicular to it the extraordinary phase index is \(n_e\). For the latter mode choose transverse \(\mathbf D\propto(\cos\theta,0,-\sin\theta)\). Applying the inverse permittivity tensor gives

\[\mathbf E\propto(\cos\theta/n_o^2,0,-\sin\theta/n_e^2).\]

The magnetic field is along \(y\), so the energy flow angle \(\psi\) from \(z\) satisfies

\[\tan\psi=-\frac{E_z}{E_x}=\frac{n_o^2}{n_e^2}\tan\theta.\]

The group velocity obtained by differentiating \(\omega(\mathbf k)=c\sqrt{k_x^2/n_e^2+k_z^2/n_o^2}\) has the same direction in this nondispersive model. This independent construction is normal to the constant-frequency ellipse. At an interface it is the tangential component of \(\mathbf k\), fixed by phase matching, that selects the transmitted wavevector. The energy path can then diverge from the corresponding wave normal.

A crystal plate as a beam displacer and as a retarder

A normally incident beam with an oblique optic axis

Let \(n_o=1.50,\ n_e=1.60\) and let the plate normal make \(45^\circ\) to the optic axis. At normal incidence the phase normal is the plate normal for both modes. For the extraordinary wave \(\psi=\arctan(2.25/2.56)=41.3^\circ\). Relative to its wave normal the energy travels about \(-3.7^\circ\) toward the optic axis. Across a 10.0 mm thick plate the lateral displacement relative to the ordinary beam is approximately 0.65 mm. This describes propagation inside a parallel plate and neglects edge clipping.

A plate that turns linear input into circular output

Propagate perpendicular to the optic axis so both eigenmodes travel in the same direction. Their accumulated phase difference is \(\Delta=k_0(n_e-n_o)d\). Linear incident polarisation at \(45^\circ\) to the eigenaxes has equal amplitudes. When \(|\Delta|=\pi/2\) modulo \(2\pi\), those orthogonal components have a quarter-period phase difference and form circular polarisation, assuming equal transmitted amplitudes. At 600 nm with \(n_e-n_o=0.10\), the smallest such thickness is \(1.50\) μm. A larger multi-order plate can give the same retardance at this wavelength but responds more strongly to bandwidth and thickness errors.

Recover retardance from a pair of polarisers

Place the crystal axes at \(45^\circ\) to the input polariser and use a second polariser crossed with the first. Express the output field in the two crystal axes, then project onto the crossed analyser; its amplitude is proportional to \((e^{i\Delta}-1)/2\). Thus the transmitted fraction is \(\sin^2(\Delta/2)\). An arbitrary phase offset common to both modes disappears. A single intensity reading determines neither the sign of \(\Delta\) nor its full order. Varying thickness or scanning wavelength can resolve the branch; the material dispersion then enters the interpretation.

Predict the transverse displacement of a crystal plate

Choose principal indices 1.50 and 1.60 and a 45° wave normal. The extraordinary beam is displaced by about −0.65 mm through a 10 mm plate; the ordinary beam follows the normal. Swap the principal indices and compare the sign and magnitude of walk-off.

Problem sheet · use energy and phase observations

Infer birefringence from a walk-off angle

At \(\theta=45^\circ\), an extraordinary energy ray makes \(\psi=40^\circ\) to the optic axis. Assuming the same model, recover \(n_e/n_o\). Is the medium positive or negative uniaxial?

Work through the solution

The ratio is

\[\sqrt{\tan45^\circ/\tan40^\circ}=1.092.\]

The extraordinary principal index exceeds the ordinary one, a positive uniaxial material. A measurement referenced to the plate normal must first be converted to the angle from the optic axis used in the equation.

Calculate the contrast loss caused by angular misalignment

An ideal half-wave plate has its eigenaxis at angle \(\alpha\) to the input polariser. Keep the analyser crossed and find the intensity transmitted when the angle is \(40^\circ\) instead of \(45^\circ\).

Work through the solution

Projection into the crystal axes and back out gives

\[I/I_0=\sin^2(2\alpha)\sin^2(\Delta/2).\]

At half-wave retardance the factor from phase is unity, and the misaligned transmission is

\[\sin^2 80^\circ=0.970.\]

The reduction alone cannot distinguish angular error from a small error in retardance.
