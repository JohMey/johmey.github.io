A lossy half-space seen through its surface impedance

An absorbing medium transmits a complex field and converts electrical work into heat. Matching one complex number at its surface predicts reflection. Integrating the heating through its depth provides an independent check that is especially revealing near the crossover between dielectric and conductor.

Choose a branch that decays into the material

Let a stationary plane wave enter the homogeneous half-space \(z>0\) at normal incidence. Use \(e^{-i\omega t}\) and fields proportional to \(e^{ikz}\), real \(\epsilon,\mu>0\) and Ohmic \(\mathbf J=\sigma\mathbf E\), \(\sigma\geq0\). Ampère's law combines the two currents into \(-i\omega(\epsilon+i\sigma/\omega)\mathbf E\). Faraday's law yields

\[H_y=kE_x/(\omega\mu).\]

Eliminating \(H\) gives

\[k^2=\omega^2\mu\epsilon(1+i t),\]
\[t=\frac{\sigma}{\omega\epsilon},\]
\[k=\beta+i\alpha .\]

Choose \(\beta>0,\alpha\geq0\) so the interior field weakens with depth. Let \(k_d=\omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}\). Equating real and imaginary parts gives \(\beta^2-\alpha^2=k_d^2\), \(2\beta\alpha=k_d^2 t\), and hence

\[\frac{\beta}{k_d}=\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{1+t^2}+1}{2}},\]
\[\frac{\alpha}{k_d}=\frac{t}{2(\beta/k_d)},\]
\[Z=\frac{E_x}{H_y}=\frac{\omega\mu}{k}.\]

The rational expression for \(\alpha\) avoids subtracting nearly equal numbers for small \(t\). As \(t\to0\),

\[\alpha/k_d\simeq t/2.\]

For \(t\gg1\), \(\beta\simeq\alpha\simeq\sqrt{\omega\mu\sigma/2}\): skin depth \(1/\alpha\) measures field-amplitude decay and the power has the shorter e-folding depth \(1/(2\alpha)\).

Read the complex reflection and the power entering the surface

Let the incident medium be lossless with impedance \(Z_1\). Continuity of tangential \(E,H\) gives

\[1+r=h,\ (1-r)/Z_1=h/Z,\]

where \(h=E(0^+)/E_{\rm inc}\). Solving,

\[r=\frac{Z-Z_1}{Z+Z_1},\]
\[h=\frac{2Z}{Z+Z_1},\]
\[1-|r|^2=Z_1\operatorname{Re}(1/Z^*)|h|^2.\]

The flux relation follows by dividing the real part of \(E H^*/2\) by the incident intensity. It includes a real admittance and is generally different from \(|h|^2\). The imaginary part of the complex Poynting vector measures exchange in quadrature with the drive. In the good-conductor limit \(Z\simeq(1-i)\sqrt{\omega\mu/(2\sigma)}\). Its positive real part is essential for passive heating, even where the reflected voltage is almost the negative of the incident one.

Recover the entire inward power from a volume measurement

Ohmic heating per volume is \(\langle p_J\rangle=\sigma|E(0)|^2 e^{-2\alpha z}/2\). Integrating from zero to infinity gives

\[P_J/A=\sigma|E(0)|^2/(4\alpha).\]

Use \(2\beta\alpha=\mu\omega\sigma\) to rewrite it as \(|E(0)|^2\beta/(2\omega\mu)\). This equals the inward mean surface flux from \(E H^*/2\). No separate efficiency factor is needed. The fraction of surface power dissipated before depth \(D\) is \(1-e^{-2\alpha D}\), so 90% of the heat is deposited by \(D=\ln10/(2\alpha)\), about 1.15 field skin depths.

Compare two frequency regimes

A crossover with comparable currents

Consider material whose lossless impedance equals the incident medium’s and whose loss ratio is \(t=2\). Then \(\beta/k_d=1.272\), \(\alpha/k_d=0.786\), and \(Z/Z_1=0.569-0.352i\). Direct complex division gives a reflected power fraction of about 0.120. The balance enters the half-space and ultimately heats it. One lossless phase length \(k_d^{-1}\) into the material, the remaining forward power is \(e^{-1.572}=0.208\) of its surface value. This illustrates substantial heating even though most incident power enters the material.

An amplitude probe and a calorimeter

Suppose a field-amplitude probe reads 1/e of its surface value at depth 0.80 mm. Then \(\alpha=1250\) m⁻¹, the power half-depth is \(\ln2/(2\alpha)=0.277\) mm and 90% of the absorbed energy is deposited by 0.921 mm. These predictions do not require knowing the incident intensity. Disagreement between the two types of measurement can reveal spatially varying \(\sigma\) or a reflected wave from a finite back surface.

Close a reflection and heating budget at every depth

At loss ratio 2 and matched lossless impedances, about 12% of the incident power is reflected. The remaining power gradually becomes heat. Change loss and impedance independently and check that the surface reflection, transmitted flux at depth and integrated heat always add to one.

Problem sheet · determine a material from two probes

Recover material parameters

At frequency \(f\) in a nonmagnetic half-space, phase measurements give \(\beta\) and amplitude measurements give \(\alpha\). Recover \(\sigma,\epsilon\), and specify the consistency condition for the dielectric model used here.

Work through the solution

Solve for \(\sigma=2\beta\alpha/(\mu_0\omega)\) and \(\epsilon=(\beta^2-\alpha^2)/(\mu_0\omega^2)\). A medium with positive real permittivity in this simple Ohmic model requires \(\beta>\alpha\). In the good-conductor limit their difference is small, making the inferred permittivity sensitive to measurement errors.

When does an infinite depth assumption fail?

For a slab of thickness \(D\), the back-face field reflection magnitude is 0.50. Bound the return amplitude at the entrance relative to the forward wave inside, ignoring subsequent round trips. How thick keeps it below 1%?

Work through the solution

The round trip attenuates the field by \(e^{-2\alpha D}\). Require \(0.50e^{-2\alpha D}<0.01\), giving \(D>\ln50/(2\alpha)=1.96/\alpha\). Phase may suppress the measured amplitude at selected frequencies, but the magnitude bound does not depend on that cancellation.
