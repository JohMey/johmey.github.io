How a conductor admits a field step and a sinusoid

A time-varying magnetic field enters a conducting slab from its surface. A suddenly imposed constant field continues to penetrate with elapsed time. A sinusoid establishes a persistent depth profile. Both behaviours follow from the same initial–boundary value equation.

Reduce Maxwell's equations to a diffusion equation

Use a uniform stationary Ohmic medium with scalar conductivity \(\sigma\) and permeability \(\mu\). Work on length and time scales for which displacement current is small compared with conduction current, \(\omega\epsilon/\sigma\ll1\) for each important Fourier component. Then

\[\nabla\times\boldsymbol B=\mu\sigma\boldsymbol E.\]

Taking a curl and using Faraday's law gives

\[\nabla\times\nabla\times\boldsymbol B=-\mu\sigma\partial_t\boldsymbol B.\]

The identity \(\nabla\times\nabla\times\boldsymbol B=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol B)-\nabla^2\boldsymbol B\), together with absence of magnetic monopoles, leaves

\[\partial_t\boldsymbol B=D_m\nabla^2\boldsymbol B,\]
\[D_m=\frac1{\mu\sigma}.\]

\(D_m\) has units of area per time. Larger conductivity slows penetration: a small electric field can drive larger currents that oppose the changing applied field. An ideal mathematical step contains arbitrarily high frequencies outside this reduction. The result describes observations made after the brief electromagnetic start-up, on distances large enough for a continuum Ohmic description.

Background · Skin depth
\[\delta=\sqrt{2/(\mu\sigma\omega)}\]

In a good homogeneous conductor under sinusoidal excitation this is the amplitude decay length. Intensity decays twice as fast in the exponent.

Solve the initial–boundary value problem for a step

Fill \(x>0\) with conductor and impose a tangential field \(B(0,t)=B_0\) for \(t>0\), with the interior initially field-free. At each finite time require \(B(x,t)\to0\) as \(x\to\infty\). There is no imposed length scale, so try \(B/B_0=F(\eta)\) with \(\eta=x/(2\sqrt{D_mt})\). The time derivative produces \(-\eta F'/(2t)\); the second space derivative times \(D_m\) gives \(F''/(4t)\). Therefore

\[F''+2\eta F'=0.\]

First integrate \(F'=Ae^{-\eta^2}\), then impose \(F(0)=1,F(\infty)=0\). The normalisation \(\int_0^\infty e^{-s^2}ds=\sqrt\pi/2\) gives

\[\frac{B(x,t)}{B_0}=\operatorname{erfc}\!\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{D_mt}}\right).\]

Any fixed fractional field level moves with \(\sqrt t\). The depth at half amplitude is

\[x_{1/2}=2\operatorname{erfc}^{-1}(0.5)\sqrt{D_mt}=0.9539\sqrt{D_mt}.\]

For copper represented by \(\sigma=5.8\times10^7\,\mathrm{S/m}\), \(\mu=\mu_0\), \(D_m=0.01372\,\mathrm{m^2/s}\). At \(0.10\,\mathrm{ms}\) the half-depth is \(1.12\,\mathrm{mm}\). Reaching twice that depth requires \(0.40\,\mathrm{ms}\).

Solve for a periodic steady state

At the same surface impose \(B(0,t)=\operatorname{Re}B_0e^{i\omega t}\) for long enough that the transient has passed. Seek \(B=\operatorname{Re}B_0 e^{i\omega t-kx}\). Substitution gives

\[D_m k^2=i\omega.\]

Select the root with positive real part, \(k=(1+i)/\delta\) with \(\delta=\sqrt{2D_m/\omega}\). Thus

\[B(x,t)=B_0e^{-x/\delta}\cos(\omega t-x/\delta).\]

At one skin depth the amplitude is \(e^{-1}\) of the surface amplitude and the oscillation lags by one radian. For the same model copper at 1 kHz, \(\delta=2.09\,\mathrm{mm}\), while the sinusoidal half-amplitude depth is \(\delta\ln2=1.45\,\mathrm{mm}\). A half-step depth and an exponential e-fold depth are different measurement definitions. Frequency and elapsed time are independent controls in the two experiments. In a slab comparable in thickness to either penetration scale the second interface supplies another boundary condition.

Read the two profiles at a shared probe

The graph plots the step response and the sinusoidal envelope against physical depth. Place the probe at 2 mm, vary the step duration and check the square-root law using the half-field depth. Hold that time fixed while quadrupling frequency. Only the periodic envelope shrinks by a factor of two in length. Time-dependent field and envelope both use normalised field-amplitude units.

Tutorial on inversion and currents

A step profile has half-field depth \(2.0\,\mathrm{mm}\) after \(0.30\,\mathrm{ms}\). Infer \(D_m\) and \(\sigma\) if \(\mu=\mu_0\).

Work through the solution
\[D_m=(0.002/0.9539)^2/0.00030=0.01465\,\mathrm{m^2/s}.\]

Conductivity is

\[1/(\mu_0D_m)=5.43\times10^7\,\mathrm{S/m}.\]

A single profile measures the product \(\mu\sigma\) unless permeability is independently known.

If \(B=B_z(x,t)\), find the sign and surface magnitude of \(J_y\) for a positive ideal step. Compare the current integrated over depth with the imposed field.

Work through the solution

Ampère gives

\[J_y=-\partial_xB_z/\mu.\]

The surface derivative is \(-B_0/\sqrt{\pi D_mt}\), hence current is positive and its magnitude falls as \(t^{-1/2}\). Its integral over all \(x>0\) is \(B_0/\mu\). The current spreads over a growing region while maintaining the total sheet current dictated by the field difference.

At a probe the AC envelope is 0.10 of the surface amplitude. What is the phase lag, and what is its time-delay equivalent at 1 kHz?

Work through the solution

Solve \(e^{-x/\delta}=0.1\), so

\[x/\delta=\ln10=2.303\,\mathrm{rad}.\]

At 1 kHz this equals

\[2.303/(2\pi\,1000)=0.367\,\mathrm{ms}.\]

This is the phase delay of a single sinusoid. A pulse contains many frequencies with different attenuation and phase shifts.

How a magnetic field relaxes into a finite conductor · Magnetism, induction and field dynamics

A conducting sheet initially shields its interior from an applied magnetic step. Given time, a normal resistive metal admits a static tangential magnetic field. The same equations give a finite slab a discrete relaxation spectrum and a periodic drive a complex penetration depth. These are different initial and boundary conditions for one diffusion operator.
