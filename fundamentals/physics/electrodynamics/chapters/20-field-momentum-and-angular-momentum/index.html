Give a region a mechanical and a field account

Charges respond to Lorentz force density \(\mathbf f=\rho\mathbf E+\mathbf J\times\mathbf B\). To predict the total force on enclosed matter, one can measure the fields on a surrounding vacuum surface. First eliminate the sources. In vacuum Gauss’s law gives \(\rho=\epsilon_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf E\), and the Ampère–Maxwell law gives

\[\mathbf J=(\nabla\times\mathbf B)/\mu_0-\epsilon_0\partial_t\mathbf E.\]

Introduce \(\mathbf g=\epsilon_0\mathbf E\times\mathbf B=\mathbf S/c^2\). Its time derivative supplies both \(\epsilon_0\partial_t\mathbf E\times\mathbf B\) and \(\epsilon_0\mathbf E\times\partial_t\mathbf B\). The first cancels the displacement-current term in the force; Faraday’s law rewrites the second.

For example, the electric terms now read \(\epsilon_0[\mathbf E(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)-\mathbf E\times(\nabla\times\mathbf E)]\). Component \(i\) is \(\partial_j[\epsilon_0(E_iE_j-\delta_{ij}E^2/2)]\), with repeated Cartesian indices summed. The magnetic terms have the same form because \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0\). Integration gives

\[T_{ij}=\epsilon_0(E_iE_j-\tfrac12 E^2\delta_{ij})+\frac1{\mu_0}(B_iB_j-\tfrac12 B^2\delta_{ij}),\]
\[\mathbf F_{\rm matter}=\oint T\mathbf n\,dA-\frac d{dt}\int\mathbf g\,dV .\]

The normal points out of the chosen volume. Positive \(T_{xx}\) denotes tension along \(x\) in this convention. For a wave travelling in \(+x\) with transverse fields, \(T_{xx}=-u\), where \(u\) is energy density. On an inlet whose outward normal is \(-\hat{\mathbf x}\), the normal traction therefore points in \(+x\).

Measure the time before light reaches a detector

A finite plane pulse in vacuum has \(|\mathbf S|=cu\). Its total momentum is \(U/c\) along the propagation direction. Surround a freely absorbing target and a length of initially empty space upstream with a vacuum control surface. While a pulse enters but has yet to reach the target, the inlet stress integral is nonzero. The electromagnetic momentum inside grows at exactly the same rate. The minus time derivative in the total force cancels the inlet contribution and the target has zero force during this interval.

Worked optical impulse

A 0.60 mJ pulse is fully absorbed after crossing the inlet. Initially and finally the region contains no pulse. Integrating the balance over that full time removes the stored-field term. The target gains \(0.60\times10^{-3}/c=2.00\times10^{-12}\) N s. For ideal normal reflection the exiting pulse carries the opposite momentum, giving twice that impulse. For a transparent window with parallel faces at normal incidence the outgoing pulse may eventually have the same vacuum momentum as the incoming pulse, while the two faces still experience transient forces.

Convert distributed field momentum into a turning impulse

Now take a long coaxial arrangement: an inner narrow cylindrical shell of radius \(a\) carries free charge \(+\lambda\) per length; an outer shell of radius \(b\) carries \(-\lambda\). There is a uniform axial magnetic field \(B_0\) over the annular gap, with end effects negligible. For a definite preparation take an ideal long solenoid producing uniform field throughout \(r<b\), and confine the initial electric field to the gap. Between shells \(E_r=\lambda/(2\pi\epsilon_0 r)\). The field momentum density points towards negative azimuth because \(\hat{\mathbf r}\times\hat{\mathbf z}=-\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\).

\[g_\phi=-\lambda B_0/(2\pi r),\]
\[L_{{\rm field},z}'=\int_a^b r g_\phi\,2\pi r\,dr=-\tfrac12\lambda B_0(b^2-a^2).\]

The prime denotes angular momentum per length. While the coil current decays slowly, Faraday’s law gives \(2\pi r E_\phi=-\pi r^2\dot B\) for radii up to \(b\). The torque on each shell per length is \(r\lambda_{\rm shell}E_\phi(r)\). Integrating from \(B_0\) to zero yields \(+\lambda B_0a^2/2\) on the inner shell and \(-\lambda B_0b^2/2\) on the outer. Their sum reproduces the initial field angular momentum. Clamps would receive those impulses if the shells were held fixed.

Worked sign and magnitude

With \(\lambda=20\) nC/m, \(B_0=0.25\) T, \(a=10\) mm and \(b=30\) mm, the initial \(L'_z=-2.0\times10^{-12}\) kg m/s. That is angular momentum per metre of apparatus. The outer shell receives nine times the inner impulse magnitude and rotates in the opposite sense. Torque from the induced azimuthal electric field supplies this transfer even though both shells started at rest.

Transfer angular momentum from a field into two shells

The inner radius is 10 mm and its line charge is +20 nC/m. For a 30 mm outer radius and an initial field of 0.25 T, the final shell impulses are +0.25 and −2.25 in units of 10⁻¹² kg m/s. Trace the four accounts during switch-off.

Find a pressure when transmission and reflection coexist

A normal beam of intensity 300 W/m² reaches a free-standing film in vacuum. Reflected power fraction is 0.20 and transmitted fraction is 0.50. Find the time-averaged pressure and identify the momentum carried away.

Work through the solution

With incident direction positive, outgoing momentum per area and time is \((T-R)I/c\). The deposited amount is

\[(1-T+R)I/c=0.70(300)/c=0.700\,\mu{\rm Pa}.\]

The absorbed part contributes \(0.30I/c\) and reflection adds \(0.40I/c\). The result assumes forward and backward beams remain normal to the film.

Reverse just the charges

In the coaxial example reverse both shell charges while keeping the axial field and current decay unchanged. Which quantities reverse?

Work through the solution

The radial field, stored azimuthal momentum and angular momentum reverse. Faraday’s induced \(E_\phi\) follows the same history because the flux decay is unchanged. Each electric force and angular impulse reverses since the charge signs reverse. This independent sign argument checks the cross product in the original momentum density.

Practicum · audit a pulse bouncing inside a partly absorbing cavity

A shutter has just prepared a short \(U_0=1.20\) mJ pulse travelling to the right in an otherwise empty one-dimensional cavity. Start the accounting after preparation. The left mirror reflects perfectly. At every encounter with the right mirror 60% of incident pulse energy is reflected, 30% passes into vacuum on the right, and 10% is absorbed. Pulse duration is short compared with the flight time across the cavity. Mirrors are mounted on massive force probes; neglect their displacement and delay any re-emission of heat. The challenge is to reconcile two large, oppositely signed readings with a small net impulse.

Audit one encounter and decide which surface terms matter

For energy \(U\) just before an encounter with the right mirror, calculate its mechanical impulse. Then find the impulse when the reflected packet hits the left mirror. Which momentum is still present between those events?

Follow the calculation and check the result

The incoming momentum is \(+U/c\). The two outgoing packets carry \(+0.30U/c\) and \(-0.60U/c\). Momentum balance therefore assigns the right mirror \(+(1-0.30+0.60)U/c=+1.30U/c\). This includes reversing the reflected packet and stopping the absorbed packet. The left mirror receives \(-2(0.60U)/c=-1.20U/c\) at the next collision. Between those events the cavity still contains \(-0.60U/c\) of field momentum. The complete system also contains the \(+0.30U/c\) transmitted packet outside the cavity.

Stop the movie at a specified time

Freeze the experiment just after the third right-mirror encounter and before the next left-mirror encounter. Find the remaining energy, radiated output, deposited heat, both probe impulses, and total field momentum.

Follow the calculation and check the result

Three incident energies in units of \(U_0\) form the sequence \(1,0.6,0.36\). Their sum is 1.96. The output energy is \(0.30(1.96)U_0=0.588U_0\), heat is \(0.196U_0\), and \(0.216U_0\) remains. The three fractions sum to unity. Right-probe impulse is

\[+1.30(1.96)U_0/c=+2.548U_0/c.\]

Only two left reflections have occurred, so the left probe reads \(-2(0.60+0.36)U_0/c=-1.920U_0/c\). Their sum is \(+0.628U_0/c\).

The escaped packets carry \(+0.588U_0/c\), but the packet still inside carries \(-0.216U_0/c\). Including these two contributions gives measured plus field momentum \((0.628+0.588-0.216)U_0/c=U_0/c\), the initial value. Counting a third left-hand reflection at this time would violate the preselected event boundary. Since \(U_0/c=4.00\times10^{-12}\) N s, the probe readings are approximately \(+10.2\) and \(-7.68\) pN s.

Sum the full history in two independent ways

Continue until no macroscopic pulse remains. Find the impulse on each mirror, then derive their combined reading directly from exterior measurements without examining any reflection.

Follow the calculation and check the result

The geometric series of right hits sums to \(U_0/(1-0.6)=2.5U_0\). The right impulse is \(3.25U_0/c\). Summing every left hit gives

\[-2(0.60)U_0/[(1-0.60)c]=-3.00U_0/c.\]

Thus the pair receives \(+0.25U_0/c\). All transmitted packets travel right and together carry \(0.30U_0/(1-0.60)=0.75U_0\). An external vacuum control surface therefore gives \((1-0.75)U_0/c\) immediately. The absorbed \(0.25U_0\) accounts for the energy stored as heat.

A detector without enough time resolution can recover the integrated exterior momentum even though it cannot resolve the alternating internal forces. Conversely, two large internal probe signals can coexist with a modest net exterior impulse. In a numerical time march record separate signed transfers and the energy still in flight after each boundary update, then vary the stopping time. A budget that closes only after the very last encounter cannot diagnose an intermediate sign mistake.

Energy flow and mechanical force from vacuum fields · Magnetism, induction and field dynamics

An external support holds a charged capacitor plate or a current-carrying coil in place. One can infer the support force from fields just outside the body, without summing forces on every microscopic charge. A second surface integral tracks the energy feeding the same apparatus. Both methods start with Maxwell's equations, but they transport different conserved quantities.
