Start with the response to one thin sheet

Two large grounded conducting plates occupy \(x=0\) and \(x=L\). A charge sheet of surface density \(\sigma\) sits at \(x=s\), far from plate edges. Translational symmetry along each plate makes the potential a function of \(x\) alone. Integrating Poisson’s equation across the charge shows that \(V\) is continuous and \(V'(s^+)-V'(s^-)=-\sigma/\epsilon_0\). The potential is linear in each empty gap. It is helpful to remove the scale \(\sigma/\epsilon_0\) and call the remaining function \(G(x,s)\).

On the left write \(G=A x\) to satisfy the first plate. On the right write \(G=B(L-x)\) to satisfy the second. Continuity imposes \(As=B(L-s)\). The derivative jump gives \(-B-A=-1\), so \(A=(L-s)/L\) and \(B=s/L\). A single expression covers both segments:

\[G(x,s)=\frac{\min(x,s)[L-\max(x,s)]}{L},\]
\[-\partial_x^2G(x,s)=\delta(x-s).\]

This Green function has units of length in this one-dimensional reduction. A source near the left plate produces a steep left slope and a shallow right slope. The left conductor acquires the greater magnitude of induced charge. Exchanging detector and source coordinates leaves \(G\) unchanged. This symmetry will turn out to be a physical consequence of the linear field equation.

Background · Reciprocity
\[\int \rho_1 V_2\,dV=\int \rho_2 V_1\,dV\]

The displayed form assumes compatible homogeneous boundary conditions and a reciprocal linear medium. Boundary work terms matter for separately driven electrodes.

Assemble many sheets and retain the boundary voltages

A slice of thickness \(ds\) has sheet charge \(\rho(s)\,ds\). Linearity allows its contribution \(\rho(s)G(x,s)\,ds/\epsilon_0\) to be added to every other slice. If the plate voltages are \(V_0,V_L\), a straight line accounts for the source-free solution satisfying those voltages:

\[V(x)=V_0(1-x/L)+V_L x/L+\frac1{\epsilon_0}\int_0^L G(x,s)\rho(s)\,ds .\]

For uniform positive \(\rho\), split the integral at \(s=x\). The left piece is \((L-x)x^2/(2L)\), and the right piece is \(x(L-x)^2/(2L)\), before multiplication by \(\rho/\epsilon_0\). Together they yield

\[V_{\rm charge}=\rho x(L-x)/(2\epsilon_0).\]

Twice differentiating recovers the volume charge everywhere, and at either plate the charge contribution is zero.

Worked field measurement between unequal plates

Let \(L=10\) mm, \(V_0=0\), \(V_L=2.0\) V, and \(\rho=1.00\,\mu{\rm C/m^3}\). The straight-line background contributes 1.00 V at the midpoint. The uniform space charge contributes \(\rho L^2/(8\epsilon_0)=1.41\) V. The maximum potential can exceed both conductor voltages in the presence of a positive source. At the midpoint the charge contribution to the field vanishes by symmetry, so the field there still has its background value \(-200\) V/m. This gives two independent observables for the same solution.

Prove that source and detector can exchange roles

For two electrostatic states occupying the same region with a linear, symmetric permittivity tensor, define potentials \(V_1,V_2\) and charges \(\rho_1,\rho_2\). Multiply the first Poisson equation by \(V_2\), the second by \(V_1\), then subtract and integrate. The two terms containing products of gradients cancel after integration by parts because of tensor symmetry. With outward normal from the dielectric region one obtains

\[\int_\Omega (V_2\rho_1-V_1\rho_2)\,d^3x=\oint_{\partial\Omega}\big[V_1\mathbf n\cdot\epsilon\nabla V_2-V_2\mathbf n\cdot\epsilon\nabla V_1\big]\,dA .\]

With both outer boundary potentials zero, the surface term vanishes. Choosing a point source for each state shows that the potential per unit charge at the other point is the same in either order. Fixed geometry, material response and boundary type matter. Replacing a grounded electrode by an isolated electrode between the two measurements changes the problem.

Worked electrode reading from a weighting potential

Take state 1 to be the real charge \(q\) inside a cavity whose electrodes are all grounded. For state 2 remove the charge, set the right electrode to 1 V and the others to zero, and divide this potential by 1 V to define \(w\). The surface normal in the identity points into the metal, so \(\mathbf n\cdot\epsilon\nabla V_1\) is the free charge density on the metal face. The identity now gives

\[Q_R=-q\,w(\mathbf x_q).\]

For parallel plates \(w=x/L\). Moving \(12\) pC from \(0.20L\) to \(0.65L\) changes the right-electrode charge by \(-5.4\) pC. Its change on the other plate is \(+5.4\) pC, as an independent sum check. Device designers can reuse one computed \(w\) for many charge positions.

Move a charge sheet between grounded electrodes

Keep the plate separation at 10 mm. At 30% of the gap, a positive sheet of 3 nC/m² induces −2.1 and −0.9 nC/m² on the left and right plates. Move the sheet through the midpoint, then reverse its charge. Predict the sign of the slope jump each time.

Practise splitting the integral and reading an induced signal

Locate a hidden sheet using both electrodes

A grounded pair of plates has induced charges of \(-3.0\) pC on the left and \(-7.0\) pC on the right from a single positive sheet of finite illuminated area in the large-plate approximation. Infer its charge and dimensionless position. Does the pair determine the width if several sheets may be present?

Work through the solution

All induced charge sums to \(-q\), so \(q=10.0\) pC. The right fraction \(0.700\) places the sheet at \(s=0.700L\). For a cloud, linearity gives

\[Q_R=-\int(x/L)\,dq.\]

The two readings yield total charge and first moment only. Two equal sheets at \(0.60L\) and \(0.80L\) give identical readings.

Bound a numerical influence coefficient

A discretisation gives

\[G(0.2L,0.7L)=0.062L,\]
\[G(0.7L,0.2L)=0.058L.\]

Which exact value should both approach? Does averaging establish convergence?

Work through the solution

Their target is

\[0.2(1-0.7)L=0.060L.\]

The discrepancy may come from depositing source charge and sampling voltage with different weights. The two errors happen to cancel in the mean here. Refine the grid and perform each measurement separately. Reciprocity alone also cannot reveal a common bias in both entries.

Green functions, the Poisson kernel and what a small electrode can influence · Electrostatic field theory and measurement

A Green function is the response of an entire boundary-value problem to a unit source. Once that response is known, a continuous charge distribution and a patterned boundary voltage can both be treated by integration. The two integrations require different kernels.
