Reconstructing a partially polarised electromagnetic wave

A detector averages enormous numbers of optical cycles. Its horizontal and vertical readings may be equal for a circularly polarised wave, a diagonal linearly polarised wave, or unpolarised light. Which additional intensities distinguish these preparations?

Start from what a polarising analyser measures

Take a quasi-monochromatic beam propagating in a fixed direction, resolved in transverse axes \(x,y\). Use complex field amplitudes with time factor \(e^{-i\omega t}\). Absorb common power and impedance factors into their units. The time- or ensemble-averaged coherency matrix \(J=\langle\boldsymbol E\boldsymbol E^\dagger\rangle\) has four real degrees of freedom because it is Hermitian. An analyser selecting the normalised complex state \(\boldsymbol a\) passes amplitude \(\boldsymbol a^\dagger\boldsymbol E\) and measures \(I_a=\boldsymbol a^\dagger J\boldsymbol a\). Thus every analyser projects the same matrix onto a distinct polarisation.

Choose \(S_0=J_{xx}+J_{yy}\), \(S_1=J_{xx}-J_{yy}\),

\[S_2=2\operatorname{Re}J_{xy},\]
\[S_3=-2\operatorname{Im}J_{xy}.\]

For this convention the state \((1,i)/\sqrt2\) has positive \(S_3\). Naming it by its explicit Jones vector avoids a handedness ambiguity between observer viewing directions. Inverting those definitions gives

\[J=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}S_0+S_1&S_2-iS_3\\S_2+iS_3&S_0-S_1\end{pmatrix}.\]

Horizontal and vertical linear analysers therefore give \((S_0\pm S_1)/2\). The \(+45^\circ,-45^\circ\) pair gives \((S_0\pm S_2)/2\). The circular pair \((1,\pm i)/\sqrt2\) gives \((S_0\pm S_3)/2\). Every pair sum recovers total intensity, providing an internal calibration check. Subtracting the complementary measurements cancels a common additive detector offset in each Stokes difference, though \(S_0\) still needs a dark correction.

Derive the physically allowed domain

Because \(I_a\ge0\) for every analyser, both eigenvalues of \(J\) are nonnegative. Solve its quadratic characteristic equation using the trace \(S_0\) and determinant \((S_0^2-S_1^2-S_2^2-S_3^2)/4\). The eigenvalues are \((S_0\pm|\boldsymbol S|)/2\), where \(\boldsymbol S=(S_1,S_2,S_3)\). Consequently \(P=|\boldsymbol S|/S_0\) lies between zero and one for a beam with nonzero intensity. A single fully polarised Jones state gives rank one and \(P=1\). An incoherent mixture of two orthogonal states of equal intensity has \(J=(S_0/2)I\) and \(P=0\).

Suppose a corrected data set gives \(I_x,I_y=70,30\); \(I_{+45},I_{-45}=65,35\); \(I_+,I_-=60,40\) in the same units. The pair sums agree at 100. Reconstructed parameters are \((100,40,30,20)\), giving \(P=\sqrt{2900}/100=0.539\). The eigenvalues are 76.93 and 23.07. Subtracting the smaller times the identity leaves a rank-one contribution of intensity 53.85 and an unpolarised remainder of intensity 46.15. This decomposition describes the observed second moments; several microscopic preparations can produce the same \(J\).

Predict a rotating analyser and identify its blind spot

For \(\boldsymbol a=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)\), expanding the projection and using double-angle identities gives

\[I_\alpha=[S_0+S_1\cos2\alpha+S_2\sin2\alpha]/2.\]

A linear analyser alone is blind to \(S_3\), even when sampled at hundreds of angles. In the example the extrema are \(50\pm25\), with the maximum at \(\alpha=\frac12\operatorname{atan2}(30,40)=18.43^\circ\). The linear fringe visibility 0.50 is slightly below the total degree 0.539. Inserting a quarter-wave plate with Jones matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-i)\) before the same analyser transforms \(S_2\) into \(S_3\) in the intensity formula. One can now obtain the previously hidden signed correlation from readings at \(45^\circ\) and \(135^\circ\).

The experiment prepares an adjustable polarised fraction with azimuth \(\psi\) and ellipticity angle \(\chi\), mixed with an unpolarised background. Its normalised Stokes vector is \(P(\cos2\chi\cos2\psi,\cos2\chi\sin2\psi,\sin2\chi)\). Make the unassisted intensity scan flat by choosing circular polarisation, then insert the fixed retarder virtually and predict the recovered difference. Setting \(P=0\) makes both scans flat. This comparison shows which measurement discriminates the two cases.

Tutorial on instrument design

A linear scan alone has \(I_{\max}=75,I_{\min}=25\). Give the range of total degrees of polarisation allowed and the maximum possible magnitude of \(S_3\).

Work through the solution

The linear magnitude is 50 and \(S_0=100\). Thus \(0.5\le P\le1\), and the positivity bound gives

\[|S_3|\le\sqrt{100^2-50^2}=86.60.\]

A circular analyser measurement fixes the remaining magnitude and sign.

An ideal beam contains 60 units of pure horizontal light and 40 units of unpolarised light. Compare its Stokes vector with a coherent superposition of horizontal and vertical components with intensities 80 and 20 and zero relative phase.

Work through the solution

The incoherent mixture has \((100,60,0,0)\) and \(P=0.6\). The coherent preparation has \((100,60,80,0)\) since \(2\sqrt{80\times20}=80\), and \(P=1\). Their horizontal and vertical readings coincide. A diagonal analyser gives 50 for the mixture and 90 for the coherent preparation.

All three measured component differences equal 70 at measured total 100. Can a physical beam have these parameters?

Work through the solution

They imply \(|\boldsymbol S|=70\sqrt3=121.2>S_0\) and a negative matrix eigenvalue. Check dark subtraction, gain calibration, alignment and whether the source changed between readings. A physical fit with stated uncertainties is required before interpreting the data as an optical state.
