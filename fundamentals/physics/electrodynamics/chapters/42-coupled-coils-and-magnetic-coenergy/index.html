Forces and torques from a matrix of flux linkages

Two measurements made while holding current fixed and flux fixed can produce different torque curves. They agree on the torque of the same physical state. A derivation from electrical work identifies precisely what must be held fixed when differentiating.

Keep track of each electrical and mechanical port

Let \(\theta\) be the mechanical angle of a pair of coils, \(i_j\) the currents entering their positive terminals and \(\lambda_j\) their linked fluxes, including all turns. Use ideal windings, a reversible magnetic medium and negligible radiation. Faraday's terminal relation is

\[v_j=d\lambda_j/dt.\]

With positive torque \(T\) doing work on the moving rotor, energy balance reads

\[dW_m=\sum_j i_j\,d\lambda_j-T\,d\theta .\]

The natural variables of magnetic energy \(W_m\) are fluxes and position. Consequently \(i_j=(\partial W_m/\partial\lambda_j)_\theta\) and \(T=-(\partial W_m/\partial\theta)_\lambda\). A current-controlled experiment supplies or removes electrical work during motion. Define the coenergy \(W'_m=\sum i_j\lambda_j-W_m\). Its differential is \(\sum_j\lambda_j\,di_j+T\,d\theta\), giving \(T=(\partial W'_m/\partial\theta)_i\). Coenergy is a Legendre transform. Its numerical value equals stored energy for linear zero-bias materials but can differ for nonlinear response.

Derive reciprocity, positivity and torque

Take \(\boldsymbol\lambda=L(\theta)\mathbf i\) with a two-port inductance matrix. From the existence of \(W'_m\), mixed derivatives at fixed angle commute, so

\[\partial\lambda_1/\partial i_2=\partial\lambda_2/\partial i_1.\]

This gives \(L_{12}=L_{21}=M\) for the given terminal orientation. With positive self-inductances, positive stored energy requires \(L_1L_2-M^2>0\) for independent currents in a nonsingular model. Integrating coenergy at fixed angle gives

\[W'_m=\mathbf i^T L\mathbf i/2.\]

The energy in its own natural variables is \(W_m=\boldsymbol\lambda^T L^{-1}\boldsymbol\lambda/2\). Differentiate \(L^{-1}L=I\) to obtain

\[(L^{-1})'=-L^{-1}L'L^{-1}.\]

Thus both torque formulas at the same state give

\[T=\tfrac12\mathbf i^T\frac{\partial L}{\partial\theta}\mathbf i .\]

For constant self-inductances and \(M=M_0\cos\theta\),

\[T=-M_0i_1i_2\sin\theta.\]

Reversing one winding reverses the coordinate definitions of its current and mutual inductance together, preserving the physical result. Under prescribed fluxes the same formula holds with currents \(\mathbf i=L(\theta)^{-1}\boldsymbol\lambda\) recalculated at every angle.

Compare a current supply with two flux-conserving loops

Start at the same state, then turn the coils

Use \(L_1=4.0\) mH, \(L_2=9.0\) mH and \(M_0=3.0\) mH. At \(\theta_0=60^\circ\), let currents be \(2.00\) A and \(1.00\) A. The flux linkages are \(9.50\) and \(12.0\) mWb. At this configuration \(T=-5.20\) mN m and the energy is \(15.5\) mJ. The units follow from an inductance times current squared, which is energy, while angle in radians is dimensionless.

Move to \(90^\circ\). Keeping both currents fixed gives \(T=-6.00\) mN m. Keeping both fluxes fixed instead gives \(i_1=9.50/4.0=2.375\) A and \(i_2=12.0/9.0=1.333\) A because the mutual term has vanished. The new torque is \(-9.50\) mN m. The two paths through state space now represent genuinely different experiments. For the fixed-current path the field energy falls by 3.00 mJ. The sources absorb 6.00 mJ and the external mechanical agent supplies 3.00 mJ, consistent with the first law.

A nonlinear constitutive curve

For one winding model a reversible flux linkage \(\lambda=L_\ell i+\Lambda\tanh(i/I_s)\), where positive \(L_\ell,\Lambda,I_s\) may depend on geometry. Integration gives

\[W'_m=L_\ell i^2/2+\Lambda I_s\ln\cosh(i/I_s);\]
\[W_m=i\lambda-W'_m.\]

Only in the linear weak-current limit do they coincide. A measured hysteresis loop would make this single-valued construction invalid without additional state variables describing material history.

Compare turning a rotor at fixed current and at fixed flux

Both protocols start at 60°. With initial currents 2 A and 1 A, the torques there coincide at −5.20 mN m but reach −6.00 and −9.50 mN m at 90°. Reverse one initial current and identify the terms changed by the corresponding constraints.

Problem sheet · independent torque measurements

Test a computed torque numerically

How would a finite-difference experiment recover torque in the preceding fixed-flux state without differentiating any matrix entries symbolically?

Work through the solution

Take the known \(\boldsymbol\lambda\), invert \(L\) at \(\theta\pm h\) separately and compute \(W_\pm=\boldsymbol\lambda^TL_\pm^{-1}\boldsymbol\lambda/2\). Estimate \(T=-(W_+-W_-)/(2h)\) using \(h\) in radians. At \(90^\circ\) the result converges to \(-9.50\) mN m. Reusing the same currents at both perturbations silently changes what the finite difference measures.

Infer the size of the conservative part of coupling

At fixed angle a small current cycle on the \(i_1,i_2\) plane produces \(\oint(\lambda_1di_1+\lambda_2di_2)\neq0\) after correcting lead resistance. What does this say about a proposed coenergy function?

Work through the solution

An exact differential has zero integral around every closed path. A repeatable nonzero result means that a single-valued coenergy of the two currents alone cannot describe these measurements. Check frequency-dependent losses, hysteresis and whether an unmeasured mechanical coordinate moved. Making the inductance matrix symmetric by averaging two readings alone cannot explain the residual work.

Flux linkage, coupled coils and magnetic coenergy · Magnetism, induction and field dynamics

A winding links flux through each turn. In two windings the current in either one can change the voltage in the other. A matrix of flux linkages describes this coupling, but predicting power or mechanical force requires specifying the current directions and the control conditions.
