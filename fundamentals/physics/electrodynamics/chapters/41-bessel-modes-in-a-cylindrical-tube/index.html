How a circular wall selects Bessel modes

A voltage pattern is imposed on the end of a long grounded tube. Its penetration length depends on the tube radius through a number that looks unfamiliar in a Cartesian Fourier series. Deriving that number also explains the proper radial weight for projecting data.

Separate Laplace’s equation without flattening the circular wall

Take an empty cylinder of radius \(a\) extending from \(z=0\) to large positive \(z\). Its side is grounded. A segmented end electrode imposes the continuous axisymmetric potential \(V(r,0)=V_0[1-(r/a)^2]\), matching zero at the rim. In cylindrical coordinates \(\nabla^2V=r^{-1}\partial_r(r\partial_rV)+\partial_z^2V=0\). Set \(V=R(r)Z(z)\). A decaying solution requires \(Z=e^{-kz}\) with \(k>0\). Then

\[(rR')'+k^2rR=0 .\]

Using \(\xi=kr\) gives Bessel's equation of order zero. The regular solution \(J_0(\xi)\) starts at one with zero derivative. Its series follows by substituting \(R=\sum c_m\xi^{2m}\): the recurrence is \(c_{m+1}=-c_m/[4(m+1)^2]\). The independent solution diverges logarithmically at the origin and would introduce an unmodelled line source. The side boundary requires \(J_0(ka)=0\), so \(k_n=j_n/a\) with \(j_n\) the positive zeros of \(J_0\). The first three are approximately \(2.40483,5.52008,8.65373\).

Derive the radial weight and coefficients

Multiply the equation for \(R_m\) by \(R_n\) and subtract the reversed equation. Integration gives \((k_m^2-k_n^2)\int_0^a rR_mR_n\,dr\) equal to a boundary term. At \(a\) both functions vanish; at zero their derivatives are regular and the factor \(r\) vanishes. Hence distinct modes are orthogonal with the radial weight \(r\). That factor represents the area of a circular ring. The squared norm follows from the derivative identities \(J_0'=-J_1\), \((tJ_1)'=tJ_0\), using the known zero at \(a\):

\[\int_0^1 xJ_0(j_nx)^2\,dx=\frac{J_1(j_n)^2}{2},\]
\[b_n=\frac{2\int_0^1 x(1-x^2)J_0(j_nx)\,dx}{J_1(j_n)^2}.\]

Use \((xJ_1(jx))'=jxJ_0(jx)\) to integrate the numerator by parts. The endpoint factor \(1-x^2\) vanishes at \(x=1\); the remaining integral is

\[2j^{-1}\int_0^1 x^2 J_1(jx)\,dx=2J_2(j)/j^2.\]

At a zero of \(J_0\), \(J_2(j)=2J_1(j)/j\). Thus \(b_n=8/[j_n^3J_1(j_n)]\), with alternating signs. The full solution is

\[V(r,z)=V_0\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n J_0(j_n r/a)e^{-j_n z/a}.\]

Each partial sum exactly grounds the side wall and decays toward infinity. The representation of the end electrode improves as modes are added. For a finite cylinder closed at \(z=L\), replace each exponential by \(\sinh[k_n(L-z)]/\sinh(k_nL)\). This satisfies both end conditions and tends to the long-cylinder result at fixed \(z\) as \(L\) grows.

Estimate a leakage length and a modal error

How far does the slowest mode survive?

For \(a=12\) mm the first modal decay length is

\[a/j_1=4.99\,\mathrm{mm}.\]

Far from the end, two axis probes separated by 5.0 mm should read voltages in ratio about \(e^{-2.40483(5/12)}=0.367\). The first coefficient \(b_1\simeq1.108\) is larger than unity because the higher modes bring the prescribed central boundary value back to exactly \(V_0\). A fit using \(V_0e^{-j_1z/a}\) would get the correct distant slope but the wrong amplitude.

Predict the accuracy of a one-mode model

The second coefficient is about \(-0.140\). On axis the magnitude of its contribution relative to the first is approximately \(0.126\,e^{-(5.520-2.405)z/a}\). At \(z=a\) this is 0.56%. At \(z=0\) higher modes cannot be neglected at the same tolerance. Near a radial node of a chosen reference mode a relative error becomes unstable; use absolute potential error measured in units of \(V_0\) there.

Reconstruct a circular electrode pattern with Bessel modes

Each retained mode exactly satisfies the side-wall condition. At the end, one mode alone overestimates the central voltage by about 10.8%. Move the detector deeper into the tube and compare three and eight modes until their interior traces coincide to the precision you need.

Problem sheet · source weighting and a radial inverse problem

Explain a nonzero axis slope in a file of radial data

Someone fits the central potential by \(V=A+Br\) near the cylinder axis. What does a nonzero \(B\) imply in the continuum problem?

Work through the solution

A smooth axisymmetric Cartesian scalar must have zero radial derivative at the axis. The term \(Br\) would give \(r^{-1}\partial_r(r\partial_rV)=B/r\), incompatible with a smooth source-free interior and finite homogeneous axial curvature. An interpolation can manufacture this feature if it treats the axis as an arbitrary material boundary.

Infer tube radius using only distant measurements

Two distant on-axis probes separated by 3.00 mm have a voltage ratio of 0.500. Infer \(a\) under first-mode dominance, then design a check against residual higher modes.

Work through the solution

From \(e^{-j_1\Delta z/a}=1/2\), \(a=j_1\Delta z/\ln2=10.4\) mm. Move both probes farther from the end by the same amount. A persistent ratio supports the one-mode approximation. If higher modes remain, their changing relative weight changes the ratio. Very small voltages may instead expose the noise floor and lead to an apparently shallower decay.
