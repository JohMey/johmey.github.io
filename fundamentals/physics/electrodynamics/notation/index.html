Electrodynamics · Notation and assumptions

Read each defining equation with its assumptions and units, then follow the link to the argument that gives the equation its meaning.

Electric displacement

\[\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P\]

Its flux measures free charge. Polarisation is dipole moment per volume. A discontinuity of normal displacement depends on free surface charge.

Derivation · How microscopic dipoles produce macroscopic charge

Maxwell capacitance coefficient

\[Q_i=\sum_j C_{ij}V_j\]

Potentials are defined relative to a stated reference. A floating electrode has a prescribed total charge and a potential determined by the other electrodes.

Derivation · Capacitance as a matrix of electrostatic energies

Potential and electric field

\[\mathbf E=-\nabla V\]

A volt per metre is a newton per coulomb. This relation describes an electrostatic field. Time-dependent induction introduces a vector-potential term.

Derivation · Charge, potential and field energy

Poynting flux

\[\mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H\]

Integrating its outward normal component gives field power leaving a closed surface. Work on matter and changing stored energy complete the balance.

Derivation · Electromagnetic waves and energy flux

Reciprocity

\[\int \rho_1 V_2\,dV=\int \rho_2 V_1\,dV\]

The displayed form assumes compatible homogeneous boundary conditions and a reciprocal linear medium. Boundary work terms matter for separately driven electrodes.

Derivation · Building a potential from point responses

Retarded time

\[t-t_r=|\mathbf r-\mathbf r_s(t_r)|/c\]

The source is evaluated at emission. For a moving source its location on the right also depends on the unknown emission time.

Derivation · Potentials, gauge freedom and the arrival of a signal

Skin depth

\[\delta=\sqrt{2/(\mu\sigma\omega)}\]

In a good homogeneous conductor under sinusoidal excitation this is the amplitude decay length. Intensity decays twice as fast in the exponent.

Derivation · How a conductor admits a field step and a sinusoid

Wave impedance

\[Z=E_t/H_t\]

The ratio has units of ohms. The field direction and mode determine the relevant tangential components. In a guide it can differ from the intrinsic impedance of the filling material.
