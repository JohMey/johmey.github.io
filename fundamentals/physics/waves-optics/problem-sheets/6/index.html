Set the depth of focus

An ideal 532 nm Gaussian should maintain at least half its peak axial intensity throughout a 4.00 mm sample centred on the waist. Find the smallest allowed waist radius and the required collimated input radius for a 75.0 mm lens forming that waist.

Show the Gaussian design

The half-intensity planes lie at \(\pm z_R\), so require \(z_R\ge2.00\) mm. The smallest waist is \(\sqrt{\lambda z_R/\pi}=18.4\) μm. For a flat incoming wavefront, \(w_{\rm in}\simeq\lambda f/(\pi w_0)=0.690\) mm. A smaller focus would raise central intensity at fixed power while decreasing the end-to-centre ratio below the required half.

Compare a beam with the fundamental mode

At 633 nm a beam has waist radius 40.0 μm and far-field half-angle 7.50 mrad. Find the product relative to the ideal Gaussian value. Under the convention \(M^2=\pi w_0\theta/\lambda\), estimate the minimum collimated input radius to make a 25.0 μm waist with a 100 mm lens.

Show the quality and focusing estimates

The measured product is \(3.00\times10^{-7}\) m rad and the ideal value is \(\lambda/\pi=2.01\times10^{-7}\) m rad. Their ratio is \(M^2=1.49\). In the paraxial, approximately Gaussian beam-quality model \(\theta=M^2\lambda/(\pi w_0)\), giving \(w_{\rm in}\simeq f\theta=1.20\) mm for 25.0 μm. This comparison needs consistent radius and divergence definitions, and a non-clipping lens.

A binary with unequal brightness

Two unresolved stars have flux ratio \(3:1\) and projected angular separation \(4.00\) milliarcseconds. Derive the minimum visibility and find its first baseline at 600 nm. What happens if the baseline rotates perpendicular to their separation?

Show the phasor addition

Normalize one flux to 3 and the other to 1. Their complex degree of coherence has magnitude \(|3+e^{i\phi}|/4\), with \(\phi=2\pi Bs/\lambda\). Its first minimum occurs at \(\phi=\pi\) and equals

\[(3-1)/4=0.50.\]

The separation is \(4.00\times10^{-3}/206265=1.94\times10^{-8}\) rad, giving \(B=\lambda/(2s)=15.5\) m. A perpendicular baseline gives zero projected separation, equal phases and unit intrinsic visibility if individual stellar disks remain unresolved.

Can a camera record what its objective resolves?

A 550 nm incoherent image is formed in air by an objective of numerical aperture 0.25 and lateral magnification 20. The detector pitch is 6.5 µm. Compare the Rayleigh separation, the object-side incoherent cutoff and the detector Nyquist limit. Predict the apparent object frequency if a 0.85 cycle/µm sinusoidal target is sampled without prefiltering.

Worked solution

The Rayleigh distance for two point emitters is 0.61λ/NA = 1.34 µm. For a sinusoidal intensity pattern the relevant quantity is the incoherent cutoff 2NA/λ = 0.909 cycle/µm. These refer to different targets. Taking the reciprocal of the Rayleigh distance would give the wrong cutoff.

At the object plane each detector pitch corresponds to 6.5/20 = 0.325 µm. Its Nyquist frequency is 1/(2 × 0.325) = 1.54 cycles/µm, above the optical cutoff. The 0.85 cycle/µm target remains at 0.85 cycle/µm. The pupil transfers it weakly because it is close to cutoff. A finite pixel further reduces its contrast by averaging.

Now reduce the magnification to 10. The effective pitch becomes 0.65 µm, with sampling frequency 1.538 cycles/µm. Since 0.85 exceeds half this frequency, the sampled pattern aliases to |0.85 − 1.538| = 0.688 cycle/µm. The optics are unchanged. Choosing M ≥ 2 × 6.5 × 0.909 ≈ 11.8 places even the optical cutoff within the detector passband.

Choose a slab width for one transverse TE mode

At 1.55 μm use \(n_1=1.50\) and \(n_2=1.45\). Find the full slab thickness at the first odd-mode cutoff. Classify full thicknesses 1.60 and 2.20 μm for TE polarization. What changes just above cutoff?

Use the half-thickness convention consistently

With full thickness \(d=2a\),

\[V=\pi d\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}/\lambda_0.\]

The odd threshold \(V=\pi/2\) gives \(d_c=\lambda_0/[2\sqrt{1.50^2-1.45^2}]=2.02\) μm. The 1.60 μm slab supports only the fundamental TE mode, while 2.20 μm also supports the first odd TE mode. Just above threshold its cladding decay constant is small and its field extends far outside the core. The count here excludes TM polarization.
