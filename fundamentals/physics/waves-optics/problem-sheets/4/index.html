Recover an aperture width

A 250 mm lens gives first minima separated by 2.40 mm under 480 nm light. Find the single-slit width. Predict the intensity fraction 0.60 mm from centre and the new separation if a 600 nm source is substituted.

Show the transform and its scale

The first minimum lies at \(X_1=f\lambda/w=1.20\) mm, so \(w=100\) μm. At \(X_1/2\), the sinc argument is \(\pi/2\) and intensity is \(4/\pi^2=0.405\) of peak. Keeping the same lens and aperture, angular width scales with wavelength. At 600 nm the first-zero separation becomes \(2.40(600/480)=3.00\) mm.

Shift or tilt?

The same slit moves sideways by 50 μm. Derive the resulting far-field phase factor and identify the intensity change. Then restore the slit position and tilt the incoming plane wave by 2.0 mrad. Predict the position of the central spot and compare both manipulations.

Show the shift theorem

Writing the translated aperture as \(A(x-x_0)\) and putting \(u=x-x_0\) in the transform gives \(U(q)=e^{-iqx_0}U_0(q)\). Its squared magnitude is unchanged. Tilt instead multiplies the aperture field by \(e^{ik\alpha x}\), replacing the pattern by \(U_0(q-k\alpha)\). The peak moves to \(X=f\alpha=0.500\) mm for the chosen sign. Either statement presumes the illuminating beam covers the translated slit uniformly and the lens pupil does not clip the pattern.

Pixel pitch or optical resolution?

An air objective with NA 0.50 images independent emitters at 600 nm onto 5.0 μm pixels with magnification 10. Calculate the Rayleigh scale, optical cutoff and detector Nyquist frequency referred to object space. Find the minimum magnification sampling the full passband.

Show the independent limits

The Rayleigh scale is \(0.61(0.600)/0.50=0.732\) μm. The incoherent cutoff is \(2(0.50)/0.600=1.67\) cycles/μm. Each pixel spans \(0.500\) μm in the object plane, so Nyquist frequency is \(1/(2\times0.500)=1.00\) cycle/μm. To capture all optical frequencies without aliasing set object pitch at most \(0.300\) μm, requiring magnification \(M\ge5.0/0.300=16.7\). Better sampling preserves more optical contrast but cannot transmit spatial frequencies outside the optical passband.
