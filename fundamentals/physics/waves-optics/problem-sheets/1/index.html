Select the direction of a pulse

A string has tension 72 N and linear density 0.020 kg/m. At one instant its displacement is \(y=Ae^{-(x/w)^2}\) with \(A=2.0\) mm and \(w=0.10\) m. Specify the initial velocity field that sends the pulse only to the right. Find the material velocity at \(x=w\) and the time for its peak to travel 3.0 m.

Relate the initial velocity to the slope

The speed is

\[c=\sqrt{72/0.020}=60\,\mathrm{m/s}.\]

A right-moving profile satisfies

\[y_t=-cy_x=2cAx\,e^{-(x/w)^2}/w^2.\]

At \(x=w\), the positive material velocity is

\[2(60)(0.0020)e^{-1}/0.10=0.883\,\mathrm{m/s}.\]

The crest travels 3.0 m in 0.050 s. Setting all velocities to zero instead produces two pulses in opposite directions, each with half the original displacement profile.

Match a measured reflection

A small junction connects strings under equal tension. A displacement pulse reflects with amplitude \(-0.20\) times the incident amplitude. Find the ratio of impedances, the ratio of linear mass densities and the transmitted power fraction for a long sinusoidal train. Does the reflected displacement sign change when the source is moved to the other side?

Invert the matching equations

Solving \(r=(1-z)/(1+z)\) with \(z=Z_2/Z_1\) gives

\[z=(1-r)/(1+r)=1.50.\]

Equal tension implies

\[\mu_2/\mu_1=z^2=2.25.\]

Four percent of the incident power reflects, so 96% transmits. Reversing incidence makes \(r=+0.20\) and leaves the reflected power fraction at 0.04. A sign reversal records phase; a power measurement alone cannot distinguish positive and negative \(r\).

Identify the boundary by spectroscopy

A string of length 0.75 m has speed 90 m/s. A vibration record contains adjacent peaks at 90 and 150 Hz. Compare the fixed–fixed and fixed–free spectra and predict one lower-frequency peak. Find how the peak spacing changes if the tension is increased by 44% without changing density.

Combine the mode conditions with the speed law

For fixed–fixed ends the frequencies are multiples of 60 Hz, inconsistent with both readings. A fixed–free string has odd multiples of \(c/(4L)=30\) Hz, producing 30, 90 and 150 Hz. The predicted lower peak is 30 Hz if the drive and probe couple to it. Increasing tension by a factor of 1.44 multiplies speed and all frequencies by 1.20. The spacing between adjacent allowed modes rises from 60 to 72 Hz.

Predict which note disappears for an off-centre pluck

A string is released from a triangular displacement of height \(h\) at \(x=L/3\). Obtain its coefficients by integrating the two constant slopes after one integration by parts. Compare the second and first modal energies and identify the first missing harmonic.

Project the two sides onto the sine modes

For pluck position \(\alpha L\), the slopes are \(h/(\alpha L)\) and \(-h/[(1-\alpha)L]\). The coefficient is

\[b_n=2h\sin(n\pi\alpha)/[\pi^2n^2\alpha(1-\alpha)].\]

At \(\alpha=1/3\) the first two sines are equal, so

\[b_2/b_1=1/4,\]
\[E_2/E_1=4(1/4)^2=1/4.\]

The third harmonic is absent because its sine vanishes at the pluck point. Moving the pluck slightly off that point restores it.

Recover a source speed from two microphones

A trolley carries a tone generator down a straight track in still air at \(c=340\) m/s. A stationary microphone ahead of it measures 714 Hz, and a stationary microphone behind it measures 646 Hz during the same steady run. Recover the trolley speed and emitted frequency without assuming a small Doppler shift. Would averaging the two readings recover the source frequency exactly?

Worked solution

With speed \(v\) towards the front microphone, the measured frequencies are

\[f_+=f_sc/(c-v),\]
\[f_-=f_sc/(c+v).\]

Taking their difference divided by their sum eliminates the unknown tone frequency. Thus

\[v=c(f_+-f_-)/(f_++f_-)=17.0\,\mathrm{m/s}.\]

Adding the reciprocal frequencies gives

\[1/f_++1/f_-=2/f_s.\]

The emitted frequency is their harmonic mean, \(2f_+f_-/(f_++f_-)=678.3\) Hz. The arithmetic mean is 680 Hz, high by 1.7 Hz. Substitution with \(v/c=0.05\) gives 678.3/0.95 = 714 Hz and 678.3/1.05 = 646 Hz.
