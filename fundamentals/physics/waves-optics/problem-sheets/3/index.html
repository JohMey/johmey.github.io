Measure a birefringent sample

A linearly polarised 2.0 mW beam of wavelength 500 nm enters a lossless plate at 45° to its fast axis. The crossed analyser transmits 0.50 mW. Find the smallest positive retardance and the corresponding thickness if \(\Delta n=0.020\). Explain why one reading cannot determine an arbitrary thickness.

Invert the analyser transmission

The measured fraction is 0.25, giving \(\sin^2(\delta/2)=1/4\). The first positive solution is \(\delta=\pi/3\). Thus \(d=\delta\lambda_0/(2\pi\Delta n)=4.17\) µm. Larger retardances with the same sine squared give the same reading. A thickness bound or a second wavelength can distinguish some of these branches. If the incident beam fails to make 45° to the plate, the inferred retardance is also biased.

Separate loss of coherence from unequal powers

Two interferometer arms separately deliver 0.90 and 0.10 mW at a detector. Together their fringes have visibility 0.30. Find the maximum visibility for perfect mutual coherence, estimate \(|g^{(1)}|\) and predict the new visibility after attenuating the brighter beam to match the weaker one.

Normalise by the unequal-arm ceiling

The perfect-coherence ceiling is \(2\sqrt{I_1I_2}/(I_1+I_2)=0.60\). Thus the measured modulus of the normalised mutual coherence is 0.50, assuming matching polarisation and spatial overlap. With balanced arms the ceiling rises to one, so visibility becomes 0.50 if attenuation is uniform and the coherence is preserved. The mean power falls to 0.20 mW. Better contrast and poorer photon statistics can occur together.

Identify a line separation from a mirror scan

A source with two equal, narrow spectral lines has its first contrast minimum 0.30 mm from equal arm lengths on a Michelson mirror stage. Find the separation in hertz, the next stage displacement giving full contrast, and the effect of orthogonal polarisations in the two arms.

Convert motion into delay first

The optical path difference at the first dip is 0.60 mm, or a delay of 2.00 ps. For the doublet \(\Delta\nu=1/(2\tau)=250\) GHz. The first revival occurs at twice the delay and hence 0.60 mm of stage motion from equal paths. With mutually orthogonal arm polarisations their vector fields have zero cross term at a polarisation-insensitive detector. A common output analyser with nonzero projections onto both can recover fringes, provided the original temporal coherence remains.
