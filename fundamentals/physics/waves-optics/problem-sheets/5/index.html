Infer mirror reflectivity

A symmetric, lossless air cavity 30.0 mm long has a transmission peak full width of 100 MHz. Determine the free spectral range and finesse. Estimate \(R\) using the high-finesse formula and predict how doubling cavity length changes width.

Show the cavity inference

The free spectral range is \(c/(2L)=5.00\) GHz and finesse is approximately 50. Writing \(x=\sqrt R\), the approximation \(\mathcal F=\pi x/(1-x^2)\) gives

\[x=[-\pi+\sqrt{\pi^2+4\mathcal F^2}]/(2\mathcal F)=0.969.\]

Thus \(R\simeq0.939\). For unchanged mirrors and negligible dispersion the finesse stays constant. Doubling length halves both free spectral range and linewidth, to 2.50 GHz and 50 MHz.

Select enough grooves

A first-order grating must separate 589.0 and 589.6 nm by the Rayleigh criterion. Determine the minimum illuminated number of grooves and the width at 1200 grooves/mm. Calculate the first-order diffraction angle near the doublet centre.

Show the finite-array calculation

Using 589.3 nm as representative wavelength gives \(N\ge589.3/0.6=982.2\), so illuminate at least 983 grooves. Their span is approximately \(983/1200=0.819\) mm. The period is 833.3 nm. The grating equation gives \(\sin\theta=589.3/833.3=0.7072\), hence \(\theta\simeq45.0^\circ\). Detector sampling, entrance-slit size and unequal line intensities may require a larger illuminated width in practice.

Identify two peaks in one setting

A cavity with 8.0 GHz free spectral range transmits two optical frequencies separated by exactly one order at around 600 nm. Determine their wavelength separation to first order. Find the first-order grating resolving power needed to distinguish them and explain what separate measurements determine.

Show the order ambiguity

From \(\nu=c/\lambda\), \(|\Delta\lambda|\simeq\lambda^2\Delta\nu/c=0.0096\) nm. Their ratio \(\lambda/|\Delta\lambda|=62\,500\) requires about 62,500 uniformly illuminated grooves in first order under the ideal criterion. A narrow cavity scan can measure lines within one order very accurately but maps frequencies apart by the free spectral range to the same phase. A sufficiently resolving independently calibrated grating can distinguish the coincident orders. A second cavity of different spacing can also change their relative alignment.
