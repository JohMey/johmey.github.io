Waves and optics · Notation and assumptions

Read each defining equation with its assumptions and units, then follow the link to the argument that gives the equation its meaning.

Fringe visibility

\[\mathcal V=(I_{\max}-I_{\min})/(I_{\max}+I_{\min})\]

Use maxima and minima from the same scan. Unequal intensities can reduce contrast in a fully coherent field.

Derivation · Coherence and interferometry

Gaussian Rayleigh range

\[z_R=\pi w_0^2/\lambda\]

Here the waist is the one-over-e field radius and the wavelength is in the medium. At one Rayleigh range its squared beam radius doubles.

Derivation · Gaussian beams and focusing

Group velocity

\[v_g=d\omega/dk\]

This is the local slope of the dispersion curve. A sufficiently narrow-band packet with weak distortion can travel with its envelope at this velocity.

Derivation · Wave packets and dispersion

Numerical aperture

\[\mathrm{NA}=n\sin\theta\]

The largest accepted angle controls which transverse spatial frequencies reach the pupil. Resolution also depends on the optical imaging and coherence assumptions.

Derivation · Images through a finite pupil

Optical path length

\[\mathcal L=\int n\,ds\]

Differences of this quantity determine phase delays in geometrical optics. For a pulse in a dispersive medium one must also account for group delay.

Derivation · Optical paths, lenses and images

Wavenumber

\[k=2\pi/\lambda\]

The wavenumber gives phase change per unit distance in a homogeneous medium. Wavelength must be specified in the medium in which propagation is calculated.

Derivation · Travelling waves and energy on a string
