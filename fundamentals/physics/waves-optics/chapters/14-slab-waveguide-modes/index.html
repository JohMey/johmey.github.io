How a dielectric slab chooses its guided modes

A plane interface supports a decaying field outside glass under total reflection. Put two interfaces around a thin core and a new question appears. Which transverse patterns reproduce themselves as the wave propagates along the guide?

Separate longitudinal and transverse variation

Take a uniform slab of half-width \(a\), core index \(n_c\), and equal claddings of lower index \(n_s\). A transverse-electric mode can be written \(E_y=F(x)e^{i(\beta z-\omega t)}\). Substitution into the scalar Helmholtz equation in each homogeneous region leaves \(F''+(n^2k_0^2-\beta^2)F=0\). A guided wave must oscillate in the core and decay outside. Those requirements give the interval \(n_sk_0<\beta<n_ck_0\). Equivalently the effective phase index \(\beta/k_0\) lies between the material indices.

Introduce \(u=a\sqrt{n_c^2 k_0^2-\beta^2}\) and \(w=a\sqrt{\beta^2-n_s^2 k_0^2}\). Adding their squares eliminates the unknown \(\beta\). The possible pairs lie on a circle of radius \(V=a k_0\sqrt{n_c^2-n_s^2}\). Here \(a\) always denotes the half-width. Using the full thickness in \(V\) would move every numerical cutoff by a factor of two.

Enforce continuity at the core boundary

Choose the even fundamental mode with \(F(0)=1\). The core solution is \(\cos(ux/a)\). In the upper cladding write \(F=\cos u\,e^{-w(x-a)/a}\), which has the same value at \(x=a\) and is bounded at infinity. Nonmagnetic media require both \(E_y\) and the tangential magnetic field, proportional to \(dF/dx\), to be continuous. Equating the two derivatives gives the mode equation

\[u\tan u=w=\sqrt{V^2-u^2},\qquad 0<u<\min(V,\pi/2).\]

On this interval the left side increases from zero, and the right side decreases. At the far end either the tangent diverges or the square root reaches zero. There is exactly one root, however thin the symmetric core. For the first odd mode, the core uses \(\sin(ux/a)\), and the derivative condition instead gives

\[-u\cot u=w.\]

Its first branch starts at \(u=\pi/2\). Consequently \(V>\pi/2\) is needed for that mode to become bound.

Resolve the tail with a root solver

At \(V=1\), bisection gives \(u=0.7391\), \(w=0.6736\). Substitution yields the same number on the two sides of the mode equation. The amplitude tail beyond each boundary extends over \(a/w=1.48a\) before dropping by a factor \(e\). At \(V=2\), the fundamental has \(u=1.0299,w=1.7145\), so the decay length falls to \(0.583a\). The first odd mode can now also exist. A guided mode may therefore spread substantially beyond the nominal guide material.

Vary \(V\) while the graph keeps the same half-width units. The display solves the transcendental equation afresh, using its monotonic branch to bracket the root. Compare the apparent width at half the field amplitude with the decay length reported in the readout. The former includes part of the oscillatory core, the latter describes only the tail. A detector measuring local intensity responds to \(|F|^2\), which has twice the logarithmic decay slope.

Tutorial on a fabrication tolerance

At a free-space wavelength of \(1.55\,\mu\mathrm m\), take \(n_c=1.50\) and \(n_s=1.45\). What is the full thickness at the first odd TE cutoff? How would decreasing the wavelength affect the number of modes?

Work through the solution

At cutoff set \(ak_0\sqrt{n_c^2-n_s^2}=\pi/2\). The full thickness is \(2a=\lambda_0/[2\sqrt{n_c^2-n_s^2}]\simeq2.02\,\mu\mathrm m\). At fixed geometry and approximately fixed indices, decreasing the wavelength increases \(V\). Additional oscillatory branches can then intersect the available circle and become guided.

Express the squared effective index in terms of \(w/V\). Then estimate it at \(V=1\), \(n_c=1.50,n_s=1.45\).

Work through the solution

From the definitions \(w^2/V^2=(\beta^2/k_0^2-n_s^2)/(n_c^2-n_s^2)\). With \(w=0.6736\), the normalised squared-index fraction is 0.454. Substitution gives

\[n_{\rm eff}=\sqrt{1.45^2+0.454(1.50^2-1.45^2)}=1.473.\]

This is a phase index obtained with the mode solution.

How does the fundamental cladding tail scale in a very thin symmetric guide?

Work through the solution

At small \(V\), \(u\) is small and \(u\tan u\simeq u^2\), hence \(w\simeq u^2\). The constraint \(u^2+w^2=V^2\) then gives

\[u\simeq V,\]
\[w\simeq V^2.\]

The field decay length in units of the core half-width grows as \(V^{-2}\), so the mode exists mathematically while occupying mostly cladding.
