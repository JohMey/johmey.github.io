Building a dielectric mirror from weakly reflecting interfaces

A single transparent dielectric interface often reflects only a few percent of the optical power. A periodic stack can reflect nearly all incident light over a finite wavelength band. Its response follows from matching both fields at every interface, including every internally reflected wave.

Propagate two continuous tangential fields

At normal incidence use the field vector \((E,HZ_0)\), with free-space impedance \(Z_0\). A forward travelling wave in a nonmagnetic lossless layer of index \(n\) has \(HZ_0=nE\); a backward wave has the opposite sign. With time factor \(e^{+i\omega t}\), forward propagation contributes \(e^{-ikz}\). Resolve the fields at the exit face into these two waves, propagate back by distance \(d\) and recombine. The input fields are the output fields multiplied by

\[M(n,\delta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\delta&i\sin\delta/n\\in\sin\delta&\cos\delta\end{pmatrix},\qquad\delta=2\pi nd/\lambda .\]

Its determinant is one. Use a product \(M_HM_L\) for a high-index layer encountered first, followed by a low-index layer. The tangential fields are continuous at every interface, so no separate interface matrices are required in this field basis. Changing the order can change the reflection phase and the way the stack matches different external media.

Compute a quarter-wave pair without numerical matrices

Choose layer thicknesses with \(n_Hd_H=n_Ld_L=\lambda_0/4\). At the design wavelength both phases are \(\pi/2\). Multiplying the two off-diagonal matrices gives the diagonal pair matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(-n_L/n_H,-n_H/n_L)\). A forward transmitted wave in substrate \(n_s\) has output admittance \(Y_s=HZ_0/E=n_s\). After \(N\) identical pairs the entrance admittance becomes

\[Y_{\rm in}=n_s(n_H/n_L)^{2N}.\]

Matching to the incident plus reflected waves in \(n_0\), \(Y_{\rm in}=n_0(1-r)/(1+r)\), therefore

\[r=\frac{n_0-Y_{\rm in}}{n_0+Y_{\rm in}},\]
\[R=|r|^2 .\]

With incident air, \(n_H=2.0\), \(n_L=n_s=1.50\), and four pairs, \(Y_{\rm in}=14.98\) and \(R=0.765\). Eight pairs give \(Y_{\rm in}\simeq149.7\) and \(R\simeq0.974\). Each extra pair multiplies the admittance at the design point, while the reflectance approaches a ceiling of one. The enhancement depends on fixed phase relations across the layers. Adding interface intensities would double-count some energy and lose the interference.

Use the same equations away from design

At a general wavelength the matrices contain both diagonal and off-diagonal terms. For total entries \(M_{ij}\),

\[Y_{\rm in}=(M_{21}+M_{22}n_s)/(M_{11}+M_{12}n_s).\]

One may instead propagate the substrate admittance backwards through each layer by this fractional linear transformation. This is the recursion used below. It avoids introducing any fictitious absorption. The number of periods mainly controls how close the stop-band reflection comes to unity, whereas the index contrast strongly influences the bandwidth. Material dispersion, roughness and optical absorption are excluded from the model.

Set the design wavelength to the fixed 600 nm reference in the experiment. Change the high index or number of pairs. Compare the on-design readout to the diagonal formula, then inspect the finite-stack ripples away from the central band. The display holds quarter-wave optical thicknesses at the design wavelength as indices change, so it represents redesigned layers.

Tutorial on a coating specification

For the worked indices find the minimum pair count with on-design intensity reflectance above 0.99.

Work through the solution

Require

\[Y_{\rm in}/n_0>(1+\sqrt{0.99})/(1-\sqrt{0.99})=398.0.\]

Thus

\[N>\ln(398.0/1.5)/[2\ln(2/1.5)]=9.70.\]

Ten complete pairs suffice in this ideal lossless calculation.

Every internal interface then has identical fields on both sides with zero reflection. The pair transfer at the design wavelength is \(-I\), which leaves the substrate admittance unchanged for any number of pairs. With a substrate of the same index only the external air-to-material mismatch remains. A chosen phase delay alone does not create contrast without a spatial modulation of index.
