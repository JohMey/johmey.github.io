Fit a weak cubic spring

A mass of 0.20 kg has a natural frequency of 10.00 Hz at negligible amplitude. At 10.0 mm amplitude it oscillates at 10.03 Hz. Assume a restoring force \(-kx-\beta x^3\). Estimate \(k\) and \(\beta\), predict the shift at 5.0 mm and test the perturbation assumption.

Show the stiffness estimates

The linear coefficient is

\[k=m(2\pi f_0)^2=790\,\mathrm{N/m}.\]

Retaining the fundamental of \(\cos^3\omega t\) gives \(\Delta f/f_0\simeq3\beta A^2/(8k)\). Hence \(\beta=8k(0.0030)/(3\times10^{-4})=6.32\times10^4\) N/m³. The positive sign describes hardening. At half amplitude the shift falls by four, to \(0.0075\) Hz. The maximum cubic-to-linear force ratio at 10 mm is \(\beta A^2/k=0.0080\), compatible with a first-order estimate.

Which equilibria can hold a bead?

A bead slides without friction on a vertical hoop of radius 0.400 m rotating about its vertical diameter. Find the critical angular speed and stable positions at 6.00 rad/s. Determine the small oscillation frequency about either tilted position and compare with operation at 4.00 rad/s.

Show the stability calculation

With angle measured from the bottom the effective potential per unit mass is \(-gR\cos\theta-\Omega^2R^2\sin^2\theta/2\). Its derivative is \(R\sin\theta(g-\Omega^2R\cos\theta)\). The critical speed is

\[\sqrt{g/R}=4.95\,\mathrm{rad/s}.\]

At 6.00 rad/s the tilted minima have \(\cos\theta=9.81/(36\times0.4)=0.6813\), hence \(\theta=\pm47.1^\circ\). Curvature divided by \(R^2\) gives \(\omega^2=\Omega^2\sin^2\theta\), or \(\omega=4.39\) rad/s. At 4.00 rad/s only the bottom is stable and \(\omega=\sqrt{g/R-\Omega^2}=2.92\) rad/s.

Tilt a symmetric double well

For dimensionless displacement \(x\), a potential is \(V/E_0=x^4/4-ax^2/2-hx\) with \(a=0.12\). Find equilibria and barrier height at \(h=0\). Determine the magnitude of bias where one metastable well disappears and explain why the transition can depend on sweep history.

Show the barrier and the loss of stability

At zero bias stationary points solve \(x(x^2-a)=0\). Curvature \(3x^2-a\) gives minima at \(\pm\sqrt{a}=\pm0.346\) and a maximum at zero. The energy barrier is

\[a^2E_0/4=0.00360E_0.\]

A minimum and adjacent maximum merge when \(3x^2=a\) and \(h=x^3-ax\). Thus

\[|h|=2(a/3)^{3/2}=0.0160.\]

A weakly damped or cold system initially captured in the unfavoured well can remain there as the bias crosses zero. In a slowly driven, damped experiment without appreciable fluctuations it switches near the disappearance of that minimum; noise can trigger earlier switching.

Reconstruct a binary orbit from a spectrum

Two spectral lines trace a circular binary with a period of 20.0 days. Their radial-velocity semi-amplitudes are 30.0 and 60.0 km/s. Take the orbit to be edge-on. Find the stellar separation, both masses and the orbital energy. How would the mass estimate change at an inclination of 60°? Use G = 6.6743 × 10⁻¹¹ in SI units and one solar mass = 1.9885 × 10³⁰ kg.

Worked solution

The two stars orbit their common centre of mass with the same angular frequency. Their speeds add to give a relative circular speed of 90,000 m/s. Over 1,728,000 s the relative separation vector describes a circle of circumference 2πa, hence a = 2.475 × 10¹⁰ m. The speed ratio gives the inverse mass ratio. The star moving at 30 km/s is twice as massive as the other star.

The relative acceleration is v²/a and the two-body attraction gives G(M₁+M₂)/a². Equating the expressions gives 3.00 × 10³⁰ kg in total, about 1.51 solar masses. The individual masses are approximately 1.01 and 0.50 solar masses. The reduced mass is two ninths of the total. Its kinetic energy is ½μv², and the total orbital energy is its negative, approximately −2.70 × 10³⁹ J.

If the inclination falls to 60°, the observed speeds are projections of the larger orbital speeds. The separation scales as 1/sin i, and the inferred mass as 1/sin³ i. The total rises by a factor of 1.54. Spectroscopy alone fixes the mass ratio and a projected mass. An independent inclination from an eclipse or astrometric orbit breaks the degeneracy.
