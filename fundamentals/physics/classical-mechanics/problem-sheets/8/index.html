Obtain the equations in the frame that moves with the body

Let \(I_1<I_2<I_3\) be distinct principal moments. Express angular momentum in body components. If there is no applied torque, explain why the time derivatives of those three components do not all vanish. Derive the equation for each angular velocity before considering any equilibrium.

Work through the solution

A body basis vector has inertial derivative \(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf e_i\). Differentiating the expansion of angular momentum in that basis gives

\[(d\mathbf L/dt)_{\rm inertial}=(d\mathbf L/dt)_{\rm body}+\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf L.\]

In principal axes the inertia tensor is constant and diagonal, so \(L_i=I_i\omega_i\). Setting the left hand side to zero gives

\[I_1\dot\omega_1=(I_2-I_3)\omega_2\omega_3,\]
\[I_2\dot\omega_2=(I_3-I_1)\omega_3\omega_1,\]
\[I_3\dot\omega_3=(I_1-I_2)\omega_1\omega_2.\]

Multiply the equations by their respective \(\omega_i\) and add. The coefficients sum to zero and the kinetic energy is constant. Dot the vector equation with \(\mathbf L\) and the cross product drops out, proving conservation of its norm. These are two independent checks on a numerical trace.

Identify the instability from two conserved quantities

Set the nominal spin to \(\omega_2=\Omega\). Linearise in the two transverse components and find the characteristic exponents. Then explain the same phenomenon without differentiating a perturbation a second time.

Work through the solution

The equation for \(\dot\omega_2\) contains only the product of the small perturbations. To first order \(\Omega\) stays fixed. Put \(a=(I_3-I_2)\Omega/I_1\),

\[b=(I_2-I_1)\Omega/I_3.\]

The remaining equations are

\[\dot\omega_1=-a\omega_3,\]
\[\dot\omega_3=-b\omega_1.\]

Both \(a\) and \(b\) are positive for positive spin, so \(\ddot\omega_1=ab\omega_1\). The eigenvalues are \(\pm\sqrt{ab}\). Changing the sense of rotation exchanges the directions that grow and decay.

At fixed angular momentum magnitude, \(\mathbf L\) lies on a sphere. Eliminate \(L_2^2\) from the energy using that sphere. The resulting difference from steady intermediate-axis energy is exact.

\[2T-|\mathbf L|^2/I_2=(1/I_1-1/I_2)L_1^2+(1/I_3-1/I_2)L_3^2.\]

One coefficient is positive and the other negative. Energy has a saddle at the intermediate orientation. At the minimum or maximum inertia axis those two coefficients have the same sign. The nearby energy contours close around those two spins. Conservation of energy alone does not select stability until one states which other invariant restricts the motion.

Read a doubling time without knowing the original tilt

Take principal moments \(0.030,0.040,0.050\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\), spin \(12\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\), and \(\omega_1(0)=0.10\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\), \(\omega_3(0)=0\). Estimate when the first component reaches 0.20. Compare with a fit that assumes an exponential from the first frame.

Work through the solution

The rates are \(a=4.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\),

\[b=2.4\,\mathrm{s^{-1}},\]
\[\sigma=\sqrt{9.6}=3.0984\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}.\]

The zero initial slope places equal weight on the increasing and decreasing solutions. Thus \(\omega_1(t)=0.10\cosh(\sigma t)\). It first doubles at \(\operatorname{arcosh}(2)/\sigma=0.425\,\mathrm{s}\). A pure growing mode doubles in \(\ln(2)/\sigma=0.224\,\mathrm{s}\). An initially horizontal tangent is compatible with linear instability.

To excite the growing mode alone, prepare \(\omega_3(0)=-\sigma\omega_1(0)/a\). The relevant marker here reads the body components of angular velocity. Projecting a fixed line of sight onto a body mark introduces a separate kinematic modulation. A vector gyroscope or a reconstruction using multiple marks separates orientation from angular velocity.

Work through the solution

For small initial amplitude the neglected correction to \(\dot\omega_2\) is quadratic. A smaller seed leaves a longer interval before the linear approximation loses accuracy. Doubling the spin doubles the local growth rate. A perfectly aligned spin is an exact solution with an unstable neighbourhood. Numerical or experimental perturbations need a finite time to grow. Once a transverse component approaches the nominal spin, the hyperbolic cosine can grow without bound while the true components are restricted by the angular momentum sphere. That separation diagnoses a depleted range of validity even in an accurate integration.
