The wobble seen from a rotor

A torque-free symmetric rotor has \(I_1=I_2=0.024\) kg m², \(I_3=0.030\) kg m² and axial angular velocity \(100\) rad/s. Find the frequency of the transverse angular-velocity components in body coordinates. For an instantaneous transverse speed of 4 rad/s, find the angle between the symmetry axis and angular momentum and the inertial precession rate.

Show both frame calculations

Euler’s transverse equations give \(\Omega_b=(I_3-I_1)\omega_3/I_1=25\) rad/s or \(3.98\) Hz. Angular momentum has transverse and axial components \(0.096\) and \(3.00\) kg m²/s. Therefore

\[\theta=\arctan(0.096/3)=1.83^\circ.\]

The body axis follows \(\dot{\mathbf e}_3=(\mathbf L/I_1)\times\mathbf e_3\), so its azimuth about fixed angular momentum advances at \(|\mathbf L|/I_1=125.1\) rad/s. A body-mounted sensor and an external camera measure different rotations.

Read damping from the ring-down

A 0.50 kg oscillator completes ten free cycles in 2.00 s. Its peak displacement decreases from 8.0 to 4.0 mm. Infer the viscous damping coefficient and the spring stiffness. Predict the displacement amplitude under a sinusoidal force of 0.080 N at the undamped natural frequency.

Show the ring-down and driven response

Write the envelope as \(A_0e^{-\gamma t}\). Then \(\gamma=\ln2/2=0.3466\) s⁻¹ and \(c=2m\gamma=0.3466\) N s/m. The damped frequency is \(\omega_d=2\pi(10/2)=31.416\) rad/s, so

\[k=m(\omega_d^2+\gamma^2)=493.5\,\mathrm{N/m}.\]

At \(\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}\) the elastic and inertial terms cancel in the complex response. The amplitude is \(F_0/(c\omega_0)=7.35\) mm and the force leads displacement by a quarter cycle.

Distinguish two mechanical faults

A driven instrument has calibrated mass 1.0 kg and static deflection 1.0 mm under a 1.0 N load. During operation the displacement lags the drive by \(45^\circ\) at 5.0 Hz. Infer viscous damping and predict what changes in static deflection and quadrature frequency if damping doubles.

Show the inference and diagnosis

Static response gives \(k=1000\) N/m. On the low-frequency side \(\tan\delta=c\omega/(k-m\omega^2)\). At \(31.416\) rad/s the elastic excess is \(13.04\) N/m, giving \(c=0.415\) N s/m. At exact quadrature \(k=m\omega^2\), so \(f=5.033\) Hz for both damping values. Static deflection is also unchanged, since the damper exerts no force at rest. Doubling damping halves the displacement at quadrature and widens the response. A stiffness change would instead shift both independent calibration observables.

Test whether a steady precession exists

At tilt \(60^\circ\) a top has \(m=0.300\) kg, \(\ell=0.0400\) m, \(I_1=0.00200\) kg m² and \(I_3=0.00100\) kg m². Find the minimum axial spin for a tilted steady precession. At axial spin 60.0 rad/s estimate the slow rate and compare it with the full solution.

Inspect the discriminant and select the slow root

Real roots require \(I_3^2\omega_3^2\ge4I_1mg\ell\cos\theta\). With \(mg\ell=0.11772\) N m the threshold is \(21.7\) rad/s. Rapid-spin balance gives

\[0.11772/0.0600=1.96\,\mathrm{rad/s}.\]

The lower root of \(0.00100\Omega^2-0.0600\Omega+0.11772=0\) is \(2.03\) rad/s. Below threshold the equations exclude steady rotation at the stipulated tilt. They still allow motion with changing tilt.
