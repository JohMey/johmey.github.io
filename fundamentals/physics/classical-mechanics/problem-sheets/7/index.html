Reconstructing an accelerating trap

An optical stage can produce either a harmonic potential or a triangular one. Use a harmonic trap to calibrate the stage acceleration, then predict the motion in a different trap whose orbit area can be integrated analytically. Record each prediction before changing a control.

Calibrate the acceleration from the trace

A \(0.50\) kg test carriage in a spring of stiffness \(8.0\,\mathrm{N/m}\) starts at rest relative to the support. After a sudden change to constant support acceleration, its first relative turning point lies at \(-0.20\,\mathrm m\) at \(0.785\,\mathrm s\). Infer the acceleration using the signed displacement. Use the time independently to check the assumed stiffness.

Work through the solution

In the support frame the equilibrium is \(Q_*=-ma/k\), and a carriage starting at the old centre reaches \(2Q_*\) at its first turning point. Thus \(Q_*=-0.10\,\mathrm m\) and \(a=1.60\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\). The half-period is

\[\pi\sqrt{m/k}=0.7854\,\mathrm s.\]

The time tests \(k/m\) without requiring the displacement calibration.

Check an independent calibration with a slow change of slope

Return the support to inertial motion and fit a \(0.25\) kg test mass in \(U=F|Q|\). Begin at \(F=2.0\) N, amplitude \(0.080\) m. Increase the slope slowly by a factor of eight. Predict the final energy, turning amplitude and period. Which combination of measured turning amplitude and slope would test preservation of orbit area?

Work through the solution

The orbit action is proportional to \(E^{3/2}/F\) at fixed mass. Hence \(E_f=0.160\times8^{2/3}=0.640\,\mathrm J\),

\[A_f=E_f/16=0.040\,\mathrm m,\]
\[T_f=4\sqrt{2mA_f/F_f}=0.1414\,\mathrm s.\]

Substitution of \(E=FA\) gives \(J^2\propto FA^3\), so the measured product \(FA^3\) should stay fixed in the slow limit. Check this product as well as the period.

Compare the two measured phase-space areas

Combine the two experiments

Restore the initial triangular slope. Ramp the calibrated stage acceleration \(a=1.60\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\) from zero over many periods, smoothly at each endpoint. Derive the new right and left turning distances relative to the support. Predict whether the orbit area changes at leading order.

Work through the solution

The inertial contribution to potential energy is \(maQ\). The two positive slope magnitudes are \(F+ma\) on the right and \(F-ma\) on the left. Both must remain positive for confinement. Integrating each half of the triangular orbit separately gives

\[J\propto E^{3/2}[1/(F+ma)+1/(F-ma)].\]

Set this equal to the original value \(2E_0^{3/2}/F\). Writing \(s=ma/F=0.20\) gives

\[E=E_0(1-s^2)^{2/3}=0.1557\,\mathrm J.\]

The right distance is \(E/2.4=0.0649\,\mathrm m\), and the left distance \(E/1.6=0.0973\,\mathrm m\). The orbit becomes asymmetric while its area is conserved to leading adiabatic order.

Design a falsification test

If both turning positions have a common, unknown camera magnification, which quantity still tests the calibrated acceleration? What limit makes the assumption of a slowly adjusted bound orbit fail?

Work through the solution

Divide the left distance by the right. The common energy and camera scale cancel, leaving \((F+ma)/(F-ma)=1.50\). As \(|ma|\) approaches \(F\), one confining slope approaches zero and the period grows. A ramp slow at zero acceleration may then fail the separation-of-time-scales condition. At \(|ma|\ge F\) the potential no longer confines both sides.
