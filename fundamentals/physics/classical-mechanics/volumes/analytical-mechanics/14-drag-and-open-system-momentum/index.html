Motion in a medium and momentum carried across a boundary

Two beads can approach exactly the same terminal speed with recognisably different histories. A sand cart can slow down while the total horizontal momentum of sand and cart stays fixed. Both observations begin by specifying which material the equation of motion follows.

Infer the force law from a falling bead

Choose downward displacement as positive. A submerged bead of volume \(V\), density \(\rho_b\) and mass \(m\) displaces fluid of density \(\rho_f\). In a fluid initially at rest the static pressure gradient supplies buoyancy \(\rho_f Vg\). Combine the two constant forces into \(m g_e=(\rho_b-\rho_f)Vg\). Assume a heavy bead, negligible wall effects and sufficiently slow changes to neglect added mass and viscous memory. A drag law must reverse sign when the velocity changes sign.

\[m\dot v=mg_e-bv,\]
\[m\dot v=mg_e-c|v|v.\]

In the first model \(b\) has units kg/s, in the second \(c\) has units kg/m. Equating drag to the effective weight gives \(v_\infty=mg_e/b\) or \(v_\infty=\sqrt{mg_e/c}\). Near zero speed both models give initial acceleration \(g_e\). Equal initial slope and equal terminal speed therefore leave the force law undetermined.

Solve both initial-value problems

Choose the characteristic time \(\tau=v_\infty/g_e\) and write \(u=v/v_\infty\), \(s=t/\tau\). Starting from rest the positive-speed branches obey \(du/ds=1-u\) and \(du/ds=1-u^2\). Separation and integration from zero give \(-\ln(1-u)=s\) and \(\operatorname{artanh}u=s\). A second integration supplies displacements that a camera can measure directly.

\[u_{\rm lin}=1-e^{-s},\]
\[z_{\rm lin}=v_\infty\tau(s-1+e^{-s}).\]
\[u_{\rm quad}=\tanh s,\]
\[z_{\rm quad}=v_\infty\tau\ln(\cosh s).\]

To start a data analysis, fit the early displacement to \(g_e t^2/2\) and determine the late speed independently. After this scaling, each curve is a prediction with no free fitting amplitude. With \(g_e=9.81\) m/s² and \(v_\infty=3.0\) m/s the time scale is 0.306 s. The competing predictions already differ by about 0.39 m/s at 0.30 s.

Recall · Which drag law has a mechanical basis here?

For a rigid sphere of radius \(a\) moving slowly in a Newtonian fluid the creeping-flow result is \(b=6\pi\eta a\). Check \(\mathrm{Re}=2a\rho_f|v|/\eta\) throughout the run. When inertia matters a pressure-drag model uses \(c=\rho_f C_D A/2\) with frontal area \(A\). Treating \(C_D\) as constant requires a range in which it varies slowly.

Reproduce with OneSimulation

Run the downloaded notebook with OneSimulation, NumPy and Matplotlib installed. The output below was recorded with the displayed calculation.

"""Drop a bead from rest in two media with equal terminal speeds."""
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from onesim.numerics.time import ClassicalRungeKutta4TimeIntegrator

g, terminal = 9.81, 3.0  # effective downward acceleration and terminal speed
step = 0.01
time = np.arange(151) * step
method = ClassicalRungeKutta4TimeIntegrator()

def rates(speed, t):
    linear, quadratic = speed
    return (g * (1 - linear / terminal),
            g * (1 - quadratic * abs(quadratic) / terminal**2))

speeds = [(0.0, 0.0)]
for t in time[:-1]:
    speeds.append(method.advance(speeds[-1], rates, step, time=t))
linear, quadratic = np.array(speeds).T
exact = terminal * np.tanh(g * time / terminal)
print(f"At 0.30 s: linear {linear[30]:.4f} m/s, quadratic {quadratic[30]:.4f} m/s")
print(f"Largest RK4 discrepancy for quadratic drag: {max(abs(exact-quadratic)):.2e} m/s")
plt.plot(time, linear, label="Linear drag")
plt.plot(time, quadratic, label="Quadratic drag")
plt.axhline(terminal, linestyle=":", color="grey", label="Terminal speed")
plt.xlabel("Time after release (s)")
plt.ylabel("Downward speed (m/s)")
plt.legend()
plt.tight_layout()
plt.savefig("physics-drag-laws.png", dpi=160)

Derive the equation for a changing collection of matter

Let a horizontal cart of instantaneous mass \(M\) and speed \(v\) collect grains at rate \(\dot M>0\). Just before collection a small parcel \(dM\) has horizontal laboratory velocity \(w\). Over \(dt\) the momentum of cart plus parcel changes by the external impulse. The incoming parcel is included on the initial side so that the comparison follows the same matter.

\[Mv+w\,dM+F_{\rm ext}\,dt=(M+dM)(v+dv).\]

Drop the product \(dM\,dv\), divide by time and keep the finite acceleration. The relative speed determines how strongly the intake changes the cart velocity.

\[M\dot v=F_{\rm ext}+(w-v)\dot M,\]
\[\frac{d(Mv)}{dt}=F_{\rm ext}+w\dot M.\]

Take a 20 kg cart rolling at 3 m/s on a level frictionless track. Grains falling vertically carry zero horizontal momentum. After collecting 10 kg, the common speed is 2 m/s. The horizontal kinetic energy falls from 90 J to 60 J. The collisions can dissipate energy even though the horizontal external impulse vanishes. The vertical momentum and gravitational energy belong to a separate balance.

Recall · Moving boundaries and signs

The formula uses laboratory-frame \(w\) immediately outside the cart and signed mass rate into the cart. For outflow use the velocity of matter as it leaves. A leaking cart with material departing at the cart's horizontal speed has no resulting horizontal acceleration.

Tutorial on identifying a mechanical system

1 · When do the two beads reach 90% of the terminal speed?

Work through the solution

The dimensionless times are \(\ln 10=2.303\) and \(\operatorname{artanh}(0.9)=1.472\). At the stated parameters these are 0.704 s and 0.450 s. An early-velocity measurement distinguishes the laws more efficiently than another terminal-speed estimate.

2 · Infer an intake speed from a measured acceleration

Work through the solution

A 12 kg cart moving at 1.5 m/s gains 0.40 kg/s and accelerates at 0.020 m/s² along a level frictionless track. From \(w=v+M\dot v/\dot M\) the grains enter at 2.10 m/s. Using \(F=d(Mv)/dt\) without an inlet flux would falsely infer an external force.

3 · Test a radius scaling before fitting a drag coefficient

Work through the solution

For spheres of the same density, effective weight scales as \(a^3\). Stokes drag then predicts \(v_\infty\propto a^2\). At fixed \(C_D\), quadratic drag gives \(v_\infty\propto a^{1/2}\). Doubling radius gives speed ratios 4 and \(\sqrt2\). Check Reynolds numbers, wall clearances and bead density before inferring a force law.
