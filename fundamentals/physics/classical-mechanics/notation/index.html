Classical mechanics · Notation and assumptions

Read each defining equation with its assumptions and units, then follow the link to the argument that gives the equation its meaning.

Action

\[S=\int_{t_1}^{t_2} L(q,\dot q,t)\,dt\]

Compare nearby admissible paths with the specified endpoint data. Stationarity gives a local equation of motion. In SI the action has units of joule seconds.

Derivation · Constraints and Lagrange’s equations

Action variable

\[J=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint p\,dq\]

This area measure is defined for a closed cycle. It can change strongly near a separatrix even under a slow parameter variation.

Derivation · Read a period from the area enclosed by an orbit

Differential cross-section

\[d\sigma/d\Omega=(b/\sin\theta)|db/d\theta|\]

For an axially symmetric single-valued scattering branch this converts incoming area to outgoing solid angle. Sum contributions if more than one impact parameter gives the same angle.

Derivation · Impact parameters and scattering cross-sections

Generalised momentum

\[p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i\]

Its units depend on the coordinate. Conjugacy to an angle gives an angular momentum. Conservation requires the associated coordinate to be cyclic in the chosen Lagrangian.

Derivation · Hamiltonian motion in phase space

Normal frequency

\[\det(K-\omega^2 M)=0\]

Small oscillations around a force-balanced configuration give this generalised eigenproblem. The ordinary frequency in hertz is the angular frequency divided by two pi.

Derivation · Normal modes and energy exchange

Reduced mass

\[\mu=m_1m_2/(m_1+m_2)\]

The relative coordinate of an isolated two-body system carries this inertia. The centre-of-mass motion remains a separate degree of freedom.
