Read a period from the area enclosed by an orbit

A track with a V-shaped potential has straight force segments but a strongly amplitude-dependent period. Its area in phase space gives a useful way to predict both the period and a slow change of the track.

Construct the closed area

For one bound degree of freedom at energy \(E\), position runs between two turning points. In a mechanical Hamiltonian \(p^2/(2m)+U(q)\), the upper branch of the phase portrait has positive momentum and the lower branch has its negative. The directed integral around one full orbit counts the enclosed area. Define the action \(J\) using the area divided by \(2\pi\). This choice makes its conjugate phase an angle measured in radians:

\[J(E)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint p\,dq=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{q_-}^{q_+}\sqrt{2m(E-U(q))}\,dq .\]

Now change the energy slightly. The integrand vanishes at each regular endpoint, so the moving-endpoint contributions to the derivative vanish. In the interior \(\partial p/\partial E=m/p=1/v\). The derivative of the upper-branch area therefore measures the time to reach the opposite turning point.

\[\frac{dJ}{dE}=\frac{T(E)}{2\pi},\]
\[\omega(E)=\frac{dE}{dJ}.\]

This argument also explains why the construction becomes delicate near a separatrix. A trajectory can spend an arbitrarily long time near an unstable equilibrium, and \(T\) can diverge even though the enclosed area stays finite.

\[J=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint p\,dq\]

This area measure is defined for a closed cycle. It can change strongly near a separatrix even under a slow parameter variation.

Carry out the integral for a constant restoring force

Use \(U(q)=F|q|\) with \(F>0\). The turning amplitude is \(A=E/F\). Integrate the positive branch from \(0\) to \(A\) and use reflection symmetry. Substitution \(s=E-Fq\) gives

\[J=\frac{4\sqrt{2m}}{3\pi F}E^{3/2},\]
\[T=\frac{4\sqrt{2mE}}{F}=4\sqrt{\frac{2mA}{F}} .\]

The answer has an independent mechanical check. From a turning point the bead accelerates uniformly with magnitude \(F/m\) until the centre. Covering a distance \(A\) from rest takes \(\sqrt{2mA/F}\), a quarter period. Doubling the amplitude increases the full period by \(\sqrt{2}\). The abrupt change of force direction at the centre leaves the velocity continuous.

Slowly steepen the track

A slowly adjusted parameter can preserve the orbit action to leading order when the motion remains periodic and many cycles fit inside the adjustment time. For this trap \(m\) stays constant, so constant \(J\) requires \(E^{3/2}/F\) to stay fixed. Therefore \(E\propto F^{2/3}\),

\[A=E/F\propto F^{-1/3},\]
\[T\propto F^{-2/3}.\]

Work done by adjusting the slope changes the energy despite the approximate conservation of action.

Start with \(m=0.25\) kg, \(F=2.0\) N and \(A=0.080\) m. The initial energy is \(0.160\) J and the period \(0.566\) s. A slow eightfold increase of \(F\) makes the energy four times larger, \(0.640\) J, and the amplitude half as large, \(0.040\) m. The period becomes \(0.141\) s. In contrast, switching slopes instantaneously at the centre leaves the kinetic energy and position unchanged at that instant. The same final slope would then give amplitude \(0.010\) m. The route by which a parameter changes matters.

Tutorial on inferring a change

After a slow adjustment the period is 64% of its original value. Find the force and amplitude ratios.

Work through the solution

From \(T_f/T_i=(F_f/F_i)^{-2/3}\), the force ratio is

\[0.64^{-3/2}=1.953125.\]

The amplitude ratio is its inverse cube root, \(0.800\). Squaring the period formula with these values provides an independent check.

Derive a relation between the area action and the product of energy and period in the V trap. What fraction of the total energy is kinetic on a time average over one orbit?

Work through the solution

The explicit formulas give \(J=ET/(3\pi)\), a quantity with units of action. Along the orbit \(p\,dq=p\dot q\,dt=2K\,dt\). Hence \(2\pi J=2T\langle K\rangle\) and \(\langle K\rangle=E/3\), leaving \(2E/3\) in potential energy. This is an independent check using a measurable time average.

A trap is weakened gradually until the amplitude doubles. Find the predicted changes in energy and period, and state when the slow-ramp argument is most vulnerable.

Work through the solution

The amplitude varies as \(F^{-1/3}\), so doubling it requires reducing \(F\) to one eighth. The energy then falls to one quarter and the period increases fourfold. A ramp duration adequate initially can become too short compared to the final period. Checking the endpoint period alone is insufficient if the control has sharp changes during the ramp.
