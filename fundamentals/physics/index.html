Fundamentals

Physics

How do a few physical laws predict an orbit, a light pattern or the energy levels of a confined particle?

CoursesCurriculum references

Courses

  1. Classical mechanics

    Use momentum and energy to derive constrained motion, solve two-body and rigid-body problems, and explain how forced and nonlinear oscillations differ.

    Problem sheetsOneSimulation lab

  2. Electrodynamics

    Calculate fields from sources and boundaries, then use Maxwell’s equations to explain radiation, field forces and the propagation of pulses in a guide or cable.

    Problem sheetsOneSimulation lab

  3. Waves and optics

    Follow a wave from its source through interfaces and apertures, then use interference and diffraction to explain what an image or a spectrum can resolve.

    Problem sheetsOneSimulation lab

  4. Thermodynamics and statistical physics

    Trace energy and entropy through processes, derive thermodynamic properties from microscopic states, and predict diffusion, heat capacity and equilibration.

    Problem sheetsOneSimulation lab

  5. Quantum mechanics

    Predict measurements from quantum states, approximate confined energies, and determine how driving, exchange and loss of phase change observable spectra.

    Problem sheetsOneSimulation lab

  6. Condensed matter and band theory

    Read a lattice from diffraction, derive its vibration and electronic bands, and use electronic transport, magnetic response and spectroscopy to identify the state of a solid.

    Problem sheetsOneSimulation lab

Curriculum references

The physics curricula at KIT, TUM and Heidelberg introduce mechanics, electricity, waves and thermal physics alongside mathematical methods, before developing quantum theory. The lectures develop the foundations of each field through derivations, worked problems and applications.
