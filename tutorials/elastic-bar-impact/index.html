An elastic bar struck at one end: one model, every method

One physics declaration, an axial elastic bar, is solved as a static bar under end traction and body force, as a travelling and reflected stress wave after an impact, and as a vibrating rod. The bar runs through both of its formulations, the second-order displacement equation and the first-order velocity-stress system, with every spatial scheme, Riemann solver, time integrator (including the structural Newmark, HHT-alpha and generalized-alpha methods), nonlinear and linear solver that applies. Each run is graded against the closed-form solution. 471 runs on the compiled OneSimulation runtime: 419 ran as declared, 52 failed as the mathematics predicts and are shown hatched with their stabilized counterpart where one exists, none was refused by the library.

A straight, homogeneous bar carries axial motion only (uniaxial stress). Its displacement \(u\), velocity \(v=\partial_t u\) and stress \(\sigma=E\,\partial_x u\) obey

\[\rho\,\partial_t v=\partial_x\sigma+f,\qquad \partial_t\sigma=E\,\partial_x v,\qquad \partial_t u=v,\]

or, eliminating \(v\) and \(\sigma\), the wave equation \(\rho\,\partial_t^2u=\partial_x(E\,\partial_xu)+f\) with the bar wave speed \(c=\sqrt{E/\rho}\). All quantities are scaled with the bar length \(L\), the density \(\rho\) and the wave speed \(c\): \(E=\rho=c=1\).

The bar is declared once with ElasticBar. Everything numerical is an argument of Numerics, and that choice also picks the formulation:

  • a continuous Lagrange element without an interface flux solves the second-order displacement equation: stationary, or in time with the structural Newmark, HHT-α and generalized-α integrators or with any generic time method through the first-order reduction \((u,v)\);
  • every scheme with an interface flux (finite volumes, finite differences, discontinuous Galerkin, continuous Galerkin with a Riemann flux) solves the velocity-stress system \(\partial_t q+\partial_x F(q)=S\) with \(q=(v,\sigma,u)\), \(F=(-\sigma/\rho,\,-Ev,\,0)\) and \(S=(f/\rho,\,0,\,v)\), whose waves travel at \(-c\), \(0\) and \(+c\).

Three cases.

1. Static bar, \(L=2\), clamped at \(x=0\), end traction \(\sigma_0=1\) at \(x=2\) and the body force \(f=1/2\): \(\sigma(x)=\sigma_0+f(2-x)\) and \(u(x)=\big(\sigma_0x+f\,x(2-x/2)\big)/E\). 2. Impact, \(L=1\): the bar is at rest; from \(t=0\) the end \(x=0\) moves with \(v_0=1\), the end \(x=1\) is free. The stress front \(\sigma=-\rho c v_0\) runs to the free end, reflects with the opposite sign and doubles the velocity behind it. At \(t=1.5\) (d'Alembert): \(\sigma=-1,\ v=1,\ u=1.5-x\) for \(x<0.5\) and \(\sigma=0,\ v=2,\ u=1\) for \(x>0.5\). 3. Vibrating rod, \(L=1\), both ends clamped, released from \(u_0=\sin\pi x+\tfrac12\sin 5\pi x\): \(u=\sin\pi x\cos\omega_1t+\tfrac12\sin5\pi x\cos\omega_5t\) with \(\omega_n=n\pi c/L\).

Grading. The impact runs are evaluated at the 400 cell centres \(x_i\) of the finest mesh: \(L_1(q)=\frac1N\sum_i|q_h(x_i)-q(x_i)|\) for stress, velocity and displacement; the wall time covers validation, binding and time stepping.

The physics, declared once

Nothing in this cell names a numerical method. The material is a generic isotropic solid in scaled units; the bar uses its Young's modulus and density.

cells, coarse_cells, end_time, static_cells, rod_cells = 400, 200, 1.5, 100, 100
material = IsotropicSolid(
    density=1.0,
    specific_heat_capacity=1.0,
    thermal_conductivity=1.0,
    youngs_modulus=1.0,
    poisson_ratio=0.3,
    thermal_expansion_coefficient=0.0,
)
rod = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(1.0, 0.0, 0.0))
bar = ElasticBar(domain=rod.line, material=material)
impact = Problem(
    name="bar impact",
    geometry=rod,
    physics=(bar,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        BarEndVelocity(boundary=rod.boundaries.start, physics=bar, value=1.0),
        BarEndTraction(boundary=rod.boundaries.end, physics=bar, value=0.0),
    ),
    initial_conditions=tuple(
        InitialValue(field, 0.0) for field in (bar.velocity, bar.stress, bar.displacement)
    ),
)
long_rod = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(2.0, 0.0, 0.0))
loaded = ElasticBar(domain=long_rod.line, material=material, body_force=0.5)
static = Problem(
    name="bar under end traction and body force",
    geometry=long_rod,
    physics=(loaded,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        BarFixedEnd(boundary=long_rod.boundaries.start, physics=loaded),
        BarEndTraction(boundary=long_rod.boundaries.end, physics=loaded, value=1.0),
    ),
)
vibrating = ElasticBar(domain=rod.line, material=material)
vibration = Problem(
    name="clamped rod released from two modes",
    geometry=rod,
    physics=(vibrating,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        BarFixedEnd(boundary=rod.boundaries.all, physics=vibrating),
    ),
    initial_conditions=(
        InitialValue(
            vibrating.displacement,
            lambda x: np.sin(np.pi * x) + 0.5 * np.sin(5 * np.pi * x),
        ),
        InitialValue(vibrating.velocity, 0.0),
        InitialValue(vibrating.stress, lambda x: np.pi * np.cos(np.pi * x) + 2.5 * np.pi * np.cos(5 * np.pi * x)),
    ),
)
meshes = {n: StructuredLineMesher(cells=n).generate(rod) for n in (cells, coarse_cells)}
static_mesh = StructuredLineMesher(cells=static_cells).generate(long_rod)
rod_mesh = StructuredLineMesher(cells=rod_cells).generate(rod)
centres = {n: mesh.cell_centers[:, 0] for n, mesh in meshes.items()}
print(
    f"impact: {meshes[cells].cell_count} cells of width {1 / cells:.4f} "
    f"(mesh check: {meshes[coarse_cells].cell_count} cells); static bar: "
    f"{static_mesh.cell_count} elements on L = 2; vibrating rod: {rod_mesh.cell_count} elements; "
    f"wave speed c = {bar.wave_speed:.3f}, impedance rho*c = {bar.impedance:.3f}"
)

The exact solutions

Written out with NumPy: the static parabola, the d'Alembert solution of the impact at \(t=1.5\) (incident front plus its reflection from the free end) and the two standing modes.

The static bar: element degree × linear solver

The stationary displacement equation \(K u=F\) with the clamped end eliminated symmetrically, so the stiffness matrix stays symmetric positive definite and conjugate gradients apply. Quadratic and cubic elements contain the exact parabola; linear elements are exact at the nodes.

Mesh check for the static bar: linear elements converge in the stress at first order (the projected stress of a linear element is piecewise constant before projection); quadratic and cubic elements reproduce the exact solution on both meshes up to round-off.

The method catalogue for the impact

The velocity-stress system is linear, so every Riemann solver is exact on it once the signal speeds are \(\pm c\): Rusanov and HLL coincide with the Godunov flux for velocity and stress; HLLC additionally restores the stationary middle wave across which only the displacement jumps, so it carries no numerical diffusion on \(u\). The catalogue is the one of the Sod notebook: slope limiters, ENO/WENO reconstructions, discontinuous elements of degree 0 to 4 with and without limiting or jump penalty, continuous elements with a Riemann flux and graph viscosity, interior penalty, SUPG, GLS or no stabilization, and finite differences including summation-by-parts. The DG jump penalty \(\gamma/h\,[u][v]\) uses \(\gamma=c\,\Delta x/2\), the scale of one upwind flux. The graph viscosity takes ElasticBarInvariantDomain: every finite state of a linear elastic bar is admissible.

The second-order displacement formulation: continuous elements without an interface flux.

Time integrators: the generic catalogue of the Sod notebook plus the structural integrators for second-order systems. Newmark() is the average-acceleration (trapezoidal) rule, Newmark(beta=1/6) the linear-acceleration rule (conditionally stable), Newmark(beta=0) the explicit central difference and Newmark(beta=0.3025, gamma=0.6) a numerically damped variant; the kick-drift methods (symplectic Euler, Stoermer-Verlet) advance the displacement with the new velocity and need the same position/velocity pair; HHT-α and generalized-α damp the highest modes with second-order accuracy, controlled by α and by the spectral radius at infinity.

Schemes that must fail, and their stabilized counterparts

The undamped displacement formulation has a purely imaginary spectrum \(\pm i\omega_k\). An explicit method whose stability region contains no piece of the imaginary axis (forward Euler, two-stage Runge-Kutta, Adams-Bashforth 2) amplifies every mode at every step, and the library refuses it before stepping; BDF3 to BDF6 and Adams-Moulton 3 lose stability on the part of the imaginary axis reached by the highest element modes at \(\Delta t=5\cdot10^{-3}\). Their stabilized counterpart adds exactly one ingredient: a volume viscosity \(\rho\nu\,\partial_x^2 v\) with \(\nu=c\,\Delta x/2\) (VolumeCIP on the displacement element), which moves the spectrum into the left half-plane. Adams-Moulton 4 has a bounded stability region and runs instead at the step \(\Delta t=0.1\,\Delta x/c\) it is stable for. On the velocity-stress system the Riemann flux already dissipates; there leapfrog (no damping at all) is unstable and takes the Robert-Asselin-Williams filter as in the Sod notebook. Fixed-point stage iterations contract only below the inverse spectral radius and run at a smaller step.

The test matrix

1. Space × Riemann solver, SSP-RK3 at the stable step (safety factor 0.5) for the velocity-stress schemes; the displacement elements run with classical RK4 at their spectral step in the extra column "second-order form". 2. Time integrator × space: every integrator on first-order and MUSCL-minmod volumes with HLLC and on linear and quadratic displacement elements. Explicit methods at their stable step, implicit generic and structural methods at \(\Delta t=5\cdot10^{-3}\) (two cells per step). 3. Nonlinear × linear solver: backward Euler on MUSCL-minmod-HLLC, \(\Delta t=5\cdot10^{-3}\). 4. Structural integrator × linear solver: the average-acceleration Newmark rule on quadratic displacement elements; its effective matrix \(M+\beta\Delta t^2K\) is symmetric positive definite. 5. Vibrating rod: element degree × structural integrator, \(\Delta t=10^{-2}\), two periods of the fundamental mode.

Representative combinations are repeated on 200 cells for the mesh check.

Run everything

One loop, the same problem object every time. A combination the library refuses is recorded with its validation message; a run whose method is unstable for the operator, or whose stage iteration does not converge, is recorded as failed.

Space × Riemann solver

The stress error of every spatial scheme with every Riemann solver. Linear schemes of higher than first order (unlimited MUSCL and QUICK, unlimited DG, Galerkin CG, central and SBP finite differences) overshoot at the two fronts, as Godunov's theorem demands, but a linear elastic state has no positivity limit, so they run; the overshoot shows in the error and in the profiles below.

Time integrator × space

A dash marks a combination outside this cut: the structural and kick-drift integrators need a second-order (displacement, velocity) system and do not apply to the first-order velocity-stress volumes.

Nonlinear × linear solver

Backward Euler on MUSCL-minmod-HLLC. The discrete solution does not depend on the solver as long as each one converges; the wall time does.

Structural integrator × linear solver

The Newmark effective matrix on quadratic displacement elements, solved by direct, Krylov and multigrid solvers. Conjugate gradients apply because the matrix is symmetric positive definite.

The vibrating rod: element degree × structural integrator

Two clamped-end modes, \(n=1\) and \(n=5\), released from rest and followed for two periods of the fundamental mode with \(\Delta t=10^{-2}\) (\(\omega_5\Delta t\approx0.16\)). The trapezoidal rule and generalized-α with \(\rho_\infty=1\) conserve the modal energy; HHT-α and generalized-α with \(\rho_\infty<1\) damp the fifth mode slightly; the central difference runs at its stability limit \(\Delta t\le2/\omega_{max}\) of the discrete operator.

Mesh check: 400 against 200 cells

The representative combinations on half as many cells. For the discontinuous fronts the \(L_1\) error falls roughly like \(\Delta x^{1/2}\) (first order) to \(\Delta x^{2/3}\) (second order).

What the library refused, and what failed

A refused combination is caught by validation or binding before any time step; a failed one started, or was refused as unstable for this operator, and has its stabilized counterpart above.

Methods that do not apply to the elastic bar

  • The structural and kick-drift integrators (Newmark, HHT-α, generalized-α, symplectic Euler, Stoermer-Verlet) advance a second-order system \(M\ddot u+C\dot u+Ku=F\); the velocity-stress volumes, differences and DG elements discretize a first-order system and have no mass-stiffness pair, so the library refuses the combination with that reason.
  • The static bar is posed in the displacement; the velocity-stress system does not contain the displacement as an independent unknown at rest (\(v\) is constant and \(\sigma\) follows from one traction condition), so the stationary cut uses the displacement elements only. The volume viscosity acts on the velocity and is not consumed by a stationary solve.
  • Conjugate gradients need a symmetric positive definite matrix and preconditioner: the stage matrix of a generic implicit method on the first-order system is not symmetric, so CG appears only for the static stiffness and the Newmark effective matrix, and there without the (nonsymmetric) ILU and two-level AMG preconditioners, which run with GMRES instead.
  • Positivity limiters and hydrostatic reconstruction protect a positive density or depth that the elastic bar does not have; Scharfetter-Gummel fluxes are for drift-diffusion; Runge-Kutta-Chebyshev and -Legendre super-steps are for parabolic operators; IMEX splits need a stiff and a non-stiff part.

Going further

Swap the free end for BarFixedEnd to reflect the front with the same sign, give the bar a damping rate, or prescribe a time-dependent end velocity value=lambda t: ...; the matrix code stays as it is.
