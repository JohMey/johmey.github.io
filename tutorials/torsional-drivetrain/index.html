A torsional drivetrain: one model, every time integrator

A chain of eight flywheels coupled by elastic shafts is declared once and advanced with every applicable time integrator, including the staggered (velocity) Verlet and symplectic Euler steps for second-order mechanics, for an undamped, a damped and a stiff variant. Each run is graded against the exact modal solution. A large-amplitude pendulum is graded against its elliptic-integral period, and its implicit steps are solved with every pair of nonlinear and linear solvers. 290 runs on the compiled OneSimulation runtime: 263 ran as declared, 27 failed as the mathematics predicts and are shown hatched (23 with a stabilized counterpart behind the hatching), none was refused by the library.

Eight flywheels with moments of inertia \(J_i\) sit on a shaft line; neighbouring wheels are coupled by shafts of torsional stiffness \(k_i\) and damping \(c_i\). With the twist \(e_i=\theta_i-\theta_{i+1}\) the shaft torque is \(\tau_i=k_i e_i+c_i\dot e_i\) and every wheel obeys

\[\dot\theta_i=\omega_i,\qquad J_i\dot\omega_i=\tau_{i-1}-\tau_i .\]

In matrix form \(M\ddot\theta+C\dot\theta+K\theta=0\) with \(K=D^\top\operatorname{diag}(k)D\), \(C=D^\top\operatorname{diag}(c)D\) and the difference matrix \(D\). The chain is free at both ends, so it has one rigid-body mode and seven elastic modes.

Reference. The system is linear, so the exact solution is its modal expansion: with the first-order matrix \(A=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-M^{-1}K&-M^{-1}C\end{pmatrix}\) and its eigen decomposition \(A=V\Lambda V^{-1}\), the state is \(x(t)=V e^{\Lambda t}V^{-1}x_0\) (e.g. Meirovitch, Fundamentals of Vibrations, ch. 7). The eigenvalues are computed with NumPy below.

Grading. The angle of the last wheel is compared with the modal solution at every accepted step; the error is the largest deviation over the run divided by the largest exact angle. The wall time covers validation, binding and time stepping.

Resolution. A lumped model has no spatial mesh: LumpedMesh carries all unknowns on one global node, here 16 state values (8 angles, 8 angular velocities). The resolution is the time step; a step check at half the step closes the notebook.

The physics, declared once

Eight equal wheels of \(J=0.02\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\) on shafts of \(k=4000\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}\). Three variants of the same declaration form the columns of the matrix:

  • undamped — no shaft damping;
  • damped — stiffness-proportional shaft damping \(c_i=\beta k_i\) with 2 % damping in the lowest elastic mode;
  • stiff coupling — undamped, but the middle shaft is a thousand times stiffer (a rigid coupling modelled elastically); its mode is about 25 times faster than the others.

The line starts at rest with a linear twist of \(\pm 5\,\mathrm{mrad}\), which excites every antisymmetric elastic mode and no rigid rotation.

wheels, inertia, stiffness = 8, 0.02, 4000.0
lowest = 2 * np.sqrt(stiffness / inertia) * np.sin(np.pi / (2 * wheels))
period, beta = 2 * np.pi / lowest, 2 * 0.02 / lowest
shafts = {
    "undamped": ((stiffness,) * (wheels - 1), (0.0,) * (wheels - 1)),
    "damped": ((stiffness,) * (wheels - 1), (beta * stiffness,) * (wheels - 1)),
    "stiff coupling": (
        tuple(stiffness * (1000.0 if i == wheels // 2 - 1 else 1.0) for i in range(wheels - 1)),
        (0.0,) * (wheels - 1),
    ),
}
twist = 0.01 * (np.arange(wheels) / (wheels - 1) - 0.5)
domain = LumpedDomain()
lines = {
    case: TorsionalDrivetrain(
        domain=domain.system,
        inertias=(inertia,) * wheels,
        shaft_stiffness=k,
        shaft_damping=c,
    )
    for case, (k, c) in shafts.items()
}
problems = {
    case: Problem(
        name=f"Torsional drivetrain, {case}",
        geometry=domain,
        physics=(line,),
        initial_conditions=tuple(
            InitialValue(line.angle(i), float(twist[i])) for i in range(wheels)
        )
        + tuple(InitialValue(line.angular_velocity(i), 0.0) for i in range(wheels)),
    )
    for case, line in lines.items()
}
mesh = LumpedMesh(domain)
end_time, step = 3 * period, period / 100
print(
    f"{wheels} wheels, {2 * wheels} state values on one global node; lowest elastic period "
    f"{1e3 * period:.2f} ms, end time {1e3 * end_time:.1f} ms, step {1e6 * step:.1f} µs"
)

The exact modal solution

The first-order system matrix of each variant and its eigen decomposition. The modal coefficients \(V^{-1}x_0\) are fixed by the initial twist; the solution at any time is a sum of damped exponentials.

The method catalogue

Every time integrator of the library: explicit one-step and multistep methods, the kick-drift methods for second-order systems (staggered velocity Verlet and symplectic Euler, which use the position/velocity structure \(\dot\theta=\omega\) that TorsionalDrivetrain declares), implicit Runge-Kutta, Adams-Moulton and BDF methods, Rosenbrock methods and the adaptive integrators. Implicit stages take Newton's method with a sparse direct solve; Rosenbrock stages take the sparse direct solve alone.

Schemes that must fail, and their stabilized counterparts

Forward Euler amplifies every undamped oscillation at any step (\(|1+i\omega\Delta t|>1\)); the library refuses it for the undamped variants. Its counterpart adds exactly one ingredient: the position update uses the new velocity (symplectic or Euler-Cromer step), at the stable step where needed. Leapfrog is unstable on the damping of the damped variant. On the stiff coupling no explicit method is stable at \(\Delta t=T_1/100\) (\(\omega_\max\Delta t\approx 7\)), and Adams-Moulton 3 and 4 are not A-stable. Each such cell is hatched; its colour and value are those of the counterpart, which is the same method at the stable step \(\Delta t_s=0.5/\omega_\max\), but at most a quarter of the declared step (methods such as Adams-Bashforth 2 amplify undamped oscillations at every step and only become accurate over the run at a smaller one; for Adams-Bashforth 2, whose growth per step is about \((\omega\Delta t)^4/4\), the step is \(0.1/\omega_\max\)), unless noted otherwise.

The test matrix

1. Time integrator × variant — every integrator on the three drivetrains at \(\Delta t=T_1/100\). 2. Pendulum period × amplitude — further below. 3. Nonlinear × linear solver — implicit midpoint steps of the large-amplitude pendulum. 4. Step check — representative combinations at half the step.

Run everything

One loop, the same problem objects every time. A combination the library refuses is recorded with its message. A run whose amplitude grows beyond ten times the exact one, or that the library stops as unstable, is recorded as failed; for it the loop appends its stabilized counterpart, which runs in the same loop.

Time integrator × variant

The relative error of the last wheel's angle over three lowest periods, and the wall time.

Time histories

The last wheel of the damped line with second-order methods against the modal solution, and the energy-conserving kick-drift step on the undamped line against forward Euler's counterpart.

The pendulum: period against the elliptic integral

A point mass on a massless rod of length \(L\): \(\ddot\theta+(g/L)\sin\theta=0\). Released from rest at \(\theta_0\), its exact period is

\[T=4\sqrt{L/g}\;K\!\left(\sin^2\tfrac{\theta_0}{2}\right),\]

with \(K\) the complete elliptic integral of the first kind (Landau and Lifshitz, Mechanics, §11). Every integrator runs four exact periods at \(\Delta t=T_0/200\) (\(T_0=2\pi\sqrt{L/g}\)) for amplitudes from 30° to 175°; the measured period is the mean spacing of the downward zero crossings, and the matrix shows its relative deviation. Forward Euler is refused (unstable for an undamped swing); its counterpart is again the symplectic Euler step. A method whose numerical damping or growth destroys the swing near the separatrix (175°) before a full period is repeated at a quarter of the step.

Nonlinear × linear solver on the pendulum

The pendulum is nonlinear, so its implicit stages need a genuine nonlinear solve. Implicit midpoint steps of the 150° swing at \(\Delta t=T_0/50\) are solved with every nonlinear solver and every linear solver for its Newton systems; the period must not depend on the solver as long as it converges.

Step check: Δt against Δt/2

Representative integrators on the damped line at half the step. The error ratio shows the observed order: about 4 for a second-order method, 2 for a first-order one.

Methods that do not apply

A lumped drivetrain has no spatial discretization, so there is no space axis, no flux and no mesh refinement; the step check takes its place. Conjugate gradients and algebraic multigrid need a symmetric positive definite stage matrix, which the first-order form \((\theta,\omega)\) does not have. Runge-Kutta-Chebyshev and -Legendre super-steps are for parabolic operators with a real spectrum; IMEX splits need a separate stiff and non-stiff part, which the declared model does not publish. JFNK never forms the Jacobian, so it has no matrix for a sparse direct (or automatic, which selects the direct) inner solve; it is paired with the Krylov solvers only.

Going further

Add gear ratios through gear_ratios, drive the first wheel through applied_torque(0), or couple the line to a controller; the matrix code stays as it is.
