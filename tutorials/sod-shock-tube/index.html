Sod's shock tube through every method

One Euler model, 400 cells, 256 runs. Every discretization family, reconstruction, limiter, Riemann solver, time integrator and solver pair OneSimulation offers for this problem, each graded against the exact Riemann solution. Every combination produces a solution: 161 run as declared, and the 46 that must fail mathematically (high-order linear spatial schemes at a shock by Godunov's theorem, time integrators and the fixed-point iteration beyond their stability limit) are shown with their stabilized counterpart. None is refused. The density error spans 1.35 × 10−3 to 1.20 × 10−2. Nothing about the physics changed between runs, only one argument.

A shock, a contact and a rarefaction

A tube of ideal gas (γ = 1.4) is split at x = 0.5. The left half holds (ρ, u, p) = (1, 0, 1), the right half (0.125, 0, 0.1). When the diaphragm goes, a rarefaction runs left and a contact and a shock run right. At t = 0.2 the shock sits at x = 0.8504.

The exact solution comes from OneSimulation's own exact Euler sampler. Every run is evaluated at the 400 cell centres. The page reports the L1 errors of density, velocity and pressure, the shock position error and the wall time.

The model, declared once

tube = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(1.0, 0.0, 0.0))
gas = CompressibleEuler(
    domain=tube.line, equation_of_state=IdealGasEquationOfState(gamma=gamma)
)
sod = Problem(
    name="Sod shock tube",
    geometry=tube,
    physics=(gas,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        TransmissiveBoundary(boundary=tube.boundaries.all, physics=gas),
    ),
    initial_conditions=(
        InitialValue(gas.density, lambda x: np.where(x < 0.5, 1.0, 0.125)),
        InitialValue(gas.momentum, 0.0),
        InitialValue(gas.total_energy, lambda x: np.where(x < 0.5, 2.5, 0.25)),
    ),
)

None of this names a method. The method is the numerics argument of Simulation. Three of the 256 choices:

numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteVolume(convection=HLLC(), reconstruction=MUSCL(), limiter=SuperbeeLimiter()),
    time_integrator=TimeMethod("ssprk2"),
)
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteElement(element=DiscontinuousLagrange(2), convection=HLLC(),
                          limiter=DGPolynomialLimiter(slope_limiter=MinmodLimiter(),
                                                      invariant_domain=EulerInvariantDomain())),
    linear_solver=SparseDirect(),
    time_integrator=TimeMethod("ssprk2"),
)
numerics = Numerics(
    spatial=FiniteVolume(convection=HLLC(), reconstruction=MUSCL(), limiter=MinmodLimiter()),
    time_integrator=BDF(order=2),
    nonlinear_solver=Newton(linear_solver=GMRES(preconditioner=ILU()),
                            jacobian="colored_finite_difference"),
)
result = Simulation(problem=sod, mesh=mesh, numerics=numerics).run(
    start_time=0.0, end_time=0.2, time_step=2e-3)

Finite volumes with first-order, MUSCL (seven limiters and the unlimited slope), ENO and WENO reconstructions; discontinuous Galerkin of degree 0 to 3; continuous elements with and without graph viscosity; finite differences. Each is crossed with the Rusanov, HLL, HLLE and HLLC solvers and advanced with SSP-RK2 at the stable step.

What the profiles look like

Every time integrator

21 explicit runs at the stable step and 31 implicit runs at Δt = 2 × 10−3, a CFL number of about 1.4 that no explicit method could take. All with HLLC fluxes.

Schemes that must fail at a shock, and their stabilized counterparts

A linear scheme of higher than first order oscillates at a discontinuity (Godunov's theorem). On Sod's problem that drives density or pressure negative, and OneSimulation reports it instead of clipping the state. Each such scheme is shown hatched in the matrices, with the result of its stabilized counterpart behind the hatching: exactly one ingredient is added, or, for time integrators that are not A-stable and for the fixed-point iteration, the step is reduced to one they are stable for. Of the 207 declared runs, 161 ran directly and 46 are shown stabilized.
