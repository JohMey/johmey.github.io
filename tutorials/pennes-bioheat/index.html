Heating of perfused tissue: one model, every method

One physics declaration, a perfused tissue layer whose skin surface is brought to 45 °C, is solved with every applicable combination of discretization family, diffusion operator, boundary enforcement, time integrator, nonlinear and linear solver, as a stationary problem and as a heating transient. Each run is graded against the closed-form steady state and the eigenfunction series of the transient. 244 runs on the compiled OneSimulation runtime: 213 ran as declared, 31 failed as the mathematics predicts and are shown hatched with their stabilized counterpart where one exists, none was refused by the library.

Blood flowing through the capillaries removes heat from warm tissue. Pennes (1948) modelled this as a sink proportional to the difference between the tissue temperature and the arterial blood temperature \(T_b\):

\[\rho c\,\partial_tT=k\,\partial_x^2T+\omega_b\rho_bc_b\,(T_b-T).\]

A tissue layer of depth \(L\) starts at \(T_b\); at \(t=0\) its skin surface \(x=0\) is brought to \(T_s=45\) °C, the deep side \(x=L\) stays at \(T_b\). The model is declared once; everything numerical is an argument of Numerics.

Reference. H. H. Pennes, J. Appl. Physiol. 1 (1948) 93; H. S. Carslaw and J. C. Jaeger, Conduction of Heat in Solids, 2nd ed. (1959), §3.4. With \(\theta=T-T_b\), \(m^2=\omega_b\rho_bc_b/k\), \(\alpha=k/\rho c\), \(w=\omega_b\rho_bc_b/\rho c\) and \(\Delta T=T_s-T_b\):

\[\theta(x,t)=\Delta T\,\frac{\sinh m(L-x)}{\sinh mL} -\Delta T\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{2n\pi}{(n\pi)^2+(mL)^2}\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\, e^{-(\alpha n^2\pi^2/L^2+w)\,t}.\]

The first term is the steady state.

Grading. Every run is evaluated at the cell centres \(x_i\): the largest temperature deviation \(\max_i|T_h(x_i)-T(x_i)|\) from the steady state (stationary solves) or from the series at \(t=5\) min (transients). The wall time covers validation, binding and the solve.

The physics, declared once

Soft tissue 30 mm deep (density 1050 kg/m³, heat capacity 3600 J/(kg K), conductivity 0.5 W/(m K)), perfused at \(\omega_b=5\times10^{-4}\) 1/s by blood with \(\rho_bc_b=1060\cdot3770\) J/(m³ K).

depth, density, capacity, conductivity = 0.03, 1050.0, 3600.0, 0.5
blood, surface, perfusion = 310.15, 318.15, 5e-4 * 1060.0 * 3770.0
cell_counts, time_cells, moment = (50, 100, 200, 400), 100, 300.0
layer = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(depth, 0.0, 0.0))
tissue = PennesBioheat(
    domain=layer.line,
    density=density,
    heat_capacity=capacity,
    conductivity=conductivity,
    perfusion_capacity_rate=perfusion,
    blood_temperature=blood,
)
heating = Problem(
    name="Pennes bioheat, skin heating",
    geometry=layer,
    physics=(tissue,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        PrescribedValueBoundary(physics=tissue, boundary=layer.boundaries.start, value=lambda time, **_: {"temperature": surface}),
        PrescribedValueBoundary(physics=tissue, boundary=layer.boundaries.end, value=lambda time, **_: {"temperature": blood}),
    ),
    initial_conditions=(InitialValue(tissue.temperature, blood),),
)
meshes = {n: StructuredLineMesher(cells=n).generate(layer) for n in cell_counts}
centres = {n: mesh.cell_centers[:, 0] for n, mesh in meshes.items()}
diffusivity, rate = conductivity / (density * capacity), perfusion / (density * capacity)
m = np.sqrt(perfusion / conductivity)
print(
    f"meshes of {', '.join(str(meshes[n].cell_count) for n in cell_counts)} cells (finest width {1e6 * depth / cell_counts[-1]:.0f} µm); "
    f"penetration depth 1/m = {1e3 / m:.1f} mm, diffusion time L²/α = {depth**2 / diffusivity / 60:.0f} min"
)

The exact solution

The steady profile on every grid, and 4000 terms of the series at \(t=5\) min.

The method catalogue

Finite differences with the central and polynomial Laplacians of order 2, 4 and 6; finite volumes with two-point fluxes; continuous Galerkin of degree 1 to 4 with symmetric and non-symmetric Nitsche enforcement of the surface temperature; discontinuous Galerkin of degree 1 to 4 with symmetric interior penalty at two penalty levels.

Schemes that must fail, and their stabilized counterparts

The heating starts with a jump at the surface and the diffusion operator is stiff. Leapfrog has no damping and is unstable on it at every step; its counterpart adds the Robert-Asselin-Williams filter. Adams-Moulton 3 and 4 have bounded stability intervals on the negative real axis (\([-6,0]\) and \([-3,0]\)). A fixed-point (Picard) iteration contracts only for steps below the inverse spectral radius. The counterpart of each is the same method at a step it is stable for. The finite-element spectra are larger: quadratic continuous elements would need steps of a few \(10^{-2}\) s and discontinuous elements a few \(10^{-3}\) s for Adams-Moulton 3 and 4 (tens of thousands of steps); those cells stay marked as failed. The fixed-step Gear BDF methods of order 3 to 6 start their history with extrapolated backward-Euler steps (TimeMethod("bdf<k>_gear", startup_policy="implicit_extrapolation")) and run at \(\Delta t=10\) s on every spatial family.

Unpreconditioned GMRES variants stall on the stiff stationary diffusion system within their default 1000 iterations; their counterpart adds an incomplete-LU preconditioner.

The test matrix

1. Space × mesh — every spatial scheme, stationary solve with Newton on 50 to 400 cells. 2. Time integrator × space — every integrator from body temperature to \(t=5\) min on 100 cells. Explicit integrators run at their stable step (safety factor 0.5) on the finite differences and finite volumes; implicit integrators with \(\Delta t=10\) s on all four families. 3. Nonlinear × linear solver — the stationary problem on 400 cells with quadratic Nitsche elements, solved by Newton, damped, trust-region and quasi-Newton and by Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov with every linear solver and by each linear solver alone, graded against the steady state. The Pennes operator with symmetric Nitsche enforcement is symmetric positive definite, so conjugate gradients and algebraic multigrid apply. The fixed-point (Picard) iteration is a stage solver of implicit steps: it runs backward Euler on the central finite differences, 100 cells, up to \(t=5\) min, graded against the series.

Nonlinear × linear solver

The stationary problem on quadratic elements: the error against the steady state must not depend on the solver pair beyond the iterative tolerances; the wall time does. Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov with an unpreconditioned Krylov solver stops at its nonlinear tolerance on the ill-conditioned element system, so its error is that tolerance, not the discretization error. The fixed-point rows run backward Euler on finite differences and are graded against the series; the last row solves the stationary problem with each linear solver alone.

Methods that do not apply to the Pennes equation

The equation has no convection: interface fluxes, reconstructions, limiters and SUPG-type stabilizations have nothing to act on. Discontinuous elements of degree 0 carry no gradient for interior-penalty diffusion. Staggered Verlet needs a second-order system. Continuous interior penalties (CIP, volume interior penalty) stabilize convection and are left out for the same reason as SUPG.

Going further

Add the metabolic heat as a source and the equilibrium shifts to \(T_b+Q_m/(\omega_b\rho_bc_b)\); a time-dependent surface temperature gives the periodic skin-heating problem. The matrix code stays as it is.
