Magnetic diffusion into a conducting slab: one model, every method

One physics declaration, magnetic diffusion into a copper plate whose faces are suddenly exposed to a uniform field, is solved with every applicable combination of discretization family (finite differences, finite volumes, Nitsche-continuous and discontinuous finite elements), time integrator, nonlinear solver and linear solver. Each run is graded against the exact Fourier series; combinations the library refuses are reported as such. 208 runs on the compiled OneSimulation runtime: 191 ran as declared, 17 failed as the mathematics predicts and are shown hatched with their stabilized counterpart where one exists, none was refused by the library.

A copper plate of thickness \(L=10\,\)mm (conductivity \(\sigma=5.96\cdot10^7\,\)S/m, permeability \(\mu_0\)) is field-free at \(t=0\). Then a uniform tangential field \(B_0=0.1\,\)T is switched on outside both faces. Eddy currents delay its entry: in the magnetoquasistatic limit the induction obeys a diffusion equation with the magnetic diffusivity \(\eta=1/(\mu_0\sigma)\),

\[\partial_t B_z=\eta\,\partial_x^2 B_z,\qquad B_z(0,t)=B_z(L,t)=B_0,\qquad B_z(x,0)=0,\]

with \(\eta\approx0.0134\,\)m²/s. MagneticDiffusion declares this balance once. Everything numerical is an argument of Numerics.

Reference. The odd sine modes of the initial deficit decay independently (Knoepfel, Magnetic Fields, §4.2; the same series as for heat conduction):

\[B_z(x,t)=B_0\Big[1-\sum_{k\ \text{odd}}\frac{4}{k\pi}\,\sin\frac{k\pi x}{L}\, \exp\!\Big(-\frac{k^2\pi^2\eta t}{L^2}\Big)\Big].\]

Early on each face sees a half-space and \(B_z\approx B_0\,\mathrm{erfc}\big(x/(2\sqrt{\eta t})\big)\); the series contains that limit.

Grading. At \(t=0.4\,\)ms (diffusion number \(\eta t/L^2\approx0.053\)) every run is evaluated at the cell centres \(x_i\): the mean absolute error \(\sum_i|B_h(x_i)-B(x_i)|\,\Delta x/L\) and the largest deviation; the wall time covers validation, binding and time stepping.

The physics, declared once

Nothing in this cell names a numerical method.

thickness, field, end_time = 0.01, 0.1, 4e-4
cells, coarse_cells, time_cells = 200, 100, 50
conductivity, mu0 = 5.96e7, float(VacuumPermeability().evaluate())
diffusivity = 1 / (mu0 * conductivity)
plate = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(thickness, 0.0, 0.0))
copper = MagneticDiffusion(domain=plate.line, permeability=mu0, conductivity=conductivity)
problem = Problem(
    name="Field soaking into a copper plate",
    geometry=plate,
    physics=(copper,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        PrescribedValueBoundary(
            physics=copper, boundary=plate.boundaries.all, value={"magnetic_induction": field}
        ),
    ),
    initial_conditions=(InitialValue(copper.magnetic_induction, 0.0),),
)
meshes = {
    n: StructuredLineMesher(cells=n).generate(plate) for n in (cells, coarse_cells, time_cells)
}
centres = {n: mesh.cell_centers for n, mesh in meshes.items()}
print(
    f"{meshes[cells].cell_count} cells of width {1e3 * thickness / cells:.3f} mm "
    f"(mesh check: {meshes[coarse_cells].cell_count} cells; time-integrator cut: "
    f"{meshes[time_cells].cell_count} cells), magnetic diffusivity {diffusivity:.4e} m²/s, "
    f"diffusion number {diffusivity * end_time / thickness**2:.4f}"
)

The exact solution

The series is summed with 2000 terms at every cell centre and on a fine plotting grid.

The method catalogue

Finite differences with central, fourth- and sixth-order Laplacians; finite volumes with the two-point flux; continuous Lagrange elements of degree 1 to 3 whose face values enter through symmetric or nonsymmetric Nitsche terms, also with continuous interior penalty; discontinuous elements of degree 1 to 3 with the SIPG flux at two penalty levels. The induction balance has no gradient-derived output, so the difference schemes take no first derivative.

Time integrators: the explicit one-step and multistep methods, implicit Runge-Kutta, Adams-Moulton and BDF methods, Rosenbrock methods and the adaptive integrators. Implicit stages take a Newton solver with a sparse direct solve; the linearly implicit Rosenbrock stages take the sparse direct solve alone. Explicit methods advance at their stable step (safety factor 0.5), implicit ones at \(\Delta t=5\,\)µs.

Schemes that must fail, and their stabilized counterparts

The diffusion operator has a real, negative spectrum reaching \(-\lambda_{\max}\sim-12\alpha/h^2\). Adams-Moulton 3 and 4 are not A-stable: their stability interval on the negative axis ends at \(\lambda\Delta t\approx6\) and \(3\), while \(\lambda_{\max}\Delta t\) is several hundred at \(\Delta t=5\,\)µs. Leapfrog has no damping: every decaying mode is amplified at any step. A fixed-point (Picard) iteration of an implicit step contracts only for \(\lambda_{\max}\Delta t<1\). BDF5 and BDF6 are stable on the negative axis but not monotone: at large \(\lambda\Delta t\) their first steps after the jump overshoot. Crank-Nicolson is A-stable but not L-stable: the stiffest modes of the initial jump at the faces are multiplied by almost \(-1\) in every step, and on the high-order and Nitsche elements the resulting overshoot leaves \([0,B_0]\). Each of these must fail; its stabilized counterpart, shown behind a grey hatching, is the same method at a step it is stable or damping for, or leapfrog with the Robert-Asselin-Williams filter. A counterpart runs only where the declared scheme actually failed.

The test matrix

Three cuts through the space of combinations:

1. Space × time integrator — every spatial scheme on 200 cells with four implicit integrators (backward Euler, Crank-Nicolson, BDF2, SDIRK2) at \(\Delta t=5\,\)µs. 2. Time integrator × space — every integrator on three spatial schemes (central differences, two-point volumes and CG P1 with Nitsche faces). An explicit method on a diffusion operator must take \(\Delta t\propto h^2\), so this cut uses 50 cells; the spatial accuracy is the subject of the first cut. Higher-degree and discontinuous elements are left out of this cut: degree and interior penalty raise \(\lambda_{\max}\), and an explicit step would have to be five to twenty times smaller; they run with the four implicit integrators of the first cut. 3. Nonlinear × linear solver — backward Euler on central differences with 50 cells (the fixed-point counterpart needs \(\lambda_{\max}\Delta t<1\), which is affordable on this grid).

The representative schemes are repeated on 100 cells for the mesh check.

Run everything

One loop, the same problem object every time. A combination the library refuses is recorded with its validation message. A run whose induction leaves the interval \([0, B_0]\) violates the maximum principle of the diffusion equation and is recorded as failed, as is a solver that does not converge. A failed run with a stabilized counterpart appends that counterpart to the list, so the same loop runs it next.

Space × time integrator

The mean induction error of every spatial scheme with four implicit integrators. Backward Euler is first order in time; at \(\Delta t=5\,\)µs its time error dominates every spatial error, while Crank-Nicolson, BDF2 and SDIRK2 reveal the spatial ranking.

Profiles of representative combinations

Time integrator × space

Explicit integrators at their stable step, implicit integrators at \(\Delta t=5\,\)µs, on 50 cells.

Nonlinear × linear solver

Backward Euler on central differences with 50 cells. The induction equation is linear, so every converging Newton variant reproduces the same discrete solution in one iteration per step; the wall time differs. The step matrix \(I+\Delta t\,K\) of the symmetric central stencil is symmetric positive definite, but this owner does not declare it so: conjugate gradients and the symmetric algebraic multigrid are refused. That is a gap of the library, shown as "rejected".

Mesh check: 200 against 100 cells

BDF2 at \(\Delta t=5\,\)µs on half as many cells. Second-order schemes should reduce the spatial error about fourfold; once it falls below the time error of the step, the ratio levels off.

What the library refused, and what failed

A refused combination is caught by validation or binding before any time step; a failed one started and left the physical range or did not converge.

Methods that do not apply to this problem

Riemann solvers, reconstructions and slope limiters act on convective fluxes; magnetic diffusion in a plate at rest has none, so finite volumes take only a diffusive face flux. SUPG, GLS and graph viscosity stabilize convection and have nothing to act on here. Staggered Verlet needs a second-order system and IMEX splits a stiff from a non-stiff part, which a single diffusion operator does not have. Continuous elements on this balance take their face values through Nitsche terms; strongly imposed values are not available for the line balance owner.

Going further

Replace a PrescribedValueBoundary by an OutwardFluxBoundary (a prescribed tangential electric field) and the matrix code stays as it is; add a column by adding one object to a dictionary.
