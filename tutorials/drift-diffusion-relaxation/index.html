Diffusive relaxation in a drift-diffusion plasma: one model, every method

One declaration of a drift-diffusion plasma (electrons, argon ions, mean electron energy) between two insulating walls is solved with every applicable combination of finite-volume, finite-difference and finite-element discretization, time integrator, nonlinear solver and linear solver. Each run is graded against the exact Fourier-mode solution. 230 runs on the compiled OneSimulation runtime: 140 ran as declared, 16 failed as the mathematics predicts and are shown hatched with their stabilized counterpart where one exists, none was refused by the library.

Electrons and \(\mathrm{Ar}^+\) ions fill a gap of width \(L=1\) cm between two insulating walls (no particle and no energy flux). Their densities obey the drift-diffusion balances

\[\partial_t n_s+\partial_x\Gamma_s=0,\qquad \Gamma_s=\pm\mu_s n_s E-D_s\,\partial_x n_s,\]

and the electron energy density \(w_e=n_e\bar\varepsilon\) obeys its own balance with the energy flux \(\Gamma_w=-\tfrac{5}{3}\,\cdot\,\)(enthalpy factor)\(\,\cdot\,(\mu_e E w_e+\partial_x(D_e w_e))\). The electrons relax momentum at a constant collision frequency \(\nu=10^{12}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\) (so \(D_e=2\bar\varepsilon/(3m_e\nu)\)), the ions have the mobility \(0.2\,\mathrm{m^2/(V\,s)}\) in argon at 300 K (so \(D_i=\mu_i k_BT/e\)), and the electric field is prescribed as zero.

Exact solution. With a uniform mean energy \(\bar\varepsilon=1\) eV and the enthalpy factor 1 the energy flux is \(\bar\varepsilon\,\Gamma_e\), so \(\bar\varepsilon\) stays uniform and both diffusivities stay constant. Each density then relaxes mode by mode:

\[n_s(x,t)=n_0\Big[1+\tfrac12\cos\tfrac{\pi x}{L}\,e^{-D_s\pi^2t/L^2} +\tfrac15\cos\tfrac{3\pi x}{L}\,e^{-9D_s\pi^2t/L^2}\Big].\]

The electrons diffuse \(D_e/D_i\approx23\) times faster than the ions, a stiff pair of time scales. This is a free-diffusion test of the transport, the walls and the energy coupling; it has no space charge (the field is prescribed), so it is not ambipolar diffusion.

Grading. At \(t_\mathrm{end}=0.2\,L^2/(\pi^2D_e)\) the electron and ion densities are evaluated at the 100 cell centres and compared with the exact solution (cell averages for finite volumes, point values otherwise): \(L_1=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i|n_h-n|/n_0\).

The physics, declared once

Nothing in this cell names a numerical method.

width, density, cells, coarse_cells = 0.01, 1e15, 100, 50
gap = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(width, 0.0, 0.0))
argon_plasma = ReactionNetworkIR(
    "argon drift-diffusion relaxation",
    "1",
    (
        SpeciesIR("electron", -1, electron_mass, role="electron"),
        SpeciesIR("Ar+", 1, 6.63e-26, transport={"mobility": {"value": 0.2, "unit": "m^2/V/s"}}),
        SpeciesIR("Ar", 0, 6.63e-26),
    ),
    (),
    metadata={"electron_momentum_transfer": {"kind": "constant_frequency", "value": 1e12, "unit": "1/s"}},
)
chemistry = Chemistry(network=argon_plasma)
gas = NeutralGas(species="Ar", pressure=1000.0, temperature=300.0)
plasma = DriftDiffusionPlasma(
    domain=gap.line,
    gas=gas,
    chemistry=chemistry,
    electron_energy=MeanElectronEnergyEquation(enthalpy_factor=1.0),
    electron_transport=MagnetizedElectronTransport(
        collisions=ElectronCollisionModel(gas=gas, chemistry=chemistry, energy_distribution=Maxwellian())
    ),
    ion_transport=IonMobilityModel(gas=gas, chemistry=chemistry),
    prescribed_inputs={"electrostatic_field": (0.0, 0.0, 0.0)},
)
relaxation = Problem(
    name="Drift-diffusion relaxation between insulating walls",
    geometry=gap,
    physics=(plasma,),
    boundary_conditions=(NoParticleFlux(boundary=gap.boundaries.all, physics=plasma),),
    initial_conditions=(
        InitialValue(plasma.electron_density, lambda x: density * (1 + 0.5 * np.cos(np.pi * x / width) + 0.2 * np.cos(3 * np.pi * x / width))),
        InitialValue(plasma.density("Ar+"), lambda x: density * (1 + 0.5 * np.cos(np.pi * x / width) + 0.2 * np.cos(3 * np.pi * x / width))),
        InitialValue(plasma.mean_electron_energy, 1.0, unit="eV"),
    ),
)
meshes = {n: StructuredLineMesher(cells=n).generate(gap) for n in (cells, coarse_cells)}
centres = {n: mesh.cell_centers[:, 0] for n, mesh in meshes.items()}
print(f"{meshes[cells].cell_count} cells of width {width / cells * 1e3:.3f} mm (coarse check: {coarse_cells} cells)")

The exact solution

The diffusivities come from the declared transport laws themselves; the solution is the sum of the two decaying modes (their cell averages carry the factor \(\mathrm{sinc}\) of half a cell).

The method catalogue

Finite volumes with Scharfetter-Gummel fluxes for particles and energy (exact for a constant drift and diffusivity across each face), with the two-point or the least-squares gradient, with linear cell reconstruction, alone or limited by Barth-Jespersen or Venkatakrishnan. Finite differences: central, fourth- and sixth-order polynomial and summation-by-parts stencils. Continuous finite elements of degree 1 to 3. Every public time integrator, and every nonlinear/linear solver pair for implicit steps.

Time steps

The plasma path takes the step it is given; explicit stability is the user's choice of step. The explicit integrators run at \(\Delta t=c\,\Delta x^2/D_e\) with \(c=0.2\) for volumes and differences and \(c=0.03\) for linear elements (the consistent mass matrix has a three times larger spectral radius, and the filtered leapfrog needs the margin); every explicit method in the catalogue is stable there except leapfrog. The implicit integrators run at \(\Delta t=t_\mathrm{end}/40\), about 25 times the explicit limit.

Leapfrog has no stable region on the negative real axis, so it is unstable for diffusion at every step; its stabilized counterpart is the Robert-Asselin-Williams filtered leapfrog. BDF4 to BDF6 and Adams-Moulton 3 and 4 are not A-stable, and a fixed-point (Picard) iteration contracts only below the inverse spectral radius of the stage; where they fail, the same method runs at a stable step.

The test matrix

1. Space — every spatial scheme with BDF2 (variable step), Newton and a sparse LU at \(\Delta t=t_\mathrm{end}/160\), small enough that the time error stays below the spatial error of the fourth-order schemes. 2. Time integrator × space — every integrator on the Scharfetter-Gummel volumes, the central differences and the linear elements. 3. Nonlinear × linear solver — backward Euler on the Scharfetter-Gummel volumes. 4. Mesh check — the space matrix on 50 cells.

Run everything

A combination the library refuses is recorded with its validation message; a run that stops because the mean energy left its positive domain, a step was rejected or a stage did not converge is recorded as failed. A stabilized variant runs only where its declared run failed.

Space

The electron and ion density errors of every spatial scheme. All runs use the same step \(t_\mathrm{end}/160\); the BDF2 time error at this step is about \(3\cdot10^{-7}\), a common floor below which the high-order schemes cannot be told apart. With the field prescribed as zero the face reconstruction and its limiter act on a vanishing drift, so the five finite-volume variants give the same Scharfetter-Gummel diffusion flux and the same error; they differ once a field drives the particles.

Profiles

Electron and ion densities at \(t_\mathrm{end}\) for representative schemes against the exact solution.

Time integrator × space

Explicit integrators at their stable step, implicit integrators at \(t_\mathrm{end}/40\).

Nonlinear × linear solver

Backward Euler on the Scharfetter-Gummel volumes. The discrete solution does not depend on the converged solver; the wall time does.

Methods that do not apply

Discontinuous elements are not offered for the plasma balances: the plasma finite-element formulation is a continuous volume formulation and refuses them with that reason. Riemann solvers, Lax-Friedrichs splitting and shock-capturing limiters target hyperbolic systems; the drift-diffusion flux is parabolic and its upwinding is built into the Scharfetter-Gummel flux. IMEX splittings and Runge-Kutta-Chebyshev super-steps are not public time declarations of this path.
