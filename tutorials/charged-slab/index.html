A uniformly charged slab: one model, every method

One physics declaration, Gauss's law for a dielectric slab carrying a uniform space charge between two electrodes, is solved with every applicable combination of discretization family (finite differences, finite volumes, continuous, Nitsche and discontinuous finite elements) and linear solver. Each run is graded against the exact parabolic potential; combinations the library refuses are reported as such. 184 runs on the compiled OneSimulation runtime: 138 ran as declared, 46 failed as the mathematics predicts and are shown hatched with their stabilized counterpart where one exists, none was refused by the library.

A dielectric layer of thickness \(d=1\,\)mm and relative permittivity \(\varepsilon_r=4\) carries a uniform space charge \(\rho=2\cdot10^{-3}\,\)C/m³. Its faces are electrodes at \(\varphi(0)=0\) and \(\varphi(d)=V=10\,\)V. Gauss's law in terms of the potential,

\[-\partial_x\big(\varepsilon\,\partial_x\varphi\big)=\rho,\qquad \varphi(0)=0,\quad \varphi(d)=V,\qquad E=-\partial_x\varphi,\]

is declared once. Everything numerical is an argument of Numerics: the discretization family with its diffusion operator and boundary treatment, and the linear solver of the stationary system.

Reference. Integrating twice gives the parabola and the linear field

\[\varphi(x)=\frac{\rho}{2\varepsilon}\,x(d-x)+V\,\frac{x}{d},\qquad E(x)=-\frac{\rho}{2\varepsilon}\,(d-2x)-\frac{V}{d}.\]

The displacement \(D=\varepsilon E\) grows by \(\rho d\) across the layer: the charge balance of the slab.

Grading. Every run is evaluated at the cell centres \(x_i\): the largest potential error, the largest field error, and the charge balance \(\varepsilon\,[E(d)-E(0)]/(\rho d)-1\) from the field at the two electrodes.

The physics, declared once

Nothing in this cell names a numerical method.

depth, voltage, charge = 1e-3, 10.0, 2e-3
epsilon = 4 * float(VacuumPermittivity().evaluate())
cells, coarse_cells = 200, 100
layer = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(depth, 0.0, 0.0))
gauss = PoissonEquation(
    domain=layer.line,
    permittivity=regionwise((), default=epsilon, unit="F/m"),
    charge_density=charge,
)
problem = Problem(
    name="Charged slab",
    geometry=layer,
    physics=(gauss,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        FixedPotential(physics=gauss, boundary=layer.boundaries.start, value=0.0),
        FixedPotential(physics=gauss, boundary=layer.boundaries.end, value=voltage),
    ),
)
meshes = {n: StructuredLineMesher(cells=n).generate(layer) for n in (cells, coarse_cells)}
centres = {n: mesh.cell_centers for n, mesh in meshes.items()}
electrodes = np.array([[0.0, 0.0, 0.0], [depth, 0.0, 0.0]])
print(
    f"{meshes[cells].cell_count} cells of width {1e6 * depth / cells:.1f} µm "
    f"(mesh check: {meshes[coarse_cells].cell_count} cells)"
)

The exact solution

The parabola and the field at every cell centre and on a fine plotting grid.

The method catalogue

Finite differences with central, fourth- and sixth-order polynomial Laplacians (each with the first derivative of the same order for the field); finite volumes with the two-point flux and the corrected central flux; continuous Lagrange elements of degree 1 to 3 with strong electrode values, symmetric and nonsymmetric Nitsche terms and continuous interior penalty; discontinuous elements of degree 1 to 3 with the SIPG flux at two penalty levels.

The stationary system is linear. Every scheme is solved with a sparse LU factorization, with Krylov solvers with and without preconditioning, and with the automatic choice. Conjugate gradients and the symmetric algebraic multigrid need a certified symmetric positive definite matrix. The one-sided boundary closures of the higher-order differences and the Nitsche terms do not provide one (the symmetric Nitsche matrix is definite only above a penalty threshold that is not certified); these pairs are marked "n/a" and listed under "Methods that do not apply".

The test matrix

Space × linear solver: every spatial scheme with every linear solver on 200 cells. The representative schemes are repeated on 100 cells for the mesh check.

A solver that must fail, and its stabilized counterpart

The condition number of the stiffness matrix grows like \(h^{-2}\) and with the element degree. Unpreconditioned GMRES then stalls within its iteration budget on the higher-order elements. Its stabilized counterpart adds exactly one ingredient, an incomplete LU preconditioner; it is shown behind a grey hatching. Unpreconditioned conjugate gradients can stall the same way on the cubic discontinuous elements; their counterpart adds the algebraic multigrid preconditioner. A counterpart is shown only where the declared solver failed.

Run everything

One loop, the same problem object every time. A combination the library refuses is recorded with its validation message; a Krylov solver that does not converge is recorded as failed.

Methods that do not apply to this problem

Gauss's law is stationary and linear: time integrators have nothing to advance and a nonlinear solver nothing to iterate. Conjugate gradients and the symmetric algebraic multigrid need a certified symmetric positive definite matrix: the one-sided closures of the fourth- and sixth-order differences make the matrix nonsymmetric, the nonsymmetric Nitsche term does too, and the symmetric Nitsche matrix is definite only above an uncertified penalty threshold. Conjugate gradients with an incomplete LU preconditioner are excluded because ILU is not symmetric. Convective fluxes, reconstructions, limiters and SUPG/GLS act on transport terms, which an electrostatic field does not have.
