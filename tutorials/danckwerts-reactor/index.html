Tubular reactor with axial dispersion: one model, every method

One physics declaration, the axial-dispersion reactor A -> B with Danckwerts inlet and outlet conditions, is solved on 400 cells with every applicable combination of discretization family, reconstruction, limiter, interface flux, stabilization, time integrator, nonlinear and linear solver, both as a stationary problem and as a transient from plug flow to the steady state. Each run is graded against the closed-form solution of Danckwerts and Wehner & Wilhelm. 271 runs on the compiled OneSimulation runtime: 250 ran as declared, 21 failed as the mathematics predicts and are shown hatched with their stabilized counterpart where one exists, none was refused by the library.

A first-order reaction \(A\to B\) runs in a tube of length \(L\) in which the fluid is partly back-mixed. Both species are transported by the mean velocity \(u\) and the axial dispersion coefficient \(D\):

\[\partial_t c_A+u\,\partial_x c_A=D\,\partial_x^2c_A-k\,c_A,\qquad \partial_t c_B+u\,\partial_x c_B=D\,\partial_x^2c_B+k\,c_A .\]

The reaction is declared once as a reaction network (ReactionIR), not as a decay rate, so both species are coupled through their reaction source. The Danckwerts conditions are total outward fluxes: the feed \(u\,c_0\) of pure \(A\) enters at \(x=0\), and only advection leaves at \(x=L\).

Reference. P. V. Danckwerts, Continuous flow systems, Chem. Eng. Sci. 2 (1953) 1; J. F. Wehner & R. H. Wilhelm, Chem. Eng. Sci. 6 (1956) 89. With \(\mathrm{Pe}=uL/D\), \(\mathrm{Da}=kL/u\), \(a=\sqrt{1+4\,\mathrm{Da}/\mathrm{Pe}}\) and \(z=x/L\)

\[\frac{c_A(z)}{c_0}=\frac{2\,e^{\mathrm{Pe}\,z/2}\Big[(1+a)\,e^{a\mathrm{Pe}(1-z)/2}-(1-a)\,e^{-a\mathrm{Pe}(1-z)/2}\Big]} {(1+a)^2e^{a\mathrm{Pe}/2}-(1-a)^2e^{-a\mathrm{Pe}/2}} .\]

Both species have the same dispersion coefficient and \(B\) is not fed, so \(c_A+c_B\) obeys the reaction-free balance with the feed \(c_0\) at the inlet: in the steady state \(c_B=c_0-c_A\) exactly.

Grading. Every run is evaluated at the cell centres \(x_i\): \(L_1(c_A)=\sum_i|c_{A,h}(x_i)-c_A(x_i)|\,\Delta x\), and the outlet conversion \(1-c_A(L)/c_0\). The wall time covers validation, binding and the solve.

The physics, declared once

A 1 m tube, \(u=1\) m/s, \(\mathrm{Pe}=20\) and \(\mathrm{Da}=2\). Nothing in this cell names a numerical method. The initial state is the plug-flow profile \(c_A=c_0e^{-\mathrm{Da}\,z}\), \(c_B=c_0-c_A\): the transients relax from it to the dispersed steady state, and the stationary solves start their Newton iteration from it. The kinetics are linear, so the concentrations are declared as signed states (nonnegative=False): an undershoot is graded as an error, not stopped as a domain violation.

length, velocity, rate, feed, peclet = 1.0, 1.0, 2.0, 1.0, 20.0
cells, coarse_cells, time_cells = 400, 200, 100
dispersion = velocity * length / peclet
tube = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(length, 0.0, 0.0))
reactor = SpeciesTransport(
    domain=tube.line,
    velocity=velocity,
    nonnegative=False,
    species=(
        TransportSpecies("A", diffusivity=dispersion),
        TransportSpecies("B", diffusivity=dispersion),
    ),
    reactions=(
        ReactionIR(
            "A -> B", (("A", 1),), (("B", 1),), {"kind": "constant", "unit": "1/s", "value": rate}
        ),
    ),
)
plug_flow = Problem(
    name="Danckwerts tubular reactor",
    geometry=tube,
    physics=(reactor,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        OutwardFluxBoundary(
            physics=reactor,
            boundary=tube.boundaries.start,
            flux=lambda **kw: np.array([-velocity * feed, 0.0]),
            jacobian=lambda **kw: np.zeros((2, 2)),
        ),
        OutwardFluxBoundary(
            physics=reactor,
            boundary=tube.boundaries.end,
            flux=lambda interior, **kw: velocity * interior,
            jacobian=lambda **kw: velocity * np.eye(2),
            wave_speed=velocity,
        ),
    ),
    initial_conditions=(
        InitialValue(reactor.concentration("A"), lambda x: feed * np.exp(-rate * x / velocity)),
        InitialValue(reactor.concentration("B"), lambda x: feed * (1 - np.exp(-rate * x / velocity))),
    ),
)
meshes = {n: StructuredLineMesher(cells=n).generate(tube) for n in (cells, coarse_cells, time_cells)}
centres = {n: mesh.cell_centers[:, 0] for n, mesh in meshes.items()}
print(
    f"{meshes[cells].cell_count} cells of width {length / cells:.4f} m, cell Peclet number "
    f"{velocity * length / cells / dispersion:.3f} (mesh check: {meshes[coarse_cells].cell_count} cells, "
    f"time-integrator cut: {meshes[time_cells].cell_count} cells)"
)

The exact solution

The closed form on every grid and on a fine line for the figures.

The method catalogue

Interface fluxes for the finite volumes and discontinuous elements: Rusanov, monotone upwind, HLL and HLLE. Finite volumes with first-order states, unlimited and limited MUSCL (six slope limiters), ENO, WENO, WENO-Z, central WENO and QUICK; discontinuous Galerkin of degree 1 to 4 with symmetric interior-penalty diffusion, with and without a jump penalty; continuous Galerkin of degree 1 to 3, plain and with SUPG, GLS, continuous interior penalty or the volume interior penalty \(h\,\nu\,[u'][v']\) (with \(\nu=\gamma u\Delta x\) it is the same operator as the continuous interior penalty, written with a numerical diffusivity, so the two rows must agree); finite-difference stencils of order 2 and 4, summation-by-parts operators and WENO5 flux splitting. Continuous elements and finite differences take no interface flux; they sit in their own column.

Time integrators: the explicit one-step and multistep methods, implicit Runge-Kutta, Adams-Moulton and BDF methods, Rosenbrock methods and the adaptive integrators. Implicit stages take Newton with the analytic Jacobian and a sparse direct solve; Rosenbrock stages take the sparse direct solve alone.

Schemes that must fail, and their stabilized counterparts

Leapfrog has no damping: on the dissipative transport operator it is unstable at every step; its counterpart adds the Robert-Asselin-Williams filter. Adams-Moulton 3 and 4 have bounded stability intervals, and a fixed-point (Picard) iteration contracts only while the step stays below the inverse spectral radius of the stage operator; at the implicit step of the time cut they must fail, and their counterpart is the same method at a step it is stable for. On quadratic continuous elements that step lies below about \(10^{-4}\) s for Adams-Moulton 4, so this cell stays marked as failed. The fixed-step Gear methods of order 3 to 6 start their history with extrapolated backward-Euler steps (TimeMethod("bdf<k>_gear", startup_policy="implicit_extrapolation")), so they run at the implicit step on every spatial family.

The test matrix

1. Space × interface flux — every spatial scheme, stationary solve with Newton on 400 cells. 2. Time integrator × space — every integrator from plug flow to \(t=2.5\) s (the slowest mode decays at least at the rate \(k+u^2/4D=7\) 1/s, so the profile is steady to \(10^{-7}\)) on 100 cells. Explicit integrators run at their stable step (safety factor 0.5) on first-order and WENO5 volumes and fourth-order differences; implicit integrators run with \(\Delta t=0.02\) s, beyond every explicit limit, on first-order and MUSCL-minmod volumes, fourth-order differences and quadratic continuous elements. 3. Nonlinear × linear solver — backward Euler on MUSCL-minmod-Rusanov volumes, 100 cells, \(\Delta t=0.02\) s up to \(t=0.5\) s, graded by the deviation from the Newton/LU run; and the stationary problem on quadratic elements, solved by Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov with each inner linear solver and by each linear solver alone (the reaction is linear, so the stationary system is affine).

Representative combinations are repeated on 200 cells for the mesh check.

Unpreconditioned GMRES variants stall on the stationary transport system within their default 1000 iterations; their counterpart adds an incomplete-LU preconditioner.

Methods that do not apply to this reactor

HLLC restores a contact wave between two outer signals; a scalar transport law has a single wave and no contact, and the library says so. LinearUpwind and CentralDifference from onesim.numerics.fvm are cell-face schemes of unstructured meshes (velocity-field upwinding and a corrected face gradient); on a line their counterparts are the MUSCL reconstruction with an upwind flux and two-point diffusion, both in the matrix. Discontinuous elements of degree 0 carry no gradient for interior-penalty diffusion. Conjugate gradients and algebraic multigrid need a symmetric operator, which advection does not give. Staggered Verlet needs a second-order system; Runge-Kutta-Chebyshev super-steps are for purely parabolic operators.

Going further

Raise the Peclet number until the central schemes oscillate, or replace the reaction by a second-order or reversible network: the matrix code stays as it is.
