Buckley-Leverett waterflooding: one model, every method

One physics declaration, water displacing oil in a porous core, is solved on 400 cells with every applicable combination of discretization family, reconstruction, limiter, numerical flux, time integrator, nonlinear solver and linear solver. Each run is graded against the exact self-similar solution with Welge's shock construction. 459 runs on the compiled OneSimulation runtime: 265 ran as declared, 125 failed as the mathematics predicts and are shown hatched with their stabilized counterpart where one exists, none was refused by the library.

Water is injected at \(x=0\) into a 1 m core of porosity \(\phi=0.2\) that initially holds only oil. With a constant total Darcy velocity \(v\) the water saturation \(S\) obeys the scalar conservation law

\[\phi\,\partial_t S+\partial_x\big(v\,f(S)\big)=0,\qquad f(S)=\frac{S^2}{S^2+m(1-S)^2},\]

with Corey's quadratic relative permeabilities and the viscosity ratio \(m=\mu_w/\mu_o=0.5\). The flux is non-convex: its S-shape makes the solution a rarefaction fan attached to a shock. The law is declared once; everything numerical is an argument of Numerics: the discretization family, the reconstruction and its limiter, the numerical flux, the time integrator and, for implicit steps, the nonlinear and the linear solver.

Reference. The exact solution (Buckley and Leverett, Trans. AIME 146, 107, 1942) is self-similar in \(\xi=x/t\). Welge's tangent construction gives the shock saturation \(S^*\) from \(f(S^*)=S^*f'(S^*)\); behind the shock each saturation \(S\in[S^*,1]\) travels with \(\xi=v f'(S)/\phi\), ahead of it \(S=0\):

\[S(x,t)=\begin{cases}(f')^{-1}\big(\phi x/(v t)\big), & x/t\le v f'(S^*)/\phi,\\ 0, & x/t>v f'(S^*)/\phi.\end{cases}\]

The core is followed until the shock has reached \(x=0.6\) m.

Grading. At the final time every run is evaluated at the 400 cell centres \(x_i\): \(L_1(S)=\sum_i|S_h(x_i)-S(x_i)|\,\Delta x\); the shock position is where the saturation crosses \(S^*/2\); the wall time covers validation, binding and time stepping.

The physics, declared once

Nothing in this cell names a numerical method.

porosity, darcy_velocity, viscosity_ratio, shock_target = 0.2, 1.0e-5, 0.5, 0.6
cells, coarse_cells = 400, 200
core = Line(start=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0), end=(1.0, 0.0, 0.0))
corey = QuadraticCoreyFractionalFlow(viscosity_ratio=viscosity_ratio)
flood = BuckleyLeverettFlow(
    domain=core.line, porosity=porosity, darcy_velocity=darcy_velocity, fractional_flow=corey
)
waterflood = Problem(
    name="Buckley-Leverett waterflood",
    geometry=core,
    physics=(flood,),
    boundary_conditions=(
        SaturationInlet(physics=flood, boundary=core.boundaries.start, value=1.0),
        SaturationOutflow(physics=flood, boundary=core.boundaries.end),
    ),
    initial_conditions=(InitialValue(flood.water_saturation, 0.0),),
)
meshes = {n: StructuredLineMesher(cells=n).generate(core) for n in (cells, coarse_cells)}
centres = {n: mesh.cell_centers[:, 0] for n, mesh in meshes.items()}

The exact solution

Welge's tangent from the origin to the fractional-flow curve gives the shock saturation; the fan behind it is the inverse of \(f'\) on \([S^*,1]\), where \(f'\) decreases monotonically.

The method catalogue

Four numerical fluxes: Rusanov, HLL, HLLE and the monotone upwind (Godunov-type) flux of the scalar law (HLL and HLLE take their signal speeds from the extrema of \(v f'(S)\) between the two traces, which the waterflood law supplies because the S-shaped flux makes the trace speeds alone too slow); seven slope limiters plus the unlimited slope; higher-order reconstructions including ENO, WENO3, WENO5, their WENO-Z variants and central WENO; finite elements with discontinuous elements of degree 0 to 4, with and without limiting or a jump penalty, and continuous elements of degree 1 to 3 with graph viscosity, continuous interior penalty, volume interior penalty, SUPG, GLS or no stabilization; and finite-difference stencils including summation-by-parts operators of order 2 and 4.

The invariant domain of the saturation is the interval \([0,1]\); it is one object, ScalarInterval, given to the discontinuous-Galerkin limiter and to the graph viscosity.

Schemes that must fail at a shock, and their stabilized counterparts

Linear schemes of higher than first order oscillate at a discontinuity (Godunov's theorem): MUSCL and QUICK without a limiter, discontinuous Galerkin without limiting or with only a jump penalty, continuous Galerkin without stabilization or with a linear SUPG/GLS term, central and summation-by-parts differences. Essentially non-oscillatory reconstructions (ENO, WENO) and the smooth limiters (Venkatakrishnan, TVB) are not strictly bounded either: their small overshoots at the inlet jump already leave \([0,1]\). Outside \([0,1]\) the fractional-flow law has no meaning; the library stops the run instead of clipping it. For each such scheme the matrix also runs the counterpart that adds exactly one ingredient — for finite volumes the invariant-domain limiter that keeps every face state in \([0,1]\) — shown behind a grey hatching.

Time integrators: the same catalogue as for every other conservation law. Implicit stages take a Newton solver with a coloured finite-difference Jacobian and a sparse direct solve; the linearly implicit Rosenbrock stages take the sparse direct solve alone. The implicit step is 1.4 times the explicit CFL limit of the fastest characteristic. Implicit stages use damped Newton: the S-shaped flux makes undamped Newton iterates overshoot \([0,1]\) at that step although the converged stage solution stays bounded (see the solver matrix).

Only backward Euler keeps the saturation in \([0,1]\) at every step size (it is unconditionally strong-stability preserving). Crank-Nicolson, the Adams-Moulton methods and BDF3 to BDF6 preserve the bounds of the limited MUSCL scheme only below a CFL number of about one; at the implicit step they overshoot at the shock and must fail. Their counterpart is the same method at a quarter of the step. A fixed-point (Picard) iteration contracts only below the inverse spectral radius of the stage operator (counterpart: an eighth of the step); undamped Newton, quasi-Newton and JFNK iterations can overshoot \([0,1]\) before they converge (counterpart: half the step). Leapfrog is undamped and unstable on the dissipative upwind operator; its counterpart adds the Robert-Asselin-Williams filter at a reduced safety factor. The adaptive Dormand-Prince pair is not strong-stability preserving either (its stages combine with negative weights) and overshoots the inlet jump; no single added ingredient makes it bounded, so it is reported as failed where it does.

The test matrix

1. Space × numerical flux — every spatial scheme with every flux, advanced with SSP-RK2 at the stable step (safety factor 0.5). 2. Time integrator × space — every integrator with the monotone upwind flux on first-order, MUSCL-minmod and WENO5 volumes; implicit integrators on the first two at 1.4 times the CFL limit. 3. Nonlinear × linear solver — backward Euler on MUSCL-minmod with the monotone upwind flux.

A few representative combinations are repeated on 200 cells for the mesh check.

Run everything

A stabilized counterpart is run only where the declared combination did not run. One loop, the same problem object every time. A combination the library refuses is recorded with its validation message; a run that stops because the saturation left \([0,1]\) or a solver did not converge is recorded as failed.

What the library refused, and what failed

A refused combination is caught by validation or binding before any time step; a failed one started and left the admissible saturation range.

Methods that do not apply to the waterflood

HLLC restores a contact wave between its two outer signals; a scalar conservation law has no contact wave, and the library refuses HLLC with a message that names HLL, HLLE and Rusanov. The linear upwind flux and the central flux need a linear (or explicitly linearized) flux and are refused for the S-shaped fractional flow with a message that names a monotone flux instead. Four groups still fail without a running counterpart and are reported as failed: finite- difference WENO5 with global Lax-Friedrichs flux splitting has no bounded (invariant-domain) variant yet; the Jameson-Schmidt-Turkel dissipation added to the 4th-order and summation-by-parts differences is not strong enough to keep the inlet jump inside \([0,1]\); WENO5 and WENO5-Z with the Rusanov flux overshoot even with the face limiter at the default step; and the adaptive Dormand-Prince pair is not strong-stability preserving. Conjugate gradients and algebraic multigrid need a symmetric positive definite operator; the upwinded transport operator is not symmetric. Runge-Kutta-Chebyshev and -Legendre super-steps are for parabolic operators; hydrostatic reconstruction is for shallow water and Scharfetter-Gummel fluxes are for drift-diffusion.

Going further

Change the viscosity ratio: a more viscous oil (\(m<1\) smaller) lowers the shock saturation and speeds up the front. Add capillary diffusion or gravity by replacing the fractional-flow object; the matrix code stays as it is.
