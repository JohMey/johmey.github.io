Landau damping: one plasma, every kinetic method

One collisionless electron plasma is solved twice: as a distribution function on a phase-space grid (Vlasov-Poisson) and as macro-particles (particle-in-cell). Every phase-space reconstruction and face flux, every particle shape function, every public time integrator and the matrix-free stage solvers are combined; each run is graded against the root of the kinetic dispersion relation. 260 runs on the compiled OneSimulation runtime: 159 ran as declared, 22 failed as the mathematics predicts and are shown hatched with their stabilized counterpart where one exists, none was refused by the library.

Electrons with a Maxwellian velocity distribution \(f_0(v)=e^{-v^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\) move through a neutralizing ion background in a periodic box of length \(L=2\pi/k\), \(k=0.5\) (units of the Debye length, the plasma frequency and the thermal speed). A small density perturbation

\[f(x,v,0)=\big(1+\alpha\cos kx\big)\,f_0(v)\]

launches a Langmuir wave. The Vlasov-Poisson system

\[\partial_t f+v\,\partial_x f-E\,\partial_v f=0,\qquad \partial_x E=1-\int f\,dv\]

is declared once. Everything numerical is an argument: the phase-space reconstruction and face flux, the particle shape function and the time integrator with its stage solvers.

Reference. For small \(\alpha\) the field oscillates at the complex frequency \(\omega=\omega_r-i\gamma\) that solves \(1+\big(1+\zeta Z(\zeta)\big)/k^2=0\), \(\zeta=\omega/(\sqrt2 k)\), with the plasma dispersion function \(Z(\zeta)=i\sqrt\pi\,w(\zeta)\) (Faddeeva function \(w\); Fried and Conte, 1961). For \(k=0.5\): \(\omega_r\approx1.4156\), \(\gamma\approx0.1533\).

Grading. The electric field energy \(W_E(t)\) oscillates at \(2\omega_r\) inside the envelope \(e^{-2\gamma t}\). Its maxima for \(1<t<14\) give the measured damping rate (slope of \(\ln W_E\)) and frequency (spacing of the maxima); the error is \(|\gamma_h-\gamma|/\gamma\).

The physics, declared once

The phase-space grid has \(64\times128\) cells on \([0,L)\times[-6,6]\); the particle model uses 64 cells and \(1024\times128\) macro-particles placed on a regular phase-space lattice and weighted with \(f(x,v,0)\) (a quiet start, so the particle noise stays far below the wave). scale reduces all counts for a quick check.

scale = 1.0
k, alpha, end_time = 0.5, 0.01, 15.0
length = 2 * np.pi / k
nx, nv = int(64 * scale), int(128 * scale)
x = length * (np.arange(nx) + 0.5) / nx
v = -6 + (np.arange(nv) + 0.5) * 12 / nv
maxwellian = np.exp(-(v**2) / 2)
maxwellian /= maxwellian.sum() * (12 / nv)  # discrete normalization: exact neutrality
electrons = SpeciesSet((SpeciesDefinition("e", "electron", charge_number=-1),))
landau = VlasovPoissonRequest(
    DistributionFunctionState(
        electrons, {"e": (1 + alpha * np.cos(k * x[:, None])) * maxwellian}, (nx,), (v,)
    ),
    VlasovPoissonEquationContract(1, 1),
)
coarse_landau = VlasovPoissonRequest(
    DistributionFunctionState(
        electrons,
        {
            "e": (1 + alpha * np.cos(k * (length * (np.arange(nx // 2) + 0.5) / (nx // 2)))[:, None])
            * np.exp(-((-6 + (np.arange(nv // 2) + 0.5) * 24 / nv) ** 2) / 2)
            / (np.exp(-((-6 + (np.arange(nv // 2) + 0.5) * 24 / nv) ** 2) / 2).sum() * 24 / nv)
        },
        (nx // 2,),
        (-6 + (np.arange(nv // 2) + 0.5) * 24 / nv,),
    ),
    VlasovPoissonEquationContract(1, 1),
)
vlasov, coarse_vlasov = (
    PeriodicVlasov(
        mesh=TensorCollocationMesh(
            axes=(length * (np.arange(n) + 0.5) / n,), coordinate_system="cartesian", periodic=(True,)
        ),
        background_charge_density=1.0,
    )
    for n in (nx, nx // 2)
)
cells, lattice_x, lattice_v, pic_alpha = int(64 * scale), int(1024 * scale), int(128 * scale), 0.05
x0 = length * (np.arange(lattice_x) + 0.5) / lattice_x
v0 = -6 + (np.arange(lattice_v) + 0.5) * 12 / lattice_v
particle_velocities = np.zeros((lattice_x * lattice_v, 3))
particle_velocities[:, 0] = np.tile(v0, lattice_x)
particles = PICSpecies(
    positions=np.repeat(x0, lattice_v)[:, None],
    velocities=particle_velocities,
    charge=-1.0,
    mass=1.0,
    weights=np.outer(1 + pic_alpha * np.cos(k * x0), np.exp(-(v0**2) / 2)).ravel()
    * length
    / np.outer(1 + pic_alpha * np.cos(k * x0), np.exp(-(v0**2) / 2)).sum(),
)
print(
    f"Vlasov: {nx} x {nv} = {nx * nv} phase-space cells; PIC: {cells} cells, "
    f"{particles.positions.shape[0]} macro-particles"
)

The exact damping rate and the two-stream growth rate

The Landau root of \(1+(1+\zeta Z(\zeta))/k^2=0\), and the growth rate of two warm counter-streaming beams (\(\pm u\), thermal spread \(\sigma\)), the real root of \(1+\tfrac12\sum_\pm\big(1+\zeta_\pm Z(\zeta_\pm)\big)/(k\sigma)^2=0\) with \(\zeta_\pm=(i\Gamma/k\mp u)/(\sqrt2\sigma)\).

The method catalogue

Phase space. The transport in \(x\) and \(v\) is a conservative finite-volume flux. The face traces are constant (first order) or MUSCL-linear with no limiter (Fromm), with one of four slope limiters, or minmod plus a positivity-preserving trace limiter; the face flux is upwind or the non-dissipative central average. The field comes from a spectral Poisson solve.

Particles. Deposition and interpolation share one shape function: nearest grid point (B-spline of degree 0), cloud-in-cell (degree 1), triangular-shaped cloud (degree 2) and the cubic B-spline.

Time. Every public integrator. Implicit stages are solved matrix-free: Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov (default) or a fixed-point iteration; Rosenbrock stages by GMRES with finite-difference Jacobian products.

A linear reconstruction without limiter, and the central flux on any traces, are not monotone: by Godunov's theorem they create undershoots where f is small (the velocity tails), and the library stops when f turns negative instead of clipping it. Their stabilized counterpart adds the positivity-preserving trace limiter to the same reconstruction and flux.

Leapfrog has no damping; on the dissipative upwind transport its computational mode grows, and it does not preserve positivity, so f turns negative within the first steps. Its stabilized counterpart is the same leapfrog on the non-dissipative central face flux with the same reconstruction, the classical leapfrog pairing. BDF4 to BDF6 and Adams-Moulton 3 and 4 are not A-stable, and a fixed-point iteration contracts only for steps below the inverse spectral radius; at the implicit step \(\Delta t=0.1\) they may fail, and their counterpart is the same method at a step it is stable for.

The test matrix

1. Phase-space reconstruction × face flux (Vlasov), SSP-RK3 at the transport stability step. 2. Time integrator × phase space (Vlasov): every integrator on first-order upwind, MUSCL-minmod and MUSCL-van Leer fluxes; explicit integrators at the transport stability step (\(\Delta t\approx0.015\)), implicit ones at \(\Delta t=0.1\), beyond any explicit limit. 3. Stage solver (Vlasov): Crank-Nicolson and SDIRK2 with every nonlinear/linear pair, Rosenbrock-Euler with every linear solver. 4. Time integrator × shape function (PIC): every integrator with cloud-in-cell, every shape function with the library's own kick-drift particle-field step and with classical RK4, \(\Delta t=0.1\) (explicit) and \(0.2\) (implicit). 5. Two-stream instability: Vlasov and PIC with four integrators. 6. Mesh check: representative Vlasov combinations on a \(32\times64\) grid.

Run everything

A refused combination is recorded with its message; a run that stops because the distribution turned negative, a stage solve did not converge or the state left the finite range is recorded as failed. The two-stream runs use beams at \(\pm1\) with spread \(0.14\) in the same box, on \(v\in[-2.3,2.3]\). They are seeded with the linear eigenfunction of the growing mode, \(\delta f\propto\mathrm{Re}\big[e^{ikx}\,(-ik f_0'(v))/(\Gamma+ikv)\big]\), so the field energy grows as \(e^{2\Gamma t}\) from the start; the growth rate is the slope of \(\tfrac12\ln W_E\) for \(1<t<5\).

Methods that do not apply

Newton with an assembled Jacobian and sparse direct or preconditioned linear solvers need a stage matrix; the phase-space stage couples every cell through the Poisson solve, so the kinetic stages are solved matrix-free (JFNK, fixed point, GMRES without preconditioner) and the assembled solvers are refused with that reason. Without a preconditioner, right and flexible GMRES generate the same Krylov iterates as GMRES. The electromagnetic particle model advances its fields with a discrete Maxwell step that has no rate form, so generic time methods drive the electrostatic model only.
