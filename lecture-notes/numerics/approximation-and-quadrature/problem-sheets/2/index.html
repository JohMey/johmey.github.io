Connect two resting positions

A carriage moves from 0 to 4 m in two seconds, starting and finishing at rest. Find the unique cubic trajectory satisfying these four endpoint data. Determine midpoint speed and the two endpoint accelerations. Can this cubic join a stationary segment with continuous acceleration?

Worked solution

With \(s=t/2\) the trajectory is \(4(3s^2-2s^3)\). Differentiating with respect to time gives \(v=12s(1-s)\) m/s, so \(v(1\ {\rm s})=3\) m/s. Acceleration is \(6(1-2s)\) m/s², giving \(+6\) and \(-6\) at the endpoints.

The velocity can join rest continuously. The stationary segment has zero acceleration, so this particular cubic cannot also give a continuous acceleration there. Enforcing both endpoint accelerations adds two constraints, motivating a higher-degree segment.

Identify what a natural endpoint buys

At \(x=0,1,2\) a rod has displacements \(0,2,0\). Construct the first segment of the natural cubic spline and find its slope at 0 and its value at 0.5. Compare with the quadratic through all three data.

Worked solution

The natural curvatures are \(M_0=M_2=0\). The interior equation gives \(4M_1=6(0-4+0)=-24\), so \(M_1=-6\). Integrate \(S''=-6x\) and impose the first two values to get \(S=3x-x^3\). The initial slope is 3 and midpoint value 1.375.

The global quadratic is \(4x-2x^2\), with initial slope 4 and midpoint value 1.5. Both match every measured value. The difference is detectable in unmeasured derivatives and in the interior prediction because the quadratic has nonzero end curvature.

Recover a missing quadratic load

On a unit-width panel a power signal is quadratic. Its endpoint values are 2 W and 6 W; its measured energy over one second is 3 W·s. Infer its midpoint value, compare the midpoint and trapezoidal estimates, and determine the sign of curvature.

Worked solution

Simpson integrates the quadratic exactly, so \(3=(2+4P_m+6)/6\), giving \(P_m=2.5\) W. The midpoint estimate is 2.5 J and the trapezoidal estimate is 4 J. Their weighted combination \((2\cdot2.5+4)/3=3\) J reproduces the measured energy.

The signal midpoint lies below the endpoint chord value 4 W, so the curvature is positive. These conclusions use the stipulated quadratic law; the same three observations cannot uniquely determine an arbitrary power history.
