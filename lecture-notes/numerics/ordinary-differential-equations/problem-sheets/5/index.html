Predict a numerical period

For velocity Verlet with \(h=0.5\), \(\omega=2\), compute the stability indicator and period in time units. Starting with \(q=0,p=2\), give the first step and verify the modified energy.

Worked solution

\(z=h\omega=1\) gives \(\cos\theta=1-1/2=1/2\), hence \(\theta=\pi/3\). Six steps make a period of 3 time units, versus the exact period \(\pi\).

The first half kick is zero, the drift gives \(q=1\), and the last half kick leaves \(p=1\). With \(c=4(1-1/4)=3\), the modified energy \((p^2+3q^2)/2\) takes the value 2 at both states.

Initial data also carry the parameter

For \(y'=-\alpha y,\ y(0)=\alpha\), find \(S=\partial y/\partial\alpha\) directly and from its differential equation. At what positive observation time is the first-order sensitivity zero if \(\alpha=2\)?

Worked solution

Direct differentiation of \(y=\alpha e^{-\alpha t}\) gives \(S=e^{-\alpha t}(1-\alpha t)\). The tangent equation is \(S'=-\alpha S-y\), with \(S(0)=1\). Its integrating factor produces \(e^{\alpha t}S=1-\alpha t\).

At \(t=1/\alpha=0.5\), the initial-amplitude increase and the increased decay rate cancel to first order. Setting the initial tangent to zero would miss this cancellation completely.

An accumulated output

Let \(y'=\alpha\), \(y(0)=0\), \(Q=\int_0^T y(t)\,dt\). Compute \(dQ/d\alpha\) directly. Derive an adjoint with terminal value zero by integrating \(zS'\) by parts, and check \(T=3\).

Worked solution

The trajectory is \(\alpha t\), so \(Q=\alpha T^2/2\). The tangent satisfies \(S'=1\), \(S(0)=0\). To recover \(\int S\), choose \(-z'=1,\ z(T)=0\), giving \(z=T-t\).

Integration by parts yields \(\int_0^T S=\int_0^T zS'\) because the boundary product vanishes. Integrating \(T-t\) gives \(T^2/2=4.5\) for \(T=3\), the same gradient.
