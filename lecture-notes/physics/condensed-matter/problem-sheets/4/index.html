1. A metal wire through a temperature sweep

A simple metal has \(n=5.0\times10^{28}\) m⁻³, \(m^*=m_e\), \(v_F=1.2\times10^6\) m/s and \(\rho=2.0\times10^{-8}\) Ω m. (a) Infer its transport lifetime and mean free path. (b) If resistivity falls by a factor ten on cooling, predict the new length at fixed \(n,m^*\). (c) What extra limitation could arise in a 100 nm wire?

Carry the conductivity estimate into a length

Use \(\tau=m_e/(ne^2\rho)\) with \(e=1.602\times10^{-19}\) C to get \(3.55\times10^{-14}\) s; multiplying by \(v_F\) gives 42.6 nm. At ten times smaller resistivity the nominal path is 426 nm. Carriers may reach boundaries before losing directed momentum through bulk scattering. Surface specularity and wire size then matter, so assigning all the resistance to a bulk lifetime can mislead.

2. How much heat could electrons carry?

A sample at 100 K has \(\rho=5.0\times10^{-8}\) Ω m and total \(\kappa=72\) W/(m K). (a) Estimate electronic and lattice contributions using \(L_0=2.44\times10^{-8}\) W Ω K⁻². (b) Compare the measured ratio \(\kappa\rho/T\) with \(L_0\). (c) Can the difference alone establish a failure of the electronic Wiedemann–Franz law?

Evaluate the residual contribution conditionally

The electronic estimate \(L_0T/\rho=48.8\) W/(m K) leaves 23.2 W/(m K) for other contributions under this model. The total measured ratio is \(3.60\times10^{-8}\) W Ω K⁻². A positive lattice heat current alone could account for the excess. Independent evidence for the electronic fraction or scattering mechanism is needed to test the electronic law.

3. What does one oscillation frequency measure?

A two-dimensional electron gas with a circular pocket and two spin states shows one reciprocal-field oscillation frequency \(F=50\) T. (a) Find the pocket area and radius from the quantisation rule. (b) Estimate sheet density. (c) What changes if there are two identical valleys?

Count states in reciprocal space

\(A_k=2\pi eF/\hbar=4.77\times10^{17}\) m⁻² and \(k_F=\sqrt{A_k/\pi}=3.90\times10^8\) m⁻¹. Counting both spins gives \(n_{2D}=2 A_k/(2\pi)^2=2.42\times10^{16}\) m⁻². Two identical valleys double the total density but do not generate a second distinct frequency. A frequency alone therefore cannot always establish degeneracy.
