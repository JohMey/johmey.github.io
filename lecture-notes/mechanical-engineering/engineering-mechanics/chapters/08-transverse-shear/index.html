Transverse shear and connections in built-up beams

Why bending generates an interface force

Stack two boards and support them as a beam. Under a transverse load their neighbouring surfaces tend to slide. A bond or row of fasteners connects the boards so they can act as one deeper section. To calculate the demand on that connection, isolate only the material above the interface over a short beam length. Even when the external load points vertically, horizontal equilibrium of this isolated piece requires longitudinal shear at its lower face.

Under elastic bending about a centroidal principal axis, longitudinal stress is \(\sigma_x=My/I\) up to the chosen sign. Its resultant on the isolated partial area \(A'\) has magnitude \(N=M Q/I\), where \(Q=\int_{A'} y\,dA\) is its first moment about the neutral axis of the complete section. For constant cross-section, the difference between adjacent resultants is \(dN=(Q/I)\,dM\). The balancing interface force is \(q\,dx\).

\[q=\frac{dN}{dx}=\frac{VQ}{I},\qquad \tau=\frac{q}{b_i}.\]

Here \(b_i\) is the local contacting width, and \(dM/dx=V\) for the sign convention used. Shear flow has units N/mm and stress has units N/mm². Evaluate \(Q\) with the portion on one side of the cut and \(I\) with the complete section. Using the second moment of an individual board defeats the assumption that the connection makes them share a curvature.

Select fastener spacing for an assembled beam

Two boards each 60 mm wide and 40 mm deep form an 80 mm deep rectangular beam. At a section carrying \(V=2.4\,\mathrm{kN}\), the complete centroidal second moment is \(60(80)^3/12=2.56\times10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}\). The upper board has area 2400 mm² and its centroid lies 20 mm above the interface, which coincides with the full neutral axis. Hence \(Q=48000\,\mathrm{mm^3}\).

The required flow is \(2400(48000)/(2.56\times10^6)=45\,\mathrm{N/mm}\). An adhesive over the full contact width would carry an average longitudinal shear stress of \(45/60=0.75\,\mathrm{MPa}\). A single row of connectors rated for 900 N each in the idealised shear mode would require a spacing no greater than \(900/45=20\,\mathrm{mm}\). For a load varying significantly over a connector spacing, integrate the flow over its tributary length instead.

Recover the shear profile in a rectangle

At any elevation \(y\) in that rectangle the partial area extends from \(y\) to \(h/2\). Its first moment is \(Q=b[(h/2)^2-y^2]/2\). Substituting \(I=bh^3/12\) gives \(\tau(y)=[3V/(2bh)](1-4y^2/h^2)\). The stress vanishes at the top and bottom free surfaces, reaching one and a half times the section-average transverse shear at mid-depth. The elastic bending stress, in contrast, vanishes at that mid-depth. A joint placed near the neutral axis can therefore carry substantial shear even when its local normal bending stress is small.

Where should fasteners become more closely spaced?

Under a uniform transverse load a simply supported beam has largest shear magnitude near the supports and zero shear at its symmetric midspan. With the same cross-section along the span, the connector force per unit length follows that shear diagram. Midspan may govern bending strength while the regions near the supports govern connector spacing.

Locate a transverse load that produces bending without twist

For a cross-section with one axis of symmetry, the shear centre lies somewhere on that axis. It can fall outside the section itself. To see why, consider a thin open channel with horizontal flanges of median length \(b\), a vertical web of median height \(h\) and uniform wall thickness \(t\). Both flanges extend to the right of the web. Apply a vertical shear \(V\); seek the transverse location of its line of action for zero torsional moment. Assume an undeformed cross-section and neglect terms of order \(t/h\) and \(t/b\).

Measure \(s\) from each free flange edge towards the web. The partial flange area for the longitudinal shear-flow formula is \(ts\), located about \(h/2\) from the horizontal neutral axis. Thus \(q_f(s)=Vtsh/(2I)\) in magnitude. Integrating along one flange gives horizontal resultant \(F_f=Vthb^2/(4I)\). Upper and lower flange forces point in opposite directions and form a couple of magnitude \(F_f h\). The web shear acts along the vertical web median and contributes no moment about that line. The vertical resultant must therefore be offset from the web by

\[e=\frac{F_f h}{V}=\frac{tb^2h^2}{4I},\qquad I\simeq\frac{th^3}{12}+2bt(h/2)^2 .\]

Following the directions of the shear flows places the shear centre on the side of the web opposite the flanges. In a symmetric I-section, left and right flange couples cancel and the shear centre instead lies on the intersecting symmetry axes.

Compare centroidal and twist-free load paths

A thin channel has \(h=80\) mm, \(b=30\) mm and \(t=2.0\) mm. Its horizontal-axis second moment is \(2(80)^3/12+2(30)(2)(40)^2=277333\,\mathrm{mm^4}\). The shear centre offset is \(2(30)^2(80)^2/[4(277333)]=10.38\,\mathrm{mm}\) to the left of the web. A 1.50 kN vertical force through the web therefore imposes a torque of magnitude \(1500(10.38)=15570\,\mathrm{N\,mm}=15.6\,\mathrm{N\,m}\) as well as bending. That additional torque can matter because a thin open section is relatively compliant in torsion.

The material centroid lies on the flange side at \(x_c=2(bt)(b/2)/(th+2bt)=6.43\,\mathrm{mm}\) to the right of the web. Loading through it increases the lever arm relative to the shear centre to 16.81 mm, giving 25.2 N m of torque. A bracket that transfers the load to the left of the web instead can reduce twist without enlarging the section. For a real channel with finite corner radii or unequal flange thicknesses, use its measured section geometry. The result also describes a free member; bracing against twist can replace a visible rotation by torsional reactions at the restraints.

One way to locate the shear centre experimentally is to apply the same small transverse load at several lateral offsets and measure twist per unit length in a region free of local load introduction. Linear elastic torsion predicts an approximately linear dependence of twist on offset. Its zero crossing locates the twist-free load line without requiring the torsion constant beforehand. A fixture restraining warping near the gauges can distort this test.
