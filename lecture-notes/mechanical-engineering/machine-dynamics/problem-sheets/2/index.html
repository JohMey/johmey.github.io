Frequency response and modes

Use complex dynamic stiffness and modal symmetry to predict what a force and a sensor can observe.

Problem sheet 2 · Chapters 3 and 4

1. Inspect a forced oscillator2. Invert measured modal frequencies3. Choose the force pattern

1. Inspect a forced oscillator

A 2.0 kg mass on a 200 N/m spring and 4.0 N·s/m damper is driven at 10 rad/s by a 1.0 N peak sinusoidal force. (a) Find the displacement amplitude and phase. (b) Find average power. (c) Repeat the amplitude calculation at 5 rad/s with the same force.

Solve the phasor balance

The natural angular frequency is 10 rad/s. At this speed the real dynamic stiffness vanishes, giving \(|X|=1/(4\cdot10)=0.0250\) m and 90° displacement lag. The velocity peak is 0.250 m/s and average input power is \(1(0.25)/2=0.125\) W. At 5 rad/s the dynamic stiffness is \(150+20i\) N/m, hence \(|X|=1/\sqrt{150^2+20^2}=6.61\) mm. Proximity to resonance has a larger effect than the static-force displacement alone suggests.

2. Invert measured modal frequencies

Two identical 4.0 kg masses have equal ground springs and a shared coupling spring. Their measured angular frequencies are 10 rad/s in phase and 16 rad/s in opposition. (a) Infer both stiffnesses. (b) Apply displacements +3 mm and −1 mm. Decompose this state into the two modes. (c) Predict the average displacement over time after release from rest.

Separate symmetric and antisymmetric coordinates

The first mode gives \(k=m\omega_s^2=400\) N/m. The other gives \(k_c=(4(16)^2-400)/2=312\) N/m. The initial mean is 1 mm and the initial half-difference is 2 mm, corresponding to \(1(1,1)+2(1,-1)\) mm. With zero initial velocities both modes start as cosines. Averaging the two signals removes the antisymmetric term, leaving \(1\,\mathrm{mm}\cos(10t)\). A single average sensor would miss a mode that is actually populated.

3. Choose the force pattern

Use the preceding system. Two actuators apply harmonic force amplitudes \(F\) and \(-F\) simultaneously. (a) Find the drive of the mean coordinate and half-difference coordinate. (b) Which frequency is resonant? (c) If one actuator fails, which resonances can now appear?

Project the load into each mode

Adding the two physical equations and dividing by two gives forcing \((F_1+F_2)/2\) for the mean, which is zero. Subtracting gives \((F_1-F_2)/2=F\) for the half-difference. Only the antisymmetric mode is driven, at 16 rad/s. With one actuator operating, the force pattern decomposes as \((F,0)=(F/2)(1,1)+(F/2)(1,-1)\), so both 10 and 16 rad/s can produce resonant responses. Real damping limits amplitudes in a steady sweep.
