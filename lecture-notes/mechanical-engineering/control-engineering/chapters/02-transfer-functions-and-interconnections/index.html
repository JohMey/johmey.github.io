Transfer functions and connections between systems

Build a model of a sensor-actuator chain from individual balances and determine which responses a measurement can reveal.

Eliminate a state in the transform domain

Chapter 1 gave a differential equation and an initial condition for a single energy store. Cascading several components leads to simultaneous equations. The Laplace transform changes the time derivative into \(sY(s)-y(0)\). Applied to \(\tau\dot y+y=Kv\), this gives \((\tau s+1)Y=KV+\tau y(0)\). With zero initial deviation the ratio \(G(s)=Y/V=K/(\tau s+1)\) describes the forced response. For nonzero initial state the extra term predicts free decay. This is why an experimental transfer function should identify a particular operating point and initialisation procedure.

The pole at \(s=-1/\tau\) locates the decaying exponential. The DC gain is \(G(0)=K\). The high-frequency magnitude of this model tends to zero because the energy store cannot change its state instantaneously in response to bounded power. A transfer function can thus be assessed from dimensions, a static balance and a short-time thought experiment before any curve fitting.

Connect the sensor and plant

A sensor may have its own lag. Let the actual plant output be \(y\) and the indicated value \(z\). With plant \(G_p=1/(4s+1)\) and sensor \(G_s=1/(s+1)\), the individual relations \(Y=G_p U\) and \(Z=G_s Y\) give \(Z/U=1/[(4s+1)(s+1)]\). For a unit step divide by \(s\); partial fractions or substitution in the differential equation gives

\[z(t)=1-\frac43e^{-t/4}+\frac13e^{-t}.\]

Check \(z(0)=0\), \(z(\infty)=1\) and \(\dot z(0)=0\). The actual plant slope begins at \(1/4\) per second, but the indicated output starts with a horizontal tangent. At 4 s the plant has reached 0.632 and the indicator only 0.516. A designer who assigns the entire delay to the plant may demand an unnecessarily powerful actuator. A designer who ignores the sensor will underestimate the phase lag in feedback.

Close a loop algebraically

Let \(C(s)\) be a controller and use the indicated output in negative feedback, so \(U=C(R-G_sY)\). Substitute this in \(Y=G_pU\) and collect all terms multiplying \(Y\). The result is \(Y/R=G_pC/(1+G_pCG_s)\). The same denominator occurs in other responses, but the numerator depends on where the signal enters. A disturbance \(D\) added at the plant input gives \(Y/D=G_p/(1+G_pCG_s)\) with zero reference. Measurement noise added to the feedback signal gives \(Y/N=-G_pC/(1+G_pCG_s)\). Forcing every disturbance to enter at the reference would predict the wrong amplitudes.

Recognise an invisible mode

A rational expression may simplify by cancellation. Consider a cascade of an integrator-like unstable stage \(1/(s-1)\) and a downstream stage \((s-1)/(s+2)\). The algebraic transfer from external input to final output is \(1/(s+2)\) at zero initial conditions. The state between stages can still grow as \(e^t\). A small mismatch in either factor also leaves an unstable component in the observed output. Physically realising two factors gives internal variables that still need checking even when their product has a well-behaved step response.

For a stable example, the measured lag above can be estimated with a first-order fit from late observations alone. The resulting single pole describes the slow tail but loses the zero initial slope. Choosing the level of model detail therefore requires stating the quantity of interest: a long-time energy estimate and a rapid feedback correction need different bandwidths. The next chapter uses the true characteristic polynomial to determine stability before assessing accuracy of tracking.

Derive two interacting liquid levels before drawing blocks

It is tempting to represent every pair of connected tanks as a cascade of two independent first-order blocks. If the pipe between the tanks is submerged at both ends, the downstream level applies a back pressure to the upstream tank. Consider small deviations \(h_1,h_2\) from equilibrium, with positive forward intertank flow \(q_{12}=(h_1-h_2)/R_1\) and outlet flow \(q_o=h_2/R_2\). The incremental hydraulic resistances measure level difference per flow and have units s/m². They can represent local tangent slopes of nonlinear valve characteristics. Take constant tank areas \(A_1,A_2\) and a perturbation \(u\) of inlet flow.

The two balances are \(A_1\dot h_1=u-q_{12}\) and \(A_2\dot h_2=q_{12}-q_o\). For zero initial deviations, write \(H_1=(1+R_1/R_2+A_2R_1s)H_2\) from the second balance and substitute it into the first. Collecting equal powers of \(s\) leads to

\[\frac{H_2}{U}=\frac{R_2}{A_1A_2R_1R_2s^2+[A_1(R_1+R_2)+A_2R_2]s+1}.\]

The static gain equals \(R_2\), since at equilibrium all three volume rates are equal and \(h_2=R_2u\). The coefficient of \(s\) contains an interaction term absent if tank 1 drains freely to atmosphere. It describes the return action of the downstream level on intertank flow. An individual transfer block measured with downstream level fixed does not capture this additional path unless it is reconnected explicitly.

Distinguish a loaded connection from a cascade in a step test

Use \(A_1=A_2=0.10\,\mathrm{m^2}\) and \(R_1=R_2=100\,\mathrm{s/m^2}\). Each isolated area–resistance product is 10 s. The interacting transfer function is \(100/(100s^2+30s+1)\). Its decay rates are \((-30\pm\sqrt{900-400})/200=-0.0382\) and \(-0.2618\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), giving time constants 26.18 and 3.82 s. Two independent identical drains would instead give \(100/(10s+1)^2\), with repeated time constant 10 s. Both have the same final gain and initial zero slope at the downstream tank.

For a sustained flow increase of \(0.00050\,\mathrm{m^3/s}\), both models predict a 0.050 m final rise. Using the two interacting time constants, the normalised step value at 20 s is \(1-[26.18e^{-20/26.18}-3.82e^{-20/3.82}]/(26.18-3.82)\simeq0.455\), giving 22.8 mm. The independent cascade predicts \(1-(1+2)e^{-2}=0.594\), or 29.7 mm. Recording intermediate levels therefore distinguishes two plant structures that an equilibrium calibration cannot separate. As \(R_1\) approaches zero the two levels equalise rapidly; the limiting first-order time constant becomes \((A_1+A_2)R_2\), consistent with a single tank of combined storage area.

There is also a check that uses both level sensors without differentiating noisy data. Add the original tank balances and integrate over the first 20 s: \(A_1[h_1(20)-h_1(0)]+A_2[h_2(20)-h_2(0)]=\int_0^{20}(u-q_o)\,dt\). The intertank flow disappears identically. In this experiment the added inlet volume is 10.0 litres, so added liquid stored plus additional outlet delivery must equal that quantity. If the two measured storage changes already exceed 10 litres, no passive choice of intertank resistance can explain the data. Conversely, outlet and storage agreement validates the external balance but leaves the internal connection undetermined. The intermediate time reading and knowledge of whether the interconnecting pipe is submerged provide the extra evidence needed for choosing the internal transfer model.
